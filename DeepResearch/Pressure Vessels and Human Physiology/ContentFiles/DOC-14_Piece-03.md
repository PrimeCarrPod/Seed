#### 14.3 Van der Waals Gas Solubility Mechanism

**14.3.1 Physical vs. Chemical Gas Solubility**

**Blood (Chemical — Hemoglobin):**
$$\text{Hb} + 4\text{O}_2 \rightleftharpoons \text{Hb(O}_2)_4$$
- **Cooperative binding** (Hill coefficient n = 2.8)
- **Capacity:** 1.34 mL O₂/g Hb → ~20 mL O₂/100 mL blood
- **pH/CO₂ dependent** (Bohr effect)

**PFCs (Physical — Van der Waals):**
$$\text{O}_{2(g)} \rightleftharpoons \text{O}_{2(\text{dissolved in PFC})}$$
- **Henry's Law:** $C = k_H \cdot P_{O_2}$
- **No chemical binding** — purely physical dissolution
- **Capacity:** Linear with pressure, no saturation

**14.3.2 Van der Waals Interactions — The Solubility Mechanism**

**London Dispersion Forces (Instantaneous Dipole-Induced Dipole):**
$$E_{\text{disp}} = -\frac{3}{4} \frac{\alpha_A \alpha_B I_A I_B}{(\alpha_A + \alpha_B) r^6} \quad \text{(simplified)}$$

Where:
- $\alpha$ = polarizability volume
- $I$ = ionization potential
- $r$ = intermolecular distance

**PFC Polarizability (High despite low α_F):**
- **Fluorine α = 0.56 Å³** (low)
- **But:** Many F atoms per molecule → **cumulative α**
- **C₈F₁₈:** α ≈ 12 Å³ (vs. C₈H₁₈: α ≈ 13 Å³)
- **Effective α per volume:** PFC > hydrocarbon (dense F packing)

**14.3.3 Intramolecular Voids — The "Free Volume" Theory**

**PFC Molecular Architecture:**
- **C-F bond length:** 1.35 Å
- **F van der Waals radius:** 1.47 Å
- **F-F distance (geminal):** ~2.2 Å
- **F-F distance (vicinal):** ~2.7 Å

**Void Creation:**
- **Bulky F atoms** create **inefficient packing**
- **Helical/twisted conformations** (to minimize F-F repulsion)
- **Result:** Nanoscale cavities (0.5–1.5 nm³) throughout liquid

**Free Volume Fraction (f_v):**
$$f_v = 1 - \frac{V_{\text{occupied}}}{V_{\text{molar}}}$$

**Typical Values:**
| Liquid | Molar Volume [cm³/mol] | Free Volume % |
|--------|------------------------|---------------|
| **Water** | 18 | ~2% |
| **n-Octane** | 158 | ~15% |
| **Perfluoro-n-octane** | 235 | **~25%** |
| **Perfluorodecalin** | 285 | **~28%** |

**PFCs have 10–15× MORE free volume than water!**

**14.3.4 Gas Accommodation in Free Volume**

**Gas Molecule Dimensions:**
| Gas | Kinetic Diameter [Å] | Volume [Å³] |
|-----|---------------------|-------------|
| **He** | 2.6 | 9 |
| **O₂** | 3.5 | 22 |
| **N₂** | 3.6 | 24 |
| **CO₂** | 3.3 | 19 |
| **Xe** | 4.1 | 36 |

**PFC Void Size Distribution:**
- **Peak:** ~1 nm diameter (10 Å)
- **Volume per void:** ~500 Å³
- **Accommodates:** 10–20 gas molecules per void

**Gas Solubility Model (Scaled Particle Theory):**
$$\ln k_H = -\frac{\Delta G_{\text{cavity}}}{RT} - \frac{\Delta G_{\text{interaction}}}{RT}$$

**Cavity Formation Energy (Dominant):**
$$\Delta G_{\text{cavity}} \propto \gamma \cdot r_{\text{gas}}^2$$
Where γ = surface tension of PFC (12–18 mN/m, very low)

**Low γ → Low cavity energy → HIGH solubility**

**14.3.5 O₂ vs. CO₂ Solubility — The CO₂ Advantage**

**Experimental Solubilities (at 25°C, 1 atm):**
| Liquid | O₂ [mL/100mL] | CO₂ [mL/100mL] | CO₂/O₂ Ratio |
|--------|---------------|----------------|--------------|
| **Water** | 4.3 | 9.0 | 2.1 |
| **Blood (Hb)** | 20 | 48 | 2.4 |
| **Perfluoro-n-octane** | 42 | **130** | **3.1** |
| **Perfluorodecalin** | 45 | **160** | **3.6** |
| **Perfluoro-tert-butylcyclohexane** | 50 | **196** | **3.9** |

**Why CO₂ > O₂ in PFCs (and water)?**
1. **Smaller kinetic diameter** (CO₂: 3.3 Å vs O₂: 3.5 Å) → easier cavity fit
2. **Higher polarizability** (CO₂: 2.9 Å³ vs O₂: 1.6 Å³) → stronger dispersion
3. **Quadrupole moment** (CO₂: -1.5 × 10⁻²⁶ esu·cm²) → favorable alignment
4. **Higher critical temperature** (CO₂: 31°C vs O₂: -118°C) → stronger condensation tendency

**CO₂/O₂ Ratio in PFCs: 3–4× (vs. 2–2.4× in blood/water)**
**Critical for TLV: CO₂ removal is EASIER than O₂ delivery!**
