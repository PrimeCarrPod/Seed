#### 14.2 C-F Bond Thermodynamics & Biological Inertness

**14.2.1 C-F Bond — The Strongest Single Bond in Organic Chemistry**

**Bond Dissociation Energies (BDE):**
| Bond | BDE [kJ/mol] | BDE [kcal/mol] |
|------|--------------|----------------|
| **C-F** | **485–540** | **116–129** |
| C-H | 410–440 | 98–105 |
| C-C | 345–370 | 82–88 |
| C-Cl | 330–350 | 79–84 |
| C-O | 350–380 | 84–91 |

**C-F is 20–30% STRONGER than C-H** — the thermodynamic basis for inertness.

**14.2.2 Why C-F is So Strong: Electronic Structure**

**Fluorine Properties:**
- **Electronegativity:** χ = 3.98 (highest of all elements)
- **Atomic Radius:** 64 pm (small, but not smallest)
- **Polarizability:** α = 0.56 Å³ (very low)

**C-F Bond Character:**
- **Ionic Character:** ~43% (Pauling: %ionic = 1 - exp[-0.25(χ_A - χ_B)²])
- **Bond Length:** 1.35–1.40 Å (short, strong)
- **Bond Dipole:** ~1.5 D (C⁺δ-F⁻δ)

**Molecular Orbital View:**
- **σ Bond:** C(sp³) - F(2p) overlap
- **High s-character** in C hybrid (sp².⁵–sp³)
- **Low-lying σ* orbital** — poor acceptor (resists nucleophilic attack)

**14.2.3 Kinetic Inertness — The Kinetic Barrier**

**Thermodynamic Stability ≠ Kinetic Inertness**
But for C-F, BOTH are extreme.

**Hydrolysis (The Ultimate Test):**
$$\text{R-F} + \text{H}_2\text{O} \rightarrow \text{R-OH} + \text{HF}$$
**Rate Constant (25°C, neutral pH):** k < 10⁻¹⁵ s⁻¹
**Half-life:** > 10⁷ years (at 25°C)

**Comparison (Alkyl Halide Hydrolysis Rates):**
| Compound | Relative Rate | Half-life (25°C) |
|----------|---------------|------------------|
| **R-I** | 10⁶ | Minutes |
| **R-Br** | 10³ | Hours |
| **R-Cl** | 1 | Days–Weeks |
| **R-F** | **10⁻¹⁰** | **Millennia** |

**14.2.4 Metabolic Inertness — Why Biology Can't Touch PFCs**

**Enzymatic Attack Vectors (All Blocked):**
| Enzyme Class | Target | PFC Susceptibility |
|--------------|--------|-------------------|
| **Cytochrome P450 (CYP)** | C-H oxidation | **NO C-H bonds** |
| **Epoxide Hydrolase** | Epoxide opening | **No double bonds** |
| **Esterase/Lipase** | Ester hydrolysis | **No esters** |
| **Amidase** | Amide hydrolysis | **No amides** |
| **GST (Glutathione S-transferase)** | Electrophilic conjugation | **No electrophilic centers** |
| **Dehydrogenase** | C-H dehydrogenation | **NO C-H bonds** |

**Result:** **Zero metabolic clearance** — PFCs are excreted unchanged.

**14.2.5 Thermal Stability — Extreme Conditions**

**Thermal Decomposition (Pyrolysis):**
- **Onset:** > 500°C (inert atmosphere)
- **Mechanism:** C-C cleavage (weaker than C-F)
- **Products:** CF₄, C₂F₆, C₃F₈, carbon (soot)

**Oxidative Stability:**
- **In Air:** Stable to > 400°C
- **In O₂:** Stable to ~350°C (slow oxidation to COF₂, CO₂)
- **Plasma/Atomic Oxygen:** Slow erosion (space applications)

**14.2.7 Design Principle: "Fluorine Shield"**

**Molecular Architecture for Inertness:**
1. **Complete fluorination** — no C-H, C-Cl, C-Br
2. **Saturated backbone** — no C=C, C≡C
3. **Symmetric/globular** — minimizes reactive surface
3. **Terminal CF₃ groups** — shields chain ends

**This "Fluorine Shield" makes PFCs the ONLY viable TLV fluids.**
