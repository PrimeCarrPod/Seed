#### 2.8 Technology Transfer: Military & Space Applications (1945–1960)

The pressurization technology developed for commercial airliners (307, Constellation, Stratocruiser) directly enabled **military high-altitude operations** and provided the **structural and physiological foundation for human spaceflight**. This section traces the technology transfer pathways.

**2.8.1 Military Bombers & Transports — Pressurization at War**

**B-29 Superfortress (1942) — First Mass-Produced Pressurized Bomber:**
- **Pressurized Compartments:** Fore (crew 5), Aft (gunner 1) — **not full fuselage**
- **ΔP:** 17.2 kPa (2.5 psi) → Cabin altitude 3,650 m at 9,100 m
- **Structure:** Circular fuselage sections (fore/aft), unpressurized bomb bay between
- **Windows:** Circular (gunner sighting) + flat armored (pilot)
- **Production:** 3,970 built — **largest pressurized aircraft fleet pre-1950**
- **Combat Experience:** 30,000+ sorties; **zero pressure-related structural failures**
- **Technology Transfer:** Circular fuselage sections → C-97 → 377; Remote turret pressure seals → EVA glove seals

**B-36 Peacemaker (1946) — Intercontinental Pressurized Bomber:**
- **Pressurized:** Full crew compartment (15 crew), two decks
- **ΔP:** 20.7 kPa (3.0 psi) → Cabin altitude 3,050 m at 12,200 m
- **Fuselage:** 3.66 m diameter circular section (same as 377 lower lobe)
- **Windows:** 12 circular (305 mm) + 4 rectangular (navigator)
- **ECS:** 6-engine driven compressors (redundancy: 4 required)
- **Legacy:** Direct structural ancestor of **C-97/377** (same tooling)

**C-97 Stratofreighter / KC-97 Stratotanker (1944/1950):**
- **Double-Bubble Fuselage:** Lower lobe 3.66 m (cargo), Upper lobe 2.74 m (crew/passengers)
- **Pressurization:** Full fuselage (cargo + crew) — **first full double-bubble pressurized**
- **ΔP:** 34.5 kPa (5.0 psi) — same as 377
- **Production:** 888 built (C-97/KC-97) — **backbone of SAC tanker fleet**
- **Operational Cycles:** 10,000+ per aircraft — **highest cycle count pre-jet**
- **Fatigue Experience:** **Window corner cracks at 8,000 cycles** → doubler retrofit (same as L-1049)
- **Technology Transfer:** Double-bubble → 377; High-cycle fatigue data → FAR 25.571

**C-119 Flying Boxcar (1947) — Tactical Pressurized Transport:**
- **Pressurized:** Crew compartment only (fore fuselage)
- **ΔP:** 17.2 kPa (2.5 psi)
- **Significance:** Demonstrated pressurization in **rough-field tactical aircraft**

**2.8.2 High-Altitude Reconnaissance — The Physiology Frontier**

**RB-36 / RB-47 / RB-57 / U-2 (1950s):**
- **Operational Ceilings:** 15,000–21,000 m (50,000–70,000 ft)
- **Cabin Pressurization:** ΔP up to 34.5 kPa (U-2: 20.7 kPa)
- **Cabin Altitude:** 3,000–4,500 m (10,000–15,000 ft)
- **Physiology:** **Full pressure suits required** (David Clark S-1, MC-3, MC-4)
- **Pre-breathe:** 2 hr 100% O₂ before flight (U-2)
- **Legacy:** **Direct ancestor of NASA EMU pre-breathe protocols**

**X-15 (1959) — Hypersonic Pressure Suit Testbed:**
- **Vehicle:** Rocket-powered, air-launched from B-52
- **Altitude:** 100+ km (space)
- **Pilot Protection:** A/P22S-2 full pressure suit (David Clark)
- **Suit Pressure:** 24 kPa (3.5 psi) 100% O₂
- **Life Support:** 10-min O₂ supply, then aircraft ECS
- **Data:** 199 flights; **zero suit failures** — validated suit architecture for Mercury/Gemini

**2.8.3 Project Mercury (1958–1963) — From Aircraft to Spacecraft**

**Structural Heritage:**
| Mercury Element | Aircraft Ancestor | Technology Transfer |
|-----------------|-------------------|---------------------|
| **Pressure Vessel** | **Titanium conical shell** (new) | Constellation/377 bulkhead design methods |
| **Window** | **Fused silica** (new) | 377 acrylic window stress analysis → fracture mechanics |
| **Hatch** | **Explosive bolts** (new) | 377 door plug seal geometry → O-ring design |
| **ECS** | **Steam boiler + LiOH** | 377 humidification → steam; Constellation LiOH → CO₂ scrubbing |
| **Suit** | **B.F. Goodrich Mark IV** | U-2/X-15 David Clark suit → modified for seated posture |

**Mercury Capsule Pressure Vessel:**
- **Geometry:** Conical frustum (base 3.9 m, height 2.9 m) + cylindrical recovery section
- **Material:** Titanium 6Al-4V (annealed) — **first titanium pressure vessel for human occupancy**
- **Thickness:** 1.5–3.0 mm (tapered)
- **ΔP:** 34.5 kPa (5.0 psi) in vacuum → **external pressure = 0**
- **Design:** **Membrane theory** (thin wall, t/r < 0.02) — no bending
- **Safety Factor:** 1.5 on yield (aerospace standard)
- **Window:** Single rectangular (300 × 300 mm), fused silica, 35 mm thick
  - **Design:** Compressive stress only (external vacuum) → no tensile fatigue

**Mercury ECS (Environmental Control System):**
- **O₂ Supply:** High-pressure spheres (2 × 1,200 L at 34.5 MPa)
- **CO₂ Removal:** LiOH canisters (2 × 2.7 kg) — 24 hr capacity
- **Thermal:** **Steam boiler** (water → steam → heat rejection via sublimator)
- **Humidity:** Condensing heat exchanger (no active humidification)
- **Cabin Gas:** 100% O₂ at 34.5 kPa (5.0 psi) — **same as Stratocruiser ΔP, but pure O₂**

**2.8.4 Gemini (1961–1966) — EVA Capability**

**Gemini Pressure Vessel:**
- **Two-man**, side-by-side, cylindrical + conical
- **Material:** Titanium 6Al-4V + Ren 41 (nickel alloy) heat shield
- **ΔP:** 34.5 kPa (5.0 psi) pure O₂
- **Hatch:** **Outward-opening** (inward on Mercury) for EVA — **first EVA hatch**
- **Window:** 4 circular (150 mm) fused silica — **circular for fatigue (lessons learned)**

**Gemini EVA Suit (G4C / G5C):**
- **Base:** David Clark X-15 suit modified
- **Pressure:** 25.5 kPa (3.7 psi) 100% O₂
- **PLSS:** **Chest-mounted** (not backpack) — umbilical to spacecraft ECS
- **Duration:** 20 min (G4C) → 2 hr (G5C with backpack PLSS)
- **Legacy:** **Direct ancestor of Apollo A7L and Shuttle EMU**

**2.8.5 Apollo (1961–1972) — Lunar Pressure Vessel**

**Command Module (CM):**
- **Geometry:** Conical (base 3.9 m, height 3.2 m) — **same mold line as Mercury**
- **Material:** Stainless steel 321 (welded) + aluminum honeycomb sandwich
- **ΔP:** 34.5 kPa (5.0 psi) 60% O₂ / 40% N₂ (launch) → 100% O₂ at 34.5 kPa (orbit)
- **Windows:** 5 (2 rendezvous, 1 hatch, 2 side) — **fused silica, multi-pane**
- **Hatch:** **Unified hatch** (inward-opening, pressure-sealed, explosive bolts for emergency)
- **ECS:** Glycol coolant loop + steam boiler + LiOH + **suit loop for EVA**

**Lunar Module (LM):**
- **Geometry:** Two-stage (descent + ascent), asymmetric
- **Ascent Stage Pressure Vessel:** Cylindrical (2.3 m dia × 2.3 m h) + front face
- **Material:** Aluminum 2219 (welded) + chem-milled skin
- **ΔP:** 34.5 kPa (5.0 psi) 100% O₂
- **Windows:** 2 triangular (forward) — **first non-rectangular spacecraft windows**
- **Hatch:** **Square** (1.0 × 1.0 m) — **first square hatch** (ingress/egress with PLSS)
  - **Stress Analysis:** Heavy frame reinforcement, K_t managed by thick flanges

**Apollo A7L Suit (ILC Dover):**
- **Pressure:** 25.5 kPa (3.7 psi) 100% O₂
- **Layers:** 21 (bladder, restraint, thermal micrometeoroid garment)
- **Joints:** Convolute (bellows) at shoulders, elbows, wrists, waist, knees, ankles
- **PLSS:** Backpack (O₂, LiOH, battery, radio, sublimator) — **7 hr EVA**
- **Legacy:** **Direct ancestor of Shuttle EMU and ISS EMU**

**2.8.6 Space Shuttle Orbiter (1972–2011) — Reusable Airliner to Orbit**

**Crew Compartment (Pressure Vessel):**
- **Geometry:** Three decks (flight, mid, equipment), cylindrical + bulkheads
- **Volume:** 71.5 m³ (2,525 ft³) — **largest space pressure vessel until ISS**
- **Material:** Aluminum 2219 (fusion welded) + graphite-epoxy bulkheads
- **ΔP:** 101.3 kPa (14.7 psi) — **sea-level pressure** (vs. 34.5 kPa Apollo)
- **Gas:** 21% O₂ / 79% N₂ — **air, not pure O₂** (fire safety post-Apollo 1)
- **Windows:** 6 forward (fused silica, 3-pane), 2 overhead, 2 aft — **all circular/oval**
- **Hatch:** Side hatch (circular, 1.0 m) + airlock hatch (circular, 0.9 m)
- **ECS:** 4 × LiOH canisters (rotating) + flash evaporator + freon coolant loops

**Shuttle EMU (Extravehicular Mobility Unit):**
- **Pressure:** 29.6 kPa (4.3 psi) 100% O₂ — **lower than Apollo (25.5 kPa) for mobility**
- **Pre-breathe:** 4 hr (ISS) or 24 hr at 10.2 kPa + 45 min O₂ (Shuttle)
- **PLSS:** Backpack (primary) + DCM (display/control module) — 7 hr EVA
- **Legacy:** **Current ISS EMU (same design, upgraded)**
