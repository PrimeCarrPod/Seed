### ABSTRACT

This document provides a comprehensive biophysical analysis of **High-Pressure Nervous Syndrome (HPNS)** — the neurological hyperexcitability syndrome that occurs when helium-oxygen (heliox) breathing mixtures are used at depths exceeding ~120 msw (400 fsw / 13 bar). Unlike nitrogen narcosis (lipid bilayer EXPANSION), HPNS results from **hydrostatic compression of the lipid bilayer** that helium fails to counteract due to its low lipid solubility. The document derives the biomechanics of bilayer compression, quantifies the resulting distortion of voltage-gated ion channels (particularly Na⁺ and Ca²⁺ channels), and analyzes the EEG and tremor phenomenology. The countermeasure — **Trimix (He/O₂/N₂)** — is analyzed as an antagonistic therapy: nitrogen's narcotic bilayer expansion precisely opposes helium's compression, restoring bilayer geometry. The document derives the optimal N₂ partial pressure for HPNS suppression at any depth, validates against clinical/experimental data, and extends to future TLV-based HPNS elimination.

**Keywords:** High-Pressure Nervous Syndrome, HPNS, helium tremors, lipid bilayer compression, ion channel distortion, trimix, nitrogen antagonism, antagonistic therapy, antagonistic gases, EEG abnormalities, myoclonic jerking

### 13. HIGH-PRESSURE NERVOUS SYNDROME (HPNS) & TRIMIX COUNTERMEASURES

#### 13.1 HPNS Phenomenology & Clinical Presentation

**13.1.1 Discovery & Historical Context**

**First Reports (1960s):**
- **US Navy Genesis Project (1963):** Heliox dives to 190 msw (620 fsw)
- **Symptoms:** Tremor, nausea, vomiting, drowsiness, EEG slowing
- **Term:** "Helium Tremors" → later "HPNS" (Bennett, 1965)

**13.1.2 Clinical Grading (Bennett Scale)**

| Grade | Depth Onset [msw] | Symptoms | Functional Impact |
|-------|-------------------|----------|-------------------|
| **0** | < 120 | None | Normal |
| **I** | 120–180 | Mild tremor (hands), slight nausea | Minor dexterity loss |
| **II** | 180–250 | Moderate tremor, drowsiness, cognitive slowing | Significant impairment |
| **III** | 250–350 | Severe tremor, myoclonic jerks, vomiting, EEG spikes | **Unable to work** |
| **IV** | > 350 | Continuous tremor, seizures, psychosis | **Life-threatening** |

**13.1.3 Tremor Characteristics (HPNS Signature)**

| Parameter | Value | Significance |
|-----------|-------|--------------|
| **Frequency** | 8–12 Hz | **Central origin** (thalamocortical) |
| **Amplitude** | 2–10 mm (progressive) | Correlates with depth |
| **Distribution** | Proximal > distal | Postural/action tremor |
| **EEG Correlate** | 8–12 Hz spikes, θ/α slowing | Cortical hyperexcitability |
| **Myoclonus** | > 300 msw | Brainstem involvement |

**13.1.4 Depth Threshold — Why 120 msw?**

**Helium Partial Pressure at Onset:**
$$P_{He} = F_{He} \cdot (P_{amb} - 0.0627) \approx 1.0 \text{ bar at 10 msw} \rightarrow 13 \text{ bar at 120 msw}$$

**Critical Helium Pressure:** ~12–13 bar absolute
**Corresponds to:** ~120 msw (400 fsw) for heliox (F_He ≈ 0.95)
