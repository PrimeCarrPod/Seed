### 11.1 Introduction: Fundamental Tectonic Contrast

The two comparative nodes sit on fundamentally different plate boundaries:
- **San Jose, Costa Rica:** Convergent margin (Cocos-Caribbean subduction)
- **San Jose, California:** Transform boundary (Pacific-North America strike-slip)

This creates radically different seismic hazard, crustal deformation, and background vibration environments for surface atoms.

### 11.2 Cocos-Caribbean Subduction Zone (Costa Rica)

**Plate geometry:**
- Cocos Plate subducts beneath Caribbean Plate
- Convergence rate: 83-89 mm/yr (NNE direction)
- Trench: Middle America Trench, ~150 km offshore
- Slab dip: ~45° (Nicoya), ~65° (Central), ~25° (Osa - flat slab)

**Wadati-Benioff zone:** Seismicity to 200 km depth.
**Volcanic arc:** Central Volcanic Cordillera (Poás, Irazú, Turrialba, Arenal).

### 11.3 Megathrust Earthquake Cycle

**Locking zone:** 0-30 km depth on plate interface.
**Slip deficit accumulation:** 83 mm/yr × coupling fraction.
**Recurrence interval:** Mw 7.5-7.8 every 50-100 years per segment.

**Historic megathrust events:**
- 1990 Mw 7.0 (Nicoya)
- 1950 Mw 7.7 (Nicoya)
- 1888 Mw 7.5 (Central)
- 1941 Mw 7.7 (Osa)

**Seismic gap:** Nicoya peninsula (last 1950) - high probability.

### 11.4 Slow Slip Events (SSEs): Aseismic Stress Release

**Discovery:** GPS networks detected transient deformation (2000s).
**Types in Costa Rica:**
- **Nicoya SSE:** Mw ~6.8, ~12-14 month recurrence, 20-30 km depth
- **Osa SSE:** Mw ~7.0, ~22 month recurrence, 30-40 km depth
- **Central Valley SSEs:** Smaller, more frequent

**SSE physics:** Rate-strengthening friction, fluid pressurization, or transitional friction.

**Surface signal:** 5-15 mm horizontal displacement over weeks-months.
**Moment release:** 20-30% of plate convergence (remaining 70-80% seismic).

### 11.5 Probabilistic Seismic Hazard Assessment (PSHA) for Costa Rica

**Methodology:** Logic tree with multiple GMPEs, source zones, maximum magnitudes.
**Key model:** 2022 CR seismic hazard model (GEM/ENSO).

**PGA at 475-year return period (Central Valley):**
- Range: 0.40g - 0.55g
- **Among highest in Central America**

**Contributing sources:**
1. Subduction interface (megathrust): 40%
2. Intraslab (intermediate depth): 25%
3. Crustal faults (local): 20%
4. Volcanic: 10%
5. Background: 5%

### 11.7 San Andreas Transform System (California)

**Plate boundary:** Pacific-North America, ~1200 km in CA.
**Slip rate:** 33-37 mm/yr (right-lateral).
**Fault geometry:** Multiple strands (SAF, Hayward, Calaveras, San Gregorio).

**San Jose, CA location:** 15-20 km NE of San Andreas (Creeping section).
**Hayward Fault:** 10 km NE of San Jose, Mw 6.8-7.0 potential.
**Calaveras Fault:** 15 km E, Mw 6.5-7.0 potential.

### 11.8 Strike-Slip Physics vs Subduction Physics

| Aspect | Subduction (CR) | Transform (CA) |
|--------|-----------------|----------------|
| Failure mode | Thrust (compression) | Strike-slip (shear) |
| Stress tensor | σ₁ vertical, σ₃ trench-normal | σ₁ horizontal, σ₃ vertical |
| Rupture area | Large (100×50 km) | Long, narrow (100×15 km) |
| Tsunami potential | High | Low (unless submarine landslide) |
| Aftershock pattern | Broad, dipping plane | Linear, vertical plane |
| Ground motion | Long duration, low freq | Short duration, high freq |

### 11.9 Rupture Directivity Effects

**Strike-slip (CA):** Bilateral or unilateral rupture.
- **Directivity pulse:** Strong forward directivity if rupture propagates toward site
- **Velocity pulse:** 1-2 s period, large amplitude
- **San Jose, CA:** Directly in path for NW-SE rupture on SAF

**Subduction (CR):** Up-dip or down-dip rupture.
- **Directivity:** Complex 3D pattern
- **Basin effects:** Central Valley amplifies long-period waves

### 11.10 Ground Motion Prediction Equations (GMPEs) Comparison

**Subduction interface (CR):**
- Abrahamson et al. (2016) - BSSA14
- Duration: 60-120 s for Mw 8
- Spectral shape: Peak at 0.5-1.0 s (long period)

**Crustal strike-slip (CA):**
- Campbell & Bozorgnia (2014) - CB14
- Duration: 20-40 s for Mw 7
- Spectral shape: Peak at 0.1-0.3 s (short period)

**Site amplification (Vs30):**
- CR Central Valley: Vs30 ~300 m/s (soft soil) → amplification 2-3×
- CA Santa Clara Valley: Vs30 ~250 m/s (very soft) → amplification 3-4×

### 11.11 Probabilistic Seismic Hazard for San Jose, CA

**PSHA (USGS 2018 NSHM):**
- PGA (475 yr): 0.55-0.65g
- PGA (2475 yr): 0.9-1.1g

**Contributing sources:**
1. San Andreas (creeping section): 30%
2. Hayward Fault: 25%
3. Calaveras Fault: 15%
4. Other crustal faults: 20%
5. Background: 10%

**Key difference:** CA hazard dominated by **crustal faults**; CR hazard dominated by **subduction interface**.

### 11.12 Microseismic Noise Fields: Oceanic vs Continental

**Primary microseism (0.05-0.1 Hz):** Ocean swell interaction with coast.
**Secondary microseism (0.1-0.3 Hz):** Wave-wave interaction in deep ocean.

**Costa Rica (isthmus, 60 km Pacific / 100 km Caribbean):**
- **Bidirectional loading** from both oceans
- Pacific swell (Southern Ocean storms) + Caribbean wind waves
- **Isotropic noise field** - similar energy from all directions
- Higher overall amplitude (two coasts)

**California (Pacific coast only):**
- **Unidirectional** Pacific swell dominance
- Strong anisotropy: energy from W-NW
- Seasonal: Southern Ocean storms (austral winter) → stronger noise

### 11.14 Volcanic Seismicity: CR Unique Hazard

**Active volcanoes near San Jose, CR:**
- **Poás (2708 m):** 30 km NW - frequent phreatic eruptions, LP events
- **Irazú (3432 m):** 25 km E - last major 1963-65, seismic swarms
- **Turrialba (3340 m):** 35 km ENE - persistent degassing, VT/LP events

**Volcano-seismic signals:**
- VT (volcano-tectonic): Brittle failure, M<3
- LP (long-period): Fluid resonance, 0.5-5 Hz
- Tremor: Continuous, 1-5 Hz
- VLP (very long period): Deep magma movement, 20-50 s

**California:** No active volcanoes near San Jose (Clear Lake 100 km N, Long Valley 250 km E).

### 11.15 Geodetic Strain Accumulation

**Costa Rica (Cocos-Caribbean):**
- GPS velocity field: 80-90 mm/yr convergence
- Strain rate: 10⁻⁷ - 10⁻⁶ /yr (shortening)
- Coupling: 70-90% on megathrust
- SSEs release strain aseismically

**California (Pacific-North America):**
- GPS velocity field: 35-40 mm/yr shear
- Strain rate: 10⁻⁷ /yr (shear)
- Creeping section (San Jose): <10% coupling
- Locked sections N/S: >80% coupling

### 11.16 Crustal Rheology and Depth-Dependent Strength

**Byerlee's law (brittle):** τ = 0.6σₙ (σₙ < 200 MPa)
**Ductile flow (quartz/feldspar):** ε̇ = Aσⁿexp(-Q/RT)

**CR thermal structure:** Hotter (volcanic arc, thin lithosphere)
- Brittle-ductile transition: 12-15 km
- Seismogenic zone: 0-15 km

**CA thermal structure:** Cooler (older crust, transform)
- Brittle-ductile transition: 15-18 km
- Seismogenic zone: 0-18 km

### 11.17 Earthquake Early Warning (EEW) Systems

**Costa Rica (SATREPS/EW):**
- 50+ stations, dense near Nicoya
- Alert time: 5-30 s for Nicoya events
- 10-60 s for Central Valley events
- Public alerts via app/siren

**California (ShakeAlert):**
- 1000+ stations (CISN)
- Alert time: 2-20 s for Bay Area events
- Integrated with BART, utilities, schools
- MyShake app public alerts

### 11.18 Building Codes and Seismic Design

**Costa Rica (Código Sísmico 2017):**
- Based on ACI 318, ASCE 7
- Seismic zone 4 (highest) for Central Valley
- Importance factors for hospitals, schools
- Mandatory for new construction

**California (CBC 2022 / ASCE 7-16):**
- Most advanced in US
- Performance-based design
- Near-fault directivity requirements
- Base isolation for critical facilities

### 11.19 Summary: Tectonic Regimes at CR vs CA

| Parameter | San Jose, CR | San Jose, CA |
|-----------|--------------|--------------|
| Plate boundary | Convergent (subduction) | Transform (strike-slip) |
| Convergence rate | 83-89 mm/yr | 33-37 mm/yr |
| Dominant faulting | Thrust (megathrust) | Strike-slip |
| Max credible Mw | 8.0-8.5 (megathrust) | 7.5-8.0 (SAF) |
| PGA (475 yr) | 0.40-0.55g | 0.55-0.65g |
| Local faults | Aguacaliente, Higuito | Hayward, Calaveras |
| Volcanic hazard | High (Poás, Irazú) | None nearby |
| SSEs | Frequent (Nicoya, Osa) | Rare (creeping section) |
| Microseismic noise | High (bidirectional) | Moderate (unidirectional) |
| Tsunami hazard | High | Low |
| Building stock | Mixed, improving | Modern, stringent |

**Key insight:** The equatorial node faces **megathrust subduction hazard** with frequent slow slip and volcanic threats. The mid-latitude node faces **crustal strike-slip hazard** with strong directivity pulses and dense fault network. Both have high hazard but fundamentally different physics.
