## 16.12 Quartz Hydrolysis: The Kinetic Bottleneck

### 16.12.1 Quartz Dissolution Mechanism

**Surface-controlled reaction (acidic/neutral pH):**
$$\text{SiO}_2 + 2\text{H}_2\text{O} \rightleftharpoons \text{H}_4\text{SiO}_4$$

**Rate-determining step:** **Si-O-Si bond hydrolysis** at surface sites

**Surface speciation (pH-dependent):**
- **pH < 2:** ≡Si-OH₂⁺ (protonated)
- **pH 2–7:** ≡Si-OH (neutral)
- **pH > 7:** ≡Si-O⁻ (deprotonated)

**Rate law (Rimstidt & Barnes, 1980):**
$$r = k_+ \times a_{\text{H}^+}^{0.5} \times (1 - \Omega) \quad \text{(pH 2–7)}$$

**Rate constants (25°C):**
- **k₊ = 10⁻¹⁰·⁵ mol/m²/s** (forward rate constant)
- **E_a = 87 kJ/mol** (activation energy)
- **n = 0.5** (H⁺ reaction order)
- **σ = 1** (far from equilibrium)

### 16.12.2 Temperature Dependence: The Cold Climate Effect

**Arrhenius plot for quartz:**
$$\ln(k) = \ln(A) - \frac{E_a}{RT}$$

**Calculated rates:**
| T (°C) | k (mol/m²/s) | Relative to 25°C |
|--------|--------------|------------------|
| **-20°C** | 10⁻¹⁸·² | 10⁻⁴·⁷× |
| **-10°C** | 10⁻¹⁶·⁸ | 10⁻³·³× |
| **0°C** | 10⁻¹⁵·⁵ | 10⁻²·⁰× |
| **10°C** | 10⁻¹⁴·³ | 10⁻⁰·⁸× |
| **25°C** | 10⁻¹³·⁵ | 1× (reference) |
| **50°C** | 10⁻¹²·¹ | 25× |
| **100°C** | 10⁻¹⁰·⁵ | 10³·⁴× |

**At -10°C (typical polar desert): quartz dissolves 2,000× slower than at 25°C.**

## 16.15 Thermal Stress Weathering: Diurnal Cycling

### 16.15.1 Thermal Stress Fatigue

**Thermal stress (Δσ):**
$$\Delta\sigma = E \times \alpha \times \Delta T / (1 - \nu)$$

Where:
- **E** = Young's modulus (50–100 GPa for rocks)
- **α** = Thermal expansion coefficient (5–10×10⁻⁶/K)
- **ΔT** = Diurnal temperature range
- **ν** = Poisson's ratio (0.2–0.3)

**Typical desert conditions:**
| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| **ΔT (diurnal)** | 20–40°C |
| **Rock tensile strength** | 5–20 MPa |
| **Cycles to failure** | 10³–10⁵ cycles |

**Typical desert (ΔT = 30°C, E = 70 GPa, α = 8×10⁻⁶/K):**
$$\Delta\sigma = 70 \times 10^9 \times 8 \times 10^{-6} \times 30 / 0.75 \approx 22 \text{ MPa}$$

**Exceeds tensile strength of most rocks** → **thermal fatigue → granular disintegration.**

## 16.20 Glacial Legacy: Rock Flour vs. Clay
