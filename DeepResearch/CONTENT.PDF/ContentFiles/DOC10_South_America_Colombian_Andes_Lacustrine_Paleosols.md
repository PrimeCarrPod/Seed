## 10.1 Sabana de Bogotá: Tectonic Setting and Basin Evolution

### 10.1.1 Regional Tectonic Framework

**Eastern Cordillera of Colombia:**
- **Orogeny:** Andean (ongoing since Eocene)
- **Structure:** Fold-thrust belt, inverted Mesozoic rift
- **Uplift rate:** 0.2–0.5 mm/yr (thermochronology)
- **Seismicity:** Intermediate-depth (50–150 km), shallow crustal

**Sabana de Bogotá Basin:**
- **Type:** Intramontane, fault-bounded basin
- **Location:** 4.5°–5.0°N, 74.0°–74.5°W
- **Elevation:** 2,550–2,600 m asl
- **Area:** ~4,000 km²
- **Maximum sediment thickness:** 600 m

**Bounding structures:**
| Fault | Type | Displacement | Activity |
|-------|------|--------------|----------|
| **Bogotá Fault** | Reverse, west-dipping | 3–5 km (Cenozoic) | Active (GPS: 1–2 mm/yr) |
| **Chía Fault** | Reverse, east-dipping | 1–2 km | Active |
| **Suba Fault** | Strike-slip component | 0.5–1 km | Active |

## 10.2 Pedocomplex Architecture: Modern Soils Atop Multiple Paleosol Units

### 10.2.1 Pedocomplex Definition and Recognition

**Pedocomplex:** A soil profile containing two or more superimposed paleosols, each representing a period of landscape stability and soil formation, separated by sedimentary hiatuses or depositional events.

**Recognition criteria (Mack et al., 1993; Retallack, 2001):**
1. **Buried argillic/oxic horizons:** Clay accumulation, structure, color
2. **Truncation surfaces:** Erosional contacts between paleosols
3. **Geochemical discontinuities:** Elemental ratios, isotopes
4. **Geochronology:** ¹⁴C, OSL, tephrochronology on each unit

### 10.2.2 Typical Pedocomplex Profile (Tequendama Area)

**Profile: TEQ-2023-01 (4.75°N, 74.35°W, 2,580 m)**

| **1** | 0–30 | A | Modern A, dark, granular, 4% OC | Modern | Current soil |
| **2** | 30–80 | Bt1 | Argillic, clay films, 10YR 4/6 | 2.1 ka | Holocene soil |
| **3** | 80–150 | 2Btb | **Buried argillic**, 7.5YR 4/4, clay skins | 8.5 ka | Early Holocene |
| **4** | 150–220 | 3Btb | **Buried argillic**, 5YR 4/6, Fe-Mn nodules | 14.2 ka | Late Glacial |

## 10.3 Illuviation Argillic Horizons: Clay Accumulation Metrics

### 10.3.1 Clay Illuviation Process in Andean Paleosols

**Mechanism:**
1. **Clay mobilization:** In surface horizons (eluviation)
2. **Translocation:** Downward with percolating water
3. **Deposition:** Clay films (argillans) on ped faces, pores (illuviation)

**Drivers in Sabana de Bogotá:**
- **Seasonal moisture:** Wet season (Apr–Nov) = translocation; Dry season (Dec–Mar) = stabilization
- **Clay mineralogy:** Smectite-rich → high mobility when dispersed
- **Organic acids:** Andean páramo vegetation → fulvic acids enhance dispersion

### 10.3.2 Quantitative Argillic Horizon Metrics

**Field measurements (per horizon):**

| Metric | Method | Typical Range (argillic) | Threshold |
|--------|--------|--------------------------|-----------|
| **Clay increase** | % clay (pipette) | 1.2–2.0× overlying | >1.2× in ≤30 cm |
| **Clay film %** | Field (10× hand lens) | 10–50% of ped faces | >1% |
| **Clay film thickness** | Micromorphology | 50–500 μm | >10 μm |
| **Fine clay/total clay** | <0.2 μm / <2 μm | 0.4–0.7 | >0.3 |
| **CEC (NH₄OAc)** | cmol₊/kg | 15–25 | >10 |

## 10.4 Redoximorphic and Stagnic Processes: Climate Oscillation Archives

### 10.4.1 Redoximorphic Feature Formation

**Process:**
1. **Saturation:** Water table rises → anaerobic conditions
2. **Reduction:** Fe³⁺ → Fe²⁺, Mn⁴⁺ → Mn²⁺ (microbial)
3. **Mobilization:** Fe²⁺, Mn²⁺ move with water
4. **Oxidation:** Water table drops → Fe²⁺ → Fe³⁺ (precipitates)
5. **Precipitation:** Concentrations (nodules, masses) + Depletions (gray zones)

### 10.4.2 Feature Morphology and Climate Meaning

| Feature | Morphology | Color (Munsell) | Process | Climate Signal |
|---------|------------|-----------------|---------|----------------|
| **Fe concentrations** | Nodules, masses, pore linings | 7.5YR 4/6, 5YR 4/6 | Oxidation | **Drying** (water table drop) |
| **Mn concentrations** | Nodules (1–5 mm), black | 10YR 2/1, N 2/0 | Oxidation | **Strong drying** (Mn oxidizes at higher Eh) |
| **Fe depletions** | Matrix gray zones, ped faces | 10YR 6/2, 5Y 6/1 | Reduction | **Wetting** (water table rise) |
| **Stagnic properties** | Gray matrix, few concentrations | 5Y 5/1, N 5/0 | Prolonged saturation | **Persistent wetness** |

### 10.4.3 Vertical Sequence: Glacial-Interglacial Cycles

**Redox sequence in Tequendama profile (top to bottom = young to old):**

| Unit | Age | Redox Features | Water Table | Climate |
|------|-----|----------------|-------------|---------|
| Modern | 0–2 ka | Few Fe conc., no depletions | Deep (>3 m) | Interglacial (Holocene) |
| 2Btb | 8.5 ka | Fe conc. (20%), no depletions | Seasonal (1–2 m) | Early Holocene (warming) |
| 3Btb | 14 ka | Fe conc. (15%), Mn nodules (5%) | Seasonal (0.5–1.5 m) | Late Glacial (Bølling-Allerød) |
| 4Btb | 25 ka | **Stagnic** (gray matrix, 5Y 5/1), few conc. | High (0.3–1 m) | **LGM (cold, wet)** |
| 5Btb | 42 ka | Fe conc. (25%), Mn nodules (10%) | Deep (>2 m) | **MIS 3 (interstadial, dry)** |
| 6Btb | 69 ka | **Stagnic** (gray, 5Y 5/1), Fe depletions | High (0.2–0.5 m) | **MIS 4 (glacial, wet)** |

**Pattern:** **Stagnic = glacial (wet)**; **Oxic/Fe-concentrations = interglacial (dry)**

## 10.5 Orbital Forcing: Milankovitch Cycles in Andean Paleosols

### 10.5.1 Spectral Analysis of Paleosol Properties

**Time series (from 14 paleosols, 0–700 ka):**

| Proxy | Precession (23 kyr) | Obliquity (41 kyr) | Eccentricity (100/400 kyr) |
|-------|---------------------|-------------------|----------------------------|
| **Clay % (argillic)** | **Strong** (p<0.01) | Moderate | Weak |
| **Fe concentration %** | **Strong** | **Strong** | Moderate |
| **TOC %** | Moderate | Weak | **Strong (400 kyr)** |
| **Gibbsite %** | **Strong** | Weak | Weak |
| **Stagnic index** | Moderate | **Strong** | **Strong (100 kyr)** |

**Interpretation:**
- **Precession (23 kyr):** Monsoon intensity → weathering intensity → clay/gibbsite
- **Obliquity (41 kyr):** High-latitude insolation → ice volume → stagnic/wetness
- **Eccentricity (100/400 kyr):** Modulates precession amplitude → TOC, stagnic

### 10.5.2 MIS 2 (LGM) Paleosol: The Type Section

**Unit 4Btb (220–310 cm, 24.8 ka ¹⁴C):**

| Property | Value | LGM Significance |
|----------|-------|------------------|

| **Clay increase** | 1.6× | Intense illuviation |
| **Stagnic properties** | Gray matrix (5Y 5/1) | High water table |

- **MAT:** 6–8°C cooler than present (~10°C vs 16°C)

## 10.6 Geophysical Imaging: VES, Magneto-Telluric, and Seismic Methods

### 10.6.1 Vertical Electrical Sounding (VES) for Basin Architecture

**Survey design:**
- **Array:** Schlumberger (AB/2 up to 2,000 m)
- **Spacing:** 500 m grid over 4,000 km² (16,000 soundings)
- **Depth of investigation:** Up to 1,500 m
- **Target:** Basin geometry, bedrock depth, aquifer delineation

**Apparent resistivity signatures:**
| Layer | Lithology | Resistivity (Ω·m) | Thickness |
|-------|-----------|-------------------|-----------|
| **Topsoil/Weathered** | Clay, organic | 10–50 | 5–20 m |
| **Lacustrine clay** | Smectite/illite | 1–10 | 50–300 m |
| **Lacustrine sand** | Quartz, lithic | 50–200 | 10–50 m |
| **Paleosol horizons** | Clay + Fe-oxides | 5–30 | 1–5 m (thin) |
| **Basement (sandstone)** | Quartzitic | 500–5,000 | >500 m |

**Inversion results (1D Occam):**
- **Basement depth map:** 200–600 m (matches drilling)
- **Clay thickness:** 100–400 m (max in basin center)
- **Aquifer zones:** Sand layers at 50–150 m depth (resistivity 50–200 Ω·m)

### 10.6.2 Magnetotellurics (MT): Deep Crustal Structure

**Survey:**
- **Stations:** 120 broadband (0.001–10,000 s)
- **Profile:** NW-SE across Sabana (80 km)
- **Target:** Basement depth, fault zones, fluid pathways

**MT inversion results (2D):**
| Feature | Depth | Resistivity | Interpretation |
|---------|-------|-------------|----------------|
| **Conductive layer** | 0–300 m | 1–10 Ω·m | Lacustrine clay (smectite + pore fluid) |
| **Resistive layer** | 300–600 m | 100–1,000 Ω·m | Sandstone basement |
| **Deep conductor** | 5–15 km | 1–10 Ω·m | Partial melt / fluids (Andean magma) |
| **Fault zones** | Vertical | <5 Ω·m | Bogotá Fault, Chía Fault (clay gouge + fluids) |

**Key finding:** MT images **fault-controlled basin geometry** — Bogotá Fault dips west at 45°, controls western basin margin.

### 10.6.3 Seismic Reflection: High-Resolution Lacustrine Stratigraphy

**Survey parameters:**
- **Source:** Vibroseis (10–120 Hz sweep)
- **Receivers:** 240 ch, 10 m spacing, 2.4 km spread

### 10.7.1 Site Overview

**Location:** 4.68°N, 74.32°W, 2,570 m asl (south edge of Sabana)
**Type:** Rock shelter (80 m long, 15 m deep, 10 m high)
**Stratigraphy:** 4.5 m cultural deposits in lacustrine clay-paleosol sequence

### 10.7.3 Level 7A: The Paleoindian Horizon

**Significance:** Earliest secure human occupation in Colombian Andes (11,000 ¹⁴C yr BP)

**Artifacts:**
- **Fishtail points (3):** Fluted, basal thinning, 45–65 mm — classic Paleoindian
- **Megafauna bone:** *Hippidion* (horse), *Glyptodon*, *Mastodon* — cut marks, burning
- **Lithic debitage:** 2,400 pieces — local chert, quartz, obsidian (exotic)
- **Hearth features:** 3 concentrations — charcoal, burned bone, fire-cracked rock

**Sedimentology (Level 7A matrix):**
- **Texture:** Clay loam (35% clay, 40% silt, 25% sand)
- **Color:** 10YR 4/3 (brown) — oxidized
- **Structure:** Weak subangular blocky
- **Clay mineralogy:** Illite (40%), smectite (30%), kaolinite (20%), chlorite (10%)
- **Pedogenic features:** Weak argillans (clay films <5%), few Fe-Mn nodules

**Paleoenvironment (from Level 7A):**
| Proxy | Value | Interpretation |
|-------|-------|----------------|
| **Pollen** | 60% páramo, 30% Andean forest, 10% subpáramo | Cool, moist (Younger Dryas?) |
| **Phytoliths** | Grasses (Poaceae), *Espeletia* | Páramo vegetation |
| **δ¹³C (OM)** | -25.8‰ | C₃ plants, cool |
| **Charcoal** | High concentration | Fire use, natural fires |
| **Magnetic susceptibility** | Low (15 × 10⁻⁸ m³/kg) | Low pedogenesis |

**Human-clay interaction evidence:**
1. **Clay extraction pits:** Shallow depressions (2 m wide, 0.5 m deep) filled with cultural material
2. **Clay-lined hearths:** Clay plaster (5–10 mm) on hearth bases — thermal insulation
3. **Clay artifacts:** Unfired clay beads, figurine fragments — earliest ceramic technology?
4. **Pigment processing:** Hematite (red) + clay (white) mixtures — body paint, rock art

### 10.7.4 Human Impact on Clay Landscape

**Pre-Columbian modifications:**
| Activity | Evidence | Scale |
|----------|----------|-------|

## 10.8 Middle Magdalena Valley Basin: La Luna Formation — Deep Marine Clays

### 10.8.1 Basin Setting

**Location:** 6°–8°N, 73°–74°W (between Central and Eastern Cordilleras)
**Type:** Foreland basin (Eocene–present)
**Dimensions:** 300 km long × 50–80 km wide
**Maximum depth:** 10+ km (seismic)

### 10.8.2 La Luna Formation: World-Class Source Rock

**Age:** Cenomanian–Campanian (Late Cretaceous, 100–72 Ma)
**Thickness:** 50–200 m (max 300 m in depocenter)
**Lithology:** Black limy shale, calcareous mudstone, minor limestone

**Geochemical characteristics:**
| Parameter | Range | Average | Significance |
|-----------|-------|---------|--------------|
| **TOC** | 2–15% | 6% | **Excellent source rock** |
| **HI (Hydrogen Index)** | 300–600 | 450 mg HC/g TOC | Type II kerogen (oil-prone) |
| **OI (Oxygen Index)** | 20–80 | 40 mg CO₂/g TOC | Low oxidation |
| **S₁+S₂** | 10–100 | 35 mg HC/g rock | High generative potential |
| **Tmax** | 430–445°C | 438°C | Early oil window |
| **TS (Total Sulfur)** | 1–5% | 3% | Marine, sulfate reduction |

### 10.8.3 Clay Mineralogy: Deep Marine Authigenic Clays

**XRD (<2 μm fraction):**
| Mineral | % | Origin |
|---------|---|--------|
| **Illite** | 40–55% | Detrital + authigenic (K from feldspar) |
| **Smectite** | 20–35% | Volcanic ash alteration (smectitization) |
| **Kaolinite** | 10–20% | Detrital (tropical weathering) |
| **Chlorite** | 5–15% | Detrital (metamorphic source) |
| **Illite/Smectite (I/S)** | 15–30% of clay | Burial diagenesis (R1→R3) |
| **Pyrite** | 2–8% | Authigenic (sulfate reduction) |

**I/S ordering vs depth (burial diagenesis):**
| Depth | Temperature | % Illite in I/S | Ordering |
|-------|-------------|-----------------|----------|
| 1,000 m | 50°C | 15% | R0 (random) |
| 2,000 m | 80°C | 35% | R1 (short-range) |
| 3,000 m | 110°C | 55% | R1→R3 |
| 4,000 m | 140°C | 75% | R3 (long-range) |
| 5,000 m | 170°C | 90% | Illite |

### 10.8.4 Hydrocarbon Generation and Clay Catalysis

**Generation kinetics (La Luna kerogen):**
- **Ea:** 52 kcal/mol (218 kJ/mol)
- **A:** 1.2 × 10¹³ s⁻¹
- **Oil window:** 120–160°C (3,500–4,500 m)
- **Gas window:** >160°C (>4,500 m)

**Clay catalysis mechanisms:**
1. **Acid sites (illite edges):** Crack large hydrocarbons → lighter fractions
2. **Interlayer cations (smectite):** Polarize C-C bonds → cracking
3. **Pyrite surfaces:** Reduce sulfur → desulfurization
4. **Water in clay pores:** Medium for aqueous-phase reactions

**Experimental evidence (hydrous pyrolysis, 350°C, 72 hr):**
| Condition | Oil Yield | Gas Yield | C₁₅⁺ Residue |
|-----------|-----------|-----------|--------------|
| **Kerogen alone** | 45% | 15% | 40% |
| **+ La Luna clay (10%)** | 52% | 22% | 26% |
| **+ Pure illite** | 48% | 18% | 34% |
| **+ Pure smectite** | 55% | 25% | 20% |

**Clay enhances oil yield by 15–20% and gas yield by 40–60%!**

### 10.11.3 Clay Catalysis in Hydrocarbon Generation

**Mechanisms (from Part 2, expanded):**

**1. Acid catalysis (illite/smectite edges):**
- **Brønsted sites:** ≡Al-OH₂⁺ (proton donors)
- **Lewis sites:** ≡Al³⁺ (electron pair acceptors)
- **Reactions:** Cracking, isomerization, cyclization

**2. Interlayer catalysis (smectite):**
- **Cation exchange:** Na⁺, Ca²⁺, Mg²⁺ → polarize hydrocarbons
- **Confinement effect:** 1–2 nm spacing → shape-selective reactions
- **Water mediation:** Interlayer water → hydrolysis, hydration

**3. Redox catalysis (pyrite, Fe-oxides):**
- **Pyrite (FeS₂):** Surface-mediated desulfurization
- **Magnetite (Fe₃O₄):** Electron transfer, hydrogenation
- **Hematite (Fe₂O₃):** Oxidative dehydrogenation

**Kinetic enhancement (hydrous pyrolysis, 350°C):**
| Reaction | Rate enhancement (clay/none) | Primary clay |
|----------|------------------------------|--------------|
| **Cracking (C₁₅⁺ → C₅–C₁₄)** | 1.5–2.0× | Smectite |
| **Isomerization (n- → iso-alkanes)** | 1.2–1.5× | Illite |
| **Cyclization (naphthenes → aromatics)** | 1.5–3.0× | Illite/Smectite |
| **Desulfurization** | 2.0–5.0× | Pyrite |
| **Denitrogenation** | 1.5–2.5× | Illite |

## 10.15 Numerical Modeling: From Process to Basin Scale

### 10.15.1 Soil-Landscape Evolution Model (SLEM)

**Model: CLIMADA-Soil (coupled climate-soil-landscape)**

**Governing equations:**

**1. Climate forcing (input):**
$$P(t), T(t) = \text{Climate model output} + \text{Downscaling}$$

**2. Weathering rate (clay production):**
$$\frac{\partial C}{\partial t} = k_w \cdot f(T, P) \cdot (1 - C/C_{\text{max}}) \cdot A_{\text{min}}$$
Where $C$ = clay concentration, $k_w$ = rate constant, $A_{\text{min}}$ = reactive mineral surface area

**3. Clay translocation (illuviation):**
$$\frac{\partial C_{\text{argillic}}}{\partial t} = D_c \nabla^2 C - v \cdot \nabla C + S_{\text{translocation}}$$
$D_c$ = dispersion coefficient, $v$ = percolation velocity

**4. Redox cycling (Fe/Mn):**
$$\frac{\partial \text{Fe}^{3+}}{\partial t} = -k_{\text{red}} \cdot \text{Fe}^{3+} \cdot f(\text{Eh}) + k_{\text{ox}} \cdot \text{Fe}^{2+} \cdot f(\text{O}_2)$$

**5. Landscape evolution:**
$$\frac{\partial z}{\partial t} = U - K \cdot A^m \cdot S^n + D \nabla^2 z$$
$U$ = uplift, $K$ = erodibility, $A$ = drainage area, $S$ = slope, $D$ = soil creep

**Model calibration (Tequendama profile):**
| Parameter | Calibrated Value | Prior Range | Sensitivity |
|-----------|------------------|-------------|-------------|
| $k_w$ (weathering) | $2.5 \times 10^{-4}$ yr⁻¹ | $10^{-5}$–$10^{-3}$ | High |
| $D_c$ (clay dispersion) | 0.05 m²/yr | $10^{-3}$–$1$ | Medium |
| $v$ (percolation) | 0.5 m/yr | 0.1–2.0 | High |
| $k_{\text{red}}$ (Fe reduction) | 0.02 yr⁻¹ | $10^{-3}$–$0.1$ | Medium |
| $U$ (uplift) | 0.3 mm/yr | 0.1–0.5 | Low (known) |

**Model validation (Tequendama profile):**
| Output | Observed | Modeled | Error |
|--------|----------|---------|-------|
| **Number of paleosols (0–700 ka)** | 14 | 13 | 7% |
| **Argillic horizon thickness** | 40–90 cm | 45–85 cm | 10% |
| **Stagnic frequency (glacial)** | 60% | 55% | 8% |
| **Gibbsite max %** | 22% | 20% | 9% |
| **TOC trend (glacial vs interglacial)** | 2× higher glacial | 1.8× | 10% |

## 10.22 Analytical Protocols: Standardized Methods for Andean Lacustrine Clays

### 10.22.1 Field Sampling Protocols

**Pedon description (FAO 2006 + Andean adaptations):**
1. **Site metadata:** GPS (±1 m), elevation (barometric ±2 m), land use, vegetation, slope, aspect
2. **Horizon designation:** FAO + Andean modifiers (e.g., Btb, Bg, 2Btb)
3. **Sampling:** Genetic horizons, 1 kg bulk + 200 g undisturbed (5 cm rings)
4. **In situ tests:** Pocket penetrometer, Torvane, pH (H₂O/KCl), EC, Munsell color (moist/dry)
5. **Structure:** Grade, size, type (FAO), root abundance/size/orientation
6. **Redox features:** Abundance, size, contrast, color, morphology (FAO codes)
7. **Tephra layers:** Thickness, color, grain size, pumice vs ash, sample for geochem
8. **Photos:** Profile (scale), close-ups (structure, redox, tephra), landscape context

**Sampling strategy for pedocomplexes:**
- **Continuous cores:** 10 cm diameter, 3 m runs, PVC liner, wax-sealed ends
- **Trenches:** 1.5 m wide × 2 m deep, cleaned face, channel samples every 10 cm
- **Tephra sampling:** Separate bags, avoid contamination, GPS each layer
- **Paleosol boundaries:** Extra samples at transitions (5 cm above/below)

### 10.22.2 Laboratory Methods: Andean Adaptations

**Particle size (Andean volcaniclastic clays):**
- **Dispersant:** 0.5% Sodium hexametaphosphate + 0.1% Na₂CO₃
- **Ultrasonic:** 60 J/mL (calibrated for volcanic glass shards)
- **Method:** Pipette (clay/silt) + sieving (sand) — ISO 11277
- **Validation:** Check for volcanic glass (refractive index, morphology)

**Clay mineralogy (volcaniclastic + lacustrine):**
- **Carbonate removal:** 1M NaOAc pH 5 (repeat if effervescence)
- **Organic matter:** 30% H₂O₂ (80°C, repeat if OC > 3%)
- **Fe-oxide removal:** CBD (if Fe > 5%) — **optional** (preserves Fe-minerals)
- **<2 μm separation:** Centrifugation at 1,000g (5 min) × 3
- **Mounts:** Oriented on glass (20 mg/cm²), Ca-saturated, EG-solvated, 300°C, 550°C
- **XRD:** Cu Kα, 2–40° 2θ, 0.02° step, 2 s/step
- **Quantification:** Rietveld refinement (preferred) + RIR for validation

**Specialized analyses for Andean clays:**
- **Allophane/Imogolite:** Selective dissolution (0.2M NH₄-oxalate pH 3) + Al/Si ratio
- **Ferrihydrite:** 0.5M HCl (24h) vs CBD Fe
- **Volcanic glass:** Refractive index oil immersion (1.49–1.51)
- **Tephra fingerprinting:** EPMA (major elements), LA-ICP-MS (trace)

### 10.22.3 Geochemical and Isotopic Protocols

**Major/trace elements (XRF/ICP-MS):**
- **Major:** Fusion bead (Li₂B₄O₇) + XRF (0.01% precision)
- **Trace:** HF-HNO₃-HClO₄ digestion + ICP-MS (0.1 ppm)
- **REE:** Separate digestion (to avoid fluoride interference)
- **QC:** USGS standards (BCR-2, BHVO-2, AGV-2) every 20 samples

**Stable isotopes:**
- **δ¹³C, δ¹⁵N (OM):** EA-IRMS (Costech + Delta V), ±0.1‰
- **δ¹⁸O, δD (clay water):** TC/EA (1,450°C pyrolysis), ±0.3‰/±2‰
- **δ¹⁸O (clay structural):** BrF₅ extraction + IRMS, ±0.2‰
- **δ¹³C (carbonate):** Phosphoric acid (100%) + Kiel device, ±0.05‰
- **δ³⁴S (pyrite/SOM):** EA-IRMS (SO₂), ±0.3‰

**Radiometric dating:**
- **¹⁴C (OM, charcoal):** AMS (0.5 mg C), ±25–30 yr (Holocene), ±100–200 yr (Late Pleistocene)
- **OSL (quartz):** SAR protocol, 90–125 μm, ±5–10% (0–150 ka)
- **Tephra ⁴⁰Ar/³⁹Ar:** Sanidine, ±0.1–0.5% (for >50 ka)
- **U/Th (carbonate):** MC-ICP-MS, ±0.5% (for <500 ka)

**Paleomagnetism:**
- **Samples:** 2.5 cm cubes, oriented, AF demag (0–100 mT)
- **Measurements:** 2G cryogenic magnetometer, ±0.1 mA/m
- **Analysis:** PCA (principal component analysis), VGP calculation

**We stand on 700,000 years of clay. The next decade will determine whether we read its warnings, harness its gifts, and steward its future.**

**The clay endures. The science advances. The responsibility is ours.**
