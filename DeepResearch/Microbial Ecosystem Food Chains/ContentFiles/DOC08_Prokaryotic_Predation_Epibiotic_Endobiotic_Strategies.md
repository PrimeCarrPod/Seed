# Document 8: Prokaryotic Predation — Epibiotic and Endobiotic Strategies
## Piece 1: Bacterial Predators — Diversity, Mechanisms, and Ecological Significance

Prokaryotic predators are bacteria that actively hunt and consume other bacteria. Unlike eukaryotic phagotrophs, they operate at similar size scales as their prey (0.5-5 μm) and employ fundamentally different mechanisms: **epibiotic** (external lysis and consumption) and **endobiotic** (invasion and intracellular consumption).

### 1.1 Taxonomy and Phylogenetic Distribution

**Major predatory bacterial groups**:

| Group | Phylum | Strategy | Example Genera |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Myxobacteria** | Myxococcota | Epibiotic (swarm) | *Myxococcus, Stigmatella, Corallococcus* |
| **Bdellovibrionales** | Bdellovibrionota | Endobiotic (periplasmic) | *Bdellovibrio, Bacteriovorax* |
| **Vampirovibrionales** | Bdellovibrionota | Endobiotic (cytoplasmic) | *Vampirovibrio, Bellovibrio* |
| **Bacteriovoracaceae** | Bdellovibrionota | Endobiotic | *Bacteriovorax* (marine) |
| **Cystobacteraceae** | Myxococcota | Epibiotic | *Cystobacter, Melittangium* |
| **Sorangiaceae** | Myxococcota | Epibiotic | *Sorangium, Viticoccus* |
| **Bradymonadaceae** | Bradymonadota | Epibiotic (biofilm) | *Bradymonas* |
| **Rickettsiales** (some) | Alphaproteobacteria | Endobiotic (intracellular) | *Rickettsia* (parasitic) |

**Phylogenetic insight**: Predation evolved multiple times independently. Myxobacteria (δ-proteobacteria) and Bdellovibrionales (distinct phylum) represent two major evolutionary solutions to bacterial predation.

### 1.2 Ecological Significance

**Quantitative impact**:
- **Biomass turnover**: Prokaryotic predators consume 10-30% of bacterial production in soils, 5-15% in aquatic systems
- **Nutrient regeneration**: Release N, P, Fe from prey biomass
- **Community structure**: Selective predation shapes bacterial diversity, size distribution, biofilm architecture
- **Biocontrol potential**: *Bdellovibrio* as living antibiotic against Gram-negative pathogens

**Habitat distribution**:
- **Soils**: Myxobacteria dominant (biofilms, solid surfaces)
- **Freshwater**: *Bdellovibrio, Bacteriovorax* (planktonic, biofilms)
- **Marine**: *Bacteriovorax* (pelagic), *Bradymonas* (biofilms)
- **Host-associated**: *Bdellovibrio* in gut, *Vampirovibrio* in microbial mats

### 1.3 Epibiotic vs Endobiotic Strategies

| Feature | Epibiotic (Myxobacteria) | Endobiotic (Bdellovibrio) |
|---|---|---|
| **Attack location** | External (prey surface) | Internal (periplasm/cytoplasm) |
| **Motility** | Social (S) + Adventurous (A) | Single-cell, fast swimming |
| **Killing mechanism** | Secreted enzymes, OMVs, contact-dependent | Lytic enzymes, periplasmic digestion |
| **Prey range** | Broad (Gram+, Gram-, fungi) | Narrow (Gram-negative only) |
| **Growth yield** | Low (diffusion loss) | High (protected niche) |
| **Sociality** | Obligate cooperative (swarms) | Solitary |

### 1.4 Myxococcus xanthus — The Model Epibiotic Predator

**Life cycle**:
1. **Vegetative growth**: Rod-shaped cells (7 μm × 0.5 μm), divide by binary fission
2. **Swarming**: Coordinated movement on surfaces via S- and A-motility
3. **Predation**: Rippling waves, enzyme secretion, cooperative feeding
4. **Development**: Under starvation → fruiting bodies → myxospores

**Motility systems**:
- **S-motility (Social)**: Type IV pili (T4P) — extension, attachment, retraction
  - *pilA, pilB, pilC, pilT* genes
  - Requires exopolysaccharides (EPS) as "road"
  - Speed: ~1-5 μm/min
- **A-motility (Adventurous)**: Focal adhesion complexes (Agl-Glt)
  - *agl, glt, mgl* genes
  - No pili required
  - Speed: ~2-4 μm/min
- **Regulation**: Frz chemosensory system (FrzCD, FrzE, FrzF) controls reversals

**Rippling behavior** (during predation):
- **Phenomenon**: Regular waves of cells moving back and forth
- **Wavelength**: ~0.5-1 mm, Period: ~5-10 min
- **Function**: Increases residence time in prey zone, enhances enzyme delivery
- **Mechanism**: Frz-dependent reversal frequency modulated by prey signals

### 1.5 Killing Mechanisms of Myxococcus

#### 1.5.1 Secreted Enzymes (Public Goods)
- **Proteases**: Metalloproteases (MepA), serine proteases
- **Lipases**: Triacylglycerol lipases, phospholipases
- **Glycoside hydrolases**: Chitinases, lysozyme-like, alginate lyase
- **Nucleases**: Extracellular DNases

**Delivery**: Free diffusion or **Outer Membrane Vesicles (OMVs)**
- OMVs: 20-200 nm vesicles budding from OM
- Cargo: Enzymes, toxins, signaling molecules
- Protection: Enzymes shielded from prey proteases
- Targeting: OMVs fuse with prey OM

#### 1.5.2 Contact-Dependent Killing (Tad/Kil Complex)
- **Tad (Tight Adhesion) / Kil system**: Type II secretion-like
- **Components**: TadA (ATPase), TadB, TadC, TadD, TadE, TadF, TadG, Flp pilin
- **Mechanism**: Pilus extends → contacts prey → delivers effectors
- **Effectors**: Toxins (nucleases, peptidoglycan hydrolases)
- **Specificity**: Requires direct cell-cell contact

#### 1.5.3 Antibiotics and Secondary Metabolites
- **Myxovirescin (TA)**: Broad-spectrum, inhibits RNA polymerase
- **Myxalamid**: Antifungal, disrupts membranes
- **Cystobactamids**: DNA gyrase inhibitors
- **Sorangicins**: Antifungal, mitochondrial inhibitors
- **Epothilones**: Microtubule stabilizers (anticancer agents)

### 1.6 Bdellovibrio bacteriovorus — The Model Endobiotic Predator

**Life cycle** (biphasic):
1. **Attack phase (free-swimming)**:
   - Small, curved rod (0.3-0.5 × 1-2 μm)
   - Single polar flagellum (100-200 μm/s)
   - Chemotaxis toward prey (amino acids, peptides)
   - **No growth** — purely predatory

2. **Growth phase (intraperiplasmic)**:
   - Enters prey periplasm → forms **bdelloplast**
   - Seals entry pore → modifies prey peptidoglycan
   - Grows as **filament** (10-50 μm) within prey
   - Septates into 3-6 progeny
   - Lyse bdelloplast → release progeny

**Host range**: Obligate predators of **Gram-negative** bacteria
- Cannot penetrate Gram-positive thick peptidoglycan
- Prey: *E. coli, Pseudomonas, Salmonella, Vibrio, Aeromonas*, etc.
- **Surface recognition**: Few surface epitopes (O-antigen, LPS) — evades prey immunity

### 1.7 Bdellovibrio Invasion Mechanics

**Attachment**:
- **Flagellar propulsion**: High-speed collision (100-200 μm/s)
- **Type IV pili** (some strains): Initial tethering
- **Outer membrane proteins**: *Bd0816, Bd3459, Bd3460* (prey recognition)

**Entry**:
- **Lytic transglycosylases** (*ltgA, ltgB, ltgC*): Cleave peptidoglycan β-1,4-glycosidic bonds
- **Peptidoglycan hydrolases**: Create pore (~50 nm)
- **Pull-through**: Flagellar motor pulls predator through pore
- **Pore sealing**: *bdelloplast* formation — prey OM + IM sealed

**Intraperiplasmic growth**:
- **Nutrient acquisition**: Secreted hydrolases digest prey cytoplasm
- **Filament formation**: Elongates without septation (MreB-independent)
- **Resource exhaustion**: Prey cytoplasm fully consumed
- **Septation**: FtsZ-ring formation at regular intervals
- **Progeny release**: Lysis of bdelloplast OM → 3-6 progeny

## Piece 2: Mathematical Models, Population Dynamics, and Evolutionary Arms Races

### 2.1 Mathematical Models of Epibiotic Predation (Myxobacteria)

#### 2.1.1 Swarm-Level Models
**Continuum model** (swarm density ρ(x,t)):
```
∂ρ/∂t = ∇·(D(ρ)∇ρ) - ∇·(v(ρ)ρ) + μ(ρ)ρ - g(ρ, B)
```
- **Density-dependent diffusion**: D(ρ) = D₀ + D₁ρ (contact inhibition)
- **Velocity field**: v = χ ∇B (chemotaxis toward prey) + v_ripple (rippling)
- **Growth**: μ(ρ) = μ_max ρ/(K_ρ + ρ) (cooperative growth)
- **Predation**: g = a ρ B / (1 + a h ρ) (Holling II at swarm level)

**Rippling dynamics** (Igoshin et al., 2001):
```
∂ρ/∂t + ∂(ρ v)/∂x = D ∂²ρ/∂x² + μ(ρ)ρ
v = v₀ tanh(β ∂ρ/∂x)  (density-dependent reversal)
```
- **Traveling waves**: ρ(x,t) = ρ(x - ct)
- **Wavelength**: λ ~ 2π√(D/μ)
- **Wave speed**: c ~ √(D μ)

#### 2.1.2 Individual-Based Models (IBM)
**Agents**: Individual cells with position, orientation, internal state
**Rules**:
- **S-motility**: Extend T4P, attach to surface/EPS, retract (force ~100 pN)
- **A-motility**: Focal adhesion, gliding engine (Agl-Glt)
- **Reversal**: Frz system — stochastic + chemosensory modulated
- **Contact**: Detect prey via surface sensors → secrete enzymes/OMVs
- **Division**: When mass > 2× birth mass → divide

**Rippling emergence** (from local rules):
- **Collision-induced reversal**: Head-on collisions trigger Frz
- **Wave propagation**: Reversals propagate as waves
- **Prey modulation**: Prey signals (LPS, peptides) alter reversal frequency

### 2.2 Mathematical Models of Endobiotic Predation (Bdellovibrio)

#### 2.2.1 Classic Predator-Prey Model (Bdellovibrio)
```
dB/dt = r B (1 - B/K) - β B P          (prey bacteria)
dP/dt = β B P - m P                     (attack phase predators)
dI/dt = β B P - δ I                     (infected bdelloplasts)
dP/dt += b δ I                          (progeny release)
```
- β: Adsorption rate (mL/cell/h) ~ 10⁻¹⁰-10⁻⁸
- δ: Lysis rate (1/latent period) ~ 1/3-1/6 h⁻¹
- b: Burst size (progeny/bdelloplast) ~ 3-6
- m: Mortality of attack phase ~ 0.1-0.5 h⁻¹

**Equilibrium**:
- B* = m/β (requires m < β K for coexistence)
- P* = r/β (1 - m/(β K))

**Stability**: Stable focus for typical parameters; oscillations damp quickly.

#### 2.2.2 Spatial Models (Biofilm vs Planktonic)
**Planktonic** (well-mixed): Classic ODE above
**Biofilm** (surface-attached):
```
∂B/∂t = D_B ∇²B + r B (1 - B/K) - β B P
∂P/∂t = D_P ∇²P + β B P - m P + b δ I
```
- **Diffusion limitation**: P diffuses slowly in biofilm matrix
- **Refuge**: Deep biofilm cells protected from predators
- **Wave fronts**: Predation waves propagate through biofilm

### 2.3 Population Dynamics Comparison

| Feature | Myxococcus (Epibiotic) | Bdellovibrio (Endobiotic) |
|---|---|---|
| **Growth coupling** | Direct (enzymes → nutrients) | Delayed (latent period) |
| **Spatial scale** | Swarm (mm-cm) | Single cell (μm) |
| **Cooperation** | Obligate (swarm) | Solitary |
| **Prey range** | Broad | Narrow (Gram-) |
| **Yield** | Low (diffusion) | High (protected) |
| **Numerical response** | Swarm aggregation | Progeny release |
| **Model complexity** | Continuum + IBM | Delay DDE + spatial |

### 2.4 Prey Resistance and Evolutionary Arms Races

#### 2.4.1 Prey Resistance Mechanisms
**Against Myxococcus**:
- **Surface modification**: LPS O-antigen variation, capsule overproduction
- **Enzyme inhibitors**: Secreted protease inhibitors, lipase inhibitors
- **Biofilm matrix**: Thick EPS prevents enzyme penetration
- **Motility**: Swarming away from swarm front
- **Toxin production**: Bacteriocins killing myxobacteria

**Against Bdellovibrio**:
- **Surface masking**: Capsule, S-layer, O-antigen length variation
- **LPS modification**: Reduced O-antigen, altered lipid A
- **Periplasmic inhibitors**: Protease inhibitors, lipoprotein inhibitors
- **Growth rate**: Fast growth outpaces predation
- **Biofilm refuge**: Deep layers inaccessible

#### 2.4.2 Coevolutionary Dynamics (Kill-the-Winner)
**Model** (Thingstad, 2000):
```
dN_i/dt = r_i N_i (1 - Σ N_j/K) - Σ_j β_ij N_i P_j
dP_j/dt = Σ_i ε_ij β_ij N_i P_j - m_j P_j
```
- **Specialist predators**: β_ij high for specific i
- **Negative frequency dependence**: Rare strains escape predation
- **Coexistence**: Many strains coexist via predator-mediated diversity

**Kill-the-Winner predictions**:
- **Diversity**: Number of coexisting strains ~ predator diversity
- **Abundance distribution**: Power-law (Zipf's law)
- **Turnover time**: ~1/predation rate (days-weeks)
- **Genomic signature**: Rapid evolution of surface genes (dN/dS > 1)

### 2.5 Experimental Evolution Studies

**Myxococcus-prey coevolution** (Velicer et al.):
- *M. xanthus* + *E. coli* coevolved for 1000+ generations
- **Prey evolution**: Increased resistance (capsule, LPS changes)
- **Predator evolution**: Broader prey range, altered enzyme profile
- **Trade-offs**: Resistant prey slower growing; generalist predators less efficient

**Bdellovibrio-prey coevolution** (Kadouri et al.):
- *B. bacteriovorus* + *E. coli* coevolved
- **Prey**: Rapid resistance (LPS truncation, capsule)
- **Predator**: Counter-adaptation (altered prey recognition)
- **Cycling**: Resistance → susceptibility cycles (Red Queen)

### 2.6 Biocontrol Applications

**Bdellovibrio as "living antibiotic"**:
- **Targets**: Gram-negative pathogens (*Pseudomonas, Acinetobacter, Klebsiella, E. coli*)
- **Advantages**: Self-replicating, evolves with resistance, penetrates biofilms
- **Challenges**: Immunogenicity, narrow host range, resistance evolution
- **Clinical trials**: Topical (wound infections), aerosol (lung infections)

**Myxobacteria as biocontrol agents**:
- **Targets**: Plant pathogens (*Xanthomonas, Ralstonia, Erwinia*)
- **Mechanisms**: Antibiotics, enzymes, competition
- **Commercial products**: *Myxococcus* spp. for soil-borne diseases

## Piece 3: Experimental Methods, Molecular Mechanisms, and Biotechnological Applications

### 3.1 Experimental Methods for Studying Prokaryotic Predation

#### 3.1.1 Plaque Assays (Bdellovibrio)
**Principle**: Predator forms clear zones (plaques) in prey lawn
**Protocol**:
1. Grow prey (e.g., *E. coli*) to mid-log
2. Mix with soft agar (0.5%), pour on hard agar plate
3. Spot predator dilutions on surface
2. Incubate 24-48 h
3. Count plaques (PFU/mL)

**Quantification**: 
- Plaque diameter correlates with predation efficiency
- Plaque morphology (halo = enzyme diffusion)

#### 3.1.2 Predation Kinetics in Liquid Culture
**Method**: Co-culture predator + prey → measure both populations over time
**Measurements**:
- **Prey**: Plate counts (CFU), flow cytometry (SYBR Green), qPCR (16S)
- **Predator**: 
  - Bdellovibrio: Plaque assay (attack phase), qPCR (*bd0816* gene)
  - Myxococcus: Plate counts (CFU on prey lawn), OD₆₀₀, qPCR (*frz* genes)

**Kinetic parameters** (from time-series):
- Adsorption rate β (Bdellovibrio)
- Latent period 1/δ
- Burst size b
- Swarm expansion rate (Myxococcus)

#### 3.1.3 Microscopy and Imaging
**Time-lapse microscopy**:
- **Phase contrast**: Swarm rippling, rippling waves, bdelloplast formation
- **Fluorescence**: 
  - Prey: GFP-labeled *E. coli*
  - Predator: mCherry-labeled *M. xanthus* / *B. bacteriovorus*
  - Membrane dyes (FM4-64): Bdelloplast visualization
- **High-speed**: Flagellar motility, pili dynamics (TIRF)

**Electron microscopy**:
- **TEM**: Bdelloplast ultrastructure, periplasmic filament, septation
- **SEM**: Swarm morphology, rippling waves, OMV secretion
- **Cryo-ET**: T4P structure, Tad pilus, OMV biogenesis

#### 3.1.4 Omics Approaches
**Transcriptomics** (RNA-seq):
- **Time-course**: Predator + prey co-culture vs monoculture
- **Key findings**: 
  - Myxococcus: Enzyme secretion peaks during predation; rippling genes (*frz*) oscillate
  - Bdellovibrio: Phase-specific genes (attack vs growth phase); host modification enzymes

**Proteomics** (LC-MS/MS):
- **Secretome**: Enzymes, toxins, OMVs
- **Surface proteome**: T4P, Tad pilus, OMPs
- **Phosphoproteomics**: Frz system phosphorylation dynamics

**Metabolomics**:
- Prey metabolites consumed by predator
- Secondary metabolites (antibiotics, siderophores)
- Signaling molecules (AHLs, A-factor)

**Genomics** (comparative):
- **Predatory gene clusters**: *frz* (Myxococcus), *hit* (Bdellovibrio)
- **Horizontal gene transfer**: Antibiotic clusters, toxin-antitoxin
- **Genome reduction**: Bdellovibrio (3.8 Mb) vs free-living (4-8 Mb)

### 3.2 Molecular Mechanisms in Detail

#### 3.1.1 Myxococcus Motility Machinery

**Type IV Pili (T4P) — S-motility**:
- **Pilus structure**: Helical polymer of PilA (15 kDa), ~6 nm diameter
- **Extension**: PilB (ATPase) — adds PilA subunits
- **Retraction**: PilT (ATPase) — pulls PilA back, generates force (~100 pN)
- **Anchoring**: PilC (inner membrane), PilQ (outer membrane secretin)
- **Regulation**: PilS/PilR (two-component), Frz (chemosensory)

**A-motility (Adventurous) — Focal Adhesion**:
- **Agl-Glt complex**: 
  - AglRQS (inner membrane) — proton motive force → conformational change
  - Glt (cytoskeletal) — connects to MreB cytoskeleton
  - Focal adhesion sites: Transient adhesion to surface
- **Motility mechanism**: 
  1. Agl complex engages surface
  2. PMF-driven conformational change → pull
  3. Glt transmits force to cell body via MreB
  3. New adhesion forms ahead, old releases

**Frz Chemosensory System** (reversal control):
- **Components**: FrzCD (receptor), FrzE (CheA-CheY fusion), FrzF (CheW), FrzA/B (CheY)
- **Signal transduction**: 
  1. Stimulus → FrzCD conformational change
  2. FrzE autophosphorylation
  3. Phosphotransfer to FrzA/B
  4. FrzZ-P (response regulator) → MglAB (GTPase switch)
- **Outputs**: 
  - T4P reversal (S-motility)
  - A-motility adhesion turnover
  - Rippling wavelength control

#### 3.1.2 Bdellovibrio Invasion Machinery

**Host recognition**:
- **Bd0816**: Outer membrane lipoprotein, binds LPS
- **Bd3459/3460**: TonB-dependent transporters, may sense prey metabolites
- **Flagellar collision**: High-speed impact (~100 μm/s) → mechanical breach

**Peptidoglycan degradation**:
- **Lytic transglycosylases (LTGs)**:
  - *ltgA* (Bd0814): Major LTG, cleaves GlcNAc-MurNAc
  - *ltgB* (Bd3458): Specialized for entry
  - *ltgC* (Bd3457): Septation during progeny formation
- **Peptidoglycan hydrolases**: Amidases, endopeptidases

**Intraperiplasmic modification**:
- **Peptidoglycan crosslinking**: Prey PG modified → prevents lysis during growth
- **Outer membrane sealing**: Prey OM proteins modified → pore sealing
- **Host metabolism shutdown**: Effectors inhibit prey transcription/translation

**Septation and progeny release**:
- **FtsZ-ring**: Forms at regular intervals along filament
- **Division proteins**: FtsA, FtsQ, FtsW, FtsI (penicillin-binding protein)
- **BDL lysis**: Prey OM lysed by phage-like holin/endolysin system

### 3.3 Secondary Metabolites and Chemical Warfare

#### 3.1.1 Myxobacterial Secondary Metabolites
**Polyketide synthases (PKS) / Non-ribosomal peptide synthetases (NRPS)**:
- **Myxovirescin (TA)**: PKS/NRPS hybrid, inhibits RNA pol
- **Myxalamid**: NRPS, antifungal
- **Cystobactamids**: NRPS, DNA gyrase inhibitors
- **Sorangicins**: PKS, antifungal
- **Epothilones**: PKS/NRPS, microtubule stabilizers
- **Chivosazoles**: PKS/NRPS, antifungal
- **Ixabepilones**: PKS/NRPS, anticancer

**Regulation**:
- **A-factor** (butyrolactone): Quorum sensing for antibiotic production
- **Frz system**: Couples predation to antibiotic production
- **Stringent response** ((p)ppGpp): Starvation triggers antibiotic synthesis

#### 3.1.2 Bdellovibrio Chemical Arsenal
- **Fewer secondary metabolites** (genome streamlined)
- **Relies on**: Prey digestion enzymes, not antibiotics
- **Potential**: Engineering for novel antimicrobial production

### 3.4 Biotechnological Applications

#### 3.1.1 Bdellovibrio as Living Antibiotic
**Advantages over chemical antibiotics**:
- **Self-amplifying**: Multiplies at infection site
- **Evolves**: Counters resistance in real-time
- **Biofilm penetration**: Active motility through matrix
- **Specificity**: Gram-negative only (spares Gram-positive microbiome)
- **No resistance to date**: Multi-mechanistic attack

**Challenges**:
- **Immunogenicity**: LPS, flagellin trigger immune response
- **Narrow host range**: Only Gram-negative
- **Resistance evolution**: Prey can evolve resistance
- **Delivery**: IV, topical, aerosol formulations

**Engineering strategies**:
- **Attenuation**: Δ*hit* mutants (cannot enter prey, but still kill)
- **Targeting**: Surface display of pathogen-specific binders
- **Armoring**: Capsule modification to evade immunity
- **Armament**: Heterologous expression of antibiotics

#### 3.1.2 Myxobacteria for Drug Discovery
**Natural products pipeline**:
1. **Genome mining**: antiSMASH, PRISM for BGC prediction
2. **Heterologous expression**: *Myxococcus* or *Pseudomonas* hosts
3. **Fermentation optimization**: Solid-surface, co-culture elicitation
4. **Structural elucidation**: NMR, X-ray crystallography
5. **SAR optimization**: Semi-synthesis, mutasynthesis

**Clinical candidates from myxobacteria**:
- **Epothilone B (Ixabepilone)**: Breast cancer (approved)
- **Cystobactamid derivatives**: Gram-negative antibiotics (preclinical)
- **Sorangicin analogs**: Antifungal (preclinical)
- **Chivosazole derivatives**: Antifungal (preclinical)

### 3.5 Ecological Modeling and Ecosystem Integration

#### 3.1.1 Prokaryotic Predators in Ecosystem Models
**State variables**: 
- B_prey: Prey bacteria
- B_epi: Epibiotic predators (Myxococcus)
- B_endo: Endobiotic predators (Bdellovibrio)

**Equations**:
```
dB_prey/dt = μ_prey B_prey - g_epi B_prey B_epi - β B_prey B_endo
dB_epi/dt = ε_epi g_epi B_prey B_epi - m_epi B_epi
dB_endo/dt = (b δ I) - m_endo B_endo - β B_prey B_endo
```

**Key parameters**:
- g_epi: Swarm grazing rate (area/time)
- β: Adsorption rate (volume/time)
- δ: Bdelloplast lysis rate

#### 3.1.2 Ecosystem Impact
**Quantitative estimates**:
- **Soil**: Myxobacteria consume 10-30% of bacterial production
- **Freshwater**: Bdellovibrio consume 5-15% of bacterial production
- **Marine**: Bacteriovorax consume 2-10% of bacterial production
- **Biofilms**: Prokaryotic predators dominant grazers (vs protists)

**Nutrient regeneration**:
- N release: ~20% of prey N per predation event
- P release: ~15% of prey P
- Fe release: Siderophore-mediated

Free-living ancestor → Facultative predator → Obligate predator → Extreme reduction
(Paralleling mitochondria/plastid evolution but in reverse — predator not endosymbiont)

### 4.2 Advanced Molecular Mechanisms

#### 4.1.1 Myxococcus Social Signaling

**A-factor (Butyrolactone) Quorum Sensing**:
- **Signal**: 3-hydroxy-2-methyl-butyrolactone
- **Synthesis**: *afsA* (acyltransferase)
- **Reception**: ArpA (receptor) → ArpB/C (response regulators)
- **Targets**: Antibiotic BGCs, sporulation genes, enzyme secretion
- **Cross-talk**: Inter-species signaling in soil microbiome

**Contact-Dependent Signaling (CDS)**:
- **Tad pilus**: Delivers signaling proteins during cell-cell contact
- **C-signal (CsgA)**: 17 kDa protein, cell-surface associated
  - *csgA* expression regulated by FruA/FruB
  - C-signal accumulation triggers fruiting body development
  - **C-signal level** = cell density proxy

**Chemosensory Array (Frz) Crosstalk**:
- Frz integrates: Nutrient status, prey signals, cell density, surface contact
- **Outputs**: Reversal frequency, enzyme secretion, antibiotic production
- **Evolutionary origin**: Homologous to eukaryotic chemotaxis (CheA/CheY)
