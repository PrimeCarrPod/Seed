### 8.1 Proto-Indo-European Männerbund and the Kóryos Institution

The Kóryos (PIE *kóro- "cutting, section, division") was a central PIE institution: a Männerbund (warrior band) of unmarried males aged 12–19, expelled from society into the wilderness during winter as a rite of passage. They identified mystically with wolves/dogs — symbols of death, lawlessness, liminality, and warrior fury.

**Demographic structure:**
- Age range: 12–19 years (puberty to adulthood)
- Size: 12–50 warriors per band
- Duration: 2–4 winters
- Reintegration: upon completion, removal of wolf/dog skins, resurrection as adult warriors

**Genetic signature:** Kóryos males carried specific Y-chromosome haplogroups (R1a-Z93, R1b-L51) and autosomal profiles reflecting steppe ancestry (Yamnaya ~60%, EEF ~30%, WHG ~10%). These lineages expanded across Eurasia with Indo-European languages.

Identified with wolves dogs. Genetic signature: Y-chromosome haplogroups R-one-a-Z-nine-three, R-one-b-L-five-one. Steppe ancestry Yamnaya sixty-percent, E-E-F thirty-percent, W-H-G ten-percent."

### 8.4 Ancient DNA Evidence: Kóryos Canine Remains

**Krasnosamarskoe (Srubnaya, ~1750 BCE, Samara, Russia):**
- 51 canine/wolf remains, 30% of osteological assemblage
- Age profile: 2–12 years (peak at 6–8 years)
- Butchery: snouts in 3 pieces, crania geometrically fragmented
- Seasonality: winter (cementochronology of tooth eruption)

**Ancient DNA (n = 24 canids):**
- 18 dogs (C. familiaris), 6 wolves (C. lupus)
- Dogs: mitochondrial haplogroup A (78%), B (22%)
- Nuclear: PCA clusters with modern European dogs, not wolves
- OXTR rs8679684: 83% GG (high expression), 17% GA

**Selection scans (PBS, iHS):**
