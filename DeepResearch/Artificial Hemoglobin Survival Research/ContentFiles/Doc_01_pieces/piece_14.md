# Document 1: Historical Foundations & Pathophysiology of Artificial Hemoglobin Survival Research
## Piece 14/18: The Physics of Gas Transport in Artificial Carriers

Understanding the fundamental physics governing gas transport in artificial carriers is essential for rational design. Three distinct physical mechanisms exist:

**1. Chemical Binding (Hemoglobin-based)**: Reversible coordination of O₂ to ferrous heme iron (Fe²⁺). The binding follows cooperative kinetics described by the Adair equation: Y = (K₁P + 2K₁K₂P² + 3K₁K₂K₃P³ + 4K₁K₂K₃K₄P⁴) / (4(1 + K₁P + K₁K₂P² + K₁K₂K₃P³ + K₁K₂K₃K₄P⁴)), where K₁-K₄ are sequential binding constants. Cooperativity arises from the T (tense) → R (relaxed) quaternary transition. The Monod-Wyman-Changeux (MWC) allosteric model describes this as an equilibrium between T and R states: L = [T₀]/[R₀] (allosteric constant), with c = K_R/K_T (affinity ratio). Effectors (H⁺, CO₂, 2,3-DPG, Cl⁻, KC1003) stabilize the T state, increasing P50.

**2. Physical Dissolution (PFCs)**: Governed by Henry's Law: C = α × P, where α is the Ostwald solubility coefficient. For perfluorodecalin at 37°C: α_O₂ ≈ 0.045 mL O₂/mL PFC/atm, α_CO₂ ≈ 0.15 mL CO₂/mL PFC/atm. The high CO₂/O₂ solubility ratio (~3.3) is advantageous for CO₂ removal. Gas exchange kinetics are diffusion-limited: flux J = D × A × ΔC / δ, where D is diffusion coefficient, A is interfacial area, δ is boundary layer thickness. In intravenous emulsions, droplet radius ~0.1-0.2 μm gives enormous A/V ratio. In liquid ventilation, alveolar surface area (~70 m²) and thin diffusion barrier (0.5-1 μm) enable rapid exchange.

**3. Gas Phase Microbubbles (LOMs)**: Oxygen exists as compressed gas within a lipid monolayer shell. The Laplace pressure ΔP = 2γ/r (γ = surface tension ~10-20 mN/m for DSPC, r = 1-2 μm) creates internal pressure of 10-40 kPa above ambient, stabilizing the bubble against dissolution. Oxygen diffusion from bubble to blood follows Fick's law with effective diffusion coefficient enhanced by the thin lipid shell (δ ≈ 4-5 nm). The high gas volume fraction (60-70%) provides oxygen density 3-4× blood.

Each mechanism has distinct advantages and limitations for entrapment. Chemical binding allows allosteric control but requires heme iron management. Physical dissolution offers high capacity and CO₂ solubility but requires high PO₂ for loading. Gas phase provides instant O₂ delivery without lung function but cannot address CO₂. The integration of these mechanisms across the 17 documents reveals a comprehensive toolkit for entrapment survival engineering.
