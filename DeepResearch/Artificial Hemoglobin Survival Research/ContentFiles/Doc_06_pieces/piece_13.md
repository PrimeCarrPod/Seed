# Document 6: Perfluorocarbons (PFCs) and the Paradigm of Liquid Ventilation
## Piece 13/18: Training, Competency, and Medical Command for PFC Deployment

The deployment of perfluorocarbons, particularly liquid ventilation (TLV/PLV), demands specialized knowledge and skills beyond standard critical care. This piece defines competency-based training curricula, certification pathways, medical command structures, and sustainment requirements for PFC deployment in entrapment rescue.

**Responder Levels and PFC Scope of Practice**:

| Level | Personnel | PFC Scope | Prerequisites |
|-------|-----------|-----------|---------------|
| **Level 1: Awareness** | All high-risk site workers (miners, submariners, tunnelers) | Recognize PFC cache, activate PFC-trained responders | None |
| **Level 2: Operational (IV PFC + PLV)** | Mine rescue medics, IDCs, USAR medics, paramedics, flight nurses | IV PFC administration, PLV initiation, monitoring | ALS/PALS, IV/IO proficiency, 1yr experience |
| **Level 3: Advanced (TLV + Complex Combos)** | Physicians (EM, CCM, Pulm/CC), Flight Surgeons, CCP | TLV initiation/management, TLV/ECCO₂R combos, weaning | Board cert (EM/CCM/Pulm), Level 2 cert, 2yr CC experience |
| **Level 4: Medical Command** | ED/CCM attendings, Toxicologists, Medical Directors | Remote TLV management, protocol deviation, ECMO decision | Level 3 cert, Medical Command course |

**Level 1: Awareness (4 Hours Initial, 2 Hours Annual Refresher)**:
*Target Audience*: All personnel at high-risk sites (mines, submarines, tunnels, altitude >2500m).

*Learning Objectives*:
1. Recognize PFC cache location and contents (color-coded: blue=IV, green=LV).
2. Activate PFC-trained responders (radio/satellite protocol).
3. Basic atmosphere awareness (O₂, CO₂, CO, H₂S, CH₄ — when PFCs indicated).
4. Assist Level 2+ responders (equipment prep, patient positioning, documentation).
5. Atmosphere safety: CH₄ >5% → NO O₂ enrichment (explosion risk).

*Skills Stations*:
- Cache access and inventory (2 min drill).
- Atmosphere monitor reading (O₂, CO₂, CH₄, H₂S, CO).
- Communication drill (standardized PFC request format).
- Patient positioning for PLV/TLV (supine, HOB 30°).

*Assessment*: Written test (80% pass) + cache access drill (2 min).

*Certification*: "PFC Awareness Responder" (valid 1 year).

**Level 2: Operational — IV PFC & PLV (40 Hours Initial, 16 Hours Annual Refresher)**:
*Prerequisites*: Current ALS/PALS, IV/IO proficiency, 1 year field experience.

*Curriculum Modules*:

**Module 1: PFC Pharmacology & Physics (8h)**:
- Henry's Law, gas solubility, FiO₂ dependency.
- IV PFC PK/PD, PLV/TLV physiology, CO₂ bottleneck.
- Dosing, reconstitution, administration, compatibility.

**Module 2: IV PFC Administration (8h)**:
- Indications: CO poisoning, gas embolism, DCS, adjunct O₂ delivery.
- Dosing: Load 1.8 g/kg over 2h, maintenance 0.5 g/kg q12h.
- 100% FiO₂ requirement — O₂ supply management.
- Monitoring: SpMet, COHb%, platelet count, liver enzymes, PFC level.
- Complications: Thrombocytopenia, volume overload, hepatosplenomegaly.

**Module 3: PLV Initiation & Management (12h)**:
- Indications: ARDS, smoke inhalation, HAPE, blast lung, meconium aspiration.
- Procedure: Intubation → recruitment → PFC instill (30 mL/kg) → ventilator setup.
- Ventilator Settings: VC 6 mL/kg, PEEP 12-15, FiO₂ 1.0 → titrate.
- PFC Management: Top-off 50-100 mL q4h, suction q1-2h, reposition q2h.
- Monitoring: PaO₂/FiO₂, driving pressure, PaCO₂, platelet count, PFC top-off volume.
- Weaning: Suction 200-300 mL q4h → remove when P/F >250 on FiO₂ 0.4.

**Module 4: CO₂ Mitigation Adjuncts (4h)**:
- THAM dosing/titration, IPRAC setup/monitoring, peritoneal dialysis.
- Integration with PLV/PLV (CO₂ mitigation ladder).
- Acetazolamide, metabolic suppression (propofol, hypothermia).

**Module 5: Scenario-Based Simulations (8h)**:
- Scenario 1: Mine fire — CO poisoning + smoke inhalation → IV PFC + PLV.
- Scenario 2: Submarine — CO₂ crisis → PLV + IPRAC + THAM.
- Scenario 3: Tunnel collapse — blast lung + crush → ErythroMer + PLV.
- Scenario 4: High altitude — HAPE → PLV + nifedipine/dexamethasone.
- Debrief with video review, protocol adherence scoring.

*Assessment*: Written exam (85% pass) + 4 simulation scenarios (pass/fail) + skills checkout (PLV setup, IV PFC dosing, THAM titration).

*Certification*: "PFC Operational Provider — IV & PLV" (valid 2 years).

**Level 3: Advanced — TLV & Complex Combinations (80 Hours Initial, 24 Hours Annual Refresher)**:
*Prerequisites*: Board certification (EM/CCM/Pulm), Level 2 cert, 2 years critical care experience.

*Additional Curriculum*:

**Module 1: TLV Mechanics & Device Mastery (16h)**:
- Liquid ventilator mechanics (pump, oxygenator, sweep gas, active expiration).
- Device setup, priming, troubleshooting (air trapping, vapor lock, circuit contamination).
- TLV settings: V_T 10 mL/kg, rate 4-6/min, sweep gas 10 L/min O₂.
- Hemodynamics in TLV (high intrathoracic pressure, reduced venous return).
- TLV weaning: transition to gas ventilation (gas vent over PFC, wean sweep gas).

**Module 2: TLV Physiology & Advanced Management (16h)**:
- TLV hemodynamics (high intrathoracic pressure, CO reduction, vasopressor needs).
- CO₂ kinetics in TLV (boundary layer, sweep gas optimization, HFLV).
- TLV for specific indications: severe ARDS, smoke inhalation, blast lung, meconium.
- TLV complications: air trapping, vapor lock, circuit contamination, hemodynamic collapse.
- TLV weaning protocol (transition to gas vent → PLV → extubation).

**Module 3: Combination Therapy Mastery (16h)**:
- PFC + ErythroMer/M101/LOMs — dosing, monitoring, titration, weaning.
- PFC + THAM/IPRAC/ECCO₂R/PD — CO₂ mitigation ladder.
- PFC + blood products, vasopressors, antibiotics, anticoagulants.
- Complex scenarios: crush + ARDS, DCS + ARDS, smoke + CO + crush.

**Module 4: TLV Device Mastery & Troubleshooting (16h)**:
- Device-specific training (Inolavent, Acutronic, prototype portable TLV).
- Circuit priming, degassing, priming volume, temperature control.
- Emergency procedures: circuit rupture, pump failure, vapor lock, oxygenator failure.
- Maintenance: cleaning, sterilization, calibration, oxygenator replacement.

**Module 5: Medical Command & Telemedicine (16h)**:
- Remote TLV management (telemetry, video, data sync).
- Protocol deviation authority, resource allocation, ethical allocation.
- Mass casualty triage with PFCs (caching, allocation, ethics).
- After-action review, data collection, registry reporting.

*Assessment*: Oral boards (3 cases) + written exam (90%) + 6 simulation scenarios (including 2 TLV, 2 combination).

*Certification*: "PFC Advanced Provider — TLV & Combinations" (valid 2 years).

**Level 4: Medical Command (24 Hours Initial, 8 Hours Annual)**:
*Target Audience*: ED/CCM attendings, Toxicologists, Medical Directors.

*Focus*: Remote consultation, protocol deviation approval, resource allocation, regulatory navigation.

**Sustainment and Quality Assurance**:

| Activity | Frequency | Level |
|----------|-----------|-------|

| Competency Re-verification | Annual | All |

**Medical Command Structure for PFC Deployment**:

*On-Scene Medical Command*:
- **Level 2 Provider**: Initiates PLV/IV PFC per protocol.
- **Level 3 Provider (On-site or Telemedicine)**: Authorizes TLV, combination deviations, weaning.
- **Level 4 Medical Command (Remote)**: Protocol deviations, resource allocation, ECMO/ECMO consult.

*Telemedicine Protocol*:
- **Bandwidth**: Minimum 1 Mbps (video + vitals + vent waveforms).
- **Platform**: Encrypted (AES-256), HIPAA-compliant (Doxy.me, Teladoc, custom).
- **Data Sync**: Vital signs (1 Hz), vent waveforms (10 Hz), labs (manual entry), carrier logs.
- **Documentation**: Shared EMR (Epic/Cerner/DoD MHS) or standalone PFC registry.

*Decision Authority Matrix*:
| Decision | Level 2 | Level 3 | Level 4 |
|----------|---------|---------|---------|
| IV PFC Initiation | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ |
| PLV Initiation | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ |
| TLV Initiation | ✗ | ✓ | ✓ |
| Combination Deviation | ✗ | ✓ (within protocol) | ✓ (any) |
| Weaning TLV | ✗ | ✓ | ✓ |
| ECMO Consult | ✗ | ✗ | ✓ |
| Protocol Deviation | ✗ | ✓ (minor) | ✓ (major) |

**Instructor Requirements**:
- **Lead Instructor**: Level 3 certified, 5+ years TLV/PLV experience, faculty development course.
- **Assistant Instructor**: Level 2 certified, 2+ years experience.
- **Ratio**: 1:4 for skills, 1:6 for simulation.
- **Currency**: Must teach ≥2 courses/year + 1 real event or high-fidelity sim/year.

**International Training Harmonization**:
- **NATO STANAG 2554**: Common PFC training standards for multinational ops.
- **ICAR MEDCOM**: Mountain rescue PLV module.
- **WHO EMT**: Type 2/3 EMT oxygen carrier module (PLV required).
- **ISMERLO/NATO SMERLO**: Submarine rescue TLV/PLV standards.

**Conclusion**: The successful deployment of PFCs — especially liquid ventilation — in entrapment rescue requires a tiered, competency-based training infrastructure that builds from basic awareness to advanced TLV mastery. The medical command structure must support real-time decision-making for complex combination therapies, with clear authority gradients and telemedicine integration. Sustainment through regular simulation, protocol updates, and after-action reviews ensures readiness. International harmonization through NATO, WHO, ICAR, and ISMERLO ensures interoperability in multinational entrapment responses. The investment in training infrastructure is modest compared to the lives saved when PFCs are deployed correctly in the right scenarios by properly trained personnel.
