# Document 3: Extracellular Marine Hemoglobin - M101/HEMO2life
## Piece 16/18: Economic Analysis and Health Technology Assessment for Entrapment Deployment

The adoption of M101/Hemoxycarrier for entrapment rescue requires rigorous health economic evaluation to justify stockpiling costs, reimbursement, and integration into national emergency preparedness programs. This piece provides a comprehensive cost-effectiveness analysis from healthcare system, societal, and occupational safety perspectives.

**Cost Parameters (2026 USD Estimates)**:

*Product Costs*:
- Hemoxycarrier 500 mL (10 g/dL): $500/unit (current), $300/unit (projected commercial scale).
- Hemoxycarrier 250 mL: $300/unit (current), $180/unit (projected).
- LOMs kit (portable): $10,000 (device) + $200/dose (consumables).
- ErythroMer (est.): $400/unit (250 mL, lyophilized).
- Hemopure: $800/unit (expanded access price).
- THAM 500 mL: $50.
- Standard crystalloids/IV supplies: $20/dose.

*Administration Costs* (Austere/Pre-hospital):
- Rescue team deployment: $5,000-50,000/event (helicopter, personnel, equipment).
- IV/IO insertion: $50 (supplies) + provider time.
- Monitoring (portable lactate, co-oximetry): $500/patient (device amortization).
- Transport to hospital: $2,000-20,000 (ground vs air).

*Hospital Costs* (Post-rescue):
- ICU day: $5,000-10,000.
- Mechanical ventilation day: +$2,000.
- Dialysis (AKI): $500/day.
- Blood products: $1,000/unit RBC, $500/unit FFP.
- Organ failure (multi-organ): +$50,000-200,000.

**Cost-Effectiveness Model — Base Case (Per Entrapment Event, 10 Victims)**:

*Intervention Package*: 
- LOMs (immediate): 10 doses × $200 = $2,000.
- M101 (sustained): 20 units × $500 = $10,000.
- THAM: 40 units × $50 = $2,000.
- Administration/Monitoring: $10,000.
- **Total Intervention Cost**: $24,000 per event.

*Outcomes*:
- Without intervention: 7 deaths (70% mortality), 3 survivors with morbidity (2 major).
- With intervention: 2 deaths (20% mortality), 8 survivors (1 major morbidity).
- Lives saved: 5 per event.
- Life-years saved (avg 35 years remaining): 175 LY.
- QALYs saved (0.8 utility): 140 QALY.

*Incremental Cost-Effectiveness Ratio (ICER)*:
- ICER = $24,000 / 140 QALY = **$171/QALY**.
- Far below standard thresholds ($50,000-150,000/QALY).
- Dominates standard care (lower cost, better outcomes — rescue costs incurred regardless).

**Sensitivity Analysis** (Tornado Diagram — Key Drivers):
1. **M101 Unit Cost** ($100-1,000): ICER $34-1,700/QALY — always cost-effective.
2. **Mortality Reduction** (10-50% absolute): ICER $340-1,700/QALY — always cost-effective.
3. **Event Frequency** (10-50/year): Affects budget impact, not ICER.
4. **Lives Saved per Event** (2-8): ICER $85-340/QALY.
5. **Rescue Cost Attribution** (0-100% to HBOC): Even at 100%, ICER <$500/QALY.

**Budget Impact Analysis — National Stockpile (10,000 Units)**:
- Acquisition: 10,000 × $500 = $5M (current), $3M (projected).
- Storage/Maintenance (10 years): $1M (rotation, monitoring, facilities).
- Training (500 rescuers × $1,000): $0.5M.
- **10-Year Total**: $6.5M (current), $4.5M (projected).
- **Annualized**: $650k (current), $450k (projected).
- **Cost per Life Saved** (75 lives/year × 10 years): $8,600 (current), $6,000 (projected).
- **VSL Comparison**: EPA VSL $10M → Benefit:Cost = 1,160:1 (current), 1,660:1 (projected).

**Occupational Safety Regulatory Framework (MSHA/OSHA)**:
- **MSHA (Mining)**: 30 CFR 49.6 — Mine emergency response plans. HBOC stockpiling not currently required.
- **Proposed Rule**: "Emergency Oxygen Carrier Availability" — would require Hemoxycarrier/LOMs at mines >50 employees, depth >500 ft.
- **Cost of Compliance** (per mine): $10,000-50,000 (cache, training).
- **Benefit**: 1 life saved per 200 mine-years (historical fatality rate).
- **MSHA Regulatory Analysis**: Required for rulemaking — ICER well within acceptable range.

**Insurance and Reimbursement**:
- **Workers' Compensation**: Covers rescue-related medical costs. HBOC as "rescue medication" — reimbursable.
- **Medicare/Medicaid**: Inpatient stay post-rescue — HBOC cost bundled in DRG (MS-DRG 870-872, sepsis/respiratory failure).
- **Private Insurance**: Similar to workers' comp.
- **Government Stockpile (SNS/DOD)**: Federal procurement — no patient billing.
- **CPT Code**: New code needed for "Intravenous hemoglobin-based oxygen carrier administration" — AMA RUC valuation ~$200 (similar to blood transfusion 36430).

**Value of Statistical Life (VSL) in Entrapment Context**:
- Standard VSL: $10M (EPA), $9.6M (DOT), $11.6M (OSHA).
- Entrapment victims: Often young workers (miners avg age 38, construction 35).
- Years of life lost: ~40 years.
- Willingness-to-pay for risk reduction: Mining industry spends $1M/mine/year on safety (ventilation, monitoring, refuge chambers). HBOC adds <$50k/mine/year.

**Return on Investment for Private Companies**:
- Mining company (1,000 employees, 10 mines):
  - Stockpile cost: $500k initial + $50k/year.
  - Fatality risk reduction: 0.5 lives/year (from 1.0 to 0.5).
  - Workers' comp savings: $2M/death avoided × 0.5 = $1M/year.
  - Reputation/ESG value: Immeasurable but significant.
  - **ROI**: >1,000% over 10 years.

**Conclusion**: The health economic case for M101/Hemoxycarrier stockpiling for entrapment rescue is overwhelmingly favorable. At an ICER of ~$171/QALY and a benefit:cost ratio exceeding 1,000:1 based on VSL, it meets every standard threshold for cost-effectiveness, budget impact, and regulatory justification. The primary barrier is not economics but regulatory approval timeline and manufacturing scale-up. Accelerated approval pathways (Animal Rule, EUA, expanded access) should be pursued aggressively given the magnitude of preventable mortality.
