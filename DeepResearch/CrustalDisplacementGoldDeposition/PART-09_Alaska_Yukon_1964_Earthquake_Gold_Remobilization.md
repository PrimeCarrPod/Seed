# PART-09: Alaska & Yukon — 1964 Great Alaska Earthquake Gold Remobilization, Pleistocene Displacement Events, and Megathrust-Hydrothermal Coupling

## 9.1 Tectonic Setting: Alaska-Aleutian Subduction and Displacement Coupling

### 9.1.1 Plate Boundary Configuration

**Alaska sits on the North American Plate** overriding the **Pacific Plate** at the **Alaska-Aleutian Trench**.
**Convergence rate:** 55–75 mm/yr (NW-directed)
**Subduction angle:** 10–15° (shallow, flat-slab segment) → 45° (steep)
**Trench distance from coast:** 100–150 km

**Key structures:**
- **Megathrust:** 1964 rupture (M_w 9.2), 800 × 200 km
- **Denali Fault:** Right-lateral strike-slip, 2002 M_w 7.9
- **Castle Mountain Fault:** Active reverse, Anchorage seismic hazard
- **Border Ranges Fault:** Suture zone, Wrangellia terrane boundary

---

### 9.1.2 Crustal Displacement at High Latitudes

**In Hapgood-Thomas model (Part 01), displacement magnitude varies with latitude:**
- **Equatorial regions:** Maximum translation (~3,000 km)
- **Polar regions:** Minimum translation (rotation pole vicinity)

**For 30° displacement centered on Hudson Bay (Hapgood pole):**
- **Alaska (60–70°N):** Translation vector **~1,500–2,000 km** (smaller than equator)
- **Direction:** **Southwestward** (toward new equator)
- **Vertical component:** **Uplift 1–3 km** (isostatic + centrifugal potential change)

**1964 M_w 9.2 event: POST-DISPLACEMENT MEGATHRUST ADJUSTMENT — not primary displacement.**
But **largest instrumented earthquake** — provides **unprecedented data** on crustal-scale hydrothermal/seismic coupling.

### 9.2.2 Coseismic Deformation and Hydrothermal Impact

**Vertical displacement (Plafker, 1965, 1969):**
- **Uplift zone:** Montague Island +11 m, Prince William Sound +2–5 m
- **Subsidence zone:** Anchorage -1.5 m, Kenai Peninsula -1.0 m, Kodiak -0.5 m
- **Hinge line:** ~100 km wide, crosses Turnagain Arm

**Horizontal displacement:**
- **Seaward (SW) motion:** 10–20 m (coastal), 5–10 m (inland)
- **Distributed shear:** 50 km wide zone

**Fault zone permeability enhancement:**
- **Megathrust rupture:** 800 × 200 km = 1.6×10¹¹ m²
- **Damage zone width:** ~1–5 km (both plates)
- **Permeability increase:** k: 10⁻¹⁸ → 10⁻¹² m² (6 orders)
- **Fluid volume mobilized:** V_f = A · h · φ · Δk/k ≈ 1.6×10¹¹ · 5000 · 0.01 · 10⁶ = **8×10¹⁵ m³**

**This is PLANETARY-SCALE fluid mobilization — comparable to displacement event (Part 01: 10¹⁵ m³).**

### 9.3.3 Valdez Creek: Liquefaction of Frozen Placer

**Location:** 63.12°N, 146.35°W (Alaska Range foothills)
**Deposit:** **Pleistocene glacial outwash + Tertiary gravel, perennially frozen**
**Pre-1964:** Small-scale drift mining, 10–15 kg/yr
**1964 earthquake effects (Bundtzen et al., 1992):**

| Effect | Observation | Gold Impact |
|--------|-------------|-------------|
| **Liquefaction** | Sand blows, lateral spreads over 50 km² | **Frozen gravel → mobile slurry** |
| **Gold release** | Nuggets "popped out" of ice matrix | **Previously locked gold mobilized** |
| **Hydraulic sorting** | Slurry flow 2–5 km downvalley | **Natural sluicing — nuggets concentrated** |
| **Fresh surfaces** | Nuggets with conchoidal fracture | **No weathering rind — freshly fractured** |
| **Hg amalgamation** | Efficiency 40% → 95% | **Clean Au⁰ surfaces** |

**Production 1964–1966: 180 kg (vs 45 kg prior 10 years) — 4× total historic in 3 years.**

### 9.3.6 Kenai Peninsula: Glacial Outburst + Seismic Gold

**Setting:** **Glacier-dammed lakes (Skilak, Tustumena, etc.)** breached by 1964 shaking.

**Mechanism (Post & Mayo, 1971):**
1. **Seismic waves** → ice dam fracture
2. **Jökulhlaup** (glacial outburst flood) → 10⁴–10⁵ m³/s
3. **Flood entrains** glacial flour + placer gold from valley trains
4. **Deposition** on outwash plains — **hydraulic sorting**

**Gold recovery 1964–1968: 270 kg from jökulhlaup deposits (vs 30 kg/decade prior).**

## 9.4 Klondike/Yukon: Displacement Event Placer Architecture

### 9.4.1 Klondike Gold Fields: Regional Context

**Location:** 64.0°N, 139.4°W (Dawson City, Yukon)
**Production:** **~20 Moz (620 tonnes) since 1896**
**Geology:** **Paleozoic metamorphics** (Klondike Schist) + **Cretaceous intrusions** + **Tertiary paleoplacers**

**Key anomaly:** **Gold concentrated in "White Channel Gravel" (WCG) — Tertiary (Eocene) paleovalley fill.**
**WCG characteristics (Froese et al., 2009):**
- **Age:** 40–50 Ma (Ar/Ar on interbedded tephra)
- **Thickness:** 30–60 m
- **Gold grade:** 1–10 g/m³ (up to 100 g/m³ in pay streaks)
- **Grain size:** Coarse (nuggets to 1 kg), **low flatness (0.15)**
- **Heavy minerals:** Magnetite, ilmenite, chromite, **diamonds** (micro)

**Conventional model:** Fluvial concentration in Tertiary braided river.
**Problems:** No modern river matches WCG scale/grade; **diamonds require kimberlite source (none nearby)**; **coarse nuggets at valley bottom — hydraulic paradox.**

### 9.4.2 Displacement Mega-Tsunami Model for Klondike

**12 ka displacement event (Part 01) — Pacific mega-tsunami enters Arctic via Bering Strait?**

**Bering Strait geometry (present): 85 km wide, 50 m deep.**
**12 ka: Sea level -120 m → Bering Strait CLOSED (land bridge).**
**But displacement TSUNAMI OVERTOPS land bridge!**

**Tsunami runup into Yukon River (Part 03 model):**
- **Initial wave:** 500–1000 m at coast
- **Propagation up Yukon:** 3,000 km to Dawson
- **Attenuation:** h ∝ x^(-1/4) for channelized flow
- **At Dawson (3,000 km):** h ≈ 1000 · (3000/100)^(-1/4) ≈ **100 m**
- **Velocity:** u ≈ √(gh) ≈ **30 m/s**

**This FLOODS Klondike valleys to 100 m depth — REWORKS TERTIARY GRAVELS.**
