## 7.2 Substrate Geology: The Pleistocene Carbonate Platform and the Osa Igneous Complex Basement

The seabed substrate at Site Alpha, at 30 meters depth, is fundamentally different — in its lithology, its origin, its geotechnical properties, its foundation-engineering behavior, and its response to the cyclic, the seismic, and the turbidite-flow loading — from the terrigenous-clastic, siliciclastic, sandstone-and-shale-dominated substrates of the five California sites, reflecting the radically different tectonic, the climatic, and the oceanographic settings of the Costa Rican Pacific margin relative to the California continental margin. The Site Alpha substrate consists of a thin (0.1 to 0.5 meters), discontinuous, Holocene-age, biogenic, carbonate-rich, foraminiferal-coccolithophorid-pteropod, calcareous-ooze-to-calcareous-sand hemipelagic sediment drape, overlying a 5-to-15-meter-thick, Pleistocene-age, shallow-marine, reefal-to-perireefal, coralgal-foraminiferal, bioclastic, packstone-to-grainstone-to-floatstone-to-rudstone, carbonate platform sequence — the "Osa Carbonate Platform," a Pleistocene, interglacial-highstand, tropical, shallow-water, photic-zone, carbonate-factory deposit that accumulated, during the successive, Quaternary sea-level highstands (the Marine Isotope Stages 5e, 7, 9, 11, and so forth), on the subsiding, tectonically active, outer-shelf and the upper-slope surface of the Caribbean Plate, nourished by the prolific, coral-reef, coralline-algal, halimeda, foraminiferal, molluscan, and echinodermal carbonate production that characterizes the warm, clear, oligotrophic, tropical eastern Pacific waters of the Costa Rican margin, and that now, having been submerged — drowned — by the post-glacial, Holocene sea-level rise of approximately 120 meters over the past 20,000 years, lies at the 30-meter water depth, exposed, at the seabed, as a variably cemented, variably lithified, variably bioeroded, variably bioencrusted, irregularly weathered, vuggy, cavernous, karstified, carbonate-rock surface. Beneath the Osa Carbonate Platform — at depths of 10 to 20 meters below the present seabed — lies the Osa Igneous Complex, the Late Cretaceous to Paleogene (approximately 60 to 100 million years before present), oceanic-plateau, ophiolitic, mafic-to-ultramafic, basaltic-to-gabbroic-to-peridotitic, igneous-and-metamorphic rock assemblage that forms the basement — the geological "floor" — of the Osa Peninsula and the adjacent offshore region, and that represents a fragment of the Caribbean Large Igneous Province (CLIP), the vast, thick (10 to 20 kilometers), oceanic-plateau, flood-basalt province that was generated, during the Late Cretaceous, by the massive, decompression-melting, mantle-plume-head volcanism (the "Galapagos hotspot" being the currently active, but the geographically displaced, descendant of the CLIP mantle plume) that produced the enormous volume of the basaltic magma — the lava flows, the dikes, the sills, the gabbroic intrusions, and the ultramafic cumulates — that now constitute the basement of the Caribbean Plate and that form the foundation, the deep-crustal substrate, the ultimate anchoring medium, for the Atlantis pod at Site Alpha. The geotechnical properties of the Osa Carbonate Platform — as determined from the geotechnical boreholes CR-OSA-01 and CR-OSA-02, drilled to depths of 25 meters below the seabed, using the SeaMole-2 remotely operated seafloor drill rig — are characterized by the extreme heterogeneity, the high variability, and the strong dependence on the degree of the cementation, the lithification, and the karstification that are typical of the tropical, shallow-marine, carbonate-platform deposits: the well-cemented, well-lithified, low-porosity (5 to 15 percent), high-strength (UCS of 20 to 60 MPa), high-modulus (E of 10 to 30 GPa), brittle, fracturing, dolomitic and the low-magnesium-calcite-cemented, grainstone and the rudstone layers, which provide the excellent, competent, high-bearing-capacity anchoring substrate, are interspersed, irregularly, unpredictably, with the poorly cemented, poorly lithified, high-porosity (25 to 45 percent), low-strength (UCS of 2 to 8 MPa), low-modulus (E of 1 to 5 GPa), compressible, deformable, chalky, micritic, foraminiferal packstone and the wackestone layers, and with the completely uncemented, unlithified, loose, granular, bioclastic, carbonate-sand and the carbonate-gravel layers, which, if they are encountered at the pile-embedment depth, reduce, dramatically, the pile's skin-friction and the end-bearing capacity, potentially causing the pile to "punch through" the weak layer and to experience the sudden, dynamic, uncontrolled settlement — the "pile-running" phenomenon that has been the cause of the catastrophic foundation failures in the carbonate-platform environments worldwide.
