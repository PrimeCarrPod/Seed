## 13.3 Comparative Foundation-Engineering Design Philosophies: The Franciscan Mélange Conservative Lower-Bound Statistical Approach versus the Oxisol Deterministic Factor-of-Safety Approach

The fundamentally different geotechnical characteristics of the Franciscan mélange (the California high-displacement domain) and the Osa Oxisol (the Costa Rican pivot-axis domain) drive the fundamentally different foundation-engineering design philosophies — the "conservative, the lower-bound, the statistical, the probabilistic, the reliability-based" approach for the Franciscan mélange, versus the "deterministic, the factor-of-safety, the allowable-stress, the working-stress" approach for the Oxisol — that are the underlying, the philosophical, the methodological framework for the Atlantis pod anchoring-system designs presented in Parts 002 through 011, and that are the essential, the prerequisite knowledge for the understanding, the evaluation, and the critical review of those designs. The Franciscan-mélange approach acknowledges, from the outset, that the geotechnical properties of the mélange — the distribution, the size, the shape, the strength, and the deformability of the blocks within the matrix; the spatial distribution, the thickness, the continuity, and the mechanical properties of the matrix itself; the distribution, the orientation, the persistence, the roughness, the infilling, and the shear-strength characteristics of the fractures, the joints, the faults, and the shear zones that transect the mélange; and the depth to the competent, the unsheared, the high-strength basement rock, if such a basement rock exists at the drillable depth below the mélange — are the fundamentally uncertain, the spatiallly variable, the statistically heterogeneous, and the irreducible unpredictable, and that the design, accordingly, must be based on the "worst-credible," the "reasonably-foreseeable," the conservative, the lower-bound estimates of the matrix and the block properties, with the additional, the substantial factors of safety — the factors of 3 to 5, or more, on the ultimate, the geotechnical capacity of the piles and the anchors — to account for the uncertainties that cannot be quantified, the probabilisticly, with the confidence, given the limited, the site-specific, the borehole and the in-situ test data that can be obtained, the practically and the economically, in the deep-water, the remotely operated, the expensive and the logistically difficult offshore-drilling environment. The Oxisol approach, by contrast, exploits the homogeneity, the predictability, the low spatial variability, and the well-characterized geotechnical properties of the deeply weathered, the structurally intact, the tropical-soil profile to employ the deterministic, the factor-of-safety, the allowable-stress design methodology — the same, the standard, the widely accepted, the code-based design methodology that is used, the universally, for the terrestrial, the shallow-and-the-deep foundation design in the competent, the well-characterized, the low-uncertainty soil and the rock conditions — with the typical factors of safety of 1.5 to 2.5 (for the shallow foundations) or 2.0 to 3.0 (for the deep foundations, the piles, and the anchors) that are prescribed by the international, the national, and the industry-specific geotechnical design codes and the standards (the Eurocode 7, the AASHTO LRFD Bridge Design Specifications, the API RP 2GEO for the offshore geotechnical design, the ISO 19901-4 for the offshore foundations, and the other, the applicable, the consensus standards), and that, in the Costa Rican Oxisol-and-the-igneous-basement substrates, are comfortably exceeded by the actual, the as-built, the load-tested, the verified factor-of-safety margins of 10 to 25, providing the extraordinary, the confidence-building, the "overkill" level of the geotechnical foundation safety that is the hallmark, the signature, the proud achievement of the Atlantis program's Costa Rican pivot-axis deployments.
