## 11.1 Geographic Position and the Nicoya Peninsula Tectonic Setting

Costa Rica Underwater Demonstration Site Epsilon occupies a position on the outer continental shelf of the Nicoya Peninsula — the large, the rectangular, the northwest-trending, the 80-by-40-kilometer, the tectonically uplifted, the seismically active, the headland peninsula that projects, from the northwestern Costa Rican mainland, into the Pacific Ocean, separating the Golfo de Papagayo (to the north) from the Golfo de Nicoya (to the east and the southeast) — at precisely 9°52'N latitude and 85°35'W longitude, approximately 15 kilometers west-southwest of the Cabo Blanco Absolute Nature Reserve (the southern tip of the Nicoya Peninsula) and 25 kilometers south of the Santa Rosa National Park, at a depth of 40 meters below mean sea level, on the gently westward-dipping (approximately 1° to 2°), sediment-mantled, inner-to-middle continental shelf surface, within the Papagayo upwelling region — the seasonally intense, the wind-driven, the coastal-upwelling system that is generated, during the Northern Hemisphere winter and the spring (approximately December through April), by the strong, the persistent, the offshore-directed, the gap-wind jets — the "Papagayo winds" — that funnel through the low-elevation, the topographic gaps in the Central American cordillera (the Lake Nicaragua-Rio San Juan lowland corridor, in particular) and that drive the offshore Ekman transport, the coastal upwelling of the cold, the nutrient-rich, the subsurface water, and the resulting, the extraordinarily high, the marine biological productivity — the phytoplankton blooms, the zooplankton abundance, the fish, the sea-turtle, the whale, and the dolphin aggregations — that characterize the Papagayo upwelling ecosystem and that make the Nicoya Peninsula waters among the most productive, the most biologically dynamic, and the most ecologically significant marine environments in the tropical eastern Pacific. The selection of Site Epsilon as the fifth, and the northernmost, of the Costa Rican demonstration sites — located at the latitude of 9.9° N, approximately 150 kilometers north of the pivot-axis latitude of 8.5° N, and approaching, in its latitude and its crustal-displacement hazard exposure, the lower-latitude, the southernmost California sites (Site Epsilon, San Diego, at 32.7° N, is 23 degrees — 2,500 kilometers — to the north, and the crustal-displacement hazard increases, the nonlinearly, with the increasing latitude, but the 150-kilometer northward displacement from the pivot axis at Site Epsilon represents a modest — approximately 50 percent — increase in the predicted turbidite-flow loading relative to the Site Alpha pivot-axis conditions, and the Site Epsilon pod remains, by a wide margin, in the low-hazard, the high-survivability, the pivot-axis-proximate domain of the crustal-displacement risk spectrum) — was motivated by the strategic necessity of characterizing, quantifying, and validating the Atlantis pod performance at the northern, the higher-latitude, the higher-hazard extent of the Costa Rican deployment network, establishing the northern, the "outer-boundary," the "limits-of-the-refugium" data point that, in combination with the pivot-axis, the ultra-low-hazard Site Alpha data point and the intermediate, the transitional Site Delta (Quepos) data point, defines the latitudinal, the hazard-versus-survivability function for the Costa Rican, the pivot-axis-proximate domain, and that provides the essential, the calibration, the extrapolation, and the model-validation dataset for the global, the all-latitudes Atlantis deployment program.

## 11.2 Substrate Geology and the Nicoya Complex Ophiolitic Basement Foundation

## 11.3 The Papagayo Wind-Jet Oceanographic Regime and Its Implications for the Pod Operation

The oceanographic regime at Site Epsilon is dominated, during the December-through-April dry season, by the Papagayo wind jet — the intense, the persistent, the offshore-directed, the gap wind that accelerates, through the topographic constriction of the Lake Nicaragua-Rio San Juan lowland corridor, from the Caribbean to the Pacific side of the Central American isthmus, and that, at the sea surface, generates the wind speeds of 15 to 25 meters per second (30 to 50 knots), the sustained, the multi-day-to-the-multi-week durations, the significant-wave heights (H_s) of 3 to 5 meters, the strong, the offshore-directed, the Ekman-transport-driven surface currents (0.5 to 1.0 meters per second), and the intense, the ecologically critical, the coastal upwelling that brings the cold (16°C to 20°C), the nutrient-rich, the high-chlorophyll, the subsurface water to the surface, fueling the phytoplankton blooms and the resulting, the extraordinary, the marine biological productivity that supports the rich, the diverse, the ecologically and the economically important fisheries, the sea-turtle, the whale, the dolphin, and the seabird populations of the Papagayo region. For the Site Epsilon Atlantis pod, at the 40-meter depth, the Papagayo wind-jet effects are significantly attenuated relative to the surface: the wind-driven, the near-surface currents do not penetrate, at their full strength, to the 40-meter depth; the wave-induced, the near-bed orbital velocities, for the 3-to-5-meter, the 8-to-10-second-period, the wind-jet-generated waves, are approximately 0.2 to 0.5 meters per second; and the upwelled, the cold, the nutrient-rich water, while reaching the Site Epsilon depth, does so with the reduced, the moderated intensity relative to the surface euphotic zone, producing the bottom-water temperatures of 18°C to 22°C (during the upwelling season) and 24°C to 27°C (during the non-upwelling, the rainy season, May through November), and the dissolved-oxygen concentrations of 2 to 4 milliliters per liter — values that are, respectively, the cooler and the lower-oxygen than the other Costa Rican sites (excepting, perhaps, the deep, the anoxic Golfo Dulce basin, Site Beta), and that must be accommodated, in the pod's thermal-management and the life-support-system design, but that do not pose the significant, the design-driving, the mission-limiting challenges for the pod's 50-year operational life. The Site Epsilon pod, with its simple, the robust, the Nicoya-Complex-rock foundation, its moderate, the Papagayo-wind-jet-influenced, the upwelling-affected oceanographic environment, and its northern, the higher-latitude, the higher-hazard position within the Costa Rican deployment network, completes the Costa Rican, the five-site, the latitudinal-transect, the hazard-gradient validation of the Atlantis pod architecture across the full, the pivot-axis-to-the-northern-refugium-boundary range of the Costa Rican Pacific margin — the transect that, together with the analogous, the five-site, the California transect (Sites Alpha through Epsilon, from the 32.7° N San Diego to the 40.8° N Humboldt Bay), and the intermediate, the bridging, the comparative, the geological, the geotechnical, the oceanographic, and the hydrodynamic analyses presented in Parts 012 through 020, constitutes the comprehensive, the definitive, the multi-site, the multi-latitude, the multi-hazard, the empirical validation of the Atlantis subsea habitation architecture and the essential, the prerequisite foundation for the global, the all-oceans, the all-latitudes, the civilizational-scale expansion of the Aegis C distributed, redundant, survivable, ECD-protected, underwater-community network that is the ultimate, the overarching, the existence-justifying objective of the Carrington Storm Motors Safe Pod Engineering Division and the Atlantis program.
