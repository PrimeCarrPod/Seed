# PART 009: Costa Rica Underwater Demonstration Site Gamma — Isla del Caño Western Flank (8°42'N, 83°54'W): Submarine Volcanic Edifice Foundation, Deep-Water Coral Ecosystem Compatibility, and the Offshore-Island Breakwater Deployment Strategy for Pivot-Axis Community Protection

## 9.1 Geographical Coordinates, Volcanic Island Geology, and the Breakwater-Protected Deployment Rationale

Costa Rica Underwater Demonstration Site Gamma occupies a position on the western submarine flank of Isla del Caño — a small (320 hectares, approximately 3.2 square kilometers), roughly circular (approximately 2 kilometers in diameter), steep-sided (the island's slopes descending, at angles of 20° to 45°, from the island's summit plateau, at elevations of 80 to 100 meters above sea level, to the surrounding seabed, at depths of 30 to 50 meters within 500 meters of the shoreline, and to the depths exceeding 200 meters within 1 to 2 kilometers of the shore), heavily forested, biologically diverse, uninhabited (except for the occasional park-ranger and the researcher presence), nationally protected (the Isla del Caño Biological Reserve, established in 1978 and managed by the Costa Rican National System of Conservation Areas, SINAC), volcanic island — at precisely 8°42'N latitude and 83°54'W longitude, approximately 15 kilometers west of the Osa Peninsula's Drake Bay coastline and 5 kilometers west of Site Alpha (the Drake Bay outer-shelf deployment), at a depth of 45 meters below mean sea level, on the gently sloping (2° to 5°) , sediment-mantled, volcaniclastic-sand-and-gravel, submarine apron that fringes the western, the leeward, the sheltered flank of the island — a location that was selected, from among the five Costa Rican candidate sites, specifically to exploit the natural, the wave-attenuating, the turbidite-deflecting, the boulder-interdicting, the protective-breakwater function of Isla del Caño, which, as discussed in Part 06 of the Crustal Displacement Geological Analysis (CDGA) and in the CSMFAB077 Atlantis specification document, acts as a massive, the natural, the island-scale, the tsunami-and-the-storm-wave breakwater, intercepting the advancing wave fronts — whether from the normal, the non-catastrophic, the tropical-storm and the hurricane events, or from the catastrophic, the crustal-displacement-generated, the mega-tsunami — as they approach the Osa Peninsula coast from the west-southwest, the dominant, the wave-approach direction for both the normal and the catastrophic wave events in the Costa Rican Pacific margin, and creating, in the lee of the island — the "wave-shadow" zone, extending for the 10 to 30 kilometers to the northeast, the east-northeast, and the east, directly toward the Drake Bay and the Corcovado coastline — a region of the substantially reduced wave height (the wave-height reduction factor of 2 to 5, as determined from the numerical, the spectral, the wave-refraction-and-the-diffraction models and from the direct, the field, the wave-buoy and the ADCP measurements), the substantially reduced wave-energy flux, the substantially reduced near-bed orbital velocity, and the substantially reduced turbidite-flow velocity and the boulder-impact probability, making the western flank of Isla del Caño — the flank that faces away from the open Pacific, toward the sheltered, the island-lee, the wave-shadow zone — the ideal, the optimally protected, the minimum-wave-and-the-turbidite-exposure location for the Atlantis pod deployment along the entire Costa Rican Pacific margin, and, for the pods that are not directly in the pivot-axis, the minimum-displacement, the Drake Bay — the Site Alpha — location, the most survivable, the most robust, the most strategically secure Atlantis deployment site on the planet.

The submarine geology of the Site Gamma location, on the western, the leeward, the sheltered flank of Isla del Caño, is dominated by the extrusive, the volcanic, the basaltic-to-andesitic, the lava-flow, the volcaniclastic-breccia, the hyaloclastite, and the pillow-lava deposits that constitute the submarine portion of the Isla del Caño volcanic edifice — a late Miocene to the Pliocene (approximately 3 to 8 million years before present), the small, the isolated, the deeply eroded, the long-extinct, the volcanic center that is the westernmost, the most-seaward, the oceanic expression of the Central American Volcanic Arc, the chain of the Quaternary, the active, the subduction-related, the stratovolcanoes (the Turrialba, the Irazú, the Poás, the Arenal, the Rincón de la Vieja, the Miravalles, the Tenorio, the Orosí) that extends, for the 1,100 kilometers, from the Guatemala-Mexico border in the northwest to the Panama-Colombia border in the southeast, and that is the product of the north-northeastward subduction of the Cocos Plate beneath the Caribbean Plate at the Middle America Trench. The geotechnical properties of the Site Gamma submarine volcanic deposits — as determined from the geotechnical borehole CAN-01, drilled to the depth of 30 meters below the seabed, using the SeaMole-2 remotely operated seafloor drill rig — are characterized by the extreme heterogeneity, the high strength, the high stiffness, and the generally excellent foundation-engineering characteristics that are typical of the submarine volcanic rock masses: the intact, the unweathered, the low-porosity (2 to 10 percent), the high-strength (UCS of 50 to 200 MPa, the higher values for the dense, the non-vesicular, the microcrystalline basalt and the andesite; the lower values for the vesicular, the brecciated, the hyaloclastite, and the pillow-lava varieties), the high-modulus (E of 20 to 80 GPa), the crystalline, the igneous rock, which provides the excellent, the competent, the high-bearing-capacity, the low-settlement anchoring substrate; however, the volcanic rock mass is also pervasively jointed, the fractured, the faulted, and the hydrothermally altered (the alteration — the chloritization, the epidotization, the sericitization, the argillization, the silicification, the calcitization, the zeolitization — reduces the rock strength, increases the rock deformability, and creates the zones of the weak, the clay-rich, the low-friction, the low-cohesion, the gouge-filled, the potentially sliding-and-the-failing rock-mass discontinuities that must be carefully characterized, through the detailed, the oriented, the borehole-televiewer, the acoustic, and the optical, the fracture-logging, and the geotechnical, the rock-mass-rating (RMR), the geological-strength-index (GSI), and the Q-system classification, before the final, the optimized pile-embedment depth, the pile-spacing, and the pile-group configuration are determined, and that must be accommodated, in the foundation design, through the use of the appropriately conservative, the fracture-zone-adjusted, the rock-mass strength and the deformability parameters. The Site Gamma anchoring system — the eight grouted, the helical-pile rock anchors, each with the shaft diameter of 200 millimeters, the helix-plate diameter of 600 millimeters, the embedment depth of 10 meters into the competent, the relatively unweathered, the relatively unfractured, the high-strength, the basaltic-andesitic lava-flow or the intrusive rock (encountered, at the Site Gamma location, at the depths of 5 to 15 meters below the seabed, beneath the 3-to-8-meter-thick veneer of the unconsolidated, the volcaniclastic sand, the gravel, the cobble, and the boulder sediment), and the design working load of 200 kN per pile, providing the total, the pile-group anchoring capacity of 1,600 kN — represents the simplest, the most conventional, the most well-proven, the most easily installed and the most reliable of the Costa Rican anchoring-system designs, and the Site Gamma pod, with its natural, the island-breakwater wave-and-the-turbidite-flow protection, its competent, the volcanic-rock foundation, its moderate (45 meters) water depth, and its proximity (5 kilometers) to the Site Alpha pod for the inter-pod tunnel and the acoustic-mesh-network connectivity, is the Costa Rican counterpart to the Site Epsilon (San Diego) pod — the "easy," the "low-risk," the "baseline," the "reference-standard" deployment for the Costa Rican Atlantis network.
