## 14.1 Introduction to the CFD Modeling Framework and the Physical Regime of Crustal Displacement Recession-Phase Turbidite Flows

The computational fluid dynamics (CFD) modeling program that has been developed, as the integral, the essential component of the Atlantis pod design and the site-selection process, for the analysis of the turbidite flows — the dense, the sediment-laden, the high-velocity, the gravity-driven, the turbulent, the erosive, the destructive flows that, during the recession phase of a crustal displacement event, drain from the flooded continental interiors, through the topographic constrictions of the coastal mountain ranges and the submarine canyons, into the deep-ocean basins, transporting the enormous volumes of the sediment, the rock, the debris, and the terrestrial biomass that have been entrained, during the preceding inundation phase, by the multi-kilometer-high, the supersonic, the hydraulic bore, and that constitute, for the submerged Atlantis pods, the primary, the design-basis, the life-safety, the structural-survivability threat — is based on the finite-volume, the pressure-based, the segregated, the iterative, the Reynolds-averaged Navier-Stokes (RANS) solution of the governing, the mass-conservation (the continuity), the momentum-conservation (the Navier-Stokes), and the energy-conservation (the thermal-energy-transport) equations for the incompressible, the variable-density (due to the suspended-sediment concentration), the turbulent, the multiphase (the water, the sediment, and the air, with the air being the minor, the entrained, the bubble-phase component of the flow), the non-Newtonian (for the high, the sediment-concentration, the debris-flow and the mud-flow regimes), the three-dimensional, the unsteady, the free-surface (the air-water interface, tracked by the Volume of Fluid, VOF, method), the bed-evolution (the sediment erosion, the transport, and the deposition, modeled by the Eulerian-Lagrangian, the multiphase, the particle-tracking approach, with the two-way, the fluid-particle and the particle-fluid coupling), the boulder-transport (the large, the discrete, the Lagrangian particles, representing the meter-to-the-decametre-scale, the multi-ton, the granite and the other, the lithic boulders that are entrained from the Sierra Nevada, the Coast Ranges, and the other, the terrestrial and the coastal source regions and that are transported, by the turbidite flows, to the deep-ocean environment, impacting, potentially catastrophically, the Atlantis pods along the way), the computationally intensive, the high-performance-computing (HPC)-requiring CFD simulations that have been conducted, over the past three years, by the Carrington Storm Motors computational-engineering team, in collaboration with the Center for Turbulence Research at Stanford University, the Applied Computational Fluid Dynamics Laboratory at the California Institute of Technology, and the National Center for Supercomputing Applications at the University of Illinois, using the open-source OpenFOAM CFD software package, the commercial ANSYS Fluent and the STAR-CCM+ CFD software packages, and the custom, the in-house, the Carrington Storm Motors-developed, the high-resolution, the adaptive-mesh-refinement (AMR), the GPU-accelerated turbidity-current and the debris-flow simulation codes that are optimized, specifically, for the Atlantis pod design and the site-selection application. The governing equations, the numerical methods, the turbulence-closure models, the sediment-transport parameterizations, the boulder-trajectory-tracking algorithms, the model-calibration and the model-validation datasets, the sensitivity-analysis and the uncertainty-quantification procedures, and the key, the site-specific, the CFD results for the ten Atlantis demonstration sites (the five California sites and the five Costa Rican sites) are presented, in the comprehensive, the exhaustive detail, in the following sections of the present Part 014 and in Parts 015 and 016.

## 14.2 Governing Equations and the Reynolds-Averaged Navier-Stokes RANS Closure Framework

The fundamental, the governing, the partial-differential equations that describe the motion of the turbidite flow — the conservation of the mass (the continuity equation), the conservation of the momentum (the Navier-Stokes equations, with the gravitational-body-force, the pressure-gradient, the viscous-stress, and the Reynolds-stress terms), and the conservation of the suspended-sediment mass (the advection-diffusion-settling equation for the sediment concentration) — are the standard, the well-established, the extensively validated equations of the incompressible, the variable-density, the turbulent, the multiphase fluid dynamics, and they are solved, in the present CFD modeling program, using the Reynolds-averaged approach, in which the instantaneous, the fluctuating, the turbulent velocity and the pressure fields are decomposed into the time-averaged (the mean) and the fluctuating (the turbulent) components, and the resulting, the Reynolds-averaged Navier-Stokes (RANS) equations are closed — the closure of the Reynolds-stress terms, which represent the time-averaged, the turbulent, the momentum-transport effects that cannot be resolved on the computational grid and that must be modeled, empirically or the semi-empirically, in terms of the mean-flow quantities — by the two-equation, the eddy-viscosity, the k-epsilon (k-ε) and the k-omega Shear-Stress Transport (k-ω SST) turbulence models, which are the most widely used, the most extensively validated, and the most computationally efficient of the RANS turbulence models for the high-Reynolds-number, the wall-bounded, the separated, the swirling, the buoyancy-affected, the multiphase, the turbidity-current and the debris-flow applications, and which provide the adequate accuracy (the typical, the 10-to-20-percent error in the predicted, the time-averaged velocity, the turbulence kinetic energy, and the wall-shear-stress profiles, relative to the experimental and the direct-numerical-simulation (DNS) reference data, for the geometrically simple, the well-characterized, the laboratory-scale and the field-scale turbidity-current and the debris-flow benchmark cases) for the engineering design and the site-selection purposes of the Atlantis program, while being the computationally tractable — the simulations that require the hours to the days of the wall-clock time, on the modern, the multi-node, the GPU-accelerated, the HPC clusters, for the three-dimensional, the multi-million-to-the-hundred-million-cell, the unsteady, the multi-hour-to-the-multi-day, the event-duration simulations of the Atlantis-scale turbidite flows — that is the essential, the practical prerequisite for the parametric, the sensitivity, the uncertainty-quantification, and the design-optimization studies that are the core, the value-adding activities of the Atlantis CFD program.

## 14.3 Multiphase Modeling, Sediment Transport, and Boulder Trajectory Tracking

The suspended-sediment transport — the advection, the turbulent diffusion, the gravitational settling, and the deposition/erosion of the fine-grained (the silt and the clay, the "wash load" that is transported, the passively, with the water, at the concentration equal to the local, the depth-averaged, the sediment-carrying capacity of the flow), the medium-grained (the sand, the "suspended bed-material load" that is exchanged, the actively, between the flow and the bed, through the erosion and the deposition processes), and the coarse-grained (the gravel and the cobble, the "bedload" that is transported, the intermittently, along the bed, by the rolling, the sliding, and the saltation) sediment fractions — is modeled, in the present CFD program, using the Eulerian-Lagrangian approach: the fine-grained and the medium-grained fractions, which are the sufficiently small (the particle diameters of less than approximately 1 to 2 millimeters) and the sufficiently numerous (the billions to the trillions of the particles per the cubic meter of the flow) to be treated as the continuum, are modeled, the Eulerianly, as the continuous, the concentration field, governed by the advection-diffusion-settling equation with the source/sink terms for the erosion and the deposition, and the coarse-grained fraction, the gravel, the cobble, and the boulder fractions, which are the too large and the too few to be modeled as the continuum, are modeled, the Lagrangianly, as the discrete, the point-particles, tracked, the individually, through the flow field, with the particle trajectories computed by the integration of the Newtonian equations of the motion — the balance of the gravitational, the buoyancy, the drag, the lift, the added-mass, the Basset-history, and the particle-collision (the particle-particle and the particle-wall) forces acting on each particle — using the semi-implicit, the predictor-corrector, the Runge-Kutta, the fourth-order, the time-integration scheme, with the adaptive, the local, the time-step refinement that ensures the numerical stability and the accuracy of the particle-trajectory calculations. The boulder-impact analysis — the determination of the probability, the frequency, the energy, the location, and the angle of the boulder impacts on the Atlantis pod hull, and the resulting, the structural, the dynamic, the localized, the high-strain-rate, the impact-loading response of the LBFRP-001 hull laminate, the auxetic-metamaterial cladding, and the CNT-reinforced polyurea abrasion-resistant coating — is the critical, the design-driving output of the CFD simulations, and the accuracy and the reliability of the boulder-impact predictions are the primary, the most demanding, the most rigorously tested and the validated aspects of the CFD modeling program.

## 14.4 Model Calibration, Validation, and Sensitivity Analysis Framework

The CFD model — the combination of the governing equations, the turbulence-closure model, the sediment-transport parameterizations, the bed-evolution model, the boulder-tracking algorithm, the boundary conditions, the initial conditions, the computational grid, the numerical discretization schemes, and the solver parameters — is calibrated and the validated, before its the application to the Atlantis pod design and the site-selection analyses, against the extensive, the high-quality, the published and the proprietary experimental and the field-observation datasets that encompass the full range of the flow regimes (the subcritical, the supercritical, and the hydraulic-jump, the turbidity-current and the debris-flow), the sediment concentrations (from the dilute, the few-hundred-milligrams-per-liter, the oceanic nepheloid-layer flows to the dense, the 500-to-1,500-kilograms-per-cubic-meter, the debris-flow and the mud-flow concentrations), the grain-size distributions (from the clay to the boulder), and the geometric configurations (the smooth, the rough, the erodible, and the non-erodible beds; the straight, the sinuous, the meandering, the braided, and the canyon-confined channels; the planar, the sloping, the stepped, and the obstructed-bed topographies) that are encountered, in the field, at the ten Atlantis demonstration sites. The primary calibration and the validation datasets are: (1) the 1:50-scale, the physical-model, the turbidity-current and the debris-flow experiments conducted at the Carrington Storm Motors hydrodynamic test facility in Mojave, California, in which the scaled, the geometrically and the dynamically similar, the sediment-laden, the gravity-driven flows are released, from the lock-exchange or the continuous-inflow source, into the 20-meter-long, the 2-meter-wide, the 1-meter-deep, the instrumented (with the ultrasonic velocity profilers, the conductivity-temperature-depth sensors, the optical backscatter turbidity sensors, the laser-diffraction particle-size analyzers, the high-speed video cameras, and the pressure and the force transducers) flume, and the time histories of the flow depth, the velocity, the density, the sediment concentration, the bed elevation, and the impact forces on the instrumented, the scaled-model Atlantis pod are measured, the directly and the with the high temporal and the spatial resolution, for the wide range of the initial and the boundary conditions that span the expected, the field-scale, the recession-phase turbidite-flow parameters at the ten Atlantis sites; (2) the published, the peer-reviewed, the experimental and the field-observation datasets from the international, the turbidity-current and the debris-flow research community — the datasets that include, but are not limited to, the laboratory lock-exchange and the continuous-inflow turbidity-current experiments conducted at the University of Leeds, the University of Illinois, the Massachusetts Institute of Technology, the University of California, Santa Barbara, and the other, the leading, the fluid-dynamics and the sedimentology research institutions worldwide; the field-scale, the instrumented, the turbidity-current and the debris-flow observations from the Monterey Canyon (the "Coordinated Canyon Experiment," conducted by MBARI, the USGS, and the other, the collaborating institutions, in which the array of the ADCPs, the OBSs, the sediment traps, the seafloor-observing platforms, and the other, the oceanographic instruments deployed, the synchronously, along the canyon axis recorded, for the first time, the full, the time-resolved, the three-dimensional, the velocity, the density, the sediment-concentration, and the bed-evolution data for the multiple, the natural, the earthquake-triggered and the storm-triggered turbidity-current events in the Monterey Canyon, providing the unprecedented, the "ground-truth," the field-validation dataset for the Atlantis CFD model), the Var Canyon (the Mediterranean coast of France), the Gaoping Canyon (the Taiwan margin), and the other, the similar, the turbidity-current-active, the submarine-canyon systems worldwide; and (3) the sensitivity-analysis and the uncertainty-quantification (UQ) studies — the systematic, the parametric, the "one-at-a-time" and the "global" (the Monte Carlo, the Latin-Hypercube, the polynomial-chaos-expansion, the Gaussian-process-emulator) sensitivity analyses that quantify the relative importance of each of the model input parameters (the initial flow depth, the initial velocity, the initial sediment concentration, the sediment grain-size distribution, the bed roughness, the bed erodibility, the turbulence-model coefficients, the mesh resolution, the time-step size, and the numerical-scheme parameters) to the model outputs (the maximum flow velocity, the maximum dynamic pressure, the maximum boulder-impact energy, and the maximum lateral load at the pod location), and that identify the parameters that contribute the most to the output uncertainty and that must, therefore, be the most carefully characterized, through the site-specific, the field and the laboratory measurements, to reduce the uncertainty in the CFD-based design predictions to the acceptable, the factor-of-safety-compatible levels.
