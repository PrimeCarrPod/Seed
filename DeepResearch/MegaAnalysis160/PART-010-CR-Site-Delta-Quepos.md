# PART 010: Costa Rica Underwater Demonstration Site Delta — Quepos-Manuel Antonio Outer Shelf (9°22'N, 84°11'W): Central Pacific Costa Rican Margin, Submarine Canyon Head Sediment Dynamics, and Mid-Latitude Costa Rican Deployment Validation for the Expanded Atlantis Network

## 10.1 Geographic Position, Regional Geological Framework, and the Central Costa Rican Margin Deployment Rationale

Costa Rica Underwater Demonstration Site Delta occupies a position on the outer continental shelf, at precisely 9°22'N latitude and 84°11'W longitude, approximately 8 kilometers southwest of the Manuel Antonio National Park and 15 kilometers south-southwest of the port city of Quepos, Puntarenas Province, at a depth of 55 meters below mean sea level, within the Quepos-Manuel Antonio submarine canyon system — the deeply incised, the dendritic, the meandering, the turbidity-current-conduit network that heads, at the shoreline near the mouth of the Rio Naranjo and the Rio Savegre, the two major, the Pacific-draining rivers of the central Costa Rican coast, and that extends, as the continuous, the sinuous, the levee-bounded, the deeply entrenched, the canyon-channel system, for approximately 60 to 80 kilometers across the narrow continental shelf and the steep continental slope, to the abyssal depths of the Middle America Trench. The selection of Site Delta as the fourth Costa Rican demonstration site was motivated by the strategic necessity of extending the Atlantis deployment network northward, away from the pivot-axis, the Osa Peninsula core region (Sites Alpha, Beta, and Gamma), and into the central Costa Rican Pacific margin — the region that, at the latitude of 9.4° N (approximately 1 degree, or 110 kilometers, north of the pivot-axis latitude of 8.5° N), is predicted, by the crustal displacement hydrodynamic models, to experience the moderately increased — the intermediate, the transitional — inundation severity (the maximum inundation elevation estimated at 200 to 300 meters, compared with the 170 meters at the Osa Peninsula pivot axis), and the correspondingly increased turbidite-flow velocity, the dynamic pressure, and the boulder-impact energy at the pod depth, providing the intermediate-risk, the intermediate-energy, the transitional validation environment that bridges the gap between the ultra-low-risk, the pivot-axis, the Osa Peninsula deployments (Sites Alpha, Beta, and Gamma) and the high-risk, the high-energy, the California deployments (Sites Alpha through Epsilon), and that enables the Atlantis program to characterize, to quantify, and to model the pod survivability as the continuous, the smoothly varying function of the latitude, the distance from the pivot axis, and the predicted, the site-specific, the crustal-displacement hydrodynamic loading parameters — the essential, the design-basis information that is required for the optimal, the risk-informed, the cost-effective siting of the future, the larger, the higher-capacity, the global Atlantis communities across the full, the continuous, the latitudinal gradient of the crustal displacement hazard.

## 10.2 Substrate Geology of the Quepos-Manuel Antonio Outer Shelf

The seabed substrate at Site Delta, at 55 meters depth, consists of a complex, the multi-layered, the variably indurated, the tectonically deformed, the accretionary-prism, the sedimentary-rock sequence that is characteristic of the central Costa Rican forearc — the region between the Middle America Trench (the oceanic trench, at the depth of 4,000 to 5,000 meters, approximately 50 to 80 kilometers offshore of the central Costa Rican coast, where the Cocos Plate subducts beneath the Caribbean Plate) and the active volcanic arc (the Cordillera Central, with its the Quaternary, the active stratovolcanoes — the Turrialba, the Irazú, the Poás, the Barva — located 80 to 120 kilometers inland from the coast), and that has been, over the past several million years, subjected to the intense, the ongoing, the subduction-driven, the compressional-and-the-shear deformation — the folding, the faulting, the fracturing, the tectonic-mélange formation, and the progressive, the offscraping, the underplating, the accretion, and the uplift of the oceanic-plate sediments and the igneous rocks onto the overriding Caribbean Plate — that has produced the structurally complex, the geotechnically heterogeneous, the foundation-engineering-challenging geological environment that the Site Delta pod must be anchored into. The substrate at the Site Delta depth consists of the following, the downward-sequence of the lithological and the geotechnical units: (1) a thin (0.5 to 2 meters), the Holocene-age, the bioturbated, the foraminiferal, the silty-clay hemipelagic sediment drape; (2) the Quepos Formation — a late Miocene to the Pliocene (approximately 3 to 8 million years before present), the moderately indurated, the deeply weathered, the fractured, the faulted, the calcareous, the siliceous, the volcaniclastic, the turbiditic sandstone, the siltstone, and the mudstone sequence, with the interbedded, the thin, the normally graded, the volcanic-ash and the tuff layers, representing the forearc-basin, the turbidite-fan, the submarine-slope-apron depositional environment that accumulated, during the late Miocene and the Pliocene, the erosional detritus from the emerging, the uplifting, the volcanic Central American isthmus; and (3) the Nicoya Complex — the Late Jurassic to the Late Cretaceous (approximately 70 to 160 million years before present), the oceanic-plateau, the ophiolitic, the basaltic, the gabbroic, the peridotitic, the serpentinized, the igneous-and-metamorphic, the accretionary-prism basement rock, representing the fragments of the Farallon Plate (and its the successor, the Cocos Plate) oceanic crust and the upper mantle that have been accreted, offscraped, and underplated onto the Caribbean Plate during the long, the multi-phase, the subduction-accretion history of the Costa Rican margin. The geotechnical properties of the Quepos Formation at Site Delta — the UCS of 5 to 20 MPa, the φ' of 25° to 33°, the c' of 100 to 400 kPa — are intermediate between the competent, the well-indurated, the moderately strong Purisima Formation of Site Alpha (Monterey) and the soft, the weak, the intensely sheared Franciscan mélange matrix of Site Beta (Farallones), and the Site Delta anchoring system — the 10 grouted, the helical-pile rock anchors, each with the shaft diameter of 200 millimeters, the helix-plate diameter of 550 millimeters, the embedment depth of 15 meters into the competent, the relatively unweathered, the relatively unfractured, the Nicoya Complex basement rock (encountered at the depths of 15 to 25 meters below the seabed, beneath the Quepos Formation and the Holocene sediment veneer), and the design working load of 220 kN per pile, providing the total pile-group anchoring capacity of 2,200 kN — represents the robust, the factor-of-14 to factor-of-22 safety margin over the predicted turbidite-flow lateral load (100 to 160 kN, as computed by the CFD simulations described in Part 016) that is the design standard for the Atlantis pod anchoring systems across all of the demonstration sites.
