## 12.2 The Tectonic-Structural Contrast: San Andreas Transform Boundary versus the Cocos-Caribbean Subduction Zone

Beyond the fundamental, the displacement-distance-driven asymmetry in the mega-tsunami hydrodynamic loading, the California and the Costa Rican continental margins differ, profoundly, in their the tectonic-structural setting, their the crustal-deformation style, their the seismicity, their the volcanism, their the geothermal regime, their the sedimentation patterns, their the geomorphological evolution, and their the geological-hazard profile — differences that, while not the primary drivers of the catastrophic-intensity divergence, significantly modulate the local, the site-specific expression of the crustal-displacement signatures and that must be understood, in detail, to interpret correctly the geological records of the two regions and to design, appropriately, the Atlantis pod anchoring systems, the seismic-resistance measures, and the other, the site-specific, the engineering adaptations that are described in Parts 002 through 011. The California continental margin, at the latitudes of the five California Atlantis demonstration sites (32° N to 41° N), is dominated by the San Andreas Fault system — the 1,200-kilometer-long, the predominantly right-lateral, the strike-slip, the transform plate boundary between the Pacific Plate and the North American Plate — and by the associated, the subsidiary, the parallel and the oblique, the strike-slip, the reverse, the thrust, and the normal faults (the San Gregorio, the Hosgri, the Calaveras, the Hayward, the Rodgers Creek, the Maacama, the Bartlett Springs, the Mendocino, the Gorda, and the numerous, the other, the lesser faults) that accommodate the approximately 35 to 50 millimeters per year of the relative plate motion and that generate the frequent, the moderate-to-large (the magnitude 5.5 to 7.9), the shallow (the 5-to-20-kilometer depth), the crustal earthquakes that are the dominant, the design-basis seismic hazard for the California Atlantis pods (the Cascadia Subduction Zone megathrust, at Site Delta, Humboldt Bay, being the exception, as discussed in Part 005). The Costa Rican Pacific margin, at the latitudes of the five Costa Rican Atlantis demonstration sites (8° N to 10° N), is dominated by the Middle America Trench — the convergent, the subduction plate boundary between the Cocos Plate and the Caribbean Plate, where the Cocos Plate descends, at the rate of 70 to 90 millimeters per year, beneath the Caribbean Plate, generating the deep (20 to 60 kilometers), the megathrust, the subduction-zone earthquakes (the magnitude 7.0 to 8.0, with the potential, as yet unrealized in the historical record but suggested by the paleoseismological evidence, for the magnitude 8.5+ events), the active, the Quaternary volcanic arc (the Cordillera Central and the Cordillera de Guanacaste), the rapid, the tectonically driven coastal uplift (1 to 5 millimeters per year at the Nicoya and the Osa Peninsulas), the steep, the narrow, the practically shelf-less continental slope, and the accretionary-prism, the tectonic-mélange, the ophiolitic-basement geology that characterize the Costa Rican forearc region. These tectonic-structural differences have the direct, the practical implications for the Atlantis pod design: the California pods, with the exception of the Site Delta pod at Humboldt Bay, must be designed to withstand the shallow, the high-frequency, the short-duration, the strike-slip earthquake ground motions, while the Costa Rican pods must be designed to withstand the deeper, the lower-frequency, the longer-duration, the subduction-zone megathrust earthquake ground motions; the California pods are deployed on the predominantly the strike-slip, the transform-fault, the structurally segmented, the basin-and-range, the Franciscan-mélange, the terrigenous-sediment-dominated continental margin, while the Costa Rican pods are deployed on the predominantly the convergent, the subduction-fault, the structurally continuous, the accretionary-prism, the ophiolitic-basement, the carbonate-platform-dominated continental margin; and the California pods, at the higher latitudes, are subjected to the substantially greater, the more energetic, the more destructive, the crustal-displacement mega-tsunami and the turbidite-flow loading than the Costa Rican pods, requiring the more robust, the higher-capacity, the deeper-embedment, the more conservative anchoring-system designs, as described, in detail, in Parts 002 through 006 (California) and Parts 007 through 011 (Costa Rica).

## 12.3 The Sedimentological Contrast: Terrigenous-Clastic Turbidite Dominance (California) versus Biogenic-Carbonate-Pelagic Dominance (Costa Rica)

The sedimentological contrast between the California and the Costa Rican continental margins — the contrast that is, in many respects, as fundamental, as consequential, and as geotechnically significant as the tectonic-structural contrast, and that is the direct, the climatic-and-the oceanographic consequence of the latitudinal difference between the two regions — further amplifies the geological divergence between the two deployment domains and imposes the distinctive, the region-specific, the foundation-engineering, the biofouling-control, and the sediment-management requirements on the Atlantis pods. The California continental margin, at the latitudes of 32° N to 41° N, is characterized by the temperate, the Mediterranean-to-the-maritime, the winter-wet, the summer-dry climate; the high, the seasonal and the episodic, the fluvial sediment discharge from the steep, the rapidly eroding, the tectonically active, the Coast Ranges and the Sierra Nevada watersheds; the broad, the gently sloping, the sediment-mantled, the terrigenous-clastic-dominated continental shelf; the high, the wave-energy, the storm-and-the-swell-dominated, the sediment-resuspending and the sediment-transporting coastal and the shelf hydrodynamic regime; and the predominance, in the shelf and the slope sedimentary sequences, of the terrigenous, the siliciclastic, the feldspathic-lithic, the arkosic-to-the-graywacke, the sandstone, the siltstone, and the mudstone deposits — the "active-margin," the "tectonically influenced," the "high-sediment-supply," the "sediment-accommodation-dominated" sedimentary system that is the typical, the global, the Pacific-Rim, the convergent-and-the-transform plate-boundary continental-margin environment. The Costa Rican Pacific margin, at the latitudes of 8° N to 10° N, is characterized by the tropical, the humid, the year-round-warm, the high-rainfall (2,000 to 6,000 millimeters per year), the rainforest-vegetated, the deeply weathered, the Oxisol-and-the-Ultisol-mantled, the low-relief, the slowly eroding, the low-sediment-yield terrestrial environment; the narrow, the steep, the sediment-starved, the biogenic-carbonate-dominated, the coral-reef-fringed continental shelf; the low, the wave-energy, the tropical, the trade-wind-and-the-hurricane-dominated, the episodically energetic, the coastal and the shelf hydrodynamic regime; and the predominance, in the shelf and the slope sedimentary sequences, of the biogenic, the carbonate, the foraminiferal, the coccolithophorid, the pteropod, the coralgal, the reefal, the pelagic, the hemipelagic, the calcareous-ooze, and the chalk deposits — the "passive-margin," the "tropically influenced," the "low-sediment-supply," the "carbonate-factory-dominated" sedimentary system that is the typical, the global, the Caribbean, the Indo-Pacific, the low-latitude, the warm-water, the oligotrophic, the carbonate-platform and the coral-reef continental-margin environment. The geotechnical, the foundation-engineering, the operational, and the maintenance implications of this sedimentological contrast are profound and are addressed, in the site-specific detail, in Parts 002 through 011; here, it suffices to note that the California pods, deployed on the predominantly the terrigenous-clastic, the siliciclastic substrates, require the anchoring systems that are designed to resist the higher, the turbidite-flow, the seismic, and the storm-wave lateral loads, the higher, the sedimentation and the burial rates, and the higher, the suspended-sediment abrasion and the erosion, while the Costa Rican pods, deployed on the predominantly the biogenic-carbonate, the pelagic substrates, require the anchoring systems that are designed to cope with the extreme, the vertical and the lateral heterogeneity, the unpredictable, the karstified, the cavernous, the variable-strength, the variable-stiffness, the carbonate-platform foundation conditions, and with the lower, the turbidite-flow, the seismic, and the storm-wave lateral loads, the lower sedimentation and the burial rates, and the lower suspended-sediment abrasion and the erosion, but with the greater, the chemical-corrosion, the bioerosion, and the carbonate-dissolution risks that are inherent in the warm, the CO₂-rich, the slightly acidic, the tropical, the shallow-marine, the carbonate-saturated-to-the-undersaturated seawater environments.
