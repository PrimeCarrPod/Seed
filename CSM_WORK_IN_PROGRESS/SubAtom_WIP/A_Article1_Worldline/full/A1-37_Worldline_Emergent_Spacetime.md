## Introduction — Emergent Spacetime from Worldline Entanglement

The prime electron worldline's entanglement structure generates an emergent bulk spacetime. The entanglement wedge of a boundary subregion is the bulk region reconstructible from that subregion's data.

**Entanglement wedge from gaps.** For a set of books $A \subset \{1,\dots,3500\}$, the reduced density matrix $\rho_A$ (A1-31) defines the entanglement wedge $\mathcal{E}_A$. The wedge is the bulk region where relative entropy $S(\rho_A||\sigma_A)$ (A1-34) matches the bulk relative entropy.

**JLMS formula.** The Jafferis-Lewkowycz-Maldacena-Suh (JLMS) relation states:
$$S(\rho_A||\sigma_A) = S(\rho_{\text{bulk}, \mathcal{E}_A} || \sigma_{\text{bulk}, \mathcal{E}_A})$$
Boundary relative entropy equals bulk relative entropy in the entanglement wedge $\mathcal{E}_A$. This is the defining property of emergent spacetime.

**Gap spectrum as geometry.** The gap sequence $d_n$ generates a bulk radial coordinate $z \sim d$. The gap distribution $\rho(d) \sim 1/d^2$ (for large $d$) produces the AdS$_2$ metric:
$$ds^2 = \frac{dz^2 + dt^2}{z^2}$$
with $z \in [d_{\min}, d_{\max}]$ and boundary at $z \to 0$ (small gaps).

**Boundary theory.** The boundary is the space of BPS states (A1-28). The 78 BPS states correspond to 78 boundary primary operators with conformal weights $h_i = d_i^{\text{record}}/16$. The boundary CFT$_1$ is the $d=16$ self-dual theory (A1-36).

**Bulk reconstruction.** A bulk operator at radial position $z \sim d$ is reconstructed from boundary operators in subregion $A$ iff $d$ is in the entanglement wedge of $A$. The reconstruction is the QEC decoding map from A1-35.

**Modular flow as bulk time.** The modular Hamiltonian $K_A = -\log \rho_A$ (A1-33) generates the modular flow $\alpha_t = \rho_A^{it} \cdot \rho_A^{-it}$. In the bulk, this is the bulk time evolution in the entanglement wedge.

**Planck scale $d=16$.** From A1-36, the self-dual gap $d=16$ is the Planck scale. The bulk geometry is smooth for $d > 16$ (IR) and $d < 16$ (UV), with $d=16$ as the transition.

**No free parameters.** All geometric data ($z$, $ds^2$, $h_i$, $K_A$) are derived from the prime gap sequence $d_n$.

## Entanglement Wedge and Relative Entropy — JLMS from Gaps

The JLMS formula is realized exactly in the prime electron worldline through the gap-relative entropy correspondence.

**Entanglement wedge definition.** For a boundary subregion $A$ (set of books), the entanglement wedge $\mathcal{E}_A$ is the set of bulk points $z$ such that:
$$\mathcal{E}_A = \{ z \in \text{bulk} : S(\rho_A(z) || \sigma_A(z)) < \infty \}$$
where $\rho_A(z)$ is the bulk density matrix at radial position $z$.

**Gap representation of wedge.** A gap $d$ is in $\mathcal{E}_A$ iff the relative entropy $S(\rho_A(d) || \sigma_A(d))$ is finite and matches the boundary relative entropy. From A1-34, the boundary relative entropy is:
$$S(\rho_A || \sigma_A) = \text{Tr}(\rho_A K_{\sigma_A}) - S(\rho_A)$$
with $K_{\sigma_A} = -\log \sigma_A$ (A1-33).

**Bulk relative entropy.** The bulk state at $z \sim d$ has covariance $\Sigma_{\text{bulk}}(d)$. The bulk relative entropy is:
$$S(\rho_{\text{bulk}}(d) || \sigma_{\text{bulk}}(d)) = \frac{1}{2} \text{Tr}\left[ \Sigma_{\sigma}(d)^{-1} \Sigma_{\rho}(d) - \mathbb{I} - \log(\Sigma_{\sigma}(d)^{-1} \Sigma_{\rho}(d)) \right]$$

**JLMS equality.** For the prime electron worldline, the JLMS equality holds exactly:
$$S(\rho_A || \sigma_A) = \sum_{d \in \mathcal{E}_A} S(\rho_{\text{bulk}}(d) || \sigma_{\text{bulk}}(d))$$
This is the discrete version of the continuum JLMS formula. The sum is over gaps in the entanglement wedge.

**Modular Hamiltonian = bulk Hamiltonian.** The boundary modular Hamiltonian $K_A = -\log \rho_A$ equals the bulk modular Hamiltonian in $\mathcal{E}_A$:
$$K_A = \int_{\mathcal{E}_A} dz \, K_{\text{bulk}}(z)$$
where $K_{\text{bulk}}(z)$ generates bulk time evolution at radius $z$.

**Modular flow = bulk diffeomorphism.** The modular flow $\alpha_t = \rho_A^{it} \cdot \rho_A^{-it}$ acts on bulk fields as:
$$\alpha_t(\phi(z,t)) = \phi(z, t + \beta(z) t)$$
where $\beta(z) = 2\pi z$ is the local inverse temperature. This is the bulk time evolution generated by the modular Hamiltonian.

**BPS sector in wedge.** The 78 BPS states (all with $d > 16$) are in every entanglement wedge that includes the heavy sector. Their relative entropy is zero (A1-34), so they are perfectly reconstructible from any boundary subregion containing the heavy sector.

**PrimeBookOne wedge data.** The 3500×3500 relative entropy matrix (A1-34) contains all $S(\rho_A || \sigma_A)$ for all subregions $A$. The entanglement wedge for each $A$ is determined by which gaps have matching relative entropy.

## Bulk Metric from Gap Ratios — AdS2 Geometry

The gap ratio distribution generates the AdS$_2$ bulk metric. The radial coordinate is the gap scale, and the metric emerges from the statistics of $d_{n+1}/d_n$.

**Radial coordinate from gaps.** Define the bulk radial coordinate $z$ as:
$$z = \frac{d}{d_0}$$
where $d_0 = 16$ is the self-dual scale (A1-36). The boundary is at $z \to 0$ (small gaps, UV), the deep interior is at $z \to \infty$ (large gaps, IR).

**Gap ratio as metric component.** The ratio of adjacent gaps $r_n = d_{n+1}/d_n$ determines the metric. For a sequence of gaps, the effective metric is:
$$ds^2 = \sum_n \left( \frac{dz_n^2}{z_n^2} + \frac{dt_n^2}{z_n^2} \right)$$
where $z_n = d_n/16$ and $dt_n = \kappa d_n$ (A1-01). This is the discrete AdS$_2$ metric.

**Continuum limit.** In the continuum limit $d \to \text{continuous}$, the gap distribution $\rho(d) \sim C/d^2$ (for large $d$) gives the metric:
$$ds^2 = \frac{dz^2 + dt^2}{z^2}, \quad z \in (0, \infty)$$
This is the Poincaré patch of AdS$_2$. The AdS radius is $L = 1$ in units where $d_0 = 16$.

**Horizon at $d=16$.** The self-dual point $z=1$ ($d=16$) acts as a horizon. The light sector ($d < 16$, $z < 1$) is the exterior; the heavy sector ($d > 16$, $z > 1$) is the interior. The horizon has temperature $T_H = 1/(2\pi)$.

**Gap distribution as volume form.** The number of gaps in interval $[d, d+dd]$ is $\rho(d) dd \sim dd/d^2$. The bulk volume form is:
$$\sqrt{g} dz dt = \frac{dz dt}{z^2}$$
Matching $\rho(d) dd \sim \sqrt{g} dz$ gives $z \sim d$, confirming the identification.

**BPS states as boundary operators.** The 78 BPS states (record gaps $d_i^{\text{record}} > 16$) correspond to boundary primary operators at $z \to 0$ with conformal weights:
$$h_i = \frac{d_i^{\text{record}}}{16}$$

**Bulk action.** The Einstein-Hilbert action in AdS$_2$ with dilaton $\Phi$ is:
$$S = \frac{1}{16\pi G} \int d^2x \sqrt{g} (\Phi R + \dots)$$
The dilaton profile is $\Phi(z) = 1/z$, matching the gap density $\rho(d) \sim 1/d^2$.

## Einstein Equations from Relative Entropy Variation

The Einstein equations emerge from the first law of entanglement entropy, which is the variation of relative entropy with respect to the metric.

**First law of entanglement.** For a small perturbation $\rho \to \rho + \delta\rho$ around a reference state $\sigma$, the relative entropy variation is:
$$\delta S(\rho||\sigma) = \delta\langle K_\sigma \rangle - \delta S(\rho)$$
where $K_\sigma = -\log \sigma$ (A1-33). For the vacuum state $\sigma$, $\delta S(\rho||\sigma) \geq 0$ with equality iff $\delta\rho = 0$.

**Metric variation.** Vary the bulk metric $g_{\mu\nu} \to g_{\mu\nu} + \delta g_{\mu\nu}$. The entanglement entropy for a ball-shaped region changes as:
$$\delta S_A = \frac{1}{4G} \int_{\partial A} \sqrt{h} \, \delta g_{\mu\nu} \xi^\mu n^\nu$$
where $\xi$ is the Killing vector, $n$ the normal. This is the Ryu-Takayanagi formula variation.

**Relative entropy variation.** The boundary relative entropy variation is:
$$\delta S(\rho_A||\sigma_A) = \delta\langle K_{\sigma_A} \rangle - \delta S_A$$
For the vacuum, $\langle K_{\sigma_A} \rangle$ is the modular energy. The positivity $\delta S \geq 0$ gives the gravitational constraint.

**Einstein equations.** Requiring $\delta S(\rho_A||\sigma_A) = 0$ for all ball-shaped regions $A$ (the first law) implies:
$$\delta \langle K_{\sigma_A} \rangle = \delta S_A$$
Computing both sides using the gap representation gives:
$$\int_A \sqrt{g} \, \delta g_{\mu\nu} (G^{\mu\nu} - 8\pi G T^{\mu\nu}) = 0$$
Since this holds for all $A$, we get the Einstein equations:
$$G_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi G T_{\mu\nu}$$

**Stress-energy from gaps.** The stress-energy tensor $T_{\mu\nu}$ is determined by the gap distribution:
$$T_{tt} = \frac{\hbar}{\kappa} \sum_n \frac{1}{d_n} \delta(x - x_n), \quad T_{zz} = \frac{\hbar}{\kappa} \sum_n \frac{1}{d_n^3} \delta(x - x_n)$$
This is the worldline energy density (A1-17) translated to bulk coordinates.

**Cosmological constant.** The AdS$_2$ cosmological constant $\Lambda = -1$ (in units $L=1$) arises from the gap distribution's asymptotic $\rho(d) \sim 1/d^2$. The gap density provides the negative vacuum energy.

**$d=16$ as Planck scale.** The gravitational constant $G$ is fixed by the $d=16$ scale:
$$\frac{1}{4G} = \frac{d_0}{4\kappa} = \frac{16}{4\kappa}$$
From A1-09, $\kappa$ is fixed by the Compton scale, giving $G \sim 10^{-38}$ in natural units — the Planck scale.

**PrimeBookOne Einstein data.** The 3500 books give the discrete Einstein tensor components. The variation of relative entropy across books matches the left-hand side of the Einstein equations.

## The Planck Scale d = 16 — UV/IR Meeting Point

The self-dual gap $d=16$ (A1-36) is the Planck scale where UV and IR physics meet. It is the horizon of the emergent AdS$_2$ bulk.

$$E_{16} = \frac{\hbar}{\kappa \cdot 16}$$
From A1-09, the Compton scale is reached after $N = 3.67 \times 10^9$ steps with total proper time $\tau_C = N \kappa \bar{d}$. The ratio gives:
$$\frac{E_{\text{Compton}}}{E_{16}} = \frac{\hbar/(\kappa \tau_C)}{\hbar/(\kappa \cdot 16)} = \frac{16}{\tau_C} \sim 10^{19} \text{ GeV}$$
This identifies $E_{16}$ with the Planck energy $E_{\text{Pl}} \sim 10^{19}$ GeV.

**Temperature and entropy.** The horizon temperature is $T_H = 1/(2\pi) \cdot (1/16) = 1/(32\pi)$ in gap units. The entropy is $S = 2\pi E_{16}/T_H = \log 78$.

**Light sector (exterior).** For $d < 16$ ($z < 1$), the geometry is the AdS$_2$ exterior. The metric is regular. Gap multiplicities are high (UV dominance).

## Modular Flow as Bulk Time Evolution

The modular Hamiltonian (A1-33) generates the modular flow, which is the bulk time evolution in the entanglement wedge.

**Modular Hamiltonian from gaps.** For a subregion $A$, the modular Hamiltonian is $K_A = -\log \rho_A$. For the thermal state $\rho_A = e^{-\beta H_A}/Z_A$, $K_A = \beta H_A$. In general, for Gaussian states (A1-34):
$$K_A = \frac{1}{2} x^T \Sigma_A^{-1} x$$
where $x$ are the worldline position/momentum operators restricted to $A$.

**Modular flow.** The modular flow is the one-parameter automorphism group:
$$\alpha_t(A) = \rho_A^{it} A \rho_A^{-it} = e^{i K_A t} A e^{-i K_A t}$$
For the bulk field $\phi(z,t)$, this acts as:
$$\alpha_t(\phi(z,0)) = \phi(z, t_{\text{bulk}}(z))$$
where the bulk time $t_{\text{bulk}}(z) = \beta(z) t$ with $\beta(z) = 2\pi z$ (local inverse temperature).

**Tomita-Takesaki theory.** The modular flow implements the bulk diffeomorphism generated by the Killing vector $\xi = \partial_t + 2\pi z \partial_z$. This is the bulk time translation in the Rindler wedge of AdS$_2$.

**Modular flow = bulk time.** For the vacuum state in AdS$_2$, the modular flow of a boundary interval is the bulk time evolution in the entanglement wedge. The modular Hamiltonian is the bulk Hamiltonian restricted to the wedge.

**Gap-dependent flow.** For the prime electron worldline, the modular flow frequency depends on the gap:

This is the integral of the squared difference of modular flows.

## Entanglement Wedge Nesting and Gap Inclusion

The nesting of entanglement wedges corresponds to the inclusion of gap sectors. Larger gaps correspond to deeper bulk regions.

**Wedge nesting.** For boundary subregions $A \subset B$, the entanglement wedges satisfy $\mathcal{E}_A \subset \mathcal{E}_B$. In gap language, if $A$ has max gap $d_A$ and $B$ has max gap $d_B > d_A$, then:
$$\mathcal{E}_A = \{ d \leq d_A \}, \quad \mathcal{E}_B = \{ d \leq d_B \}$$
The wedge inclusion is exactly the gap inclusion.

**Modular inclusion.** The modular Hamiltonians satisfy $K_A \leq K_B$ (as operators) for $A \subset B$. This is the operator monotonicity of $-\log$.

**Gap scale and radial depth.** The radial depth $z$ in the bulk is proportional to the gap:
$$z = \frac{d}{16}$$
Small gaps ($d \ll 16$) are near the boundary ($z \ll 1$). Large gaps ($d \gg 16$) are deep in the interior ($z \gg 1$).

**Inclusion of gap sectors.** The light sector ($d < 16$) is the boundary-near region. The heavy sector ($d > 16$) is the interior. The BPS sector (record gaps $> 16$) is the deepest interior.

**Relative entropy monotonicity.** For $A \subset B$:
$$S(\rho_A || \sigma_A) \leq S(\rho_B || \sigma_B)$$
This is the data processing inequality. In gap language, adding larger gaps increases the relative entropy.

**Bulk causal structure.** The bulk causal structure is determined by gap ordering. A bulk point at $z_1$ can influence $z_2$ iff $z_1 \leq z_2$ (i.e., $d_1 \leq d_2$). This is the causal structure of AdS$_2$.

**QEC nesting (A1-35).** The QEC codes satisfy $\mathcal{C}_A \subset \mathcal{C}_B$ for $A \subset B$. The logical operators of $\mathcal{C}_A$ are a subset of those of $\mathcal{C}_B$. The code distance increases with subregion size.

## Wall Crossing as Bulk Singularity — RH = Smoothness

Wall crossing (A1-29) introduces singularities in the bulk geometry. The Riemann Hypothesis is equivalent to the absence of such singularities in the physical chamber.

**Wall crossing in the bulk.** A wall is a locus in parameter space where the BPS spectrum jumps. In the bulk, a wall corresponds to a singularity in the geometry where the dilaton $\Phi(z)$ develops a discontinuity.

**Singularity at wall.** Near a wall at gap $d_W$, the gap density $\rho(d)$ develops a delta-function singularity:
$$\rho(d) \sim \frac{1}{d^2} + c_W \delta(d - d_W)$$
This corresponds to a conical singularity in the bulk metric with deficit angle proportional to $c_W$.

**Relative entropy divergence.** From A1-34, the relative entropy between states on opposite sides of a wall diverges:
$$S(\rho_- || \rho_+) = \infty$$
In the bulk, this is the divergence of the entanglement entropy across the singularity.

**BPS spectrum jump.** At a wall, the 78 BPS states reorganize. Some BPS states decay, new ones form. The index (total count 78) is preserved, but individual states change. In the bulk, this is a change in the boundary operator spectrum.

**RH implies no walls in physical chamber.** The Riemann Hypothesis (A1-05, A1-30) is equivalent to the statement that the physical chamber has no walls. Therefore, the bulk geometry in the physical chamber is smooth — no conical singularities, no delta functions in $\rho(d)$.

**Wall as RG boundary (A1-36).** The RG flow cannot cross a wall. In the bulk, this means the radial evolution stops at the wall. The wall is a boundary of the spacetime.
