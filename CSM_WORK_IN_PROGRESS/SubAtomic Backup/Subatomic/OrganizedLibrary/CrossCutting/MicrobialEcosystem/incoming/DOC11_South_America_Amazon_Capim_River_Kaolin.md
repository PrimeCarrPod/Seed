## 11.1 Geological Setting: The Amazon Craton and Capim River Basin

### 11.1.1 Regional Tectonic Framework

**Amazon Craton:**
- **Age:** Archean to Paleoproterozoic (3.0–1.8 Ga)
- **Provinces:** Central Amazon (Archean), Maroni-Itacaúnas (Paleoproterozoic), Ventuari-Tapajós (Paleoproterozoic), Rio Negro-Juruena (Paleoproterozoic)
- **Structure:** Stable cratonic nucleus, surrounded by Phanerozoic basins
- **Basement:** Granite-gneiss complexes, greenstone belts, granulites

**Capim River Basin:**
- **Location:** Eastern Pará State, Brazil (1°–2°S, 48°–50°W)
- **Drainage:** Capim River → Guamá River → Baía do Guajará → Atlantic
- **Area:** ~15,000 km²
- **Relief:** Lowland Amazon Basin (50–200 m asl)
- **Climate:** Tropical rainforest (Af), MAP 2,500–3,000 mm, MAT 26–27°C

### 11.1.2 Stratigraphic Column: Cretaceous Capim Formation

| Unit | Age | Thickness | Lithology | Kaolin Potential |
|------|-----|-----------|-----------|------------------|
| **Barreiras Formation** | Miocene–Pleistocene | 20–50 m | Sand, clay, laterite | Overburden (weathered) |
| **Capim Formation** | **Cretaceous (Albian–Cenomanian)** | **50–150 m** | **Kaolinitic clay, sand, lignite** | **PRIMARY ORE** |
| **Itapecuru Formation** | Cretaceous (Aptian–Albian) | 30–100 m | Sand, clay, carbonate | Minor |
| **Basement** | Paleoproterozoic | — | **Granite-gneiss, migmatite** | **SOURCE ROCK** |

### 11.1.3 Capim Formation: Depositional Environment

**Sedimentology (from 200+ cores):**
- **Facies association:** Fluvial-deltaic to estuarine
- **Channel facies:** Cross-bedded sand, basal lag (kaolin clasts)
- **Floodbasin facies:** **Massive kaolinitic clay** (ore), laminated clay-silt
- **Crevasse splay:** Sandy kaolinitic clay
- **Lignite seams:** 0.5–3 m thick, 10–30% of section
- **Paleocurrents:** NW-SE (from cratonic interior to coast)

**Key insight:** Kaolin formed **in situ** by intense weathering of basement, then **reworked and concentrated** in fluvial-deltaic channels and floodbasins — not purely residual.

### 11.2.2 Weathering Stoichiometry: Granite → Kaolinite

**Overall reaction (biotite-gneiss → kaolinite + gibbsite + Fe-oxides):**

$$\text{K}_{0.8}\text{Na}_{0.5}\text{Ca}_{0.3}\text{Mg}_{0.4}\text{Fe}_{0.6}\text{Al}_{2.5}\text{Si}_{3.5}\text{O}_{10}(\text{OH})_2 + 14\text{H}^+ + 7\text{H}_2\text{O} \rightarrow 0.8\text{K}^+ + 0.5\text{Na}^+ + 0.3\text{Ca}^{2+} + 0.4\text{Mg}^{2+} + 0.6\text{Fe}^{3+} + 2.5\text{Al}^{3+} + 3.5\text{H}_4\text{SiO}_4$$

**Simplified to kaolinite + gibbsite:**
$$2\text{KAlSi}_3\text{O}_8 + 2\text{H}^+ + 9\text{H}_2\text{O} \rightarrow \text{Al}_2\text{Si}_2\text{O}_5(\text{OH})_4 + 2\text{K}^+ + 4\text{H}_4\text{SiO}_4 \quad \text{(feldspar)}$$
$$\text{K}(\text{Mg,Fe})_3\text{AlSi}_3\text{O}_{10}(\text{OH})_2 + 7\text{H}^+ + 3\text{H}_2\text{O} \rightarrow \text{K}^+ + 3\text{Mg}^{2+}/\text{Fe}^{2+} + \text{Al}^{3+} + 3\text{H}_4\text{SiO}_4 \quad \text{(biotite)}$$
$$\text{Al}^{3+} + 3\text{OH}^- \rightarrow \text{Al(OH)}_3 \quad \text{(gibbsite)}$$
$$\text{Fe}^{2+} + \frac{1}{4}\text{O}_2 + \frac{5}{2}\text{H}_2\text{O} \rightarrow \text{FeOOH} + 2\text{H}^+ \quad \text{(goethite)}$$

## 11.3 Eopaleozoic Weathering Duration: Time-Integrated Mass Loss

### 11.3.1 Weathering Duration Constraints

**Geochronology:**
- **Basement crystallization:** 2.1–1.8 Ga (U-Pb zircon)
- **Major weathering event:** Eopaleozoic (Cambrian–Ordovician, 540–470 Ma)
- **Capim Formation deposition:** Cretaceous (110–95 Ma)
- **Weathering duration:** ~400–600 Myr (Eopaleozoic to Cretaceous)

**Paleogeography (Eopaleozoic):**
- **Position:** ~10–15°S (Gondwana)
- **Climate:** Tropical, high CO₂ (10–15× PAL)
- **Vegetation:** Non-vascular (mosses, lichens) — no deep roots
- **Atmosphere:** High pCO₂ → intense carbonic acid weathering

### 11.3.2 Weathering Rate Calculations

**Mass loss rate (from mass balance):**
$$\text{Total loss} = 157\ \text{t per 100 m}^3 = 1.57\ \text{t/m}^3$$

**Over 500 Myr:**
$$\text{Rate} = \frac{1.57\ \text{t/m}^3}{5 \times 10^8\ \text{yr}} = 3.1 \times 10^{-9}\ \text{t/m}^2/\text{yr} \ (\text{per m depth})$$

**But:** Weathering front propagation:
- **Weathering front velocity:** ~1–5 m/Myr (tropical cratons)
- **Profile depth:** 50–100 m (typical)
- **Time to develop:** 10–100 Myr

**Resolution:** Intense Eopaleozoic weathering (10–50 Myr) created deep profile; Cretaceous erosion/redeposition concentrated kaolin.

### 11.3.3 Paleoweathering Indices: Chemical Index of Alteration (CIA)

**Source rock (granite-gneiss):** CIA = 45–50
**Saprolite (deep):** CIA = 85–92
**Capim kaolin (ore):** CIA = 95–99

**CIA progression:**
$$\text{CIA} = \frac{\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3}{\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3 + \text{CaO}^* + \text{Na}_2\text{O} + \text{K}_2\text{O}} \times 100$$

**Capim ore CIA = 98%** → **Near-complete removal of mobile cations**

## 11.4 Anoxic Weathering: Euhedral Kaolinite Genesis

### 11.4.1 Redox Conditions During Weathering

**Evidence for anoxic weathering:**
1. **Euhedral kaolinite crystals** — slow growth in stable conditions
2. **Low Fe/Ti** — Fe²⁺ mobile in anoxic, removed; Ti immobile
3. **Organic matter preservation** — lignite seams in Capim Formation
4. **Siderite (FeCO₃) nodules** — anoxic pore water
5. **Pyrite (FeS₂) in lignite** — sulfate reduction

**Redox sequence:**
1. **Oxic surface:** Fe³⁺ oxides form (thin)
2. **Water table:** Fluctuates, but deep anoxic zone develops
3. **Anoxic zone:** Fe²⁺ mobile, leached; organic acids accumulate
4. **Kaolinite precipitation:** Slow, ordered growth in Fe-depleted fluid

### 11.4.2 Euhedral Crystal Growth Kinetics

**Crystal morphology (SEM):**
- **Shape:** Hexagonal plates, pseudo-hexagonal
- **Size:** 0.5–5 μm (median 1.2 μm)
- **Aspect ratio:** 10:1 to 50:1 (diameter:thickness)
- **Defect density:** Very low (Hinckley Index 1.8–2.2)

**Growth rate estimation:**
- **Layer addition rate:** ~10⁻¹⁰ m/s (from laboratory)
- **Crystal thickness (10 layers):** ~7 nm
- **Time per crystal:** ~10⁴–10⁵ years
- **Consistent with:** Slow growth in stable, anoxic pore fluids

### 11.4.3 Structural Perfection and Industrial Quality

**Hinckley Index (HI) distribution:**
| Zone | HI Range | Mean | % Crystals >2 μm |
|------|----------|------|------------------|
| **High-grade (core)** | 1.8–2.3 | 2.0 | 45% |
| **Mid-grade** | 1.2–1.8 | 1.5 | 25% |
| **Low-grade (margins)** | 0.6–1.2 | 0.9 | 10% |

**Crystallite size (Scherrer, 001 peak):**
- **High-grade:** 150–250 nm (100–170 layers)
- **Mid-grade:** 80–150 nm (50–100 layers)
- **Low-grade:** 30–80 nm (20–50 layers)

**Industrial implication:** High structural perfection → **excellent rheology, high brightness, low viscosity**

## 11.9 Rheology and Colloidal Behavior: The Science of Capim Kaolin Slurries

### 11.9.1 Deflocculation Mechanism: Why Capim Kaolin is Special

**Structural advantages:**
1. **High crystallinity (HI 2.0)** → fewer edge defects → less edge-face attraction
2. **Low Fe/Ti** → fewer charged impurity sites
3. **Euhedral crystals** → uniform shape → predictable packing
4. **Near-theoretical density** → minimal intra-particle porosity

**Deflocculant adsorption (sodium silicate):**
- **Adsorption density:** 0.8 mg/m² (vs 1.5 mg/m² for Georgia kaolin)
- **Mechanism:** Silicate anions adsorb on edge Al-OH sites → charge reversal
- **Critical coagulation concentration (CCC):** 0.05% Na₂SiO₃ (vs 0.15% typical)

**Zeta potential vs pH (Capim high-grade):**

### 11.14.2 HPA Process Route: Acid Leach + Crystallization

**Selected route: HCl Leach → AlCl₃·6H₂O Crystallization → Calcination**

**Process flow:**
```
CAPIM KAOLIN (Ultra grade)
     |
  CALCINATION (750°C, 1 hr) -> METAKAOLIN (amorphous Al₂O₃·2SiO₂)
     |
  ACID LEACH (HCl, 20%, 90°C, 4 hr, L/S=5)
     |    Al₂O₃ + 6HCl -> 2AlCl₃ + 3H₂O
     |    SiO₂ + 6HCl -> SiCl₄ + 2H₂O (volatile)
     |    Fe₂O₃ + 6HCl -> 2FeCl₃ + 3H₂O
     |    TiO₂ + 4HCl -> TiCl₄ + 2H₂O (volatile)
     |
  SOLID-LIQUID SEPARATION (filter press, 30 bar)
     |    Residue: Silica gel (SiO₂·nH₂O) -> byproduct
     |    Filtrate: AlCl₃ + FeCl₃ + impurities
     |
  IMPURITY REMOVAL (Solvent extraction, 3-stage)
     |    Extractant: TBP (tributyl phosphate) in kerosene
     |    FeCl₃ extraction: 99.9% removal
     |    Ti, Na, K, Ca: >99% removal
     |
  CRYSTALLIZATION (AlCl₃·6H₂O, cooling -10°C to 20°C)
     |    Controlled nucleation, 100–300 μm crystals
     |    Mother liquor recycle (95% HCl recovery)
     |
  DEHYDRATION (Fluid bed dryer, 150°C, N₂ atmosphere)
     |    AlCl₃·6H₂O -> AlCl₃ (anhydrous)
     |
  HYDROLYSIS (AlCl₃ + 3H₂O -> Al(OH)₃ + 3HCl, 100°C)
     |    HCl gas recovery (99%)
     |    Al(OH)₃ precipitate (gibbsite)
     |
  CALCINATION (1,150°C, 2 hr, rotary kiln)
     |    2Al(OH)₃ -> Al₂O₃ + 3H₂O
     |
  FINAL PRODUCT: **HPA (4N: 99.99%, 5N: 99.999%)**
```
