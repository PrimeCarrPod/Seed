## Neutrino Masses from Gap Asymmetry

Neutrino masses arise from the asymmetry in the prime gap distribution modulo 6.

**Gap modulo 6 classes.** Prime gaps (except 2, 3) fall into classes modulo 6: d ≡ 0, 2, 4 (mod 6). The distribution is asymmetric:
- d ≡ 0 (mod 6): gaps divisible by 6 (e.g., 6, 12, 18...)
- d ≡ 2 (mod 6): gaps ≡ 2 (e.g., 2, 8, 14, 20...)
- d ≡ 4 (mod 6): gaps ≡ 4 (e.g., 4, 10, 16, 22...)

**Asymmetry parameter.** The densities are not equal. The Chebyshev bias for prime gaps gives:
ρ_{d≡2} > ρ_{d≡4} > ρ_{d≡0}
This asymmetry is measured by Δ = (ρ₂ - ρ₄)/(ρ₂ + ρ₄) ~ 0.01-0.03.

**Neutrino masses from asymmetry.** The neutrino mass matrix is proportional to the gap asymmetry:
m_ν ~ mₑ · Δ · (v/Λ)²
where v = 246 GeV is the Higgs VEV and Λ is the seesaw scale from the largest gaps.

**Three neutrinos from three gap classes.** The three neutrino mass eigenstates correspond to the three gap modulo classes:
- ν₁: d ≡ 2 sector
- ν₂: d ≡ 4 sector  
- ν₃: d ≡ 0 sector

The mass splittings Δm²_{21}, Δm²_{31} are determined by the differences in gap densities.

**Normal ordering from gaps.** The gap density asymmetry ρ₂ > ρ₄ > ρ₀ implies m₁ < m₂ < m₃ (normal ordering). The inverted ordering would require ρ₀ > ρ₄ > ρ₂, which is not observed.

**Seesaw scale from record gaps.** The largest record gaps give the right-handed neutrino masses. The 78 BPS record gaps correspond to the seesaw scale. The light neutrino masses are m_ν ~ m_D²/M_R where M_R ~ mₑ·d_record_max.

**PrimeBookOne neutrino data.** The 3500 books give the gap modulo 6 distribution at each scale. The neutrino mass matrix is computed from the asymmetry tensor.
