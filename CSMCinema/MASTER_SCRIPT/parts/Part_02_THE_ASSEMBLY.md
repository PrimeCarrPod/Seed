EXT. MOUNT PELION - SUMMIT CLEARING - DAWN

Ancient pines. Thirty meters tall. Needle bed thick. The air smells of resin and destiny.

ARGUS (40s, shipwright, hands like oak roots, eyes that listen more than they speak) kneels at the base of the largest pine. Eyes closed. Hands on bark.

ATHENA (appearing as geometry, not words — pure mathematical intuition transmitted through grain, through ring-patterns, through the stress calculations of three hundred years of growth)

Argus opens his eyes. Understanding.

ARGUS
(whispering)
Fifty oars. One hull. One purpose.

He raises the axe. Hesitates. The tree seems to lean toward him.

ARGUS (CONT'D)
(to the tree)
I'm not cutting you. I'm freeing you. You've been growing toward this shape for three hundred years.

The first axe falls. The timber cracks like bone.

EXT. MOUNT PELION - LATER

The keel is laid. Argus works in silence. The hull takes shape.

Footsteps. Not human rhythm. Each step compresses the needle bed by three centimeters.

HERACLES (30s, 340 kg, demigod, Nemean lion glow faint on his skin, club stays at base camp) ascends. He carries no weapon. He is the weapon.

He stops ten meters from Argus. Looks at the keel. Looks at the trees. Looks at the space between what is and what will sail.

HERACLES
I heard the mountain speak. The trees told me the ship needs an anchor who can row. I volunteer.

Argus doesn't look up.

ARGUS
The ship needs no anchor. It needs a storm that knows when to calm.

HERACLES
(laughing — shakes pine needles from thirty meters up)
Then I am that storm.

---

EXT. MOUNT PELION - LATER

ORPHEUS ascends playing his lyre. The trees lean. The stone vibrates. Argus pauses — the first interruption since dawn.

ORPHEUS
The ocean taught me a song. It said the ship would need a voice when the wind stops. I am that voice.

ARGUS
The wind never stops.

ORPHEUS
(smiling)
Then I'll sing with it.

The hull resonates at a frequency it didn't have before. Orpheus didn't just arrive. He tuned the ship.

---

EXT. MOUNT PELION - LATER

CASTOR AND POLLUX ascend side by side. Neither ahead. Neither behind. They walk in perfect synchronization — not practiced. Not performed. Biological.

CASTOR
We heard the ship call twice.

POLLUX
Once for each of us.

CASTOR/POLLUX
(in unison, not planned)
The voyage requires both the blade and the fist. We are both. We are one. We volunteer.

Argus looks up for the second time.

ARGUS
The blueprint said two as one. I thought it meant a marriage. It meant you.

The twins board. The ship doesn't sink deeper. It rises — as if the ship has been waiting for exactly this mass.

---

EXT. MOUNT PELION - LATER

ATALANTA ascends alone. She carries a bow. She needs no invitation.

ATALANTA
I heard the call.

ARGUS
I didn't send one.

ATALANTA
The ship did.

(to Argus)
I hunt my own quarry. The fleece is mine to chase. I don't follow. I don't serve. I don't owe.

ARGUS
What do you do?

ATALANTA
I run. I track. I kill. And I don't miss.

ARGUS
Then welcome aboard. We'll need someone who doesn't miss.

---

EXT. MOUNT PELION - DAY

JASON arrives last. He looks at the half-built ship. At the crew gathering. At Argus working in silence.

JASON
Will it float?

ARGUS
(without looking up)
Will you?

JASON
I don't know how to sail.

ARGUS
Then you'll learn. The ship doesn't need a captain. It needs a purpose. You have one?

JASON
(thinking)
To restore what was stolen. To finish what my father started. To prove that the gods don't choose — they recognize.

ARGUS
(nodding)
That'll do.
