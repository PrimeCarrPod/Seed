## PRE-SCENE ABSURD DETAIL

### Geography

Iolcus sits on the eastern coast of Thessaly, where the Pelion mountain range meets the Pagasetic Gulf. The town is built on a gentle slope — the harbor at sea level, the market square thirty feet up, the palace sixty feet up. The geography is a hierarchy. The higher you live, the more power you have. The lower you live, the more connected you are to the sea.

The market square is cobblestone. The cobblestones are local granite, grey-blue, worn smooth by three thousand years of feet. Each stone is different. Each stone has a history. The stone where Jason stands — that stone has been stood on by fishermen, by merchants, by kings, by prophets. That stone has seen everything. That stone knows what's coming.

The harbor below is natural. Protected by a breakwater built in the time of Jason's grandfather. The water is shallow — ten feet deep at the dock. The water is clear. You can see the bottom. You can see the fish. You can see the anchors. You can see the history.

The Olympus mount rises behind the town. Not THE Olympus — that's further north. This is a smaller Olympus. A local Olympus. A Olympus that says: even our mountains are named after gods. Even our mountains are divine.

### Architecture

The market square is surrounded by three buildings:

**The Fish Market (North Side):** Open-air. Wooden stalls. Canvas awnings (red and white striped). The stalls are old. The wood is salt-warped. The canvas is patched. But the fish is fresh. The fish is always fresh. The fish is the lifeblood of Iolcus.

**The Brick Ovens (East Side):** Three ovens. Dome-shaped. Clay brick. The ovens have been burning for three generations. The ovens are never allowed to go out. The ovens are the heart of Iolcus. The ovens make the bread. The bread feeds the people. The people are the town.

**The Palace (South Side):** Stone. Marble columns (four of them — Doric, not yet Corinthian, this is an older time). The palace was built by Jason's grandfather. The palace was meant for a king who deserved it. The palace is too big for Pelias. The palace knows this. The palace is waiting.

The balcony where Pelias stands is twelve feet above the square. The balcony is marble. The railing is marble. The railing has carvings — fish, waves, the symbols of Iolcus. The carvings are worn. The carvings are old. The carvings have been touched by a thousand hands gripping the railing and looking down at the square and wondering what's coming.

The wind is from the east. The wind carries salt. The wind carries fish. The wind carries the smell of the sea. The wind is the wind of Iolcus. The wind has been blowing for three thousand years. The wind will blow for three thousand more.
