## MUSICAL KEY

**D minor** — The key of destiny. The key of prophecy. The key of footsteps on stone. D minor is the sound of something about to happen. It's the key of Mozart's Requiem. The key of Beethoven's Ninth. The key that says: the ordinary world is about to end.

---

## TEMPO

**72 BPM** — The heartbeat of the ordinary world. Not the 60 BPM of rest. Not the 100 BPM of excitement. 72 BPM is the heartbeat of waiting. Of watching. Of knowing something is coming but not knowing what.

---

## LEITMOTIF: THE SANDAL THEME

**Description:** A single note, repeated, uneven. Left foot bare = higher pitch (D5). Right foot sandal = lower pitch (D4). The rhythm is not regular. It's the rhythm of prophecy. The rhythm of one person walking into a town that doesn't know it's about to change forever.

**Instrumentation:** Solo bodhran (Irish frame drum). One hand bare, one hand with a sandal wrapped in leather. The sound is ancient. Pre-Greek. Pre-civilization. The sound of feet on stone before feet knew they were making history.

**Recurrence:** The Sandal Theme will return in every part of this addendum. Sometimes literal (footsteps). Sometimes metaphorical (the uneven rhythm of fate). Sometimes transformed (the fleece itself makes the same uneven sound when it moves). By Part 11, the Sandal Theme will have been played by every instrument in the orchestra.

---

## VISUAL PALETTE

**Colors:** Gold and grey. Dawn light through fog. The wet gleam of fish scales (silver-green). Smoke from brick ovens (grey-white, curling). The bronze of weapons on the balcony (tarnished green-gold). The dog's coat (brown-black, wet). Jason's hair (gold — the only pure gold in the scene, the only color that matters).

**Lighting:** Pre-dawn. The sun is below the horizon but its light is already bouncing off Olympus mount, creating a secondary illumination that makes everything look like it's lit from below. Shadows fall upward. The world is inverted. The ordinary world is already turning inside out.

**Fog:** Density moderate. Visibility 200 yards. The fog doesn't obscure — it reveals. It makes the visible world look like a memory. Like something that's already happened and is now being remembered.

**Camera:** Static. Wide. The Bunraku puppeteer's eye. The camera doesn't move because the world doesn't move. Only the characters move. Only the characters matter.

## NARRATOR ONE PRELUDE

Before the first word. Before the first step. Before the first choice. There was the ordinary world. And the ordinary world was breathing. And the breathing was uneven. And the unevenness was prophecy. And the prophecy was one sandal, bare, on stone. Listen. Can you hear it? The sound of the ordinary world ending? That's not thunder. That's not lightning. That's a footstep. Bare. On stone. The call begins.

## PRE-SCENE ABSURD DETAIL

### Geography

Iolcus sits on the eastern coast of Thessaly, where the Pelion mountain range meets the Pagasetic Gulf. The town is built on a gentle slope — the harbor at sea level, the market square thirty feet up, the palace sixty feet up. The geography is a hierarchy. The higher you live, the more power you have. The lower you live, the more connected you are to the sea.

The market square is cobblestone. The cobblestones are local granite, grey-blue, worn smooth by three thousand years of feet. Each stone is different. Each stone has a history. The stone where Jason stands — that stone has been stood on by fishermen, by merchants, by kings, by prophets. That stone has seen everything. That stone knows what's coming.

The harbor below is natural. Protected by a breakwater built in the time of Jason's grandfather. The water is shallow — ten feet deep at the dock. The water is clear. You can see the bottom. You can see the fish. You can see the anchors. You can see the history.

The Olympus mount rises behind the town. Not THE Olympus — that's further north. This is a smaller Olympus. A local Olympus. A Olympus that says: even our mountains are named after gods. Even our mountains are divine.

### Architecture

The market square is surrounded by three buildings:

**The Fish Market (North Side):** Open-air. Wooden stalls. Canvas awnings (red and white striped). The stalls are old. The wood is salt-warped. The canvas is patched. But the fish is fresh. The fish is always fresh. The fish is the lifeblood of Iolcus.

**The Brick Ovens (East Side):** Three ovens. Dome-shaped. Clay brick. The ovens have been burning for three generations. The ovens are never allowed to go out. The ovens are the heart of Iolcus. The ovens make the bread. The bread feeds the people. The people are the town.

**The Palace (South Side):** Stone. Marble columns (four of them — Doric, not yet Corinthian, this is an older time). The palace was built by Jason's grandfather. The palace was meant for a king who deserved it. The palace is too big for Pelias. The palace knows this. The palace is waiting.

The balcony where Pelias stands is twelve feet above the square. The balcony is marble. The railing is marble. The railing has carvings — fish, waves, the symbols of Iolcus. The carvings are worn. The carvings are old. The carvings have been touched by a thousand hands gripping the railing and looking down at the square and wondering what's coming.

### Weather

Pre-dawn. Temperature: 54 Fahrenheit. Humidity: 78 percent. Fog density: moderate. Visibility: 200 yards.

The fog comes from the sea. The fog is the sea breathing. The fog rolls in every morning and burns off every afternoon. The fog is the town's way of saying: we are between worlds. We are between sea and land. We are between known and unknown. We are between the ordinary and the extraordinary.

The wind is from the east. The wind carries salt. The wind carries fish. The wind carries the smell of the sea. The wind is the wind of Iolcus. The wind has been blowing for three thousand years. The wind will blow for three thousand more.

### Biology

**The Dog:** The dog is a Molossian. Large. Brown-black coat. Wet from the fog. The dog is old — ten years, maybe twelve. The dog has lived in the market square his whole life. The dog knows every cobblestone. The dog knows every stall. The dog knows every person. The dog is the market square's memory. The dog is the market square's conscience. The dog knows what's coming. The dog always knows.

**The Fish:** The fish on the stalls are overnight catch. Mackerel. Sardines. Sea bass. The fish are silver-green. The fish are still glistening. The fish are still dying. The fish are the reason Iolcus exists. The fish are the reason the town is here. The fish are the reason Jason is here. (Not directly. But the fish are part of the pattern. Everything is part of the pattern.)

**The Smoke:** The smoke from the brick ovens is white-grey. The smoke curls. The smoke curls like thread. The smoke curls like fate. The smoke curls like the spine of a story that hasn't decided what it is yet. The smoke rises twelve feet and dissipates into the fog. The smoke is the breath of Iolcus. The smoke is the town saying: we are here. We are alive. We are waiting.

### History

This market square has been here for three hundred years. Before the market square, there was a temple. Before the temple, there was a grove. Before the grove, there was nothing. Just stone. Just sea. Just the ordinary world waiting to become extraordinary.

Jason's grandfather built the palace. Jason's father played in this square. Jason was born in the palace. Jason was sent away from this square. Jason has returned to this square. The square remembers. The square always remembers.

Twelve years ago, Pelias stood on this balcony and watched Aristaeus taken away. Twelve years ago, Pelias stood on this balcony and took the crown. Twelve years ago, Pelias stood on this balcony and became the thing he feared: a usurper. A thief. A king who doesn't deserve the throne.

Now Pelias stands on this balcony again. And the square knows. The square always knows.

### Sensory

**Sound:** The cart rattle (2 Hz). The dog pads (1.5 Hz). The wind through empty square (white noise). The fish sizzling on the stalls (high frequency, fading). The smoke curling (silence — smoke makes no sound but you can hear it if you listen). Pelias' breath (shallow, 88 BPM). The ordinary world breathing.

**Smell:** Salt. Fish. Woodsmoke. Bread baking. The sea. The fog. The stone. The smell of a town that lives by the sea. The smell of a town that knows the sea gives and the sea takes.

**Touch:** The fog is wet. The cobblestones are cold. The marble railing is colder. The crown is heavy. The robes don't fit. The hands are shaking. The ordinary world is uncomfortable. The ordinary world has been uncomfortable for twelve years.

**Taste:** Salt on the tongue. Fear in the mouth. The taste of a king who knows his time is ending. The taste of a town that doesn't know its world is about to change. The taste of prophecy.

The pattern is here. The pattern is always here. The pattern is in the cobblestones. The pattern is in the fog. The pattern is in the fish scales. The pattern is in the smoke. The pattern is in the dog's knowing eyes. The pattern is in the king's shaking hands. The pattern is in the empty square waiting to be filled.

## PRE-SCENE ABSURD DETAIL

### Geography

The balcony is twelve feet above the square. The balcony is marble. The railing is marble. The carvings on the railing are fish, waves, the symbols of Iolcus. The carvings are worn. The carvings have been touched by a thousand hands.

The distance between Pelias and Jason is forty yards. Forty yards of cobblestone. Forty yards of morning air. Forty yards of prophecy.

### Architecture

The palace behind Pelias is stone. Marble columns. Four of them. Doric. The columns were quarried from Mount Pelion. The columns are three hundred years old. The columns have seen kings come and go. The columns are patient.

The throne room behind the columns is dark. The throne is visible from the balcony. The throne is gold. The throne is empty. The throne is waiting.

### Weather

The fog is thinning. Visibility now three hundred yards. The sun is still below the horizon but the light is stronger. The light is gold. The light is the color of Jason's hair. The light is the color of the fleeze. The light is prophecy.

### Biology

**Pelias:** Heart rate now ninety-two beats per minute. Respiration shallow. Hands shaking. Eyes bloodshot. The biology of fear. The biology of guilt. The biology of a king who knows his time is ending.

**Jason:** Heart rate fifty-two beats per minute. Respiration steady. Hands open. Eyes up. The biology of confidence. The biology of purpose. The biology of a hero who doesn't know he's supposed to be afraid.

### History

Twelve years ago, Pelias stood on this balcony and watched his brother taken away. Twelve years ago, Pelias stood on this balcony and took the crown. Twelve years ago, Pelias became the thing he feared.

Now Pelias stands on this balcony again. And the balcony knows. The balcony always knows.

### Sensory

**Sound:** Pelias' breath (shallow, rapid). The wind (from the east, carrying salt). The dog (pads on cobblestone). The cart (rattle, fading). The ordinary world (breathing, uneven).

**Smell:** Salt. Fear. The smell of a king who knows. The smell of a town that doesn't.

**Touch:** The marble railing (cold). The crown (heavy). The robes (don't fit). The hands (shaking). The ordinary world (uncomfortable).

**Taste:** Fear. Guilt. The taste of a usurper. The taste of a prophecy about to be fulfilled.

### Metaphysical

The pattern is tightening. The threads are gathering. The debt is about to be called.

Clotho spins faster. Lachesis measures the distance: forty yards. Atropos positions her shears: not yet. Hecate watches: the debt at Anauron is about to be collected.

### Musical

**Theme:** The Sandal Theme continues. But now there's a second voice. A low drone. D2. The voice of the throne. The voice of guilt. The voice of Pelias.

**Harmony:** Dm + D2 drone. The chord of the usurper. The chord that waits for the end.

**Dynamics:** pp. Still quiet. But louder than before. The ordinary world is getting louder. The ordinary world is ending.

### Cyclical

This scene connects to:
- **Scene 6, Part 11** (The Return) — Pelias is gone. The throne is empty. The square remembers.
- **Scene 2, Part 05** (The Father) — Pelias on his flagship. The same fear. The same guilt.
- **Scene 1, Part 09** (Gaia) — The throne is stone. The stone remembers. The stone endures.
