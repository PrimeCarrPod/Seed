# Document 1: Historical Foundations & Pathophysiology of Artificial Hemoglobin Survival Research
## Piece 16/18: Colloidal Stability and Rheology in Microcirculation

The rheological behavior of artificial oxygen carriers in the microcirculation (vessels <100 μm diameter) determines their ability to reach hypoxic tissues. Native RBCs (7-8 μm diameter) deform to traverse capillaries (3-5 μm) via tank-treading motion, with transit times of 0.5-1 second. Their biconcave disc shape and membrane cytoskeleton (spectrin-actin network) enable this deformation while maintaining surface area-to-volume ratio.

**Hemopure (polymerized bovine Hb)**: Polydisperse particles 10-500 nm (peak ~30 nm). Too small for significant margination or deformation; behaves as Newtonian fluid at low shear, shear-thinning at high shear. Viscosity at 10 g/dL: ~3.5 cP (vs blood ~4 cP at 45% Hct). No yield stress. Microvascular perfusion: uniform distribution but no preferential flow to hypoxic zones.

**M101 (Arenicola Hb)**: Hexagonal bilayer nanoparticles, 150 × 25 nm (disc-shaped). Aspect ratio ~6:1. Behaves as non-Newtonian fluid with yield stress ~0.5 Pa. In microchannels, aligns with flow (Jeffery orbits), reducing viscous dissipation. Margination to vessel walls is enhanced by shape, potentially improving NO scavenging at endothelium—but M101's low NO reactivity mitigates this.

**ErythroMer**: Toroidal nanoparticles ~200 nm diameter, 50 nm central depression. Soft PEGylated lipid shell (bending modulus ~10 kBT). Deforms significantly in microchannels: aspect ratio changes from 1:1 to 3:1 at shear rates >100 s⁻¹. Transit time through 5 μm constrictions: 1.5-2× native RBC. Advantage: can traverse partially occluded vessels (thrombi, leukocyte plugs) that block native RBCs. The toroidal shape maximizes surface-to-volume ratio for gas exchange.

**PFC emulsions**: Droplets 0.1-0.2 μm, spherical, rigid interface (surfactant monolayer). Newtonian fluid, viscosity 1.5-2 cP at 20-30% volume fraction. No deformation, no margination. Uniform perfusion but cannot access collapsed capillaries. High density (1.8-1.9 g/mL) causes gravitational separation in large vessels (sedimentation velocity ~1 μm/s).

**LOMs**: 0.9-4 μm diameter, deformable lipid monolayer. Size-matched to RBCs. In pulmonary capillaries, deform and transit without obstruction (validated in vitro). In systemic microcirculation, collapse within seconds as O₂ diffuses out—transient presence only.

For entrapment scenarios with crush injury, compartment syndrome, or microvascular thrombosis, ErythroMer's deformability and M101's margination provide unique advantages in reperfusing ischemic tissue. This is quantified in Document 5 (ErythroMer Architecture) and Document 3 (M101).
