Gertrude Ederle swam the English Channel that same summer — August 6, 1926 — fourteen hours and thirty-one minutes in the water, the first woman to do it, beating the men's record by nearly two hours. She was nineteen years old. She was partially deaf from a childhood illness. The water was fifty-eight degrees. She wore a two-piece bathing suit that she had designed herself because the standard wool swimsuits of the era weighed twenty pounds when wet and would have drowned her. She covered herself in lanolin and olive oil and Vaseline to stay warm, and when she emerged on the shore at Kingsdown, England, she had turned the English Channel from a barrier into a door, and the key was her own body, and nobody had told her that women couldn't do this thing — or rather, they had told her, repeatedly, and she had simply not believed them, which is the Beatrix Kiddo approach to gatekeeping, applied to oceanography.
