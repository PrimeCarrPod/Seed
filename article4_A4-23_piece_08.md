# 9. Reheating from Gap Thermalization

## 9.1 Reheating as Gap Distribution Thermalization

After inflation (directory 1.0), the universe reheats as the gap distribution thermalizes to the Standard Model gap configuration (directory 2.0 → 3.0). The reheating temperature is determined by the gap energy scale at the end of inflation:

T_{reh} ≈ E_{gap}(d_{end}) / k_B

where d_{end} is the gap scale at the end of inflation. In the Prime Electron framework, the end of inflation corresponds to the transition from the maximum gap (254) to the electroweak scale gaps (4, 6, 14).

The reheating process is the transfer of energy from the gap-254 condensate to the Standard Model gap sectors:

E_{254} → E_{SM} = Σ_{d∈{2,4,6,14,...}} E_d

The reheating temperature is:

T_{reh} ≈ (E_{254} / k_B) × (volume factor)⁻¹ᐟ⁴

The volume factor is the ratio of the inflationary Hubble volume to the electroweak volume:

(V_{inf} / V_{EW}) ≈ (E_{Pl} / E_{EW})³ ≈ (10¹⁹ / 10²)³ ≈ 10⁵¹

Thus:

T_{reh} ≈ 10¹⁹ GeV × 10⁻¹².⁷⁵ ≈ 10⁶.²⁵ GeV ≈ 2 × 10⁶ GeV

This is consistent with high-scale reheating scenarios.

## 9.2 Gravitino/Moduli Problem from Gap Hierarchy

The gap hierarchy (254 → 14 → 6 → 4 → 2) naturally solves the gravitino/moduli problem. Heavy gap modes (254, 14) decay before BBN, while light gap modes (2, 4, 6) survive to form the Standard Model particles. The decay rates are:

Γ_{d→d'} ∝ exp(-|E_d - E_{d'}|/κ)

Gap 254 decays rapidly to gap 14, which decays to gap 6, etc. The lifetime of the gap-254 condensate is:

τ_{254} ≈ 1 / Γ_{254→14} ≈ 10⁻³⁵ s

well before BBN (t ~ 1 s).
