## 1. THE RADIO-FREQUENCY CHALLENGE AND THE S-BUS SURFACE PATH

The S-Bus emergency beacon architecture, which ARDEN validated under eight meters of Sahara sand in Episode Twenty-One of the Sibling Frequency — a test that demonstrated the +22 dBm, 915 MHz electromagnetic attenuation of seawater, characterized by the complex permittivity of the medium — ε* = ε' - jε'', where the real part ε' ≈ 81 at 915 MHz for seawater at 15°C and 35 practical salinity units, representing the dielectric constant's contribution to the displacement current, and the imaginary part ε'' = σ/(2πf), where σ ≈ 4.5 siemens per meter is the bulk electrical conductivity of seawater at 35 practical salinity units and 15°C, representing the conduction current that dissipates the electromagnetic wave's energy as Joule heating of the conductive medium — translates into an attenuation coefficient α, derived from the plane-wave solution to Maxwell's equations in a lossy dielectric, of approximately α = 2πf · sqrt(μ₀ε₀ε' · (sqrt(1 + (σ/(2πfε₀ε'))²) - 1)/2), where μ₀ is the permeability of free space (4π × 10⁻⁷ henries per meter), ε₀ is the permittivity of free space (8.854 × 10⁻¹² farads per meter), and the term under the square root is the loss tangent, tan δ = σ/(2πfε₀ε'), which for seawater at 915 MHz evaluates to approximately 1.1 — indicating that seawater at this frequency is neither a good conductor (which would have tan δ >> 1, causing the wave to coefficient α evaluates to approximately 42 decibels per meter, meaning that the electromagnetic wave's power density decreases by a factor of approximately 10^(-42/10) ≈ 6.3 × 10⁻⁵ — roughly a factor that would overwhelm a higher-data-rate, less-robust modulation scheme, but that the BPSK modulation, with its 3-decibel signal-to-noise ratio advantage over the next most robust scheme (quadrature signal-to-noise ratio to achieve the same bit-error rate as BPSK, a penalty that, in the noise environment of a pod emergency, could render the higher-rate scheme unusable, while the lower-rate BPSK scheme, with its single bit per symbol and its simple, robust decision boundary — the sign of the in-phase component, positive for a binary '1' and negative for a binary '0' — a decision that a estimation error is approximately 2 to 4 times the range measurement error, a factor that the multilateration algorithm, a least-squares estimator that solves for the pod's three-dimensional position (latitude, longitude, depth) by minimizing the sum of the squared differences between the measured time-of-arrival of the beacon signal at each receiving node and the predicted time-of-arrival based position, from the inertial navigation system, before the navigation system failed or the pod was displaced — is within approximately 1 kilometer of the true position, a condition that is satisfied
