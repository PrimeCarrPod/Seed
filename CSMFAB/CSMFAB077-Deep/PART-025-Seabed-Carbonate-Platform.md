# CSMFAB077-DEEP-025 — SEABED CARBONATE PLATFORM: BEARING CAPACITY, DISSOLUTION RISK, HOLOCENE SEDIMENT LAYER PROPERTIES

**Document ID:** CSMFAB077-DEEP-025 | **Parent:** CSMFAB000000000077 | **Version:** 2.0 | **Part:** 025 of 160 — Block 3

The seabed at the Osa deployment site consists of a thin layer — approximately 0.5 to 2 meters thick — of Holocene biogenic sediment, primarily composed of the calcareous skeletal remains of the marine organisms that inhabit the Golfo Dulce: the tests of the foraminifera (microscopic, single-celled protists that secrete a calcite shell, which, upon the organism's death, sinks to the seabed and accumulates as a calcareous ooze), the fragments of the mollusk shells (the bivalves and the gastropods that live on and in the seabed, their shells broken by the wave action and by the predation of the crustaceans, the fragments accumulating as a coarse, poorly sorted sediment that the geotechnical analysis classifies as a sandy silt — approximately 40 percent sand, 50 percent silt, and 10 percent clay, by weight, with the sand fraction consisting primarily of the shell fragments, which are angular (because the shell is brittle and fractures along the crystalline planes of the calcite, producing fragments with sharp edges, a morphology that increases the internal friction angle of the sediment — the angular particles interlock, resisting shear, and giving the sediment a friction angle of approximately 32 to 38 degrees, as measured by the direct shear tests on the piston-core samples that the Charlemagne survey vessel collected, a friction angle that is comparable to the friction angle of a medium-dense sand, and that, combined with the cohesion that the fine fraction — the silt and the clay — provides, yields a sediment that is both strong and stable, with a bearing capacity — calculated from the Terzaghi bearing-capacity equation using the measured friction angle and cohesion (the cohesion intercept, from the triaxial tests, is approximately 10 to 25 kilopascals, reflecting the weak cementation of the sediment by the calcium carbonate that precipitates from the pore water, a cementation that the geotechnical analysis has assessed as stable — it does not dissolve under the conditions of the Atlantis pod's deployment, because the pore water in the carbonate platform is saturated with respect to calcite, a saturation that the geochemical modeling has confirmed, using the USGS PHREEQC software, which simulates the chemical equilibria between the sediment, the pore water, and the overlying seawater, and which predicts that the calcite cement will neither dissolve nor precipitate over the pod's 50-year design life, a prediction that the long-term immersion tests — in which samples of the carbonate sediment were immersed in the Golfo Dulce seawater for 5,000 hours at 15 degrees Celsius, and the changes in the sediment's strength, porosity, and mineralogy were measured — have validated, showing no detectable change in any of the measured properties, a validation that confirms the geotechnical stability of the Osa seabed and that supports the use of the same helical pile anchoring design that was developed for the Monterey site, with the reduction in the pile's pullout capacity — from 1,200 kilonewtons per pile at Monterey to 800 to 1,000 kilonewtons at Osa, as discussed in PART-015 — being offset by the reduction in the predicted lateral load, from 200 to 500 kilonewtons at Monterey to approximately 100 to 200 kilonewtons at Osa, a reduction that maintains the factor of safety against pullout at greater than 4.0, a value that the Aegis C safety analysis considers acceptable for the Osa site, where the consequences of an anchoring failure, while still catastrophic for the affected pod, are less severe for the overall Atlantis mission (the Osa community has four pods, and the loss of one pod would leave three for the post-event recovery, whereas the Monterey community has six pods, and the loss of a pod — while still recoverable — would represent a larger fractional loss of the community's capacity, a difference that the ZIRCONIA analysis has factored into the allocation of the safety margins, with Monterey receiving the higher factor of safety, reflecting its greater strategic importance as the primary data-collection and technology-validation site, a strategic importance that the Atlantis program management has affirmed in the prioritization of the Atlantis resources, a prioritization that this PART-025, by quantifying the geotechnical properties of the Osa seabed, enables.
