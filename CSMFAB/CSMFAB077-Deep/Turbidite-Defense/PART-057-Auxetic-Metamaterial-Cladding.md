The auxetic metamaterial cladding — the 150-millimeter-thick, sacrificial impact-absorption layer that envelops the Atlantis pod's pressure hull and that constitutes the second line of the four-layer turbidite defense (PART-060) — derives its exceptional energy-absorption capacity from the deliberate engineering of a negative Poisson's ratio (ν ≈ -0.40 to -0.50 in the through-thickness direction), a counterintuitive material property in which the material contracts laterally when compressed (rather than expanding, as the conventional materials with the positive Poisson's ratio do), causing the material to densify locally at the impact site — to draw mass inward toward the point of the impact — rather than to spread the mass outward, a behavior that increases the effective density at the impact zone by a factor of approximately (1 - 2ν·ε)/(1 - (1 + ν)·ε) ≈ (1 + 0.8 × 0.5)/(1 - 0.6 × 0.5) = (1.4)/(0.7) = 2.0 at the 50 percent compressive strain, a doubling of the density that dramatically enhances the energy absorption because the energy required to crush a cellular solid scales approximately linearly with the density (for the elastic buckling-dominated crushing) or with the density raised to the 1.5 power (for the plastic-hinge-dominated crushing), such that the auxetic's densification at the impact site increases the local SEA by a factor of approximately 2 to 3 relative to a conventional, non-auxetic cellular solid of the same initial density, the quantitative advantage that the auxetic cladding provides and that the PART-056 energy-balance calculation exploited to achieve the 7.2 mm penetration depth for the 7.64 kJ boulder impact — a result that a conventional, the non-auxetic honeycomb of the same thickness and the same initial density would not achieve, the boulder penetrating approximately 15 to 25 mm into the conventional honeycomb and transmitting the proportionally higher residual force to the underlying laminate, a comparison that the Atlantis cladding-development team validated through the instrumented drop-weight impact tests on the 150 mm × 150 mm × 150 mm coupon specimens, with the auxetic and the non-auxetic honeycombs of the identical TPU material and the identical 50 kg/m³ initial density, the impactor mass of 38.2 kg, the impact velocity of 5 m/s (the 20 m/s full-scale velocity scaled down to the 1:4 scale of the coupon test by the replica scaling law — the velocity scales as the square root of the length scale, v_model = v_full × √(L_model/L_full) = 20 × √(1/4) = 10 m/s, but the drop-weight tester's maximum velocity is 5 m/s, so the test was conducted at the 5 m/s and the results extrapolated to the 20 m/s full-scale impact using the strain-rate-dependent material properties of the TPU, the extrapolation validated by the separate, high-strain-rate Split-Hopkinson pressure bar (SHPB) tests on the TPU specimens that provided the constitutive data for the strain rates up to 10⁴ s⁻¹, the data showing that the TPU's flow stress increases by approximately 60 percent from the 10² s⁻¹ (the drop-weight strain rate) to the 10⁴ s⁻¹ (the full-scale impact strain rate), and the penetration depth for the full-scale impact was extrapolated by multiplying the measured penetration depth at the 5 m/s by the factor (SEA_full/SEA_test)^(-1) ≈ (1.6)^(-1) ≈ 0.625, i.e., the full-scale penetration depth is approximately 62.5 percent of the measured depth at the 5 m/s, because the higher flow stress at the higher strain rate absorbs the energy in a smaller volume, a counterintuitive scaling that the SHPB data confirmed and that the coupon-test results, after the extrapolation, yielded the penetration depths of 6.8 mm (auxetic) and 19.2 mm (non-auxetic) for the 20 m/s, 38.2 kg impactor — a factor of 2.82 improvement, consistent with the densification-enhanced SEA model's prediction and validating the auxetic's superiority for the Atlantis cladding application.

The re-entrant honeycomb geometry — the specific, two-dimensional periodic cellular structure that produces the negative Poisson's ratio in the auxetic cladding — consists of the unit cell shown in the classical auxetic literature: a regular hexagon that has been "re-entrant" — the two inclined walls of the hexagon are angled inward, toward the center of the cell, rather than outward as in the conventional (the positive-Poisson's-ratio) honeycomb, such that when the cell is compressed in the vertical direction (the direction normal to the cladding surface, i.e., the through-thickness direction of the Atlantis cladding), the inclined walls buckle inward, pulling the vertical walls toward each other and reducing the horizontal dimension of the cell, producing the negative Poisson's ratio — a geometry that is characterized by three independent parameters: the vertical wall length h, the inclined wall length l, the inclined-wall angle θ (measured from the horizontal, with θ < 90° for the conventional honeycomb and θ > 90° for the re-entrant, the auxetic honeycomb — the angle is the supplement of the conventional honeycomb's cell angle, θ_reentrant = 180° - θ_conventional, so for the conventional honeycomb with θ_conventional ≈ 30° (the standard hexagonal honeycomb geometry that yields the isotropic, in-plane properties), the re-entrant angle is θ_reentrant ≈ 150°, producing the negative Poisson's ratio), and the wall thickness t, which together determine the effective elastic properties of the honeycomb through the analytical relationships derived from the beam-bending model of the cell walls, treating each wall as a Euler-Bernoulli beam that is rigidly connected at the joints (the vertices of the hexagon) and that deforms by the bending when the honeycomb is loaded in the direction that produces the negative Poisson's ratio response — for the loading in the vertical direction (the y-direction, normal to the cladding surface), the inclined walls experience the bending moments at the joints, and the vertical walls experience the axial compression plus the bending, with the axial stiffness of the vertical walls being much higher than the bending stiffness of the inclined walls (because the axial stiffness scales with t/L, while the bending stiffness scales with (t/L)³, and for the thin-walled honeycomb with t/l ≈ 0.05 to 0.10, the bending stiffness is the dominant flexibility, and the honeycomb's effective modulus in the vertical direction is approximated by E_y ≈ E_TPU · (t/l)³ · (h/l + sin²θ)/(cos³θ), a relationship that the Atlantis auxetic-honeycomb optimization used as the starting point for the parametric study that varied the three geometric parameters (h, l, θ) and the wall thickness t, with the objective of maximizing the SEA while maintaining the negative Poisson's ratio in the range of ν = -0.3 to -0.5 (the range that the finite-element analysis of the full cladding, conducted in Abaqus/Explicit using the S4R shell elements to model the individual cell walls and the general-contact algorithm to model the self-contact of the collapsing cell walls, demonstrated produces the optimal balance between the densification (enhanced by the more negative ν) and the structural integrity (degraded by the excessive cell-wall buckling at the very negative ν, which can cause the premature shear-band formation — the localization of the crushing into the narrow band of cells, with the rest of the cladding remaining undeformed, a failure mode that reduces the effective SEA because only a fraction of the cladding volume participates in the energy absorption, a phenomenon that the finite-element parametric study identified at ν < -0.5, where the shear-band instability occurred at the compressive strain of approximately 15 to 20 percent — far below the 50 percent strain at which the homogeneous crushing absorbs the maximum energy — and that the optimization avoided by constraining the Poisson's ratio to ν ≥ -0.45, the constraint that the final, optimized geometry satisfies (ν ≈ -0.42 in the through-thickness direction, a value that the coupon-test measurements (using the digital-image correlation, DIC, to track the surface displacements during the compression test) confirmed to be -0.41 ± 0.03, within the acceptable range and providing the densification-enhanced SEA that the analytic model predicted.

The optimized re-entrant honeycomb parameters for the Atlantis cladding, determined through the multi-objective genetic algorithm (the NSGA-II, with the population size of 100 and the 200 generations, the same optimization framework that the laminate design (PART-043) employed, the design variables being h/l (the ratio of the vertical to the inclined wall length, ranging from 0.5 to 2.0), θ (ranging from 120° to 165°), t/l (ranging from 0.03 to 0.12), and the cell size (the distance between the adjacent vertical walls, s = 2l·cos(180° - θ), which determines the number of the cells per unit area and the overall density of the honeycomb, with s ranging from 8 to 25 mm based on the manufacturing constraints of the 3D printing process described below), and the objective functions being the SEA (to be maximized, computed from the area under the compressive stress-strain curve up to the 60 percent strain from the Abaqus/Explicit simulation of the unit-cell model under the periodic boundary conditions, which simulate the behavior of an infinite array of the identical cells and which eliminate the boundary effects that would distort the response of a finite-size specimen, a computational efficiency that the periodic-boundary-condition approach provides — the unit cell of only a few dozen elements captures the bulk response of the infinitely large honeycomb, reducing the simulation time from approximately 12 hours for the full, 150 mm × 150 mm × 150 mm specimen to approximately 3 minutes for the unit cell, a factor of 240 speedup that enabled the 20,000 simulations required for the genetic-algorithm optimization within the 5-day computational campaign on the Mojave CFD cluster) and the peak transmitted stress (to be minimized, the stress at the cladding-laminate interface that the laminate must withstand, computed from the Abaqus simulation as the reaction force at the fixed boundary — the laminate — divided by the contact area, a stress that the optimization targets to be below the 15 MPa (the laminate's compressive-fatigue endurance limit, ensuring that the repeated impacts — the pod may experience tens to hundreds of boulder strikes during the turbidite flow phase, although most will be the smaller, sub-0.1-meter fragments that the cavitation bubble curtain (PART-058) deflects, and only the largest boulders will penetrate the curtain and strike the cladding, the expected number of the cladding-impacting boulders per event being approximately 3 to 8, based on the CFD boulder-trajectory statistics (PART-041), and the cladding must survive all of them without the transmitted stress ever exceeding the fatigue limit — a multi-impact durability requirement that the optimization incorporated through the constraint that the transmitted stress must be below the 15 MPa fatigue limit for at least 10 consecutive impacts at the representative, 20 m/s, 38.2 kg impactor, a constraint that the optimized geometry satisfied, with the transmitted stress remaining below 12 MPa for all 10 simulated impacts in the final validation simulation, a 20 percent margin below the fatigue limit that provides the confidence in the cladding's multi-impact survivability). The optimized parameters are: h/l = 1.2 (the vertical walls are 20 percent longer than the inclined walls, providing the structural stability against the global buckling), θ = 152° (the re-entrant angle, corresponding to the Poisson's ratio of ν ≈ -0.42), t/l = 0.08 (the wall thickness of approximately 0.6 mm for the inclined-wall length of l = 7.5 mm, a thin wall that is at the lower limit of the 3D printer's resolution — the printer, the Stratasys F900, a fused-deposition-modeling (FDM) machine that extrudes the molten TPU filament through the 0.5 mm diameter nozzle and deposits the material layer-by-layer, the layer thickness of 0.25 mm, the minimum feature size being approximately 0.5 mm (twice the nozzle diameter for the unsupported walls, where the material must bridge the gap between the adjacent walls and the minimum bridge length for the 0.5 mm nozzle is approximately 2 to 3 mm, which the inclined walls' length of 7.5 mm comfortably exceeds, and the minimum wall thickness is approximately 0.5 mm — the nozzle diameter — below which the extruded bead is too narrow to form the continuous, load-bearing wall, and the optimized t/l of 0.08 corresponds to the wall thickness of t = 0.08 × 7.5 = 0.6 mm, just above the 0.5 mm minimum, the printability of the optimized geometry being a critical constraint that the optimization incorporated from the outset through the explicit, the inequality constraints on the geometric parameters that the additive-manufacturing engineer, DRIFT, provided based on the FDM process-capability study — a series of 50 test prints of the re-entrant honeycomb specimens with the varying wall thicknesses, the cell sizes, and the build orientations, the specimens being mechanically tested to confirm that the as-printed properties match the design properties, and the study establishing the printability envelope within which the optimized geometry must lie, a concurrent-engineering approach that the Atlantis cladding development exemplifies and that the program's quality-assurance plan documents as the formal "Design for Additive Manufacturing" (DfAM) process, a systematic methodology for the integration of the manufacturing constraints into the early design phase, before the detailed optimization, to avoid the costly redesign cycles that occur when the optimized design proves unmanufacturable — a lesson that the aerospace and the medical-device industries have learned through the painful experience with the early adoptions of the metal and the polymer additive manufacturing, and that the Atlantis program's DfAM process, informed by those industry lessons, has successfully implemented for the auxetic cladding's production, resulting in the first-article cladding panels that meet the design specifications within the dimensional tolerances of ±0.2 mm (the panel thickness) and ±0.1 mm (the cell-wall thickness), the tolerances that the laser profilometer inspection (PART-044) verified and that the ABS surveyor (PART-055) accepted as the compliant with the fabrication-quality requirements.

The 3D printing process parameters for the F900 production of the Atlantis cladding panels — each panel being a 1.0 m × 0.5 m × 0.15 m rectangular block, with the integrated, the frangible-fastener mounting tabs (the six titanium inserts that are co-printed into the panel during the build, the inserts being placed into the build chamber at the predetermined Z-height and the FDM process depositing the TPU around and over the inserts, encapsulating them within the honeycomb structure, a "print-in-place" assembly technique that eliminates the post-print bonding of the inserts (which would be a the weak, the adhesive-bonded joint that could detach under the impact loading), and that the DfAM process developed specifically for the Atlantis cladding) — are: the extruder temperature, 235°C ± 3°C (the TPU's melt-temperature range is 220 to 250°C, and the 235°C provides the optimal balance between the melt viscosity — low enough to flow through the 0.5 mm nozzle and to bond to the previously deposited layer, high enough to maintain the shape of the deposited bead without the excessive sagging — and the thermal degradation (the TPU begins to degrade at the temperatures above 260°C, and the 235°C is safely below this threshold, with the TGA data showing the less than 0.1 percent mass loss at 235°C over the 24-hour build duration of a single panel, a thermal stability that is essential for the large, the multi-hour builds where the material resides in the heated extruder for the extended period, and the Atlantis production process monitors the extruder temperature with the dual, the redundant thermocouples, and any deviation beyond the ±3°C band triggers the automatic build-pause and the extruder-temperature recalibration, a quality-control interlock that the DfAM process specification requires and that the F900's onboard control system implements through the closed-loop temperature control, a standard feature of the industrial FDM machines); the build-chamber temperature, 80°C ± 5°C (the elevated chamber temperature reduces the thermal gradients between the newly deposited layer and the previously deposited layers, minimizing the warping and the delamination that are the common defects in the large, the thick FDM parts, and the 80°C is the maximum chamber temperature that the F900 can maintain without the degradation of the machine's electronic components, a limitation that the Atlantis production team worked within, and the part's warping — measured as the deviation from the flatness of the bottom surface after the build is complete and the part has cooled to the room temperature — is less than 0.5 mm over the 1.0 m length, a flatness that the mounting system (the frangible fasteners) accommodates through the flexible, the compliant attachment to the hull, the fasteners having the axial compliance of approximately 0.5 mm/kN that absorbs the small, the residual warping without preloading the cladding panel or the hull laminate, a compliance that the fastener design (PART-064) provides and that is essential for the reliable, the rattle-free attachment of the replaceable panels); the build-plate adhesion, achieved by the polycarbonate (PC) build sheet with the TPU-compatible adhesive coating (the 3M 300LSE transfer tape, applied to the PC sheet before the build, and the first layer of the TPU bonds to the tape, anchoring the part to the build plate throughout the build, after which the tape is peeled from the part and the part is ready for the installation — the tape leaves no residue on the part, a cleanliness requirement that the Atlantis production process specifies to avoid the contamination of the cladding surface that would degrade the bonding of the MXene coating (PART-045) to the hull, even though the cladding does not receive the MXene coating (the cladding is sacrificial and is replaced, not recoated), the no-residue requirement is a general, the good-manufacturing-practice (GMP) standard that the Atlantis quality system applies to all the parts that enter the pod assembly area, preventing the spread of the contaminants that could affect the critical, the pressure-boundary components); and the build orientation, with the panel's through-thickness direction (the impact-loading direction) oriented vertically (the Z-direction) on the build plate, so that the deposited layers are perpendicular to the impact direction, maximizing the interlayer adhesion (the weakest mechanical property of the FDM parts is the interlayer tensile strength, which is typically 50 to 70 percent of the in-layer strength, and the loading direction that maximizes the strength is the one that aligns the load with the layers rather than pulling the layers apart — for the cladding panel, the impact load is the through-thickness compression, which presses the layers together, and the interlayer tensile weakness is irrelevant, a deliberate orientation selection that the DfAM analysis identified as the critical factor for the impact survivability: if the layers were oriented parallel to the impact direction (the panel printed flat on the build plate), the impact would tend to split the layers apart — the interlayer delamination — a failure mode that the vertical orientation prevents, the compressive load pressing the layers together and the auxetic honeycomb's cell walls buckling in the bending, not in the tension, a loading mode that the FDM parts withstand well because the bending strength is dominated by the in-layer properties, not by the interlayer adhesion, and the vertical-build orientation therefore maximizes the cladding's impact resistance — a DfAM insight that the Atlantis cladding team validated through the comparison tests of the vertically and the horizontally printed panels, with the vertically printed panels surviving the 10-impact sequence at the full design energy without the delamination, while the horizontally printed panels delaminated at the second or the third impact, a stark demonstration of the build-orientation criticality and a validation of the DfAM process's predictions, the test results being documented in the cladding qualification report that the ABS surveyor reviewed and approved as the part of the Atlantis certification (PART-055).
