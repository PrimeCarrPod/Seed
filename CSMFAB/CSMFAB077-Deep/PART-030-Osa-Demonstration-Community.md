# CSMFAB077-DEEP-030 — OSA DEMONSTRATION COMMUNITY: COMPLETE DEPLOYMENT PLAN, SURFACING PROTOCOL, EMERGENCY EVACUATION TO SURFACE

**Document ID:** CSMFAB077-DEEP-030 | **Parent:** CSMFAB000000000077 | **Version:** 2.0 | **Part:** 030 of 160 — Block 3 (FINAL)

The deployment of the Osa demonstration community follows a four-phase sequence, analogous to the Monterey sequence described in PART-020 but adapted to the specific logistical constraints of the Costa Rica site — the greater distance from the Mojave fabrication facility (approximately 5,500 kilometers by sea, versus 600 kilometers for Monterey, a distance that the Charlemagne fleet's heavy-lift vessel covers in approximately 12 days, at the cruising speed of 12 knots, with a refueling stop at the Panama Canal — a transit that the Atlantis logistics team has coordinated with the Panama Canal Authority, securing the reservation for the vessel's transit through the canal's Miraflores and Pedro Miguel locks, a transit that is essential because the Charlemagne vessel, with its 25-metric-ton deck crane and its 90-meter length, is too large to pass through the alternative — and highly uncertain — route around Cape Horn, a route that the Atlantis logistics team considered and rejected, because the weather in the Southern Ocean is unpredictable and the additional transit time — approximately 30 days — would delay the deployment schedule beyond the permitted window (the SETENA permit authorizes the deployment only during the dry season, from December through April, when the weather in the Golfo Dulce is calm and the sea state is conducive to the precise ROV operations), a window that the deployment sequence, with its one-pod-per-month pace, comfortably accommodates, provided that the first pod — OSA-01 — is deployed in early December, and the remaining three pods are deployed in January, February, and March, respectively, a schedule that the Atlantis deployment manager — a former U.S. Navy submariner with 20 years of experience in the coordination of complex underwater operations, including the salvage of the USS Guardian, a minesweeper that ran aground on the Tubbataha Reef in the Philippines in 2013, an operation that required the precise coordination of the salvage vessels, the ROVs, the divers, and the environmental response team, a coordination that the deployment manager — Commander (ret.) Evelyn Reyes — credits with teaching her the importance of meticulous planning, clear communication, and the ability to adapt to the unexpected, skills that she has applied to the Atlantis deployment plan and that the Atlantis program's management recognizes as essential for the successful execution of the Osa deployment, a execution that this PART-030, by describing the deployment sequence and the emergency protocols, enables.
