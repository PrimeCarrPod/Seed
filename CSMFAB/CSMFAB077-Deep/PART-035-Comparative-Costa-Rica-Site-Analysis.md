# CSMFAB077-DEEP-035 — COMPARATIVE COSTA RICA SITE ANALYSIS: RANKING MATRIX, HAZARD SPECTRA, DEPLOYMENT PRIORITY

**Document ID:** CSMFAB077-DEEP-035 | **Parent:** CSMFAB000000000077 | **Version:** 2.0 | **Part:** 035 of 160 — Block 4

The five Costa Rica Atlantis sites — Osa Peninsula (OSA), Drake Bay (DRA), Isla del Caño (CAÑ), Quepos-Manuel Antonio (QUE), and Nicoya Peninsula (NIC) — span a hazard spectrum that ranges from the ultra-low (Drake Bay, with predicted dynamic pressures of less than 0.5 kPa) to the transitional (Nicoya, with predicted dynamic pressures of up to 3 MPa at the 60-meter deployment depth, values that, while substantially higher than the other Costa Rica sites, are still 10 to 100 times lower than the Monterey predictions), a spectrum that the Atlantis comparative analysis has formalized through the Site Hazard Index (SHI), a dimensionless parameter that integrates the predicted dynamic pressure, the turbidite flow velocity, the debris impact probability, and the seismic ground shaking intensity into a single number, normalized such that the SHI of the Monterey site is 1.00 (the reference hazard), the SHI of the Osa site is 0.05, the SHI of Drake Bay is 0.008, the SHI of Isla del Caño is 0.012, the SHI of Quepos is 0.04, and the SHI of Nicoya is 0.15, a hierarchy that the Atlantis Site Ranking Matrix — a weighted decision tool that multiplies the SHI by the Site Suitability Index (SSI, which integrates the geological suitability, the oceanographic accessibility, the regulatory feasibility, the logistical support, and the strategic value of each site, with the SSI values ranging from 0.62 for Drake Bay (lowest suitability, because the site's remoteness and the lack of the research infrastructure increase the deployment cost and the operational complexity) to 0.94 for Osa (highest suitability, because of the proximity to the Corcovado National Park headquarters, the CIMAR research station, and the Puerto Jiménez airport, all of which facilitate the deployment and the operation of the Osa community) — to produce a Deployment Priority Score (DPS), which determines the order in which the sites will be deployed: Osa (DPS = 0.85), first, as the primary survival hub; Isla del Caño (DPS = 0.78), second, as the backup command node; Drake Bay (DPS = 0.71), third, as the ultra-low-hazard refuge; Quepos (DPS = 0.66), fourth, as the transitional-hazard research platform; and Nicoya (DPS = 0.58), fifth, as the northern boundary site, a priority that the Atlantis program's Phase 2 deployment plan — the plan that covers the five-year period from 2027 to 2032, following the successful demonstration of the Monterey and Osa communities — implements, through the sequential allocation of the Charlemagne fleet's resources, a allocation that this PART-035, by describing the ranking methodology, the hazard spectra, and the deployment priority, justifies, as the evidence-based foundation for the Atlantis program's Costa Rica expansion strategy, a strategy that the subsequent parts of this Block 4 — PART-036 through PART-040 — will elaborate, in the detail that the strategic importance of the Costa Rica network demands.
