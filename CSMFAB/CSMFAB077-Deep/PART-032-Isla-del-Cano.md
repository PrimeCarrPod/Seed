# CSMFAB077-DEEP-032 — ISLA DEL CAÑO: VOLCANIC SUBSTRATE ANCHORING, THE BREAKWATER EFFECT, CORAL ECOSYSTEM COMPATIBILITY

**Document ID:** CSMFAB077-DEEP-032 | **Parent:** CSMFAB000000000077 | **Version:** 2.0 | **Part:** 032 of 160 — Block 4

Isla del Caño, a small (approximately 3 square kilometers) volcanic island located approximately 15 kilometers west of the Osa Peninsula at 8°42'N, 83°53'W, rises abruptly from the continental shelf, its basaltic and andesitic rocks — the products of the Neogene volcanic activity that built the Central American volcanic arc, a chain of volcanoes that extends from Guatemala to Panama and that is the surface expression of the subduction of the Cocos Plate, a subduction that, at the latitude of Isla del Caño, occurs at a depth of approximately 100 to 150 kilometers, generating the magma that feeds the volcanoes and that, in the case of Isla del Caño, built a small shield volcano that has been extinct for at least 2 million years, its cone eroded by the waves and the tropical weathering into the low, forested hump that is visible from the mainland on clear days — forming a natural breakwater that shelters the seabed on the island's leeward (eastern) side, where the Atlantis deployment is planned, from the prevailing westerly swells and from the crustal displacement wave, a sheltering that the CFD simulations described in PART-027 have quantified: the island reduces the wave height at the deployment site, relative to the open-water wave at the same latitude, by a factor of approximately 2 to 4, a reduction that is additional to the pivot-axis attenuation that the Drake Bay site enjoys, making the Isla del Caño site, in terms of wave loading, essentially equivalent to an inland lake — the maximum predicted dynamic pressure at the pod's depth of 20 meters (the water depth on the island's leeward shelf, which slopes gently from the shoreline to a depth of approximately 50 meters at a distance of 2 kilometers, with the deployment site selected at the 20-meter isobath, where the seabed is a flat, sandy plain that the ROV surveys have confirmed is free of the coral heads that characterize the shallower parts of the shelf) is less than 0.1 kilopascals, a value that is smaller than the pressure fluctuations that are caused by the routine passage of the wind-driven surface waves, and that is, for the Atlantis pod, indistinguishable from zero, a condition that makes the Isla del Caño site the safest of all the Atlantis deployment sites, a safety that the Atlantis program has recognized by designating the Isla del Caño pod — designated CAÑ-01 — as the backup to the Osa hub, the pod that will house the Aegis C command-and-control backup system, a redundant copy of the Osa hub's S-Bus server, the FEATHER mesh router, and the satellite communication system, a backup that the Aegis C fault tree analysis has identified as necessary to achieve the overall network survival probability of greater than 0.999 that the Atlantis program has committed to, a survival probability that the Isla del Caño site, by its exceptional environmental benignity, supports.
