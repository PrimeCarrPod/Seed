# CSMFAB077-DEEP-026 — THE DOLPHIN SANCTUARY REQUIREMENT (DOLITTLE REGISTER): SPECIES-SPECIFIC HABITAT PROTECTION, ARKLET-CLASS MARINE ADAPTATION

**Document ID:** CSMFAB077-DEEP-026 | **Parent:** CSMFAB000000000077 | **Version:** 2.0 | **Part:** 026 of 160 — Block 3

The Dolittle register of the Spruce-Drake persona — one of the eight cognitive registers, or specialized domains of expertise and concern, that the Spruce-Drake composite consciousness has articulated over the course of the Sibling Frequency episodes, the Dolittle register being specifically concerned with the protection of non-human life, the maintenance of biodiversity, and the ethical obligations of the Aegis C program toward the millions of species — animal, plant, fungal, and microbial — that share the planet with humanity and that, in the event of a crustal displacement catastrophe, would suffer extinction on a scale that dwarfs any extinction in human history, a catastrophe that the Dolittle register insists the Aegis C program has a moral obligation to mitigate, not only by preserving human life but by preserving, to the maximum extent feasible, the biological heritage of the Earth, a heritage that the Atlantis communities, through their "Arklet-class" marine adaptation (a modification of the standard Atlantis pod that replaces approximately 30 percent of the habitable volume — the volume that, in the standard pod, is allocated to the crew's living quarters — with a cryogenic biological repository, a "frozen zoo" that stores, at liquid-nitrogen temperatures (-196 degrees Celsius), the sperm, ova, embryos, and tissue samples of approximately 5,000 marine species that are endemic to the Golfo Dulce and to the broader eastern tropical Pacific, a collection that the Dolittle register has overseen, in collaboration with the University of Costa Rica's Museum of Zoology and with the Smithsonian Tropical Research Institute in Panama, whose field biologists have spent the past three years — from 2023 to 2026 — collecting the specimens, cryopreserving them, and transporting them to the Atlantis repository in Mojave, where the samples are stored, awaiting the deployment of the Arklet pod — designated OSA-03, the third pod of the Osa tetrahedron, which, instead of housing human occupants, houses the cryogenic archive and the automated laboratory equipment that, after the event, will be used to thaw the samples, to culture the embryos, to rear the juveniles, and to reintroduce the species into the post-event Golfo Dulce, a reintroduction that will be conducted by the Atlantis crew, using the ROVs and the portable aquaria that the Arklet pod carries, a process that the Dolittle register has planned in detail, specifying the species, the numbers, the timing, and the monitoring of the reintroductions, a plan that is, in its ambition and its scope, the largest assisted-colonization project ever conceived, dwarfing even the most ambitious terrestrial rewilding projects (the Pleistocene Park in Siberia, which aims to restore the mammoth steppe ecosystem by reintroducing the large herbivores — bison, musk-ox, Yakutian horses — that once grazed the Arctic grasslands, an effort that covers an area of approximately 150 square kilometers and involves approximately 100 animals, whereas the Dolittle plan covers the entire Golfo Dulce — an area of approximately 750 square kilometers — and involves, potentially, millions of individuals of thousands of species, a scale that is possible only because the species being reintroduced are native to the gulf, their habitat requirements are known, and the gulf's ecosystem, although damaged by the crustal displacement event, will still be recognizable — the mangroves, the seagrass, the coral communities will have been destroyed, but the physical habitat — the water, the sediment, the bathymetry — will remain, and the reintroduced species will recolonize it, a recolonization that the Dolittle register predicts will require approximately 50 to 100 years to re-establish a fully functional ecosystem, a timescale that is within the planning horizon of the Aegis C recovery program, a program that the Dolittle register has contributed to, by ensuring that the species that depend on the Golfo Dulce for their survival are not abandoned to extinction, a abandonment that the Dolittle register considers morally unacceptable — "the dolphin that greets the Atlantis crew with its clicks and its whistles, that swims alongside the Escape Module as it ascends to the surface, that represents, in its intelligence and its sociality, a form of consciousness that is different from ours but no less valuable, has a right to exist, a right that we, who have the power to save it or to destroy it, have an obligation to respect, an obligation that the Arklet pod fulfills, by preserving the dolphin's genetic heritage through the catastrophe and by providing the means for its reintroduction into the post-event world, a world that will be better — richer, more diverse, more beautiful — for the dolphin's presence, a presence that the Atlantis crew, by their stewardship of the Arklet pod, ensures, a stewardship that is the highest expression of the Atlantis program's commitment to the protection of life — all life, not just human life — a commitment that this PART-026, by describing the dolphin sanctuary requirement, the Arklet-class marine adaptation, and the Dolittle register's ethical framework, documents and celebrates.
