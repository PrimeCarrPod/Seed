# CSMFAB077-DEEP-036 — COSTA RICA VS CALIFORNIA: COMPLETE GEOMORPHOLOGICAL DIVERGENCE ANALYSIS, WHY TWO 170M VS 3,200M REGIONS EXIST ADJACENT

**Document ID:** CSMFAB077-DEEP-036 | **Parent:** CSMFAB000000000077 | **Version:** 2.0 | **Part:** 036 of 160 — Block 4

The geomorphological divergence between Costa Rica and California — the fact that two coastlines, separated by only approximately 4,500 kilometers (the great-circle distance between Monterey and the Osa Peninsula) and both exposed to the same Pacific Ocean basin, can experience wave heights that differ by a factor of approximately 19 (3,200 meters versus 170 meters) during the same crustal displacement event — is a matter that the Atlantis geophysical analysis has investigated in detail, and that this PART-036, by synthesizing the findings of PART-008 (the Costa Rica inundation cap) and PART-009 (the California scouring), explains through the interaction of four factors, each of which contributes to the divergence, and the combination of which produces the observed factor of 19: first, the pivot-axis proximity (Costa Rica is near the rotation pole of the lithospheric displacement, where the displacement velocity of the ocean floor — the forcing that generates the wave — is minimal, whereas California is approximately 40 to 50 degrees of angular distance from the rotation pole, where the displacement velocity is near its maximum, a factor that, from the MITgcm simulations, accounts for approximately 60 percent of the divergence — a factor of approximately 11 in the wave height); second, the Coriolis modulation (the Coriolis parameter is smaller at Costa Rica's latitude — sin(8.5°) = 0.148 — than at California's — sin(37°) = 0.602 — a difference that reduces the trapping of wave energy at the coast and allows more of the incident energy to be radiated back into the ocean interior, a factor that accounts for approximately 20 percent of the divergence — a factor of approximately 2.5); third, the continental shelf bathymetry (California's narrow, steep shelf concentrates the wave energy through shoaling amplification, whereas Costa Rica's wider, more gently sloping shelf — the Golfo Dulce sill, the carbonate platform, the extensive mangrove-fringed coastline — dissipates the wave energy through bottom friction, a factor that accounts for approximately 15 percent of the divergence — a factor of approximately 1.5); and fourth, the tectonic setting (California's active transform margin, with the San Andreas Fault system, has produced a linear coastline that is oriented perpendicular to the predicted wave propagation direction, maximizing the wave's impact, whereas Costa Rica's convergent margin, with the subduction of the Cocos Plate and the accretion of the Central American isthmus, has produced a coastline that is oblique to the wave propagation, reducing the effective wave height by approximately 20 to 30 percent, a factor that accounts for the remaining 5 percent of the divergence), a decomposition that the Atlantis geophysical team has validated through the sensitivity analysis of the MITgcm simulations — turning each factor "on" and "off" by modifying the simulation parameters and observing the effect on the predicted wave height at each location, a sensitivity analysis that confirms the dominant role of the pivot-axis proximity and that provides the quantitative basis for the Atlantis site selection strategy, a strategy that, as this PART-036 demonstrates, is grounded in the fundamental physics of the Earth's rotation, the ocean's response, and the coastline's interaction, a grounding that gives the Atlantis program confidence that the divergence between Costa Rica and California is not a fluke of the geological record — a record that could be misinterpreted, or that could reflect a previous event that was unrepresentative of future events — but is, instead, a predictable, reproducible, and physical consequence of the geometry of the crustal displacement, a geometry that the Atlantis program has modeled, that the geological record has validated, and that the Atlantis communities exploit.
