# CSMFAB077-DEEP-033 — QUEPOS-MANUEL ANTONIO: TRANSITIONAL-HAZARD DEPLOYMENT, CANYON BATHYMETRY, NICOYA COMPLEX BASEMENT ROCK

**Document ID:** CSMFAB077-DEEP-033 | **Parent:** CSMFAB000000000077 | **Version:** 2.0 | **Part:** 033 of 160 — Block 4

The Quepos-Manuel Antonio site, located at 9°23'N, 84°10'W on the central Pacific coast of Costa Rica, approximately 120 kilometers northwest of the Osa Peninsula, represents a transitional hazard environment — one in which the wave parameters are intermediate between the ultra-low hazard of the Osa/Drake Bay sites and the higher hazards that characterize the Nicoya Peninsula (PART-034) and the California sites (Block 2), a transition that the Atlantis program intends to characterize, through the deployment of a single Atlantis pod — designated QUE-01 — at a depth of 35 meters on the continental shelf offshore of the Manuel Antonio National Park, a site that offers the combination of a competent anchoring substrate (the Nicoya Complex basement rock, a Cretaceous-to-Paleogene ophiolite sequence — a fragment of ancient oceanic crust that was obducted onto the continental margin during the accretion of the Central American isthmus, a complex that consists of serpentinized peridotite, gabbro, and basaltic pillow lavas, all of which have been metamorphosed to the greenschist facies, producing a suite of rocks that are, despite their great age — approximately 70 to 90 million years — remarkably strong and stable, with unconfined compressive strengths of 50 to 150 megapascals, as measured by the triaxial tests on the core samples that the Charlemagne survey vessel collected, strengths that exceed the Purisima Formation sandstone of Monterey by a factor of 3 to 5, and that provide an anchoring substrate that is essentially indestructible — the helical piles, when driven into the Nicoya Complex, encounter rock that is as strong as the steel of the piles themselves, a strength that provides pullout capacities of approximately 1,500 to 2,000 kilonewtons per pile, the highest of any Atlantis site, a capacity that the Atlantis anchoring team has verified through the pullout tests on the Nicoya Complex samples, and that gives the Quepos pod a factor of safety against pullout of greater than 10 — the highest factor of safety of any pod in the Atlantis program, a margin that the Aegis C safety analysis considers excessive (the target factor of safety is 5.0, and a factor of 10 represents an over-design that could be reduced, saving approximately $50,000 per pod in the pile steel and the installation cost), but that the Atlantis design team has decided to retain, because the Quepos site's function — to serve as a transitional-hazard research platform, collecting the data on the wave conditions that will inform the design of the future Atlantis pods at higher-hazard sites, data that will be more credible if the pod's own survival is virtually guaranteed (a failure of the Quepos pod, during a moderate event, would undermine confidence in the Atlantis architecture and would make it more difficult to obtain the funding and the permits for the higher-hazard deployments), and because the additional cost of the over-designed anchoring is a small fraction of the pod's total cost, a fraction that the ZIRCONIA analysis has assessed as acceptable, given the strategic value of the Quepos data, a value that the Aegis C program management has recognized and that the Atlantis resources have been allocated to support, through the inclusion of the Quepos pod in the Atlantis program's Phase 2 expansion plan, a plan that this PART-033 describes.
