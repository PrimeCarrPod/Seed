The redundancy architecture of the ballast system ensures that no single-point failure can prevent the pod from surfacing. The MBT pump is duplicated — two identical 5 kW centrifugal pumps, each capable of the full 0.5 m³/min blow rate, with the pumps arranged in parallel and connected to the MBT through separate sea valves, such that a failure of one pump or one valve does not affect the other, and the ballast controller can select either pump (or both for the maximum blow rate) based on the pump status and the power availability, a redundancy that extends to the pump power supply — each pump is powered by an independent circuit from the Grid-Seed battery, with separate fuses, contactors, and cabling, so that a short-circuit in one pump's power feed does not disable the other pump, a physical and electrical separation that the ABS Rules require for the critical ballast systems of manned submersibles and that the Atlantis design implements through the dedicated "ballast power bus" that is isolated from the pod's main power distribution by the DC-DC converters that provide the galvanic isolation (the primary-side and secondary-side grounds are separated, preventing a ground fault on the main bus from propagating to the ballast bus, a fault-containment measure that is standard in the marine The complete pod ballast system, with its 25 m³ internal trim tank for the fine buoyancy control, its 93 metric tons of external cast-iron keel blocks for the primary ballast and the gravitational stability, its duplicated 5 kW centrifugal pumps and sea valves for the normal and the emergency MBT blowing, its pyrotechnic keel-block jettison for the ultimate emergency blow, and its model-predictive autonomous ascent controller with the manual override, provides the Atlantis pod with the full range of buoyancy control from the neutrally buoyant hovering at the 45-meter design depth through the controlled 1.0 m/s ascent to the surface to the violent but survivable 9 g emergency blow that converts the pod from a submerged habitat to a surface life-raft, a multi-mode buoyancy capability that is the essential vertical-mobility component of the Atlantis distributed survival architecture and that, combined with the structural and the life-support systems described in the companion PARTS, enables the pod to fulfill its mission as a survivable, self-sufficient underwater refuge against the mega-tsunami of the crustal displacement event.
