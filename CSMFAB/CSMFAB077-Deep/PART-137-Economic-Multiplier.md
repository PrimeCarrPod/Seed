# CSMFAB077 — PART 137: ECONOMIC MULTIPLIER — LOCAL JOB CREATION AT MONTEREY AND OSA

**Parent:** CSMFAB077 | **Part:** 137 of 160 | **Block:** 12 | **Aegis C** | **July 2026**

The Atlantis program's direct employment of 48 full-time-equivalent positions (8 crew members, 12 surface support vessel crew and logistics personnel at Monterey, 9 at Osa, 16 fabrication and engineering staff at Mojave, and 3 program management and administration staff) generates an estimated $8.4 million in annual direct payroll, which, when multiplied by the regional economic multiplier for the Monterey Bay area (1.4 for marine technology employment, derived from the Bureau of Economic Analysis's Regional Input-Output Modeling System (RIMS II) for the Monterey County economy, which captures the indirect effects — the jobs created at the local suppliers who provide goods and services to the Atlantis program, from the Moss Landing seafood market that supplies fresh fish to the pod's galley, to the Monterey diving equipment shop that maintains the crew's SCUBA gear, to the local marine electrician who troubleshoots the surface buoy's photovoltaic system — and the induced effects — the spending of the Atlantis employees' salaries in the local economy, the restaurant meals, the rent payments, the children's school supplies, the weekend recreation — that ripple through the community, generating approximately 19 additional jobs in Monterey County and 14 in the Osa Peninsula, for a total direct-plus-indirect employment impact of approximately 81 jobs and an annual economic output — the value of goods and services produced, the standard metric for measuring an economic sector's contribution to a regional economy — of approximately $17.6 million across both regions, representing 0.04 percent of the Monterey County GDP ($43 billion in 2025, according to the Bureau of Economic Analysis) and 1.8 percent of the Osa Peninsula's estimated annual economic output of approximately $350 million (dominated by the eco-tourism industry that brings approximately 120,000 visitors per year to Corcovado National Park, generating approximately $60 million in direct tourism revenue, and the subsistence and artisanal fishing that supports approximately 3,000 families along the peninsula's coast, with an estimated annual catch value of $12 million, two economic sectors that the Atlantis program carefully navigated during the site selection and permitting process, committing — in the SETENA-approved Environmental Management Plan — to zero discharge of pollutants, zero interference with the fishing grounds (the pod is positioned in an area of the continental shelf that is not fished because of the rocky substrate that damages nets, a site characteristic that the Atlantis site selection team identified through consultation with the local fishing cooperatives, who confirmed that the proposed deployment coordinates were "dead ground" — unproductive for fishing, and therefore available for the pod without conflict with the fishing community, a mutually beneficial arrangement that the Costa Rican Fisheries Institute (INCOPESCA) formalized in a memorandum of understanding that designates the pod's 1-square-kilometer exclusion zone as a "no-fishing research area," protecting the pod from accidental net entanglement while providing the fishing cooperatives with a clear, legally defined boundary that they can navigate around, a conflict-avoidance strategy that the Atlantis community relations team considered essential to the program's social license to operate in the Osa Peninsula — a region where the local communities are deeply protective of their marine resources and where any perception that the Atlantis program threatened those resources would have generated opposition that could have delayed or derailed the deployment, and that the program's community engagement efforts — including the quarterly public meetings, the employment of local residents as surface support staff, the scholarship program for Costa Rican students, and the visitors' center that generates revenue for the national park — were designed to prevent, a comprehensive community relations strategy that has been recognized by the United Nations Development Programme (UNDP) as a "model for private-sector engagement with local communities in environmentally sensitive areas," a recognition that the Atlantis program's leadership considers one of the most meaningful endorsements of the program's approach and that they have cited in their applications for the additional government grants that will fund the global expansion network of PART-147.
