For a surface structure — such as the CSMFAB076 geodesic dome — the full dynamic pressure of the wave front, plus the subsequent hydrostatic pressure of the stalled water column, must be resisted by the structure's physical and electromagnetic defenses. For a submerged structure at depth D below the mean sea surface, the additional hydrostatic pressure contributed by the wave is attenuated according to the relationship P(z) = ρg(H·e^(-kz) + z) where k = 2π/λ is the wave number and λ is the wavelength, which for a megatsunami of this scale is on the order of hundreds of kilometers, meaning that k is extremely small and the exponential attenuation term e^(-kz) approaches unity for depths relevant to continental shelf installations — however, and this is the critical distinction, the DYNAMIC pressure component, the component that destroys structures through impact rather than through sustained compression, is attenuated far more rapidly with depth because it depends on the orbital velocity of water particles, which decreases exponentially with depth according to the same wave number k, such that at a depth of 45 meters — the design depth for the Monterey demonstration site — the orbital velocity of water particles during a 3,200-meter wave passage is reduced by approximately 85 to 95 percent compared to surface particle velocities, depending on the wave period, which for a crustal displacement event is estimated at several hours based on the Thomas model of a near-instantaneous 90-degree crustal shift occurring within a quarter to half a day. The submerged structure therefore experiences primarily the hydrostatic pressure increase of the additional water column — approximately 320 atmospheres at the surface, but only the existing depth pressure plus the weight of the additional 3,200 meters, which at 45 meters depth totals approximately 326.5 atmospheres — a pressure that the lonsdaleite-BFRP composite, with its documented compressive strength exceeding 1.2 gigapascals and its successful performance at equivalent hydrostatic pressures during the deep-water testing phase of the Aegis C materials qualification program, can withstand with a safety factor exceeding 3.5.
