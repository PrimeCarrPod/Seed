## 7.1 Karst Geomorphology: The Yucatán Platform as a Clay Trap

### 7.1.1 Tectono-Stratigraphic Framework

The Yucatán Peninsula constitutes the emergent portion of the **Yucatán Platform**, a stable carbonate platform that has persisted since the Jurassic. Its geological simplicity belies pedological complexity:

**Stratigraphic column (simplified):**
| Unit | Age | Thickness | Lithology | Karst Relevance |
|------|-----|-----------|-----------|-----------------|
| **Unconformity** | — | — | — | Major dissolution surface |
| **Paleogene** | Paleocene–Eocene | 50–150 m | Limestone, dolomite | High porosity, early karst |
| **Cretaceous (Upper)** | Campanian–Maastrichtian | 200–400 m | Limestone, chalk, marl | **Primary aquifer, main karst** |
| **Cretaceous (Lower)** | Albian–Cenomanian | 300–500 m | Limestone, evaporites | Deep aquifer, limited karst |
| **Jurassic** | — | >1000 m | Dolomite, anhydrite | Basement, hydrocarbon reservoir |

**Structural setting:**
- **Dip:** 0.5–1° northward (regional)
- **Fractures:** NW-SE (tectonic), NE-SW (stress relief)
- **Faults:** Few, low displacement (<50 m)
- **Uplift:** 0.05–0.1 mm/yr (isostatic + eustatic)

### 7.1.2 Karst Landform Assemblage

**Surface forms (from air/LiDAR):**
| Landform | Maya Name | Density | Clay Association |
|----------|-----------|---------|------------------|
| **Cenote** | Dzonot | 1–5/km² | Clay-lined walls, organic sediment |
| **Rejollada** | Rejollada | 5–20/km² | Deep clay fill (2–10 m) |
| **Doline** | Tzek'el | 20–100/km² | Thin soil, epikarst clay |
| **Polje** | Kax | <1/km² | Alluvial clay, seasonal wetland |
| **Karren** | — | Ubiquitous | Epikarst clay veneer |

**Subsurface forms (from cave surveys):**
- **Cave passages:** >1,000 km mapped (Ox Bel Ha: 270 km, Sac Actun: 380 km)
- **Phreatic tubes:** Horizontal, clay-floored
- **Vadose shafts:** Vertical, clay-rimmed
- **Breakdown chambers:** Clay-sealed collapse zones

### 7.1.3 Epikarst: The Critical Clay-Limestone Interface

**Definition:** The weathered, fractured upper zone of limestone (1–10 m thick) where:
- **Porosity:** 15–35% (fractures + dissolution pores)
- **Permeability:** 10⁻⁶–10⁻³ m/s (anisotropic)
- **Clay content:** 5–30% (infill + residue)

**Epikarst clay mineralogy:**
| Clay Mineral | % of <2 μm | Origin |
|--------------|------------|--------|
| Kaolinite | 40–60% | Insoluble residue |
| Smectite | 20–30% | Volcanic ash alteration |
| Illite | 10–20% | Detrital + authigenic |
| Palygorskite | 5–15% | Mg-rich microenvironment |
| Gibbsite | 0–5% | Al-residue (rare) |

**Hydrologic function:**
- **Storage:** 100–500 mm water in epikarst
- **Recharge:** 20–40% of rainfall → rapid infiltration
- **Filtration:** Clay removes pathogens, nutrients, metals

## 7.2 Limestone Dissolution Kinetics: Carbonate Weathering Engine

### 7.2.1 Dissolution Thermodynamics

**Calcite dissolution reaction:**
$$\text{CaCO}_3 + \text{H}_2\text{CO}_3 \rightleftharpoons \text{Ca}^{2+} + 2\text{HCO}_3^-$$

**Equilibrium constant (25°C):**
$$\log K = 1.85 \quad (\text{for } \text{CaCO}_3 + \text{CO}_2 + \text{H}_2\text{O})$$

**CO₂-driven dissolution:**
$$[\text{Ca}^{2+}]_{\text{eq}} = \sqrt{K \cdot P_{\text{CO}_2} \cdot K_{\text{H}} \cdot K_1 / [\text{H}^+]}$$

At $P_{\text{CO}_2} = 10^{-2.5}$ atm (soil air), pH 5.5:
$$[\text{Ca}^{2+}]_{\text{eq}} \approx 1.2\ \text{mM} \quad (48\ \text{mg/L})$$

### 7.2.2 Kinetic Rate Law

**Surface-controlled rate (Plummer et al., 1978):**
$$R = k_1 [\text{H}^+] + k_2 [\text{H}_2\text{CO}_3] + k_3 [\text{H}_2\text{O}]$$

At pH < 5: $R \approx k_1 [\text{H}^+]$
At pH 5–7: $R \approx k_2 [\text{H}_2\text{CO}_3]$
At pH > 7: $R \approx k_3$

**In Yucatán soil zone:**
- $P_{\text{CO}_2} = 10^{-2.0}$ to $10^{-1.5}$ atm (high root respiration)
- pH = 5.5–6.5 (buffered by carbonate)
- **Dominant term:** $k_2 [\text{H}_2\text{CO}_3]$

**Rate constants (25°C):**
- $k_1 = 0.15\ \text{mol/m}^2/\text{s}$
- $k_2 = 2.5 \times 10^{-4}\ \text{mol/m}^2/\text{s}$
- $k_3 = 10^{-6}\ \text{mol/m}^2/\text{s}$

**Calculated dissolution flux:**
$$F = R \times A_s \approx 10^{-9}\ \text{mol/m}^2/\text{s} \times 10^4\ \text{m}^2/\text{m}^3 = 10^{-5}\ \text{mol/m}^3/\text{s}$$

**Annual limestone loss:** ~0.05–0.1 mm/yr (surface), up to 1 mm/yr in fractures

### 7.2.3 Organic Acid Enhancement: Beyond Carbonic Acid

**Organic acids (oxalic, humic) increase dissolution 3–10×:**
$$R_{\text{total}} = R_{\text{H}_2\text{CO}_3} + \sum k_{\text{OA}} [\text{OA}]$$

**Oxalic acid mechanism (from Document 2):**
1. **Proton donation:** Lowers pH at mineral surface
2. **Chelation:** $\text{Ca}^{2+} + \text{C}_2\text{O}_4^{2-} \rightarrow \text{CaC}_2\text{O}_4$ (precipitate)
3. **Surface complexation:** Weakens Ca-O bonds

**Net effect:** Dissolution rate $\propto [\text{oxalate}]^{0.5}$ at low concentrations

### 7.2.4 Insoluble Residue Accumulation: The Clay Factory

**Mass balance for 1 m³ limestone dissolution:**
| Component | Initial (kg) | Dissolved (kg) | Residual (kg) | Fate |
|-----------|--------------|----------------|---------------|------|
| CaCO₃ | 2,710 | 2,700 | 10 | Ca²⁺ + 2HCO₃⁻ (groundwater) |
| Insoluble residue | 10–50 | 0 | 10–50 | **Clay + Fe-oxides** |
| Organics | 0.1–1 | 0 | 0.1–1 | Humus |
| **Total** | **2,720** | **2,700** | **20–60** | — |

**Clay accumulation rate:**
$$\frac{d(\text{Clay})}{dt} = \frac{\text{Residue%} \times \text{Dissolution rate}}{\rho_{\text{clay}}}$$

For 2% residue, 0.05 mm/yr dissolution:
$$\text{Clay accumulation} \approx 0.001\ \text{mm/yr} = 1\ \text{m/Myr}$$

**But:** Focused in fractures/pockets → local rates 100–1000× higher

## 7.3 Terra Rossa and Rendzinas: The Clay-Limestone Endmembers

### 7.3.1 Rendzina (Leptosol): Thin, Carbonate-Rich

**Genesis:** Direct weathering of hard limestone, minimal residue accumulation
**Profile:**
- **A:** 0–15 cm, 15–25% clay, 5–10% OC, pH 7–8, 100% base sat
- **AC:** 15–30 cm, limestone fragments, clay coatings
- **R:** Hard limestone

**Clay mineralogy:** Smectite > Illite > Kaolinite (ash-derived)
**CEC:** 25–40 cmol₊/kg (high — organic + smectite)
**Agriculture:** Shallow-rooted crops (corn, beans), drought-prone

### 7.3.2 Terra Rossa (Rhodic Ferralsol/Luvisol): Deep, Clay-Rich

**Genesis:** Insoluble residue + allochthonous input + in situ weathering
**Profile (deep variant):**
- **A:** 0–20 cm, 25% clay, 2–3% OC, pH 5.5–6.5
- **Bt:** 50–300+ cm, 50–70% clay, kaolinite + hematite + gibbsite
- **BC:** Saprolitic limestone with clay infill

**Clay mineralogy:** Kaolinite (50–60%) + Gibbsite (5–15%) + Hematite (10–20%)
**Color:** 10R–2.5YR (dark red) — hematite dominance
**CEC:** 10–20 cmol₊/kg (low-activity clays)
**Agriculture:** Deep-rooted perennials (cacao, citrus, timber)

### 7.3.3 Transition Zone: The "Red Soil Belt"

**Location:** 18.5°–20°N (Chetumal to Valladolid)
**Width:** 50–100 km
**Gradient:** Rendzina → Terra Rossa over 50 km
**Controls:**
- Rainfall: 900 → 1,400 mm/yr
- Dry season: 6 → 2 months
- Limestone purity: High → Lower (more residue)

## 7.4 Clay-Filled Karst Pockets: Geometry, Hydrology, and Maya Agriculture

### 7.4.1 Pocket Formation: Process-Based Model

**Coupled dissolution-infill equations:**

**Dissolution (fracture widening):**
$$\frac{dw}{dt} = \frac{2 M_{\text{CaCO}_3}}{\rho_{\text{rock}}} R(C_{\text{eq}} - C) \approx 10^{-5}\ \text{m/yr}$$

**Infill (clay settling):**
$$\frac{dh_{\text{clay}}}{dt} = \frac{C_{\text{sed}} \times v_{\text{settle}}}{\phi_{\text{clay}}} \approx 10^{-4}\ \text{m/yr}$$

Where $C_{\text{sed}}$ = suspended sediment concentration (g/L), $v_{\text{settle}}$ = settling velocity

**Steady-state pocket geometry:**
$$\frac{dw}{dt} \approx \frac{dh_{\text{clay}}}{dt} \times \tan\theta$$

### 7.4.2 Pocket Geometry Statistics (From 500 Boreholes + GPR)

| Parameter | Distribution | Mean | Median | 90th %ile |
|-----------|-------------|------|--------|-----------|
| **Depth (m)** | Lognormal | 2.8 | 2.2 | 5.5 |
| **Diameter (m)** | Power law (α=1.8) | 4.2 | 3.0 | 10.2 |
| **Aspect ratio (D/d)** | Uniform | 2.5 | 2.0 | 4.5 |
| **Clay fill (%)** | Beta(3,2) | 65% | 68% | 90% |
| **Volume (m³)** | Lognormal | 120 | 45 | 450 |

**Spatial clustering:** Pockets align with fracture corridors (NW-SE)
**Density:** 5–25 pockets/ha (higher in recharge zones)

### 7.4.3 Hydraulic Properties of Pocket Clay

| Property | Pocket Clay | Surface Soil | Host Limestone |
|----------|-------------|--------------|----------------|
| **Porosity** | 0.40–0.50 | 0.50–0.60 | 0.01–0.15 (fractures) |

| **θ_FC (m³/m³)** | 0.40–0.48 | 0.30–0.38 | — |

**Function:** 
- **Perched aquifers** above clay seals
- **Slow release** to deep aquifer
- **Root access** during dry season
