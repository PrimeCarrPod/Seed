## 6.10 Carbon Sequestration Potential: Deep Tropical Clay Profiles as C Sinks

### 6.10.1 Stabilization Mechanisms in Oxisols

**Physical protection:** Microaggregates (pseudosilt) occlude organic matter
- **Occluded C:** 40–60% of total OC in Bt horizons
- **Mean residence time:** 10³–10⁴ years (¹⁴C dating)

**Chemical protection:** 
- **Fe/Al-organic complexes:** Ligand exchange on oxide surfaces
- **Kaolinite intercalation:** Limited (1:1 layer, low expansion)
- **Polyphenol-Fe complexes:** Recalcitrant, high molecular weight

**Biochemical recalcitrance:**
- **Lignin-derived:** Aromatic, resistant
- **Black carbon (charcoal):** Pyrogenic, >1000 yr MRT
- **Microbial necromass:** Amino sugars (muramic acid, glucosamine)

### 6.10.2 Carbon Depth Distribution Model

**Two-pool exponential model:**
$$C(z) = C_{\text{fast}} e^{-z/z_f} + C_{\text{slow}} e^{-z/z_s}$$

**Parameters (Los Santos Oxisol):**
| Pool | C₀ (kg C/m³) | z* (cm) | MRT (yr) | % Total |
|------|--------------|---------|----------|---------|
| Fast (O + A) | 25 | 15 | 10–50 | 15% |
| Slow (Bt) | 3.5 | 120 | 1,000–5,000 | 85% |

**Total C stock (0–300 cm):** 18.2 kg C/m² = 182 t C/ha

### 6.10.3 ¹⁴C Depth Profiles: Chronology of Stabilization

| Depth (cm) | ¹⁴C Age (yr BP) | Δ¹⁴C (‰) | Interpretation |
|------------|-----------------|----------|----------------|
| 0–10 | Modern | +50 | Active cycling |
| 30 | 250 | -30 | Decadal turnover |
| 80 | 1,200 | -150 | Centennial |
| 150 | 3,800 | -350 | Millennial |
| 250 | 8,500 | -650 | Early Holocene |
| 400 | 18,000 | -880 | LGM / Pleistocene |

**Inference:** Deep C is **not** primarily from downward transport
- ¹⁴C ages > soil formation age (pedogenesis ~100 ka)
- Suggests **in situ stabilization** of ancient C, or
- **Physical mixing** (bioturbation, cryoturbation absent) minimal

**Preferred mechanism:** Microaggregate occlusion protects C for millennia

### 6.10.4 Climate Change Vulnerability

**Warming scenario (+3°C by 2100):**
- Q₁₀ for decomposition ≈ 2
- Fast pool loss: 30–50% (decadal)
- Slow pool loss: 5–15% (centennial) — **protected**
- **But:** Drying → microaggregate breakdown → slow pool exposed

**Net projection (CMIP6 ensemble):**
- Tropical Oxisol C loss: 10–25 Pg C by 2100 (RCP 8.5)
- **Positive feedback:** 0.05–0.15 °C additional warming

## 6.11 Yucatán Peninsula: Abrupt Geomorphic Transition to Karst

### 6.11.1 Tectono-Stratigraphic Setting

**Yucatán Platform:** Passive margin, carbonate platform since Jurassic
**Lithology:** Cretaceous–Paleogene limestone (200–500 m thick)
- **Upper units:** Permeable, fossiliferous, karstified
- **Lower units:** Less permeable, dolomitized
**Structure:** Gentle northward dip (0.5–1°), regional fractures NW-SE

### 6.11.2 Climate Gradient: South (Wet) → North (Dry)

| Location | Latitude | MAP (mm) | Dry Months | Vegetation | Soil Order |
|----------|----------|----------|------------|------------|------------|
| **Chetumal (S)** | 18.5°N | 1,400 | 2–3 | Tropical moist forest | Oxisol/Ultisol |
| **Cancún (NE)** | 21.2°N | 1,100 | 4–5 | Tropical dry forest | Rendzic Leptosol |
| **Mérida (NW)** | 20.9°N | 900 | 5–6 | Tropical dry forest / savanna | Rendzic Leptosol |
| **Progreso (N)** | 21.3°N | 700 | 6–7 | Thorn scrub | Calcisol/Leptosol |

**Karst development increases northward** (longer dry season → more aggressive dissolution)

### 6.11.3 Soil-Limestone Interface: The "Epikarst" Zone

**Epikarst:** Weathered, fractured limestone immediately below soil
- **Thickness:** 1–10 m
- **Porosity:** 10–30% (fractures + dissolution cavities)
- **Hydrology:** Rapid infiltration, lateral flow on fracture networks

**Soil-epikarst boundary:** Sharp (cm-scale)
- Soil: Reddish clay (Terra Rossa)
- Epikarst: White/gray limestone with red clay infill

## 6.12 Rendzinas and Terra Rossa: Thin Soils on Limestone

### 6.12.1 Rendzina (Leptosol) Profile: Northern Yucatán

**Location:** Near Mérida, 15 m elevation, MAP 900 mm
**Classification:** Rendzic Leptosol (WRB) / Lithic Rendoll (USDA)
**Parent material:** Hard Cretaceous limestone

**Profile:**
| Horizon | Depth (cm) | Description | Clay % | Color | Notes |
|---------|------------|-------------|--------|-------|-------|
| **A** | 0–15 | Humic, granular, high OM | 15–20 | 10YR 3/2 | 4% OC, pH 7.2 |
| **AC** | 15–30 | Transition, limestone fragments | 10–15 | 10YR 5/3 | 40% rock fragments |
| **R** | 30+ | Hard limestone | — | — | Fractured, clay-lined |

**Key properties:**
- **Base saturation:** 100% (carbonate buffered)
- **pH:** 7.5–8.2
- **CEC:** 25–35 cmol₊/kg (high for thin soil — organic + clay)
- **Effective rooting depth:** 10–30 cm (limited by rock)

### 6.12.2 Terra Rossa Profile: Southern Yucatán

**Location:** Chetumal area, 20 m elevation, MAP 1,400 mm
**Classification:** Rhodic Ferralsol (if deep) / Chromic Luvisol (if argillic)
**Genesis:** Insoluble residue accumulation + allochthonous input

**Profile (deep variant, >1 m soil):**
| Horizon | Depth (cm) | Clay % | Color | Minerals | Notes |
|---------|------------|--------|-------|----------|-------|
| A | 0–20 | 25 | 5YR 3/3 | Kaol, Gibbs, Goeth | 3% OC |
| BA | 20–50 | 35 | 5YR 4/6 | Kaol, Gibbs | Clay films |
| Bt1 | 50–120 | 55 | 2.5YR 3/6 | Kaol, Gibbs, Hem | Thick cutans |
| Bt2 | 120–250+ | 65 | 10R 3/6 | Kaol, Gibbs, Hem | 3 m+ deep |

**Differences from Costa Rican Oxisols:**
- Higher pH (5.5–6.5 vs 4.5–5.0)
- Less gibbsite (carbonate buffers Al)
- More hematite (oxidizing, well-drained)
- **Lower OC at depth** (no deep roots in limestone)

## 6.13 Karst Pocket Geometry: Fracture-Controlled Clay Trap Volumetrics

### 6.13.1 Pocket Formation Mechanism

**Process:**
1. **Fracture initiation:** Tectonic (regional NW-SE) + stress relief
2. **Dissolution widening:** CO₂-charged water → Ca²⁺ + 2HCO₃⁻
3. **Sediment infill:** Surface clay washes in + in situ residue
4. **Pocket growth:** Positive feedback (more clay → more water retention → more dissolution)

### 6.13.2 Pocket Geometry Statistics (From GPR and Drilling)

**Survey:** 500 m × 500 m grid, 100 MHz GPR + 50 boreholes

| Parameter | Mean | SD | Range | Distribution |
|-----------|------|-----|-------|--------------|
| **Pocket depth** | 2.8 m | 1.5 m | 0.5–8.0 m | Lognormal |
| **Pocket diameter** | 4.2 m | 2.8 m | 1.0–15 m | Power law |
| **Clay fill %** | 65% | 15% | 30–95% | Beta |
| **Pocket density** | 12/ha | 5/ha | 3–25/ha | Poisson |

**Volume of clay in pockets (per ha):**
$$V_{\text{clay}} = \sum_{i=1}^{N} \frac{\pi}{4} D_i^2 d_i f_{\text{clay},i}$$

Typical: **2,000–5,000 m³/ha** of clay stored in pockets

### 6.13.3 Hydraulic Significance: Clay Seals vs Conduits

**Clay-filled pockets as aquitards:**
- Vertical K: 10⁻⁸–10⁻⁶ m/s (clay) vs 10⁻³ m/s (karst conduit)
- **Pockets compartmentalize aquifer** → perched water tables

**But:** If pockets connect vertically → **rapid recharge pathways**
- **Critical threshold:** Clay fill < 40% → preferential flow
- **Management implication:** Protect clay-rich pockets for groundwater quality

## 6.14 Surface vs Pocket Clay Concentration: 3× Enrichment Factor Derivation

### 6.14.1 Mass Balance: Clay Accumulation in Pockets

**Surface soil (15 cm thick, 17% clay):**
$$\text{Clay}_{\text{surf}} = 0.15 \times 0.17 = 0.0255\ \text{m} = 25.5\ \text{mm}$$

**Pocket (2 m deep, 50% clay):**
$$\text{Clay}_{\text{pocket}} = 2.0 \times 0.50 = 1.0\ \text{m} = 1000\ \text{mm}$$

**Enrichment factor:**
$$EF = \frac{\text{Clay}_{\text{pocket}} / d_{\text{pocket}}}{\text{Clay}_{\text{surf}} / d_{\text{surf}}} = \frac{0.50}{0.17} = 2.94 \approx \mathbf{3\times}$$

### 6.14.2 Porosity Reduction: 10% Decrease → Hydraulic Impact

**Surface soil porosity:** 0.55 (17% clay, granular)
**Pocket clay porosity:** 0.45 (50% clay, massive)

**Kozeny-Carman equation:**
$$K = \frac{\rho g}{\mu} \frac{\phi^3}{S^2 (1-\phi)^2}$$

Where $S$ = specific surface area (m²/m³)

**For clay soils:** $S \propto \text{clay%} \times \text{SSA}_{\text{clay}}$

**Hydraulic conductivity ratio:**
$$\frac{K_{\text{pocket}}}{K_{\text{surf}}} = \left(\frac{0.45}{0.55}\right)^3 \left(\frac{1-0.55}{1-0.45}\right)^2 \left(\frac{17 \times \text{SSA}}{50 \times \text{SSA}}\right)^2 \approx 0.1$$

**Pocket clay has ~10× lower K** than surface soil

### 6.14.3 Water Retention Enhancement

**Volumetric water content (θ) at field capacity (FC = -33 kPa):**
- Surface (17% clay): θ_FC ≈ 0.30 m³/m³
- Pocket (50% clay): θ_FC ≈ 0.45 m³/m³

**Available water (FC - WP) in 2 m pocket vs 0.15 m surface:**
- Pocket: $(0.45 - 0.25) \times 2000 = 400\ \text{mm}$
- Surface: $(0.30 - 0.15) \times 150 = 22.5\ \text{mm}$

**Pocket stores 18× more available water** per unit area!

### 6.15.4 Container Gardening: Ancient Maya "Hydroponics" in Pockets

**Practice:** Maya cultivated in karst pockets (chultunes, rejolladas)
**Soil mixture:** Pocket clay + organic waste + ash + charcoal
**Dimensions:** 1–3 m diameter, 1–4 m deep
**Crops:** Maize, beans, squash, chaya, cacao

**Modern analysis of ancient pocket fills:**
- **OC:** 5–12% (vs 1–3% natural)
- **P (Mehlich-3):** 50–200 ppm (vs 5–20 ppm natural)
- **Charcoal:** 10–30% (biochar amendment)
- **pH:** 6.0–7.0 (vs 5.0–5.5 natural)

**Yield estimate:** 2–3× natural soil yields
**Sustainability:** Continuous for 500+ years (radiocarbon dates)
