### 11.1 Introduction: Plate Tectonics at Different Latitudes

The two comparative nodes sit on fundamentally different plate boundaries. San Jose, Costa Rica lies near the **Cocos-Caribbean subduction zone** - a convergent boundary where dense oceanic crust dives beneath lighter continental crust. San Jose, California sits on the **San Andreas transform boundary** - a strike-slip fault where plates slide past each other horizontally.

This contrast represents the two end-members of plate boundary types, with profoundly different seismic hazard, crustal deformation, and geodynamic implications.

**Comparative Node Parameters:**

| Parameter | San Jose, CR (Node A) | San Jose, CA (Node B) |
|-----------|----------------------|----------------------|
| Plate Boundary | Cocos-Caribbean Subduction | San Andreas Transform |
| Convergence/Slip Rate | 83-89 mm/yr convergence | 35-50 mm/yr strike-slip |
| Boundary Type | Convergent (subduction) | Transform (strike-slip) |
| Seismogenic Depth | 0-50 km (interface), 50-200 km (slab) | 0-15 km (crustal) |
| Maximum Credible Mw | 9.0+ (megathrust) | 8.0-8.3 (strike-slip) |
| Recurrence (M>7) | ~50-100 yr (interface) | ~150-300 yr (major segments) |
| Dominant Stress Regime | Compressional (thrust) | Strike-slip (shear) |

### 11.2 Cocos-Caribbean Subduction Zone (CR Node)

#### 11.2.1 Plate Geometry and Kinematics

**Cocos Plate:** Young oceanic plate (0-25 Ma), formed at East Pacific Rise and Cocos-Nazca spreading center.

**Caribbean Plate:** Complex plate with oceanic and continental components.

**Convergence vector at CR trench:**
- Rate: 83-89 mm/yr
- Azimuth: ~35° (NE-directed)
- Obliquity: ~20° (partitioned into trench-normal and trench-parallel)

**Slab geometry:**
- Trench depth: ~2,700 m
- Slab dip: ~60° (steep) at 100 km depth, shallows to ~30° at 200 km
- Wadati-Benioff zone: Well-defined to 200+ km depth
- Maximum seismicity depth: ~200 km

**Slab tear:** Northern edge of Cocos slab (near Nicaragua) shows tear/segmentation.

#### 11.2.2 Megathrust Earthquake Cycle

**Coupling zones (from GPS/InSAR):**
- **Nicoya Peninsula:** Strongly coupled (locking depth ~30 km)
- **Osa Peninsula:** Weakly coupled (creeping)
- **Central Costa Rica:** Transitional

**Megathrust earthquake sequence (historical):**
- 1854: Mw ~7.5 (Nicoya)
- 1900: Mw ~7.0
- 1950: Mw 7.7 (Nicoya)
- 1990: Mw 7.0
- 2012: Mw 7.6 (Nicoya) - filled 1950 gap

**Recurrence interval:** ~50-60 years for Mw 7.5-7.7 on Nicoya segment
**Slip deficit accumulation:** ~4-5 m since 2012

**Maximum credible earthquake:** Mw 9.0+ (full margin rupture, 500+ km)

#### 11.2.3 Slow Slip Events (SSEs)

**Nicoya Peninsula SSE:**
- Recurrence: ~12-14 months
- Duration: 2-4 weeks
- Magnitude: Mw 6.8-7.0 (equivalent)
- Slip: 5-10 cm
- Depth: 25-40 km (transition zone)
- Moment release: ~20% of seismic moment

**Osa Peninsula Deep SSE:**
- Recurrence: ~20-22 months
- Duration: 1-2 months
- Magnitude: Mw 7.0-7.2
- Depth: 30-45 km
- **Tremor association:** Yes, deep non-volcanic tremor

**Partitioned SSE:** Trench-parallel component on strike-slip faults (e.g., Haciendas fault)

**Physics:** Rate-and-state friction, fluid pressure, transitional rheology.

#### 11.2.4 Slab Deformation and Intermediate-Depth Seismicity

**Double seismic zone:** Two parallel planes of seismicity in slab (upper: dehydration embrittlement, lower: shear instability).

**CR region:** Double zone observed at 50-150 km depth.

**Dehydration reactions:**
- Antigorite breakdown: ~600°C, 2.5 GPa (~80 km)
- Chlorite breakdown: ~650°C, 2.8 GPa (~90 km)
- Lawtonite breakdown: ~550°C, 2.2 GPa (~70 km)

**Intermediate-depth earthquakes (70-200 km):**
- Mechanism: Dehydration embrittlement, transformational faulting
- Stress drop: Higher than crustal (5-20 MPa vs 1-5 MPa)
- Aftershock productivity: Lower

### 11.3 San Andreas Transform System (CA Node)

#### 11.3.1 Fault Geometry and Segmentation

**San Andreas Fault (SAF) system:** ~1,200 km long, right-lateral strike-slip.

**Major segments (north to south):**
1. **North Coast:** 1906 rupture (Mw 7.9), creeping section north
2. **Santa Cruz Mts:** 1989 Loma Prieta (Mw 6.9)
3. **Creeping section:** Parkfield to San Juan Bautista (aseismic creep ~25 mm/yr)
4. **Cholame:** 1857 Fort Tejon (Mw 7.9), locked
5. **Carrizo Plain:** 1857 rupture, high slip rate
6. **Mojave:** Complex, multiple strands
7. **San Bernardino:** Transverse Ranges, compressional bend
8. **Coachella:** Southernmost, high hazard

**San Jose, CA location:** Santa Cruz Mountains segment (near 1989 Loma Prieta epicenter).

#### 11.3.2 Slip Rate and Paleoseismology

**Geodetic slip rate:** 35-50 mm/yr (varies by segment)
**Geologic slip rate:** 25-35 mm/yr (long-term average)
**Creeping section:** ~25 mm/yr aseismic

**Paleoseismic recurrence (key sites):**
- **Wrightwood (Mojave):** 4-10 events in 1500 yr, mean ~150 yr
- **Frazier Mountain (Carrizo):** 6 events in 1200 yr, mean ~200 yr
- **Vedanta (Santa Cruz):** 4 events in 700 yr, mean ~170 yr
- **Parkfield (creeping):** M6 every ~22 yr (quasi-periodic)

**1857 Fort Tejon earthquake:** Mw 7.9, 350 km rupture, ~9 m slip
**1906 San Francisco earthquake:** Mw 7.9, 470 km rupture, ~5 m slip

#### 11.3.3 Fault Structure and Damage Zones

**Principal slip zone:** ~1-10 m wide, gouge-rich
**Damage zone:** ~100-1000 m wide, fractured rock
**Fault core:** Multiple slip surfaces, clay gouge

**SAF at San Jose (Santa Cruz Mts):**
- Multiple strands (San Andreas, Shannon, Berrocal)
- Restraining bend (compressional)
- High topography (Santa Cruz Mountains)
- Transpressional deformation

#### 11.3.4 Off-Fault Deformation

**Distributed shear:** ~20-30% of plate motion on secondary faults
**Major secondary faults:** Hayward, Calaveras, San Gregorio, Greenville

**Hayward Fault:** Mw 6.8-7.0 potential, 168 yr since 1868
**Calaveras Fault:** Mw 6.5-6.8, creeping at depth
**San Gregorio Fault:** Offshore, Mw 7.2 potential

**Block rotation:** Santa Cruz Mountains block rotates clockwise (~1°/Myr)

### 11.4 Seismic Hazard Analysis: PSHA Methodology

#### 11.4.1 Probabilistic Seismic Hazard Analysis (PSHA)

**Hazard integral:**
$$\lambda(a > a^*) = \sum_{i=1}^{N_s} \nu_i \int_{m_{min}}^{m_{max}} \int_{r_{min}}^{r_{max}} f_{M|S}(m) f_{R|S}(r) P(A > a^* | m, r, S_i) dr dm$$

Where:
- $\nu_i$ = annual rate of earthquakes on source $i$
- $f_{M|S}$ = magnitude distribution
- $f_{R|S}$ = distance distribution
- $P(A > a^* | m, r, S_i)$ = ground motion exceedance probability (from GMPE)

#### 11.4.2 Logic Tree Framework

**Epistemic uncertainty branches:**
1. **Source model:** Characteristic vs Gutenberg-Richter, segmentation
2. **GMPE selection:** Multiple GMPEs with weights
3. **Maximum magnitude:** $M_{max}$ distribution
4. **Recurrence model:** Time-independent vs time-dependent

**Typical logic tree size:** 100-1000 branches

**Weight assignment:** Expert elicitation, data-based (likelihood)

#### 11.4.3 Ground Motion Prediction Equations (GMPEs)

**Functional form (NGA-West2):**
$$\ln Y = f_{mag}(M) + f_{dist}(R, M) + f_{site}(V_{S30}, Z_{1.0}, Z_{2.5}) + f_{style}(F) + f_{basin}(Z_{1.0}) + f_{hyp}(Z_{TOR}) + \epsilon$$

**Key GMPEs for subduction (CR):**
- **BCHydro (2013):** Cascadia/Japan/Chile subduction
- **Kanno et al. (2006):** Japan subduction
- **Zhao et al. (2006):** Global subduction
- **Garcia et al. (2005):** Mexico subduction

**Key GMPEs for crustal strike-slip (CA):**
- **Abrahamson et al. (2014) - ASK14:** NGA-West2
- **Bozorgnia et al. (2014) - BSSA14:** NGA-West2
- **Campbell & Bozorgnia (2014) - CB14:** NGA-West2
- **Chiou & Youngs (2014) - CY14:** NGA-West2
- **Idriss (2014) - Idriss14:** NGA-West2

**Subduction vs crustal GMPE differences:**
- Subduction: Larger $M_{max}$, deeper sources, different attenuation
- Crustal: Shallow sources, higher frequency, directivity effects

#### 11.4.4 Site Amplification: $V_{S30}$ and Basin Effects

**$V_{S30}$ (time-averaged shear velocity to 30 m):**
- CR (Central Valley): 200-400 m/s (alluvial basin)
- CA (Santa Clara Valley): 250-500 m/s (deep basin)

**Basin depth parameters:**
- $Z_{1.0}$: Depth to $V_s = 1.0$ km/s
- $Z_{2.5}$: Depth to $V_s = 2.5$ km/s

**CR basin:** $Z_{1.0} \approx 0.5-1.0$ km, $Z_{2.5} \approx 2-3$ km
**CA basin:** $Z_{1.0} \approx 0.3-0.8$ km, $Z_{2.5} \approx 1.5-2.5$ km

**Nonlinear site response:** $G/G_{max}$ and $\xi$ curves (Darendeli, 2001)

### 11.6 Earthquake Early Warning (EEW)

#### 11.6.1 EEW Principles

**P-wave detection:** First arrival (non-destructive) → estimate magnitude/location
**S-wave arrival:** Destructive shear waves → warning time = (S-P time) - processing time

**Alert time:** $T_{alert} = \frac{D}{v_S} - \frac{D}{v_P} - T_{proc} \approx \frac{D}{8} - T_{proc}$ (seconds)

Where $D$ = distance to epicenter, $v_P \approx 6$ km/s, $v_S \approx 3.5$ km/s.

**Blind zone:** Radius where $T_{alert} < 0$ (no warning possible)
- Typical: 20-30 km radius

#### 11.6.2 EEW Systems: ShakeAlert (CA) vs SARA (CR)

**ShakeAlert (US West Coast):**
- Stations: ~1,600 (CISN, PNSN, etc.)
- Algorithms: EPIC, FinDer, PLUM
- Latency: 2-5 seconds
- Public alerts: Wireless Emergency Alerts (WEA), apps (MyShake)
- **San Jose, CA:** ~10-15 s warning for SAF M7 (distance ~20 km)

**SARA (Costa Rica):**
- Stations: ~100 (RSN, UCR)
- Algorithm: Based on ShakeAlert/EPIC
- Latency: 5-10 seconds
- **San Jose, CR:** ~5-10 s warning for Nicoya M7.6 (distance ~80 km)
- **Blind zone:** Larger for subduction (closer to trench)

#### 11.6.3 EEW Performance Metrics

| Metric | ShakeAlert (CA) | SARA (CR) |
|--------|----------------|-----------|
| Detection time | 1-2 s | 2-4 s |
| Magnitude accuracy (M>5) | ±0.3 | ±0.4 |
| Location accuracy | ±5 km | ±10 km |
| Alert latency | 2-5 s | 5-10 s |
| Blind zone radius | 20-30 km | 30-50 km |
| Public delivery | WEA, apps, APIs | Apps, sirens, radio |

**Key challenge for CR:** Subduction zone geometry → deeper hypocenters, larger blind zones.

### 11.8 Fault Rheology and Earthquake Physics

#### 11.8.1 Rate-and-State Friction

**Dieterich-Ruina law:**
$$\tau = \sigma_n \left[ f_0 + a \ln\left(\frac{V}{V_0}\right) + b \ln\left(\frac{V_0 \theta}{D_c}\right) \right]$$

**State evolution (aging law):**
$$\frac{d\theta}{dt} = 1 - \frac{V\theta}{D_c}$$

**Parameters:**
- $a$ = direct effect (~0.01)
- $b$ = evolution effect (~0.015)
- $D_c$ = critical slip distance (~10-100 μm)
- $(a-b)$ = stability parameter

**Velocity-weakening ($a-b < 0$):** Unstable, seismic
**Velocity-strengthening ($a-b > 0$):** Stable, aseismic

#### 11.8.2 Depth-Dependent Rheology

**CR Subduction Interface:**
| Depth | Temperature | Rheology | Behavior |
|-------|-------------|----------|----------|
| 0-15 km | < 300°C | Velocity-weakening | Seismogenic (locked) |
| 15-30 km | 300-450°C | Transitional | SSE zone |
| 30-50 km | 450-600°C | Velocity-strengthening | Creeping |
| > 50 km | > 600°C | Ductile | Stable sliding |

**CA San Andreas (crustal):**
| Depth | Temperature | Rheology | Behavior |
|-------|-------------|----------|----------|
| 0-3 km | < 150°C | Velocity-strengthening | Creep/aseismic |
| 3-15 km | 150-350°C | Velocity-weakening | Seismogenic |
| 15-20 km | 350-450°C | Transitional | Deep creep |
| > 20 km | > 450°C | Ductile | Stable |

#### 11.8.3 Pore Fluid Pressure Effects

**Effective normal stress:** $\sigma_n' = \sigma_n - p_f$

**Overpressure ratio:** $\lambda = p_f / \sigma_n$

**CR subduction:** High $\lambda$ (0.8-0.95) from dehydration, compaction
- Reduces effective stress → promotes slip
- Explains shallow SSEs, tremor

**CA San Andreas:** Near-hydrostatic $\lambda$ (0.4-0.6)
- Exception: Creeping section (high $\lambda$ from clay minerals)

**Fluid pressure diffusion:** Controls SSE propagation speed
$$D = \frac{k}{\phi \mu c_t} \approx 1 \text{ m}^2/\text{s} \quad \text{(fault zone)}$$

Where $k$ = permeability, $\phi$ = porosity, $\mu$ = viscosity, $c_t$ = compressibility.

### 11.9 Tsunami Hazard

#### 11.9.1 CR: Subduction Zone Tsunami

**Sources:** Megathrust earthquakes, submarine landslides, volcanic collapse

**Tsunami generation:** Vertical seafloor displacement
$$\eta(x,y) = \frac{1}{2\pi} \iint \frac{\partial u_z}{\partial t} \frac{1}{r} dx' dy'$$

**Tsunami hazard (CR):**

#### 11.9.2 CA: Strike-Slip Tsunami

**Sources:** Strike-slip (minor vertical), submarine landslides, far-field

**SAF tsunami potential:** Low (horizontal motion dominant)

**Far-field hazard:** Cascadia, Alaska, Chile, Japan, Hawaii

#### 11.14.2 Physics-Based Earthquake Forecasting

**Rate-state forecasting (Dieterich):**
$$\lambda(t) = \frac{\lambda_0}{1 - \frac{\lambda_0 D_c}{a\sigma} \ln\left(1 + \frac{t}{t_a}\right)}$$

Where $\lambda_0$ = background rate, $t_a = a\sigma / \dot{\tau}$.

**Coulomb stress triggering:**
$$\Delta CFS = \Delta \tau - \mu' \Delta \sigma_n$$

Where $\mu'$ = effective friction coefficient.

**Operational forecasting:** UCERF3 (CA), RELM tests, CSEP evaluations.

### 11.15 Detailed Subduction Zone Thermal-Petrological Structure

#### 11.15.1 Thermal Model of CR Subduction Zone

**Thermal parameter:** $\phi = v_{convergence} \times \text{slab age} \approx 85 \text{ mm/yr} \times 20 \text{ Ma} \approx 1700 \text{ km}^2/\text{Myr}$

**Slab surface temperature at 100 km depth:** ~600-700°C (for this thermal parameter)

**Mantle wedge corner flow:** 
- Upwelling beneath arc: $w \approx 1-5$ cm/yr
- Temperature: 1100-1300°C
- Melting: Flux melting from slab fluids

**Serpentinization of mantle wedge:**
- Depth: 50-100 km
- Reaction: $\text{Olivine} + \text{H}_2\text{O} \to \text{Serpentine} + \text{Brucite}$
- Volume increase: ~30-40%
- Seismic signature: Low $V_p/V_s$ (~1.6-1.7)

#### 11.15.2 Metamorphic Reactions and Fluid Release

**Key dehydration reactions in subducting slab:**

| Reaction | Depth | T (°C) | P (GPa) | Fluid Released |
|----------|-------|--------|---------|----------------|
| Lawsonite $\to$ Epidote | 60-80 km | 450-550 | 1.8-2.5 | 11-12 wt% |
| Chlorite $\to$ Garnet | 70-90 km | 550-650 | 2.2-2.8 | 10-13 wt% |
| Antigorite $\to$ Olivine | 80-120 km | 600-750 | 2.5-3.5 | 13 wt% |
| Phengite $\to$ Kyanite | 100-150 km | 650-800 | 3.0-4.0 | 4-5 wt% |

**Fluid flux estimate:** ~$10^{-11}$ m/s (Darcy flux) → ~0.3 m/yr

**Pore pressure evolution:** $\frac{\partial p}{\partial t} = D \nabla^2 p + S$ where $S$ = fluid source

**Overpressure development:** $\lambda = p_f/\sigma_n \to 0.9$ in sealed zones

#### 11.15.3 Arc Volcanism and Magma Generation

**CR volcanic arc:** Central American Volcanic Arc (CAVA)
- Active volcanoes near CR: Poás, Irazú, Turrialba, Arenal
- Distance from trench: ~150-200 km
- Magma composition: Basaltic andesite to dacite
- Eruption frequency: Poás (frequent phreatic), Irazú (1963-65), Arenal (1968-2010)

**Melt generation:** Flux melting + decompression melting (corner flow)
**Melt fraction:** 1-5% in wedge
**Melt extraction:** Focused by dunite channels

### 11.16 Transform Fault Mechanics: Detailed Physics

#### 11.16.1 Fault Zone Structure at Depth

**SAF at San Juan Bautista (creeping section):**
- **Surface:** Fault trace, offset features
- **0-3 km:** Velocity-strengthening gouge (smectite, illite-smectite)
- **3-8 km:** Transition zone, mixed behavior
- **8-15 km:** Velocity-weakening, seismogenic
- **15-20 km:** Transition to ductile
- **> 20 km:** Distributed shear in lower crust

**SAF at Parkfield (transition):**
- **Creeping at surface:** ~25 mm/yr
- **Locked at depth:** 6-12 km
- **M6 earthquakes:** Quasi-periodic (~22 yr)

**SAF at Cholame (1857 rupture):**
- **Fully locked:** 0-15 km
- **High slip deficit:** ~6-9 m since 1857

#### 11.16.2 Off-Fault Damage and Plasticity

**Damage zone width:** $W_d \approx 100 \times (\text{slip})^{0.5}$ (empirical)
- For 300 km total slip: $W_d \approx 1.7$ km (matches observations)

**Plastic strain off-fault:**
$$\varepsilon_p = \frac{1}{2G} \int \frac{\tau(r) - \tau_y}{r} dr$$

Where $\tau_y$ = yield stress, $G$ = shear modulus.

**Cosismic off-fault deformation:** Up to 20-30% of total moment

**Postseismic relaxation:** Afterslip + viscoelastic relaxation + poroelastic rebound

#### 11.16.3 Fault Zone Guided Waves

**Trapped waves in low-velocity fault zone:**
- **Fault zone width:** ~100-200 m
- **Velocity reduction:** 20-40% (Vp, Vs)
- **Q-factor:** 20-50 (high attenuation)

**Dispersion relation:**
$$\omega^2 = c_g^2 k^2 + \frac{n^2 \pi^2 c_g^2}{W^2}$$

Where $c_g$ = shear wave speed in fault zone, $W$ = width, $n$ = mode number.

**Observed:** High-frequency (2-10 Hz) guided waves on SAF, used to image fault zone structure.

### 11.17 Earthquake Source Physics: Radiation Patterns and Directivity

#### 11.17.1 Radiation Patterns

**Double-couple point source:**
$$u_i(\mathbf{x}, t) = \frac{1}{4\pi\rho} \frac{M_{jk}}{R} \frac{\partial}{\partial x_j} \frac{\partial}{\partial x_k} G(\mathbf{x}, t)$$

Where $M_{jk}$ = moment tensor, $G$ = Green's function.

**Far-field P-wave amplitude:**
$$A_P \propto M_0 \sin 2\delta \sin \lambda \cos \theta + \dots$$

**Far-field S-wave amplitude:**
$$A_S \propto M_0 \text{(radiation pattern depending on mechanism)}$$

**Thrust fault (CR subduction):**
- P-wave: Four-lobed, compressional quadrants perpendicular to fault
- S-wave: SV polarized in dip direction, SH along strike

**Strike-slip (CA transform):**
- P-wave: Four-lobed, compressional quadrants at 45° to fault
- S-wave: SH dominant, SV minimal

#### 11.17.2 Directivity Effects

**Rupture velocity:** $v_r \approx 0.7-0.9 v_S$

**Forward directivity (strike-slip):**
- Pulse duration: $T_p \approx \frac{L}{v_r} \left(1 - \frac{v_r}{v_S}\cos\theta\right)$
- Amplitude enhancement: $A_{max} \propto \frac{1}{1 - v_r/v_S}$

**For $v_r/v_S = 0.8$:** $A_{max} \approx 5\times$ ambient

**Megathrust directivity:**
- Unilateral (updip): Strong long-period pulse
- Bilateral: Shorter duration, less amplification

**San Jose, CA directivity:** In forward directivity zone for NW rupture on SAF
**San Jose, CR directivity:** Variable (depends on rupture direction)

#### 11.17.3 Stress Drop and Scaling Relations

**Stress drop:** $\Delta\sigma = \frac{7}{16} \frac{M_0}{r^3}$ (circular crack)

**Self-similar scaling:** $\Delta\sigma \approx$ constant (1-10 MPa)

**Subduction interface (CR):** $\Delta\sigma \approx 3-10$ MPa
**Crustal strike-slip (CA):** $\Delta\sigma \approx 1-5$ MPa
**Intraslab (CR):** $\Delta\sigma \approx 10-30$ MPa (higher)

**Corner frequency:** $f_c = 4.9 \times 10^6 \frac{v_S}{(\Delta\sigma/M_0)^{1/3}}$ (Hz, with $M_0$ in N·m)

**CR Mw 7.6:** $f_c \approx 0.2-0.4$ Hz
**CA Mw 7.0:** $f_c \approx 0.5-1.0$ Hz

### 11.18 Building Code Implementation Details

#### 11.18.1 CR: CFE + International Codes

**Legal framework:** 
- **Ley de Construcción (1996):** Mandates seismic design
- **Reglamento de Diseño Sísmico (CFE-2012):** Technical requirements
- **Adopted standards:** ACI 318-14, ASCE 7-10, Eurocode 8 (reference)

**Seismic zoning (CFE):**
- **Zone 4:** Entire country (PGA > 0.3g)
- **Subzones:** Based on soil, proximity to faults

**Design spectrum (CFE):**
$$S_a(T) = \begin{cases} S_{DS}(0.4 + 0.6T/T_0) & T < T_0 \\ S_{DS} & T_0 \le T \le T_S \\ S_{D1}/T & T > T_S \end{cases}$$

Where $T_0 = 0.2 S_{D1}/S_{DS}$, $T_S = S_{D1}/S_{DS}$

**Site coefficients (CFE):**
- Soil type S1 (rock): $F_a = 1.0, F_v = 1.0$
- Soil type S2 (stiff soil): $F_a = 1.2, F_v = 1.5$
- Soil type S3 (soft soil): $F_a = 1.4, F_v = 2.0$
- Soil type S4 (very soft): Site-specific analysis required

**CR Central Valley:** Mostly S3/S4 → significant amplification

#### 11.18.2 CA: CBC / ASCE 7-16 Risk-Targeted Approach

**Risk-targeted ground motion (MCE_R):**
$$S_{SUH} = C_{RS} S_{UH}$$

Where $C_{RS}$ = risk coefficient (1.0-1.5), $S_{UH}$ = uniform hazard spectrum.

**Collapse probability target:** 1% in 50 years

**Site class (ASCE 7):**
- A: Hard rock ($V_{S30} > 1500$ m/s)
- B: Rock (760-1500)
- C: Very dense soil (360-760)
- D: Stiff soil (180-360)
- E: Soft soil (< 180)
- F: Special (peat, liquefiable, etc.)

**CA Santa Clara Valley:** Class D/E (deep basin)

**Basin amplification (ASCE 7-16):**
$$F_{basin} = \exp\left[-0.0014(Z_{1.0})^2 + 0.5(Z_{1.0}) - 0.5\right] \quad \text{for } Z_{1.0} < 500 \text{ m}$$

**CA $Z_{1.0} \approx 300-800$ m:** $F_{basin} \approx 1.2-1.8$

#### 11.18.3 Structural Detailing Requirements

**CR (Concrete SMRF per ACI 318):**
- **Column transverse reinforcement:** $\rho_s \ge 0.09 f'_c/f_{yh}$ (confining)
- **Beam longitudinal:** $\rho_{min} = 0.25\sqrt{f'_c}/f_y$ (top/bottom)
- **Joint shear:** $V_j \le \phi V_{n,j}$ (strict limits)
- **Development length:** $l_d = 1.3 \frac{f_y d_b}{\sqrt{f'_c}}$ (seismic)

**CA (Steel SMRF per AISC 341):**
- **Connection:** Prequalified (RBS, WUF, KBF) per FEMA 350
- **Panel zone:** $V_{panel} \le 0.55 F_y d_c t_w$
- **Column-web doubler plates:** When required
- **Protected zone:** No welding, no holes

**CA (Concrete SMRF per ACI 318-19):**
- **Stricter than CR:** More confinement, tighter spacing
- **Boundary elements:** Required for walls ($\varepsilon_t > 0.002$)
- **Coupling beams:** Special detailing for $h_w/l_w < 2$

### 11.19 Seismic Risk and Loss Estimation

#### 11.19.1 FEMA P-58 Methodology

**Loss calculation:**
$$\text{Loss} = \sum_{i=1}^{N_{ds}} \sum_{j=1}^{N_{cmp}} \text{Qty}_{ij} \times \text{UnitCost}_{ij} \times P(DS_i | IM)$$

**Fragility functions (lognormal):**
$$P(DS \ge ds | IM) = \Phi\left[\frac{\ln(IM) - \ln(\theta_{ds})}{\beta_{ds}}\right]$$

Where $\theta_{ds}$ = median capacity, $\beta_{ds}$ = dispersion.

**CR building inventory:**
- Concrete frame: ~60%
- Masonry: ~25%
- Steel/wood: ~15%
- **Vulnerability:** Higher for pre-1990 construction

**CA building inventory:**
- Wood frame: ~55% (residential)
- Concrete/steel: ~25% (commercial)
- Unreinforced masonry: ~5% (mostly retrofitted)
- **Vulnerability:** Lower due to code evolution + retrofits

#### 11.19.2 Scenario Loss Estimates

**CR Scenario: Mw 7.6 Nicoya repeat (2012-type)**
- PGA in Central Valley: 0.3-0.5 g
- Expected casualties: 100-500
- Economic loss: $1-5 B
- Displaced: 10,000-50,000

**CA Scenario: Mw 7.0 Santa Cruz Mts (Loma Prieta repeat)**
- PGA in San Jose: 0.4-0.7 g
- Expected casualties: 50-200
- Economic loss: $10-50 B (higher asset value)
- Displaced: 5,000-20,000

**CA Scenario: Mw 7.8 SAF Southern Section (ShakeOut)**
- PGA in San Jose: 0.2-0.4 g (distance ~300 km)
- Long-duration shaking (basin effects)
- Regional impact: $200 B+

### 11.21 Tectonic Geomorphology and Landscape Evolution

#### 11.21.1 CR: Subduction Zone Topography

**Forearc uplift:** Interseismic (locked) → subsidence, coseismic → uplift
- **Nicoya Peninsula:** ~1-2 m/kyr net uplift
- **Marine terraces:** MIS 5e (125 ka) at 20-40 m elevation

**Arc volcanism:** Builds topography (volcanoes 2000-3500 m)
**Erosion:** High precipitation (3000+ mm/yr) → rapid erosion
**Exhumation rates:** 0.5-1.5 mm/yr (thermochronology)

#### 11.21.2 CA: Transform Topography

**SAF landforms:** Offset streams, shutter ridges, sag ponds, pressure ridges
**Slip rate from geomorphology:** $v = \frac{\text{offset}}{\text{age}}$
- Wallace Creek: 35 mm/yr (3.5 m / 100 yr)
- Carrizo Plain: 33 mm/yr

**Transpressional ranges:** Santa Cruz Mts, San Gabriel Mts, Transverse Ranges
- Uplift rates: 0.5-2 mm/yr
- Exhumation: 0.2-1 mm/yr

**Basin subsidence:** LA Basin, Santa Clara Valley, San Joaquin Valley
- Rates: 0.1-1 mm/yr

### 11.22 Summary: Tectonic Regimes as Latitudinal Organizers

The tectonic boundary type is a **primary determinant** of the latitudinal geophysical environment:

**Subduction (CR):**
- Deep seismicity (to 200+ km)
- Megathrust + intraslab + crustal sources
- Slow slip, tremor, SSEs
- High tsunami hazard
- Volcanic arc
- Forearc cycling (uplift/subsidence)
- Strong rotational stress coupling (extensional at equator)

**Transform (CA):**
- Shallow seismicity (0-15 km)
- Strike-slip dominated
- Creeping vs locked segmentation
- Low tsunami hazard
- No volcanism (except transtensional)
- Block rotation, distributed shear
- Strong Coriolis influence on deformation

**The equatorial vs mid-latitude position modulates these regimes:**
- CR: Rotational extension reduces normal stress on megathrust
- CA: Coriolis affects fault zone hydrology, damage asymmetry
