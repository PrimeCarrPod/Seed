# FINDINGS AND DECLARATIONS — Act 09: Water Infrastructure & Drought Resilience Act

## SECTION 201. CONGRESSIONAL FINDINGS

The Congress finds the following:

**(1) Aging Infrastructure Crisis**
The United States' water infrastructure is aging and deteriorating. Many water systems are over 100 years old. The American Society of Civil Engineers grades U.S. water infrastructure a C-. An estimated 6 billion gallons of treated water are lost daily to leaks.

**(2) Water Scarcity Emergency**
Climate change is increasing water scarcity, particularly in the western United States. The Colorado River, which supplies water to 40 million people, has seen flows decline 20% since 2000. Lake Mead and Lake Powell, the nation's largest reservoirs, are at historically low levels.

**(3) Flood Risk Escalation**
Flooding causes $17 billion in damages annually. Climate change is intensifying extreme precipitation events. An estimated 13 million Americans live in flood-prone areas. Current flood management infrastructure is inadequate for future conditions.

**(4) Water Waste**
The United States wastes an estimated 17% of daily water production through leaks and inefficiencies. Agricultural water use accounts for 80% of western water consumption, much of it through inefficient irrigation systems. Industrial water use has declined but remains significant.

**(5) Climate Change Acceleration**
Climate change is intensifying both droughts and floods. Average temperatures have increased 1.8°F since 1900, reducing snowpack and increasing evaporation. Sea level rise threatens coastal water infrastructure. Extreme weather events are becoming more frequent and intense.

**(6) Infrastructure Investment Gap**
The EPA estimates $472 billion is needed over 20 years to maintain and upgrade drinking water infrastructure. Current spending levels address less than 40% of the need. State and local governments lack the fiscal capacity to close this gap alone.

**(7) Environmental Justice**
Water infrastructure failures disproportionately affect low-income communities and communities of color. Lead contamination in Flint, Michigan, and Jackson, Mississippi, exemplify this disparity. Tribal nations face significant water access challenges. Rural communities often lack basic water infrastructure.

**(8) Economic Impact**
Water infrastructure failures cost the U.S. economy $14 billion annually. Water scarcity threatens $1 trillion in economic activity in the western United States. Flood damages reduce property values and increase insurance costs. Agricultural losses from drought total $8 billion annually.

## SECTION 202. DECLARATIONS OF POLICY

**SEC. 202.** It is the policy of the United States that:

**(1) Water Security as National Priority**
Water security shall be a national priority on par with national defense and public health. All federal agencies shall prioritize water security in their planning, budgeting, and operations.

**(2) Climate Resilience Integration**
Climate change adaptation shall be integrated into all water infrastructure planning, construction, and management decisions.

**(3) Public Health Protection**
The protection of public health through safe drinking water shall be the paramount consideration in all water management decisions.

**(4) Science-Based Decision Making**
Water management decisions shall be based on the best available science. Research and development shall be prioritized to improve understanding of hydrology, treatment technologies, and conservation strategies.

**(5) Collaborative Governance**
Water management shall be a collaborative effort among federal, state, Tribal, local, and private stakeholders. Decision-making shall be shared across watershed boundaries.

**(6) Environmental Justice**
Water infrastructure investments shall prioritize the needs of environmental justice communities. Funding shall be targeted to communities with the greatest need and least capacity.

**(7) Fiscal Responsibility**
Water infrastructure funding shall be managed with the highest standards of fiscal responsibility. Programs shall be evaluated regularly for effectiveness and efficiency.

**(8) Innovation and Technology**
Innovation and technology development shall be encouraged in all aspects of water management. Federal programs shall accelerate the deployment of proven technologies.

**(9) Water Conservation**
Water conservation shall be promoted as the most cost-effective approach to addressing water scarcity. Federal programs shall incentivize water efficiency across all sectors.

**(10) Ecosystem Protection**
Water management decisions shall consider ecosystem health. Environmental flows shall be maintained to support aquatic ecosystems and biodiversity.

## SECTION 203. PURPOSES

**SEC. 203.** The purposes of this Act are to:

**(1)** Modernize the nation's aging water infrastructure through targeted investments and technical assistance.

**(2)** Build drought resilience through water recycling, desalination, conservation, and watershed management.

**(3)** Reduce flood risk through levee improvements, floodplain restoration, and stormwater management.

**(4)** Promote water conservation across all sectors through incentives, standards, and education.

**(5)** Adapt water systems to climate change through resilient infrastructure and integrated planning.

**(6)** Ensure safe and affordable drinking water for all Americans.

**(7)** Protect aquatic ecosystems and maintain environmental flows.

**(8)** Create economic opportunities through water infrastructure investment.

**(9)** Advance environmental justice through prioritized funding for disadvantaged communities.

**(10)** Strengthen federal coordination of water policy and programs.

---

## SECTION 204. DEFINITIONS

**SEC. 204.** In this Act:

**(1) "Drinking Water System"**
A system for the collection, treatment, storage, and distribution of potable water to the public.

**(2) "Wastewater System"**
A system for the collection, treatment, and disposal of municipal wastewater.

**(3) "Stormwater System"**
A system for the collection, conveyance, and treatment of stormwater runoff.

**(4) "Water Conservation"**
Practices and technologies that reduce water consumption or improve water use efficiency.

**(5) "Water Recycling"**
The treatment and reuse of wastewater for beneficial purposes.

**(6) "Desalination"**
The removal of salt from seawater or brackish water to produce potable water.

**(7) "Environmental Justice Community"**
Any community identified as disadvantaged under the Justice40 Initiative or equivalent screening tool.

**(8) "Tribal Nation"**
Any federally recognized Indian Tribe, Alaska Native village, or Native Hawaiian community.

**(9) "Climate-Resilient Infrastructure"**
Infrastructure designed to withstand projected climate change impacts over its design life.

**(10) "Integrated Water Resources Planning"**
A planning process that considers water supply, water quality, flood management, and ecosystem needs holistically.

**(5) Groundwater Depletion**
Over-pumping of groundwater is causing land subsidence and aquifer depletion. Some aquifers may take centuries to recover.

**(6) Snowpack Decline**
