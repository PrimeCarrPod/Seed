## SEC. 1310. APPENDIX F — GLOSSARY OF TERMS

**SEC. 1310.** The following terms are defined for purposes of this Act:

**(1) "Adaptive Management"**
A systematic process for continually improving management policies and practices by learning from the outcomes of operational programs.

**(2) "Biomass"**
Organic material derived from vegetation management activities, including trees, shrubs, slash, and other plant material.

**(3) "Carbon Pricing"**
A fee on fossil fuel combustion based on carbon dioxide equivalent (CO2e) emissions.

**(4) "Community Wildfire Protection Plan (CWPP)"**
A plan developed by a community in consultation with federal, state, and local agencies that identifies and prioritizes areas for hazardous fuel reduction treatments.

**(5) "Covered Conductor"**
An electrical conductor encased in a thick layer of insulating material rated to withstand momentary contact with vegetation without arcing or ignition.

**(6) "Cultural Burning"**
The intentional use of fire by Indigenous peoples for cultural, spiritual, and land management purposes.

**(7) "Defensible Space"**
The area within 100 feet of a structure where vegetation and combustible materials are managed to reduce fire spread potential.

**(8) "Fire-Adapted Community"**
A community that has taken proactive measures to reduce wildfire risk through vegetation management, building code enforcement, defensible space compliance, and emergency preparedness planning.

**(9) "Fire-Resistant Building Material"**
Any construction material that meets or exceeds the Class A fire resistance rating under ASTM E84.

**(10) "Fuel Break"**
A strategically located barrier of reduced fuel density designed to slow or stop fire spread.

**(11) "Green Bond"**
A debt instrument issued by the U.S. Treasury specifically designated for climate resilience and environmental infrastructure investment.

**(12) "Grid Hardening"**
The physical modification of electrical infrastructure to reduce ignition risk during high-wind, low-humidity events.

**(13) "Hazardous Fuel"**
Any vegetative material, live or dead, that constitutes a fire hazard and threat to public safety, property, or natural resources.

**(14) "High Fire Risk Area"**
Any area designated as Fire Hazard Severity Zone "High" or "Very High" by the state fire marshal.

**(15) "Ignition-Resistant Construction"**
Building methods and materials designed to resist ignition from ember exposure, radiant heat, and direct flame contact.

**(16) "Microgrid"**
A group of interconnected loads and distributed energy resources within clearly defined electrical boundaries that acts as a single controllable entity with respect to the grid.

**(17) "Prescribed Fire"**
Any fire intentionally ignited in accordance with a written prescription plan for the purpose of achieving land management objectives.

**(18) "Public Safety Power Shutoff (PSPS)"**
The preemptive de-energization of electrical lines during extreme fire weather conditions.

**(19) "Traditional Ecological Knowledge"**
The evolving knowledge acquired by Indigenous and local peoples over hundreds or thousands of years through direct contact with the environment.

**(20) "Wildland-Urban Interface (WUI)"**
The zone where structures and other human development meet or intermingle with undeveloped wildland or vegetative fuels.

## SEC. 1311. APPENDIX G — IMPLEMENTATION TIMELINE

**SEC. 1311.** The following implementation timeline is provided for reference:

**(1) Phase 1 — Immediate Actions (Months 1-12)**
(A) Establishment of the National Wildfire Prevention Fund
(B) Development of the National Wildfire Prevention Strategy
(C) Development of grid hardening standards
(D) Development of the WUI building code
(E) Hiring of implementation staff
(F) Development of grant and loan application procedures
(G) Initiation of workforce recruitment
(H) Deployment of emergency detection cameras
(I) Development of prescribed fire certification program
(J) Initiation of public education campaigns

**(2) Phase 2 — Rapid Scaling (Months 13-36)**
(A) Scaling of vegetation management to 10 million acres annually
(B) Scaling of prescribed fire to 5 million acres annually
(C) Deployment of 5,000 detection cameras
(D) Hiring of 15,000 additional prevention workers
(E) Retrofitting of 100,000 structures to fire-resistant standards
(F) Hardening of 10,000 miles of power lines
(G) Certification of 500 Fire-Adapted Communities
(H) Completion of 500 Community Wildfire Protection Plans

**(3) Phase 3 — Full Implementation (Months 37-60)**
(A) Scaling of vegetation management to 15 million acres annually
(B) Scaling of prescribed fire to 8 million acres annually
(C) Deployment of 10,000 detection cameras
(D) Hiring of 25,000 additional prevention workers
(E) Retrofitting of 500,000 structures to fire-resistant standards
(F) Hardening of 25,000 miles of power lines
(G) Certification of 2,000 Fire-Adapted Communities
(H) Completion of 2,000 Community Wildfire Protection Plans

**(4) Phase 4 — Maturation (Months 61-120)**
(A) Scaling of vegetation management to 25 million acres annually
(B) Scaling of prescribed fire to 10 million acres annually
(C) Full deployment of detection camera network
(D) Hiring of 50,000 additional prevention workers
(E) Retrofitting of 2,000,000 structures to fire-resistant standards
(F) Hardening of 50,000 miles of power lines
(G) Certification of 5,000 Fire-Adapted Communities
(H) Completion of 5,000 Community Wildfire Protection Plans

## SEC. 1314. APPENDIX J — ENVIRONMENTAL JUSTICE ANALYSIS

**SEC. 1314.** (a) DISADVANTAGED COMMUNITY BENEFITS.—

**(1) SET-ASIDE.—**Not less than 40% of Fund expenditures shall benefit disadvantaged communities.

**(2) PRIORITY.—**Disadvantaged communities shall receive priority for:
(A) Defensible space assistance
(B) Fire-resistant retrofit grants
(C) Job training and employment
(D) Community Wildfire Protection Plan development
(E) Fire-Adapted Communities certification

**(b) TRIBAL COMMUNITY BENEFITS.—

**(1) SET-ASIDE.—**Not less than 10% of Fund expenditures shall benefit Tribal communities.

**(2) PRIORITY.—**Tribal communities shall receive priority for:
(A) Cultural burning programs
(B) Tribal wildfire prevention funding
(C) Technical assistance and training
(D) Co-management agreements

**(c) RURAL COMMUNITY BENEFITS.—

**(1) SET-ASIDE.—**Not less than 20% of Fund expenditures shall benefit rural communities.

**(2) PRIORITY.—**Rural communities shall receive priority for:
(A) Vegetation management assistance
(B) Grid hardening assistance
(C) Volunteer fire department support
(D) Economic development programs

## SEC. 1315. APPENDIX K — TRIBAL CONSULTATION REQUIREMENTS

**SEC. 1315.** (a) GOVERNMENT-TO-GOVERNMENT CONSULTATION.—

**(1) IN GENERAL.—**The Secretary shall consult with Tribal nations on all activities affecting Tribal lands or resources.

**(2) TIMING.—**Consultation shall occur at the earliest possible stage of planning.

**(b) TRIBAL IMPLEMENTATION PLANS.—

**(1) DEVELOPMENT.—**Tribal nations may develop Tribal implementation plans that align with the national implementation plan.

**(2) APPROVAL.—**Tribal implementation plans shall be approved by the Secretary.

**(c) CULTURAL BURNING PROTECTION.—

**(1) TRIBAL SOVEREIGNTY.—**Nothing in this Act shall be construed to limit the right of Tribal nations to conduct cultural burns on their lands.

**(2) CO-MANAGEMENT.—**The Secretary shall enter into co-management agreements with Tribal nations for cultural burning on federal lands within their ancestral territories.
