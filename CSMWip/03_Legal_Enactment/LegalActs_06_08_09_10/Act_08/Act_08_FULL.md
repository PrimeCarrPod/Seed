## SECTION 3. DEFINITIONS

**SEC. 3.** In this Act:

**(1) "Critical Technology"**
Any technology designated as critical by the Secretary of Commerce, including but not limited to semiconductors, rare earth minerals, pharmaceuticals, artificial intelligence, quantum computing, biotechnology, and advanced energy technologies.

**(2) "Supply Chain"**
The network of organizations, people, activities, information, and resources involved in producing and delivering a product or service from supplier to customer.

**(3) "Domestic"**
Produced or manufactured within the United States or its territories.

**(4) "Foreign Entity of Concern"**
Any entity owned by, controlled by, or subject to the jurisdiction of a foreign country of concern, as designated by the Secretary of State.

**(5) "Strategic Reserve"**
A stockpile of critical materials or products maintained by the federal government to ensure supply chain resilience.

# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY — Act 08: Critical Technology Supply Chain Act

## OVERVIEW

The Critical Technology Supply Chain Act of 2026 establishes a comprehensive framework for securing and strengthening the United States' critical technology supply chains. The Act addresses vulnerabilities in semiconductor manufacturing, rare earth minerals processing, pharmaceutical production, and other critical technology sectors.

---

## THE PROBLEM

The United States faces critical vulnerabilities in its technology supply chains:

**(1) Semiconductor Dependence**
The U.S. share of global semiconductor manufacturing has declined from 37% in 1990 to 12% today. Over 80% of advanced semiconductor manufacturing occurs in Taiwan and South Korea. A disruption in semiconductor supply would cost the U.S. economy $500 billion annually.

**(2) Rare Earth Dependence**
China controls 60% of global rare earth mining and 85% of rare earth processing. Rare earth minerals are essential for defense systems, electric vehicles, wind turbines, and consumer electronics. A disruption in rare earth supply would cripple multiple U.S. industries.

**(3) Pharmaceutical Dependence**
Over 80% of active pharmaceutical ingredients (APIs) are manufactured overseas, primarily in China and India. The COVID-19 pandemic exposed critical vulnerabilities in the pharmaceutical supply chain. Drug shortages affect millions of Americans annually.

**(4) National Security Risks**
Foreign dependence in critical technology supply chains poses significant national security risks. Adversaries could use supply chain leverage as a geopolitical weapon. The U.S. military depends on foreign suppliers for critical components.

---

## THE SOLUTION

The Critical Technology Supply Chain Act addresses these vulnerabilities through:

**(1) Domestic Manufacturing Incentives**
$60 billion in incentives for domestic semiconductor manufacturing, including tax credits, grants, and loan guarantees.

**(2) Rare Earth Development**
$30 billion for domestic rare earth mining, processing, and recycling, reducing dependence on Chinese suppliers.

**(3) Pharmaceutical Resilience**
$25 billion for domestic pharmaceutical manufacturing, including API production and finished dosage manufacturing.

**(4) Strategic Reserve**
$20 billion for a National Critical Technology Reserve to buffer against supply disruptions.

**(5) International Partnerships**
$10 billion for allied supply chain agreements with trusted partners.

**(6) Research and Development**
$15 billion for critical technology research and development.

**(7) Workforce Development**
$10 billion for workforce training and education programs.

**SEC. 101.** This title may be cited as the "Semiconductor Supply Chain Security Act of 2026."

---

## SEC. 102. FINDINGS SPECIFIC TO SEMICONDUCTORS

**SEC. 102.** The Congress finds the following with respect to semiconductors:

**(1) Manufacturing Decline**
The U.S. share of global semiconductor manufacturing has declined from 37% in 1990 to 12% in 2026. Over 80% of advanced semiconductor manufacturing occurs in Taiwan and South Korea.

**(2) National Security Risk**
The U.S. military depends on foreign suppliers for critical semiconductor components in weapons systems and military equipment. This dependence poses significant national security risks.

**(3) Economic Impact**
A disruption in semiconductor supply would cost the U.S. economy $500 billion annually. The 2020-2023 semiconductor shortage cost the global economy $500 billion.

**(4) Technology Leadership**
The United States maintains leadership in semiconductor design but has lost leadership in manufacturing. Rebuilding domestic manufacturing capacity is essential for maintaining technology leadership.

**(5) Workforce Shortage**
The semiconductor industry faces a severe workforce shortage. Over 100,000 skilled workers are needed in semiconductor manufacturing.

**(6) Investment Gap**
The United States invests less in semiconductor manufacturing than competitors. China invests $150 billion annually in semiconductor manufacturing. The CHIPS and Science Act of 2022 was a first step but is insufficient.

## SEC. 103. PURPOSES OF TITLE I

**SEC. 103.** The purposes of this title are to:

**(1)** Increase the U.S. share of global semiconductor manufacturing from 12% to 24% by 2036.

**(2)** Create 300,000 direct jobs in semiconductor manufacturing.

**(3)** Invest $60 billion in domestic semiconductor manufacturing.

**(4)** Maintain U.S. technology leadership through research and development.

**(5)** Ensure national security through secure semiconductor supply chains.

**(6)** Strengthen allied cooperation on semiconductor supply chains.

---

## SEC. 104. DEFINITIONS FOR TITLE I

**SEC. 104.** In this title:

**(1) "Semiconductor"**
A material that has electrical conductivity between that of a conductor and an insulator, used in electronic devices, including but not limited to integrated circuits, microprocessors, memory chips, and sensors.

**(2) "Fabrication Facility" or "Fab"**
A manufacturing facility where semiconductors are produced from raw materials.

**(3) "Advanced Packaging"**
The process of assembling and testing semiconductor chips after fabrication.

**(4) "Node"**
A specific semiconductor manufacturing process technology, measured in nanometers (nm).

**(5) "Compound Semiconductor"**
A semiconductor made from two or more elements, such as gallium nitride (GaN) or silicon carbide (SiC).

# TITLE II — RARE EARTH MINERALS

## SEC. 201. SHORT TITLE

**SEC. 201.** This title may be cited as the "Rare Earth Minerals Security Act of 2026."

---

## SEC. 202. FINDINGS SPECIFIC TO RARE EARTH MINERALS

**SEC. 202.** The Congress finds the following with respect to rare earth minerals:

**(1) Chinese Dominance**
China controls 60% of global rare earth mining and 85% of rare earth processing. The United States imports 80% of its rare earth minerals from China.

**(2) Essential Applications**
Rare earth minerals are essential for defense systems, electric vehicles, wind turbines, consumer electronics, medical devices, and clean energy technologies.

**(3) Supply Chain Vulnerability**
The concentration of rare earth mining and processing in China creates significant supply chain vulnerability. China has used rare earth exports as a geopolitical weapon in the past.

**(4) Domestic Resources**
The United States has significant rare earth deposits, including the Mountain Pass mine in California. These resources can be developed to reduce foreign dependence.

**(5) Environmental Challenges**
Rare earth mining and processing create environmental challenges, including radioactive waste, water pollution, and habitat destruction. Strict environmental standards must be maintained.

**(6) Recycling Opportunity**
Rare earth recycling can reduce dependence on primary mining. Current recycling rates are less than 5%. Increased recycling can supplement domestic production.

---

## SEC. 203. PURPOSES OF TITLE II

**SEC. 203.** The purposes of this title are to:

**(1)** Reduce U.S. dependence on Chinese rare earth imports from 80% to 40% by 2036.

**(2)** Develop domestic rare earth mining and processing capacity.

**(3)** Create 100,000 direct jobs in rare earth industries.

**(4)** Invest $30 billion in domestic rare earth production.

**(5)** Promote rare earth recycling and sustainability.

**(6)** Ensure national security through secure rare earth supply chains.

## SEC. 204. DEFINITIONS FOR TITLE II

**SEC. 204.** In this title:

**(1) "Rare Earth Minerals"**
The 17 chemical elements in the periodic table that are essential for modern technology, including neodymium, dysprosium, terbium, lanthanum, cerium, praseodymium, samarium, europium, gadolinium, holmium, erbium, thulium, ytterbium, lutetium, scandium, and yttrium.

**(2) "Rare Earth Processing"**
The separation and refining of rare earth minerals into individual rare earth elements.

**(3) "Rare Earth Recycling"**
The recovery of rare earth minerals from end-of-life products and manufacturing waste.

**(4) "Magnet"**
A device made from rare earth minerals that produces a magnetic field, used in electric motors, wind turbines, and consumer electronics.

---

# TITLE III — PHARMACEUTICAL SUPPLY CHAIN

## SEC. 301. SHORT TITLE

**SEC. 301.** This title may be cited as the "Pharmaceutical Supply Chain Security Act of 2026."

---

## SEC. 302. FINDINGS SPECIFIC TO PHARMACEUTICALS

**SEC. 302.** The Congress finds the following with respect to pharmaceuticals:

**(1) Foreign Dependence**
Over 80% of active pharmaceutical ingredients (APIs) are manufactured overseas, primarily in China and India. This dependence creates significant supply chain vulnerability.

**(2) Drug Shortages**
Drug shortages affect millions of Americans annually. The COVID-19 pandemic exposed critical vulnerabilities in the pharmaceutical supply chain, resulting in widespread drug shortages.

**(3) National Security Risk**
Foreign dependence in pharmaceutical supply chains poses significant national security risks. Adversaries could use pharmaceutical supply as a geopolitical weapon.

**(4) Public Health Impact**
Drug shortages result in increased mortality, reduced quality of care, and increased healthcare costs. Ensuring domestic pharmaceutical production is essential for public health.

**(5) Economic Impact**
The United States spends $500 billion annually on pharmaceuticals. Domestic production would create 200,000 jobs and reduce pharmaceutical costs.

**(6) Quality Concerns**
Foreign pharmaceutical manufacturing raises quality concerns. Domestic production ensures compliance with FDA quality standards.

---

## SEC. 303. PURPOSES OF TITLE III

**SEC. 303.** The purposes of this title are to:

**(1)** Reduce U.S. dependence on foreign pharmaceutical imports from 80% to 50% by 2036.

**(2)** Create 200,000 direct jobs in pharmaceutical manufacturing.

**(3)** Invest $25 billion in domestic pharmaceutical production.

**(4)** Prevent drug shortages through domestic production.

**(5)** Ensure national security through secure pharmaceutical supply chains.

**(6)** Reduce pharmaceutical costs through domestic competition.

## SEC. 304. DEFINITIONS FOR TITLE III

**SEC. 304.** In this title:

**(1) "Active Pharmaceutical Ingredient (API)"**
Any substance or mixture of substances intended to be used in the manufacture of a drug product and that, when used in the production of a drug, becomes an active ingredient of the drug product.

**(2) "Finished Dosage Form"**
A pharmaceutical product in the form in which it is marketed for use, including tablets, capsules, injectables, and topicals.

**(3) "Generic Drug"**
A pharmaceutical product that is equivalent to a brand-name drug in dosage, strength, route of administration, quality, performance, and intended use.

**(4) "Essential Medicine"**
A medicine included on the World Health Organization Model List of Essential Medicines or the FDA list of essential medicines.

## SEC. 1111. APPENDIX F — GLOSSARY OF TERMS

**SEC. 1111.** The following terms are defined for purposes of this Act:

**(1) "Active Pharmaceutical Ingredient (API)"**
Any substance or mixture of substances intended to be used in the manufacture of a drug product.

**(2) "Critical Technology"**
Any technology designated as critical by the Secretary of Commerce.

**(3) "Domestic"**
Produced or manufactured within the United States or its territories.

**(4) "Foreign Entity of Concern"**
Any entity owned by, controlled by, or subject to the jurisdiction of a foreign country of concern.

**(5) "Rare Earth Minerals"**
The 17 chemical elements in the periodic table that are essential for modern technology.

**(6) "Semiconductor"**
A material that has electrical conductivity between that of a conductor and an insulator.

**(7) "Strategic Reserve"**
A stockpile of critical materials or products maintained by the federal government.

**(8) "Supply Chain"**
The network of organizations, people, activities, information, and resources involved in producing and delivering a product or service.
