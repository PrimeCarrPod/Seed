## 7. Governance Structures -- Community Trusts, Development Corporations, Oversight Boards

Effective mutual benefit requires governance that transforms community from passive beneficiary to active co-governor. This chapter designs the institutional architecture for Tardigradia-Chile community partnership: trusts that hold equity, development corporations that deploy capital, oversight boards that veto harm, and dispute resolution that prevents capture.

### 7.1 Community Benefit Trust -- Fideicomiso de Beneficio Comunitario (FBC)

#### 7.1.1 Legal Structure -- Chilean Trust Law (Law 19,802 / Law 20,712)

| Element | Specification |
|---------|---------------|
| Legal Form | Fideicomiso (Trust) under Law 19,802, modified by Law 20,712 (2014) |
| Settlor | Tardigradia Chile SpA |
| Trustee | Independent trust company (Sociedad Fiduciaria) -- bids every 5 years |
| Beneficiaries | Residents of host communes (defined by municipal registry, updated annually) |
| Protector | 3-person panel: 1 community elected, 1 municipal, 1 independent expert |
| Duration | 99 years or life of Tardigradia operations, whichever longer |
| Governing Law | Chile, courts of host region |

#### 7.1.2 Trust Assets and Income Streams

| Asset | Source | Annual Value (10-unit cluster) | Restrictions |
|-------|--------|------------------------------|--------------|
| Class B Equity (15% parent) | Tardigradia formation | Dividends ~$1.1M/yr | Cannot sell <10 yrs; tag-along rights |
| Class B Equity (20% opco) | Opco formation | Included above | Same |
| Revenue royalty | 2% gross revenue | ~$2.0M/yr | Unrestricted |
| Water credits | Aegis-Forge zero-water | ~$355k/yr | Water projects only |
| Environmental bond coupons | Green bond issuance | ~$2.2M/yr | Community discretion |
| Supplier development returns | Equity in SMEs | Variable (target $500k/yr by Y5) | Reinvest or distribute |

Total projected Year 5: **~$6.2M/yr** for ~12,000 beneficiaries = **$517/person/yr** trust income (additional to $3,000 direct investment).

#### 7.1.3 Distribution Policy

- **Core Services (40%)**: Health, education, water, sanitation -- paid directly to providers
- **Productive Investment (30%)**: Microgrants, cooperative capital, SME loans -- managed by Development Corp
- **Direct Distribution (20%)**: Annual per-capita payment to registered residents (transparency: public registry)
- **Reserve/Endowment (10%)**: Building perpetuity fund for post-mining transition

Decision process: Annual Assembly (all beneficiaries) approves budget. Protector panel approves quarterly disbursements. Trustee executes.

### 7.2 Community Development Corporation -- Corporación de Desarrollo Comunitario (CDC)

#### 7.2.1 Structure -- Private Non-Profit (Law 20,500 / Law 19,862)

| Element | Specification |
|---------|---------------|
| Legal Form | Corporación de Derecho Privado sin Fines de Lucro |
| Members | Community orgs (Juntas de Vecinos, Indigenous associations, unions, NGOs) |
| Board | 9 directors: 4 community elected, 2 municipal, 2 Tardigradia, 1 independent |
| Executive Director | Hired by board, 3-year term, performance-based |
| Staff | 8-12 professionals (investment, social, technical, admin) |
| Budget | Trust allocation (30% = ~$1.8M/yr) + project grants (Corfo, Gore, international) |

#### 7.2.2 Investment Mandate -- "Productive Diversification Beyond Mining"

| Sector | Target Allocation | Instrument | Example Projects |
|--------|------------------|------------|------------------|
| Agriculture/Water | 25% | Grants + concessional loans | Drip irrigation, greenhouse cooperatives, olive/oil |
| Tourism/Heritage | 15% | Equity + grants | Community lodges, archaeology tours, dark sky (Atacama) |
| Renewable Energy | 20% | Project finance equity | Community solar (net billing), green hydrogen pilot |
| Technology/Training | 15% | Grants | Coding bootcamps, magnet tech certifications, FabLabs |
| Housing/Infrastructure | 15% | Subsidized loans | Home improvement, community centers, broadband |
| Financial Inclusion | 10% | Revolving fund | Community credit union, microinsurance |

Investment committee: 5 members (2 community, 1 municipal, 1 Tardigradia, 1 independent). Decisions by majority, community veto on displacement-risk projects.

#### 7.2.3 Accountability and Transparency

- **Public dashboard**: Real-time fund flows, project status, beneficiary counts (web + community kiosks)
- **Annual social audit**: Independent NGO (e.g., FIMA, OLCA) reviews equity, effectiveness, capture risk
- **Participatory budgeting**: 20% of annual budget allocated via community vote (1 person = 1 vote, registered residents)
- **Whistleblower channel**: Anonymous reporting to Protector panel + Contraloría General de la República

### 7.3 Operational Oversight Board -- Comité de Vigilancia Operacional (CVO)

#### 7.3.1 Mandate -- Real-Time Operational Veto

The CVO is NOT a consultative body -- it has binding veto authority on:

1. Water extraction > permitted baseline
2. Emissions exceedance > 10% of RCA limits
3. Tailings facility modification (design, capacity, closure)
4. Community health impact (epidemiological threshold breach)
5. Indigenous cultural site disturbance
6. Emergency shutdown request (any director can trigger 72-hr review)

#### 7.3.2 Composition and Operations

| Role | Appointment | Term | Veto Weight |
|------|-------------|------|-------------|
| Community Rep (x3) | Elected by community assembly | 2 years (staggered) | 1 each |
| Municipal Rep (x2) | Mayor appointment (council ratify) | 2 years | 1 each |
| Independent Expert (x2) | Protector panel appoints | 3 years | 1 each |
| Tardigradia Rep (x1) | Company appoints | At will | 1 (no veto) |
| Sernageomin Liaison | Sernageomin designates | Permanent | Advisory |
| DGA Liaison | DGA designates | Permanent | Advisory |

Quorum: 5 of 8 voting members. Veto: 4 of 7 community+municipal+expert votes. Tardigradia rep participates, informs, cannot veto.

Meeting cadence: Monthly scheduled + emergency within 24 hours. Minutes public within 48 hours (redacted for trade secrets).

#### 7.3.3 Data Access -- Information Symmetry

Tardigradia provides CVO real-time access to:
- SCADA feeds: Water flow, energy use, emissions (continuous)
- Lab results: Water, air, soil, tailings (within 24 hrs of receipt)
- Production data: Throughput, grades, recovery, waste volumes (daily)
- Incident reports: All safety/environmental events (immediate)
- Financial flows: Trust payments, CDC investments, supplier payments (monthly)

Community laboratory (funded by Tardigradia, operated by CDC) duplicates 20% of samples for independent verification.

### 7.4 Dispute Resolution -- Escalation Ladder

| Level | Mechanism | Timeline | Binding |
|-------|-----------|----------|---------|
| 1 | Direct negotiation (CVO + Tardigradia ops) | 15 days | No |
| 2 | Mediation (Centro de Arbitraje y Mediación CAM Santiago) | 30 days | If agreed |
| 3 | Expert determination (3 experts: 1 each party, 1 joint) | 60 days | Yes (technical) |
| 4 | Arbitration (CAM Santiago, 3 arbitrators, Spanish, Chile law) | 120 days | Yes (all) |
| 5 | Courts (host region) | Standard | Yes |

Cost allocation: Tardigradia pays 70% of mediation/arbitration costs. Community legal aid fund ($200k/yr from trust) covers community costs.

### 7.5 Anti-Capture Safeguards

- **Term limits**: No director serves >2 consecutive terms (4 years max)
- **Conflict of interest**: Annual declarations, automatic recusal, public registry
- **Political party ban**: No active party officials on boards (verified by Servel)
- **Rotation**: Trustee company rebid every 5 years; CDC Executive Director 3-year max (1 renewal)
- **Transparency**: All meetings livestreamed, minutes public, financials real-time
- **Independent evaluation**: Every 3 years by international NGO (e.g., Natural Resource Governance Institute)
