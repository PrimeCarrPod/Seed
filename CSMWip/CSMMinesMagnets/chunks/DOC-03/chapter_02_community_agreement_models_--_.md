## Community Agreement Models -- Comparative Analysis
## 2. Community Agreement Models -- Comparative Analysis of Chilean Mining Convenios

Chilean mining companies have developed five major community agreement (convenio) templates over two decades. This chapter dissects each model, quantifies value flows, and extracts design principles for Tardigradia's mutual benefit framework. The goal is not replication but synthesis -- combining the strongest elements into a new agreement architecture suited for REE processing and magnet manufacturing.

### 2.1 The Five Reference Agreements -- Deep Dive

#### 2.1.1 Codelco-Andacollo (2008, renewed 2018) -- The Municipal Partnership Model

| Element | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| Parties | Codelco Chile, Municipality of Andacollo, Diaguita Communities |
| Annual Value | $12M (2023) / $14M (2024 adjusted) |
| Duration | 10 years with 5-year review |
| Governance | Tripartite committee: Codelco, Municipality, Community reps (3 each) |

Value distribution (2024):
- Local hiring quota: 40% of operational workforce (currently 38% achieved)
- Scholarship fund: 120 university/technical scholarships/year ($1.2M)
- Health: Codelco funds 60% of Andacollo hospital operating budget ($3.5M)
- Water: $2M/yr for aquifer monitoring, rural potable water, irrigation efficiency
- Productive development: $1.5M for agriculture, tourism, artisan cooperatives
- Community infrastructure: $2M for roads, community centers, sports facilities
- Environmental monitoring: Independent community lab ($800k/yr)

Innovation: Municipal co-investment -- Andacollo matches Codelco 1:3 on productive projects. Municipality becomes equity partner in outcomes, not just recipient.

#### 2.1.2 Antofagasta Minerals -- Los Pelambres (2014, renewed 2022) -- The Water Security Model

| Element | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| Parties | Minera Los Pelambres (Antofagasta Minerals), Caimanes/Quilimari communities, Choapa Province municipalities |
| Annual Value | $8M base + $3M water infrastructure fund |
| Duration | 8 years |
| Governance | Water Council (company, communities, DGA, municipality) + Development Fund board |

Value distribution:
- Water infrastructure: $3M/yr for desalination pipeline (400 L/s), aquifer recharge, irrigation modernization
- Education: $1.5M for technical training (mining maintenance, water management), school infrastructure
- Productive development: $1M for small agriculture, goat farming, artisanal fishing support
- Health: $800k for telemedicine, mobile clinics, mental health programs
- Environmental: Community water quality monitoring network (15 stations, real-time data to communities)
- Local employment: 35% target from Choapa Province (current 32%)

Innovation: Water as organizing principle. All benefits trace to water security. Community Water Council has veto power on water-affecting operational changes.

#### 2.1.3 Candelaria-Tierra Amarilla (2017) -- The Large-Scale Fund Model

| Element | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| Parties | Lundin Mining (Candelaria), Municipality of Tierra Amarilla, Diaguita Communities |
| Annual Value | $25M/yr + $200M endowment fund |
| Duration | Life of mine (estimated 2040+) |
| Governance | Fund administered by independent foundation (50% community, 25% company, 25% independent) |

Value distribution:
- Endowment fund: $200M invested, only returns spent (target 4% real = $8M/yr perpetuity)
- Annual operations: $25M for hiring (40% local), health center, scholarships, housing, roads
- Environmental monitoring: $2M/yr independent, community-accessible data portal
- Local procurement: 30% target from Atacama Region SMEs
- Cultural heritage: $500k/yr for Diaguita language, archaeology, ceremony spaces

Innovation: Perpetuity design. The $200M endowment ensures benefits continue AFTER mine closure. Foundation governance prevents political capture.

#### 2.1.4 Carmen de Andacollo -- Teck (2010, renewed 2020) -- The Health-Education Model

| Element | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| Parties | Teck Carmen de Andacollo, Municipality of Andacollo, Diaguita Communities |
| Annual Value | $10M |
| Duration | 10 years |
| Governance | Joint committee + independent auditor |

Value distribution:
- Health: $3M for hospital modernization, specialist visits, emergency transport
- Education: $2.5M for technical mining program at local liceo, scholarships, STEM labs
- Local procurement: 35% target, supplier development program for Andacollo SMEs
- Employment: 45% local hiring (highest in sector), apprenticeship pipeline
- Environment: $1M for tailings monitoring, dust suppression, biodiversity offsets
- Community infrastructure: $1M for civic center, sports, cultural preservation

Innovation: Health-education dual focus. Technical mining program at liceo creates direct pipeline to Teck jobs -- community owns the skills.

#### 2.1.5 Sierra Gorda -- KGHM (2016) -- The Small Community Model

| Element | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| Parties | KGHM Sierra Gorda, Municipality of Sierra Gorda, Atacameño Communities |
| Annual Value | $5M |
| Duration | 5 years (shortest) |
| Governance | Direct municipality-company agreement + community assembly |

Value distribution:
- Basic services: Municipality runs on this agreement (60% of budget). Water, electricity, waste, health post.
- Employment: 50% local hiring target (Sierra Gorda pop 3,200 -- limited pool)
- Training: $500k for heavy equipment, maintenance, safety certifications
- Community center: $1M capital + $200k/yr operating
- Environmental: $500k for air/water monitoring, community lab

Innovation: Municipality as primary counterparty. Small community, high dependency -- agreement IS the municipal budget.

### 2.2 Cross-Model Synthesis -- Design Principles for Tardigradia

| Principle | Source Model | Tardigradia Application |
|-----------|--------------|------------------------|
| Per-capita investment floor | All models | $3,000/person/year minimum direct community investment |
| Municipal co-investment | Codelco-Andacollo | Host municipality matches 1:4 on productive projects |
| Water as organizing principle | Los Pelambres | Aegis-Forge zero-water advantage = water credits to community |
| Perpetuity fund | Candelaria | $50M endowment per Aegis-Forge cluster (returns only) |
| Skills pipeline | Carmen de Andacollo | Magnet manufacturing apprenticeships at local liceos |
| Municipality as partner | Sierra Gorda | Mayors on Tardigradia Chile board, veto on community-impacting decisions |
| Independent governance | Candelaria, Carmen | 1/3 community, 1/3 company, 1/3 independent experts |
| Environmental veto | Los Pelambres | Community lab data triggers operational review |

### 2.3 Quantitative Comparison -- Per-Capita Investment

| Agreement | Community Pop | Annual Value | Per Capita | Per Capita Rank |
|-----------|---------------|--------------|------------|----------------|
| Sierra Gorda | 3,200 | $5M | $1,563 | 5 |
| Carmen de Andacollo | 10,000 | $10M | $1,000 | 6 |
| Codelco-Andacollo | 10,000 | $14M | $1,400 | 4 |
| Los Pelambres | 8,500 | $11M | $1,294 | 3 |
| Candelaria | 12,000 | $25M | $2,083 | 1 |
| **Tardigradia Target** | **Variable** | **$3,000 x Pop** | **$3,000** | **Floor** |

Tardigradia sets a FLOOR of $3,000/person/year -- 44% above the best existing model (Candelaria). This is justified by: (1) Aegis-Forge zero-water/zero-tailings premium, (2) REE processing value-add in Chile vs. export concentrate, (3) Magnet manufacturing jobs (higher skill, higher wage), (4) Counter-cyclical revenue (Fe/REE vs Cu).
