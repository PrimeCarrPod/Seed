## NGO and Civil Society Actors
## 9. NGO and Civil Society Actors -- OLCA, FIMA, Community Radio, Water Defense

Civil society in Chile mining regions is organized, networked, and influential. NGOs, community radio stations, water defense groups, and indigenous organizations shape public opinion, file legal challenges, monitor compliance, and negotiate directly with companies. Tardigradia must map this landscape -- not to manage it, but to engage authentically.

### 9.2 Engagement Modes -- From Opposition to Partnership

| Mode | Description | Example | Tardigradia Posture |
|------|-------------|---------|---------------------|
| Adversarial | Lawsuits, protests, international complaints | FIMA vs. Dominga, OLCA + Peine vs. Escondida water | Respect right to oppose; do not litigate against communities |
| Monitoring | Independent environmental/social audits | Community water testing (MODATIMA), dust monitoring | Fund and participate -- transparency builds trust |
| Dialogue | Structured multi-stakeholder forums | Codelco-Andacollo mesa, Candelaria-Tierra Amarilla mesa | Initiate and resource -- not just attend |
| Partnership | Co-design projects, shared governance | Teck-Andacollo health center, Antofagasta Minerals water fund | Aspire to this -- co-create value |

### 9.3 Strategic Engagement Plan for Tardigradia

1. Pre-entry transparency: Share Aegis-Forge technical specs (zero water, zero tailings) with OLCA, FIMA, MODATIMA BEFORE project submission. Invite technical review.

2. Community monitoring funding: Provide $50k/year/commune for independent water/air/soil monitoring by community radio + local university. Data public, real-time.

3. Indigenous technical capacity: Fund Atacameno/Diaguita/Aymara technical advisors for Consulta (legal right under ILO 169). Budget: $100k/community/process.

4. Media partnership: Advertise on community radio (Radio Semilla, Radio Tierra) -- not corporate ads, but community programming sponsorship (health, education, culture).

5. Grievance mechanism: Independent ombudsperson (mutually agreed) with binding resolution for community complaints. Not company-run.

6. Policy alignment: Publicly support water code reform (priority for human consumption), glacial protection law, tailings safety law. Be on the right side of history.

### 9.4 Risk of Non-Engagement

Companies that ignore civil society: Dominga (rejected), Los Bronces (blocked expansion), Pascua-Lama (abandoned after Barrick/Goldcorp spent $8B). Cost of engagement: ~$2M/year. Cost of opposition: billions in stranded assets.
