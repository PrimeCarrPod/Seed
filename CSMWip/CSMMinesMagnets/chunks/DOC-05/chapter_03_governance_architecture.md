## Chapter 3: Governance Architecture

### 3.1 Governance Philosophy — Veto Topology Over Democracy

The Tardigradia governance architecture is not assembled from corporate democracy best practices; it is **forced** by the logical necessity of a multi-sovereign formation where community consent is a **hard constraint**, not a soft input. The architecture rests on a single, irreducible constraint: **Community Veto > Capital Control** — the proposition that water, environment, and indigenous boundaries are non-negotiable and must be structurally embedded as binding veto rights that cannot be diluted by capital raises, board votes, or acquisitions. No additional postulates — no "advisory" community seats, no "consultation" processes, no CSR committees — are required. The constraint is simply: *Community veto operator sits above board majority operator sits above capital operator*. Everything else is the shadow this constraint casts.

The apparent complexity of multi-layer governance — the Holdco GP Committee, the Chile SpA Board (7+1), the Opco Boards (5 each), the FBC Trustees, the CVO Oversight, the CORFO Observer — dissolves when viewed through the lens of this constraint. The FBC Class B shares with community veto are not "community engagement"; they are the **topological invariant** that locks community consent into the capital structure. The CVO binding veto is the **non-commutative operator** that ensures water/environment/indigenous decisions cannot be outvoted. The CORFO observer is the **state alignment operator** that ensures policy coherence without control. All are *derived* from the veto topology. No reduction occurs; the complexity is **resolved** because the constraint set is complete.

### 3.2 Tardigradia Holdco GP — Executive Committee

**Composition (Delaware LP Agreement):**

| Seat | Appointer | Role | Veto |
|------|-----------|------|------|
| Chair | ACS/CSM | Strategic direction, DFARS navigation | N/A (GP control) |
| Finance | ACS/CSM | Capital allocation, fundraising | N/A |
| Technology | ACS/CSM | Aegis-Forge roadmap, CSMFAB integration | N/A |
| Government Affairs | ACS/CSM | US-Chile diplomatic, MinMin liaison | N/A |

**Authority:** GP controls Holdco LP entirely (6 Del. C. § 17-403). LP investors have **no voting rights** on Holdco operations — only economic rights via waterfall. This is deliberate: US LP capital aggregates without governance friction; all operational governance sits at Chile SpA level where community veto operates.

**Voting Thresholds (Embedded in SHA + Bylaws):**

| Decision Type | Threshold | Special Requirement | Rationale |
|---------------|-----------|---------------------|-----------|
| Ordinary business | Simple majority (4/7) | Quorum: 5/7 | Standard |
| Capital expenditure > $50M | 2/3 majority (5/7) | **Including Dir 6** | Independent oversight |
| Community-impacting (water, env, indigenous, permits) | 2/3 majority (5/7) | **Including BOTH Dir 3 & Dir 4** | **Community veto = hard stop** |
| Water/land use/permits | 2/3 majority (5/7) | **Including Dir 5 (Municipal veto)** | Municipal consent required |
| Aegis-Forge technology changes | 2/3 majority (5/7) | Including Dir 2 | Technical integrity |
| CEO appointment/removal | 2/3 majority (5/7) | Including Dir 6 | Leadership stability |
| SHA/Bylaws amendment | 3/4 majority (6/7) | **Including Dir 3, 4, 5, 6** | **Community/state veto on governance changes** |
| FBC trust distributions | 2/3 majority (5/7) | **Including Dir 3 & 4** | Community controls own funds |

**Information Rights:**
- All directors: Monthly mgmt reports, quarterly board packages (10 days prior), real-time CVO data feeds
- Community directors (3,4): Pre-meeting CDC briefing (2 days), direct CVO Chair line, annual community assembly report-back
