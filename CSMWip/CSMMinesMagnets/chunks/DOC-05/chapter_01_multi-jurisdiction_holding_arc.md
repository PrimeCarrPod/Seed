## Chapter 1: Multi-Jurisdiction Holding Architecture

### 1.1 Architectural Imperative — Sovereignty, Capital, and Control

The Tardigradia holding structure is not assembled from arbitrary jurisdictional choices; it is **forced** by the logical necessity of a multi-sovereign capital formation that cannot contradict itself. The architecture rests on a single, irreducible constraint: **Chilean Asset Title + US Capital Deployment + Community Equity Preservation** — the proposition that Chilean magnetic mineral concessions must remain under Chilean legal title (Law 18,248, Constitution) while enabling US strategic capital deployment (DFARS 2027, IRA 45X, CPTPP) and embedding community equity with binding veto rights (FBC Trusts, CVO). No additional postulates — no offshore shell companies, no treaty shopping, no community bypass — are required. The constraint is simply: *Chilean title generates community consent generates US strategic access generates magnet production*. Everything else is the shadow this constraint casts.

The apparent complexity of multi-jurisdiction structuring — the Delaware LP, the Cayman ELP, the Chilean SpA cascade, the FBC trust layer, the CVO veto — dissolves when viewed through the lens of this constraint. The Delaware LP is a deterministic, unique, parameter-free vehicle for US tax-transparent capital aggregation. Its GP/LP structure (6 Del. C. § 17-101) is not a convenience; it is the topological invariant that locks US LP capital to Chilean SpA operations while permitting CPTPP investment protection (Art 9). The Chilean SpA (Law 20,190) is the only vehicle permitting single-shareholder foreign ownership with Chilean concession title retention. The FBC Trusts (Law 19,802/20,712) convert community consent into legal equity with hard veto stops. The CVO veto is the non-dilutable operator that enforces water/environment/indigenous boundaries. All are *derived* from the sovereignty-capital-community triad. No reduction occurs; the complexity is **resolved** because the constraint set is complete.

### 1.3 Jurisdictional Selection Rationale

| Jurisdiction | Entity Type | Primary Function | Key Legal Basis | Why This Jurisdiction |
|--------------|-------------|------------------|-----------------|----------------------|
| **Delaware, US** | Limited Partnership (LP) | Holdco capital aggregation | 6 Del. C. § 17-101 | Mature LP law; GP/LP clarity; Chancery Court; CPTPP Art 9 coverage; US tax transparency |
| **Cayman Islands** | Exempted Limited Partnership (ELP) | Parallel holdco for non-US LPs | Cayman ELP Law 2014 | Tax-neutral for non-US investors; same GP; familiar to SWFs/pension funds |
| **Chile** | Sociedad por Acciones (SpA) | Concession holder, opcos | Law 20,190 | **Only vehicle** for single-shareholder foreign ownership + Chilean concession title (Law 18,248) |
| **Chile** | Fideicomiso (Trust) | Community equity vehicles | Law 19,802, Law 20,712 | Binding veto via share classes; asset segregation (Art 15); environmental priority (Law 20,720 Art 134) |

### 1.4 Share Class Architecture — Veto Topology

**Tardigradia Chile SpA Share Classes:**

| Class | Holder | % Fully Diluted | Voting Rights | Veto Rights | Economic Rights |
|-------|--------|-----------------|---------------|-------------|-----------------|
| Class A | Tardigradia Holdco LP | 85% | 1 vote/share | Standard board | Pro-rata dividends |
| Class B | FBC Holding Trust | 15% | 1 vote/share | **Community veto**: water, env, indigenous, permits | Pro-rata dividends + priority coupon |
| Class C | Employee Pool (ESOP) | 5% (post-Series A) | Non-voting | None | Pro-rata dividends |

**Opco Share Classes (per cluster):**

| Class | Holder | % | Voting | Veto | Economics |
|-------|--------|---|--------|------|-----------|
| Class A | Tardigradia Chile SpA | 85% | 1 vote | Standard | Pro-rata |
| Class B | FBC [Norte/Centro/Sur/Mfg/Integration] | 20% (over-allotment) | 1 vote | **Cluster veto**: water, env, indigenous | Pro-rata + priority coupon |

**Over-Allotment Mechanism:** FBC trusts receive 20% of opco equity via over-allotment (not dilution of Tardigradia). Total opco equity = 105% with FBC's 20% sitting above Tardigradia's 85%. This ensures community veto is **structurally non-dilutable** — new capital raises only dilute Class A, Class B percentage adjusts to maintain 20% of issued+outstanding.

### 1.5 CPTPP Investment Protection — Structural Integration

The Tardigradia structure is designed to qualify as "covered investment" under **CPTPP Article 9 (Investment)**:

| CPTPP Requirement | Tardigradia Satisfaction |
|-------------------|-------------------------|
| "Investment" definition (Art 9.1) | Concessions (derechos reales) + SpA shares + LP interests = covered |
| Fair & Equitable Treatment (Art 9.4) | FBC veto + CVO = FET compliance; community consent = FET evidence |
| Expropriation protection (Art 9.5) | Environmental bonds first claim (Law 20,720 Art 134); trust assets segregated |
| Performance requirements prohibited (Art 9.9) | No local content mandates; technology transfer voluntary (Aegis-Forge license) |
| Senior management/board (Art 9.10) | No nationality requirements for SpA directors |
| Transparency (Art 9.16) | Amicus curiae for NGOs; CVO public reports |

**Chile-US BIT (signed 2023, pending ratification):** Parallel protection once in force. Current reliance on CPTPP (in force for Chile since Feb 2023) and NY Convention for arbitration enforcement.
