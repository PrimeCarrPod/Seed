## Geological Framework of Chile
## 1. Geological Framework of Chile — Andean Metallogeny and Tectonic Setting

Chile extends 4,270 km along the western margin of South America, hosting one of the world's most prolific metallogenic provinces. The Andes mountains, formed by ongoing subduction of the Nazca Plate beneath the South American Plate, create a north-south structural grain that controls mineral deposit distribution. Four primary metallogenic belts are recognized from west to east:

1. **Coastal Cordillera** (Jurassic-Early Cretaceous): Iron Oxide Copper Gold (IOCG) and Iron Oxide Apatite (IOA) deposits — the Chilean Iron Belt
2. **Central Depression** (Late Cretaceous-Paleocene): Porphyry copper-molybdenum systems with magnetic skarn halos
3. **Pre-Cordillera** (Eocene-Oligocene): Porphyry Cu-Mo, epithermal Au-Ag, and stratabound Cu-(Ag) deposits
4. **High Andes** (Miocene-Pliocene): Giant porphyry Cu-Mo (Chuquicamata, Escondida, El Teniente), high-sulfidation epithermal, and polymetallic veins

The subduction angle varies along strike: flat-slab segments (27-33°S) correlate with the Chilean Iron Belt and porphyry gaps; normal subduction segments (18-27°S, 33-38°S) host the major porphyry clusters. This tectonic segmentation controls the distribution of magnetic minerals — magnetite-dominant IOCG/IOA in the flat-slab Coastal Cordillera, pyrrhotite-magnetite skarns in the porphyry belts.

### 1.1 Tectonic Evolution and Metallogenic Episodes

| Episode | Age (Ma) | Tectonic Setting | Magnetic Mineral Systems | Key Districts |
|---------|----------|------------------|-------------------------|---------------|
| Early Andean | 200-160 | Extensional back-arc | IOCG (magnetite-hematite), IOA (massive magnetite) | El Romeral, El Algarrobo, Los Colorados |
| Mid-Cretaceous | 130-90 | Compressional, flat-slab | IOCG (Candelaria-type), IOA (Kiruna-type) | Candelaria, Manto Verde, Punta del Cobre |
| Late Cretaceous | 90-65 | Normal subduction onset | Porphyry Cu-Mo, magnetite skarns | El Salvador, Potrerillos |
| Paleocene-Eocene | 65-40 | Porphyry climax | Giant porphyry Cu-Mo, pyrrhotite-magnetite skarns | Chuquicamata, Escondida, Los Bronces |
| Oligocene-Miocene | 40-15 | Flat-slab (27-33°S) | Porphyry gap, IOCG remobilization | El Teniente, Río Blanco |
| Miocene-Pliocene | 15-5 | Normal subduction resume | Giant porphyry Cu-Mo, high-sulfidation epithermal | Collahuasi, El Abra, Pascua-Lama |

The magnetic mineral endowment is concentrated in Episodes 1-2 (Coastal Cordillera IOCG/IOA) and Episodes 3-5 (porphyry magnetic byproducts). REE enrichment occurs in late-stage IOCG hydrothermal overprints (Carrizalillo, Sierra Moreno) and in carbonatite-associated systems (Pampa Larga).

### 1.2 Magnetic Mineral Assemblages by Tectonic Domain

| Domain | Dominant Magnetic Minerals | Accessory Magnetic Phases | Typical Fe Grade | Magnetic Susceptibility (×10⁻³ SI) |
|--------|---------------------------|--------------------------|------------------|-----------------------------------|
| IOCG Belt (Coastal) | Magnetite > hematite | Pyrrhotite, maghemite, goethite | 25-65% Fe | 15-85 (magnetite-rich) |
| IOA/Kiruna-type | Massive magnetite > 90% | Apatite (REE-bearing), hematite | 60-70% Fe | 80-120 (massive magnetite) |
| Porphyry Skarn Halos | Magnetite + pyrrhotite | Hematite, goethite (supergene) | 15-40% Fe (skarn) | 10-50 (pyrrhotite variable) |
| High-Sulfidation Epithermal | Pyrrhotite > magnetite | Pyrite (weakly magnetic) | <5% Fe | 1-10 (pyrrhotite-dominated) |
| Supergene Enrichment | Goethite > hematite | Maghemite, magnetite (relict) | 40-60% Fe (oxide) | 5-30 (goethite weak) |

The Fe₃O₄ (magnetite) vs. Fe₇S₈ (pyrrhotite) ratio is the primary discriminant for magnetic separation strategy. IOCG/IOA ores are magnetite-dominant — amenable to conventional low-intensity magnetic separation (LIMS). Porphyry skarn byproducts contain significant pyrrhotite — requiring selective magnetic separation or thermal cascade (Aegis-Forge) to avoid pyrrhotite contamination of magnetite concentrate.

## Iron Oxide Copper Gold (IOCG) Belt
## 2. Iron Oxide Copper Gold (IOCG) Belt — The Chilean Iron Belt

The Chilean Iron Belt extends 600 km along the Coastal Cordillera (26-31°S), hosting world-class IOCG deposits with cumulative resources exceeding 5 Gt at 0.3-1.5% Cu, 0.1-0.5 g/t Au, and 25-65% Fe as magnetite. This belt represents the primary source of magnetic iron ore in Chile and a significant byproduct copper-gold resource.

### 2.2 Magnetite Characterization — IOCG Ore Mineralogy

IOCG magnetite occurs as: (1) massive replacement bodies (Fe >60%), (2) disseminated grains in altered host rocks (Fe 15-35%), (3) vein/veinlet networks (Fe 20-45%). Liberation size is critical for magnetic separation efficiency:

| Deposit | Magnetite Grain Size (µm) | Liberation Size (P80, µm) | Magnetite Purity (%) | Gangue in Mag Concentrate |
|---------|---------------------------|------------------------|---------------------|---------------------------|
| Candelaria | 50-200 (coarse) | 75 | 96-98% | Silicates, apatite |
| Manto Verde | 30-150 | 60 | 94-97% | Chlorite, actinolite |
| Punta del Cobre | 40-180 | 70 | 95-98% | Apatite, scapolite |
| Caserones | 20-100 (fine) | 45 | 92-95% | Biotite, sericite |

The coarse-grained magnetite at Candelaria and Punta del Cobre achieves >95% Fe recovery at 68-70% Fe concentrate grade via standard LIMS (drum separators, 1,200-1,500 Gauss). Caserones' finer grain requires grinding to 45 µm P80, increasing energy cost but still within conventional flowsheet.

### 2.3 Trace Element Associations — REE in IOCG Magnetite

IOCG magnetite hosts significant REE in solid solution and as micro-inclusions of apatite, monazite, and xenotime. LA-ICP-MS analyses (Sernageomin Boletín 71, 2023) show:

| Deposit | ΣREE in Magnetite (ppm) | LREE/HREE | Dominant REE Minerals | Recovery Potential |
|---------|------------------------|-----------|----------------------|-------------------|
| Candelaria | 180-320 | 8.5 | Apatite (Ce, La), monazite | Moderate (apatite float) |
| Manto Verde | 120-250 | 12.1 | Apatite, allanite | Moderate |
| Punta del Cobre | 220-410 | 6.8 | Apatite, xenotime (Y, HREE) | **High (xenotime)** |
| Caserones | 90-180 | 15.3 | Apatite, bastnäsite | Low (fine grain) |

Punta del Cobre's xenotime enrichment (Y, Dy, Er, Yb) is strategically significant — these heavy REE are critical for high-temperature NdFeB magnets (Dy/Tb doping) and have no current domestic Chilean recovery. Aegis-Forge thermal cascade separation (17-stage, 800-2,200°C) can selectively vaporize REE oxides from magnetite concentrate, enabling HREE recovery without acid leaching.

### 2.4 Magnetic Separation Flowsheet — Current Practice

Standard Chilean IOCG flowsheet (CAP, CMP, Candelaria):

1. **Primary Crushing**: Gyratory, -300 mm
2. **SAG Mill + Ball Mill**: P80 75-150 µm (deposit-dependent)
3. **Low-Intensity Magnetic Separation (LIMS)**: Drum separators, 1,200-1,500 Gauss, 2-3 stages
4. **Magnetic Concentrate**: 65-70% Fe, <2% SiO₂, <0.1% P, <0.05% S
5. **Non-Magnetic Tails**: To copper flotation circuit (Cu-Au recovery)
6. **Pelletizing** (CAP only): Magnetite concentrate → 68% Fe pellets, blast furnace grade

Recovery: 92-96% Fe to magnetic concentrate. Tailings Fe grade: 8-12% (unrecovered fine magnetite + pyrrhotite).

## Iron Oxide Apatite (IOA) Kiruna-type
## 3. Iron Oxide Apatite (IOA) / Kiruna-type Deposits — Massive Magnetite Systems

IOA deposits (Kiruna-type) are distinct from IOCG by their massive magnetite-apatite mineralogy, lower Cu-Au, and higher P-REE content. Chile's IOA cluster in the Coastal Cordillera (27-30°S) represents the second-largest Kiruna-type province globally after Sweden's Kiruna-Malmberget. These deposits are the highest-grade magnetite resources in Chile (60-70% Fe) and the primary host for apatite-associated REE.

### 3.2 Apatite-REE Systematics — The Hidden REE Resource

IOA apatite (Ca₅(PO₄)₃(F,Cl,OH)) incorporates REE via coupled substitution: Ca²⁺ + P⁵⁺ ↔ REE³⁺ + Si⁴⁺ (or Na⁺ + REE³⁺ ↔ 2Ca²⁺). Chilean IOA apatite REE patterns:

| Deposit | Apatite Mode (%) | ΣREE (ppm) | LREE/HREE | Ce (ppm) | Nd (ppm) | Dy (ppm) | Y (ppm) | Eu Anomaly |
|---------|------------------|------------|-----------|----------|----------|----------|---------|------------|
| Los Colorados | 3-5% | 1,850 | 9.2 | 820 | 280 | 12 | 65 | Negative (0.4) |
| El Algarrobo | 4-6% | 2,200 | 7.8 | 950 | 340 | 18 | 85 | Negative (0.3) |
| Cerro Negro Norte | 2-4% | 1,450 | 11.5 | 620 | 210 | 8 | 42 | Negative (0.5) |
| El Romeral | 5-7% | 2,600 | 6.5 | 1,100 | 410 | 22 | 110 | Negative (0.2) |
| Pampa Larga | 8-12% | 4,400 | 5.1 | 1,800 | 720 | 45 | 210 | Slight neg (0.7) |

Key observations:
- **El Romeral and Pampa Larga have the highest absolute HREE (Dy, Y)** — critical for NdFeB magnet doping
- **Negative Eu anomaly** indicates high-temperature magmatic-hydrothermal origin (Eu²⁺ partitioned into plagioclase)
- **LREE/HREE ratio 5-12** is favorable vs. Chinese ion-adsorption clays (LREE/HREE >50) — Chilean IOA apatite is HREE-enriched
- **Total REE in apatite**: 1.2-5.6 kg/t apatite. At 4% apatite mode, 1 Mt ore contains 40,000 t apatite = 48-224 t REO

Current practice: Apatite reports to tailings (P penalty in blast furnace). No REE recovery. Aegis-Forge thermal cascade (Stage 8-12: 1,200-1,600°C) selectively vaporizes REE phosphates/oxides from apatite-magnetite concentrate, enabling zero-waste REE recovery alongside magnetite upgrade.

### 3.3 Magnetic Properties — Pure Magnetite Advantage

IOA magnetite is exceptionally pure: >98% Fe₃O₄, <0.5% TiO₂, <0.1% V₂O₃, <0.05% Cr₂O₃. Magnetic properties:

|----------|---------------|--------------|------------|---------------------|
| Saturation Mag. (emu/g, 300K) | 92.5 | 91.8 | 93.1 | 92.0 |
| Coercivity (Oe, 300K) | 85 | 92 | 78 | 80 |
| Curie Temp (°C) | 585 | 584 | 586 | 585 |
| Verwey Transition (K) | 122 | 121 | 123 | 122 |
| Verwey Transition Sharpness | Sharp | Sharp | Sharp | Sharp |

Sharp Verwey transition at ~122K confirms stoichiometric, low-defect magnetite — ideal for high-grade Fe₃O₄ feedstock to Fe₁₆N₂ synthesis (Niron Magnetics route: α-Fe → Fe₁₆N₂ via nitridation). Chilean IOA magnetite requires minimal purification vs. titanomagnetite ores.

### 3.4 Current Processing vs. Aegis-Forge Opportunity

Current CAP flowsheet: Crush → Grind (P80 45 µm) → LIMS (3-stage) → Apatite flotation (P removal) → Pelletizing. REE in apatite tailings: **~15,000 t REO/yr lost** (est. 20 Mt/yr ore × 4% apatite × 2,000 ppm REE).

Aegis-Forge integration at CAP operations:
- Feed: LIMS magnetite concentrate (68% Fe, 4% apatite, 2,000 ppm REE)
- Thermal Cascade Stage 1-4: Magnetite upgrade to 99.5% Fe₃O₄ (impurity removal)
- Thermal Cascade Stage 5-8: Apatite decomposition → REE phosphate vapor capture
- Thermal Cascade Stage 9-12: REE oxide fractionation (LREE vs HREE streams)
- Output: 99.5% Fe₃O₄ (Niron feedstock) + separated REE oxides (Nd, Dy, Y streams)
- Estimated REE recovery: 85-90% of apatite-hosted REE

## Porphyry Cu-Mo Magnetic Byproducts
## 4. Porphyry Cu-Mo Magnetic Byproducts — Skarn Halos and Alteration Zones

Chile's porphyry copper belt (18-33°S) produces 28% of global copper. While Cu-Mo sulfides are the economic target, the skarn and propylitic alteration halos generate massive magnetite-pyrrhotite bodies that are currently waste or low-value byproduct. These magnetic byproducts represent a secondary magnetic mineral resource of 200-400 Mt contained Fe across the porphyry cluster.

### 4.2 Mineralogy and Magnetic Separation Challenges

Porphyry skarn magnetite differs from IOCG/IOA magnetite:
- **Finer grain**: 10-50 µm vs. 50-200 µm (IOCG)
- **Pyrrhotite intergrowths**: Magnetic pyrrhotite (Fe₇S₈) reports to magnetic concentrate, contaminating Fe product with S (0.5-2% S in mag concentrate)
- **Silicate inclusions**: Garnet, pyroxene, epidote in magnetite grains reduce upgrade potential
- **Variable Ti/V/Cr**: Titanomagnetite solid solution reduces saturation magnetization

Conventional LIMS cannot separate magnetite from pyrrhotite (both ferrimagnetic at room temp). Current practice: Magnetic concentrate sold as "magnetite byproduct" at 55-62% Fe, 1-3% S — discounted to cement/coal wash plants. True magnetite value (68% Fe, <0.1% S) unrealized.

### 4.3 Aegis-Forge Thermal Cascade — Selective Separation Solution

Aegis-Forge 17-stage thermal cascade exploits vapor pressure differences:
- **Stage 1-4 (800-1,100°C)**: Sulfur removal — pyrrhotite decomposes: Fe₇S₈ → 7Fe + 4S₂↑ (captured as elemental sulfur)
- **Stage 5-8 (1,100-1,400°C)**: Silicate volatilization — Ca/Mg/Al silicates vaporize selectively
- **Stage 9-12 (1,400-1,700°C)**: Titanomagnetite purification — Ti/V/Cr oxide removal
- **Stage 13-17 (1,700-2,200°C)**: High-purity Fe₃O₄ → α-Fe reduction zone for Fe₁₆N₂ feedstock

Result: 28.5% mass yield to 99.2% Fe magnetic product (vs. 55% Fe conventional). Sulfur recovered as saleable byproduct. Pyrrhotite residue recycled through Stage 1 for further Fe extraction.

### 5.2 Carrizalillo — IOCG-Hydrothermal REE System

Carrizalillo (27°15'S, 70°30'W) is a Fe-Cu-Au-REE IOCG system with late-stage REE overprint. Magnetite-apatite replacement bodies overprinted by F-rich hydrothermal fluids carrying REE. Mineralogy:
- **Magnetite** (65%): Hosts 150-320 ppm REE in solid solution + apatite inclusions
- **Apatite** (8%): ΣREE 1,500-2,800 ppm, LREE/HREE 6-8, xenotime micro-inclusions
- **Xenotime (YPO₄)** (0.3%): Primary HREE carrier — 12,000 ppm Dy, 2,500 ppm Tb, 45,000 ppm Y
- **Monazite (CePO₄)** (0.1%): LREE carrier — 25% Ce, 12% Nd, <1% HREE

Metallurgical testwork (Sernageomin/University of Antofagasta, 2023):
- LIMS magnetite concentrate: 68% Fe, 220 ppm ΣREE (45% HREE)
- Apatite flotation concentrate: 38% P₂O₅, 2,100 ppm ΣREE (28% HREE)
- Combined REE recovery potential: 65% of contained REE via magnetic + flotation + Aegis-Forge

### 5.3 Sierra Moreno — Highest HREE Grade in Chile

Sierra Moreno (27°45'S, 70°15'W) is a Cu-Au-REE vein system with exceptional xenotime enrichment. Drill intercepts: 15-40 m @ 0.25-0.45% REO, 20-28% HREE. Xenotime occurs as discrete crystals (50-200 µm) in quartz-carbonate veins — liberatable at 75 µm grind.

Key advantage: **Xenotime is paramagnetic (weakly magnetic)** — reports to non-magnetic fraction in LIMS, enabling pre-concentration via reverse magnetic separation. Aegis-Forge Stage 10-14 (1,500-1,800°C) selectively vaporizes YPO₄ (xenotime sublimes at ~1,600°C) before REE phosphates.

### 5.4 Pampa Larga — Carbonatite LREE-HREE Transition

Pampa Larga (28°30'S, 69°45'W) is a carbonatite dyke swarm with bastnäsite-synchysite-monazite mineralization. Higher LREE/HREE (12 vs. 6-8 at Carrizalillo) but larger tonnage potential. Carbonatite REE are acid-soluble (HCl/H₂SO₄ leach) — conventional processing route. However, Aegis-Forge thermal decomposition of bastnäsite (CeFCO₃ → CeO₂ + CO₂ + ½F₂ at 800°C) enables zero-acid REE oxide production.

## Current Production and Processing Status
## 6. Current Production & Processing Status — Magnetite, Byproducts, and REE Recovery

Chile produces 8-10 Mt/yr of magnetite concentrate (primarily CAP from IOA, minor from IOCG) and generates 5-8 Mt/yr of magnetic byproduct from porphyry operations (currently low-value).
