# TITLE IV — BUILDING STANDARDS

## SEC. 601. SHORT TITLE

**SEC. 601.** This title may be cited as the "Fire-Resistant Building Standards Act of 2026."

---

## SEC. 602. FINDINGS SPECIFIC TO BUILDING STANDARDS

**SEC. 602.** The Congress finds the following with respect to building standards:

**(1) Structure Ignitability**
Structure ignitability is the primary determinant of structure loss during wildfires. Over 90% of structures destroyed in wildfires are ignited by embers, not direct flame contact. Fire-resistant building materials and designs can reduce structure ignition probability by 80%.

**(2) Building Code Deficiency**
Current building codes in most Wildland-Urban Interface jurisdictions do not require fire-resistant construction materials or designs. Only 5 states have adopted comprehensive WUI building codes. The majority of structures in the WUI are built to standards that do not address wildfire risk.

**(3) Retrofit Cost-Effectiveness**
Retrofitting existing structures with fire-resistant materials is cost-effective. The average retrofit cost of $15,000-25,000 per structure is far less than the average wildfire loss of $250,000 per structure. Every dollar invested in fire-resistant construction returns $10 in avoided losses.

**(4) Insurance Incentives**
Insurance companies are increasingly refusing to insure structures in the WUI that do not meet fire-resistant construction standards. Fire-resistant construction can reduce insurance premiums by 30-50%.

**(5) Life Safety**
Fire-resistant construction provides additional time for evacuation, reducing the risk of fatalities. Structures built to fire-resistant standards maintain structural integrity 3x longer during fire exposure.

**(6) Community Resilience**
Fire-resistant construction at the community level reduces the cascading fire spread that destroys entire neighborhoods. Communities with fire-resistant construction standards experience 70% lower structure loss rates.
