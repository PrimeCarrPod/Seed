## WILDFIRE PREVENTION AND GRID RESILIENCE ACT OF 2026

## SECTION 201. CONGRESSIONAL FINDINGS

The Congress finds the following:

**(1) Wildfire Crisis Severity**
Wildfires have increased in frequency, intensity, and destructiveness over the past two decades. The six largest fire seasons in United States recorded history have occurred since 2020. In 2025 alone, wildfires consumed 12.4 million acres, destroyed 47,000 structures, caused 312 fatalities, and generated $89 billion in direct damages.

**(2) Climate Change Acceleration**
Climate change is extending fire seasons, increasing the geographic extent of fire-prone areas, and intensifying fire behavior beyond historical norms. Average fire season length has increased from 120 days in 1990 to 265 days in 2025. Average temperatures in fire-prone regions have increased 2.3°F since 1980, reducing snowpack and extending the dry season.

**(3) Electrical Infrastructure Ignition Source**
Electrical infrastructure has been identified as the ignition source for multiple catastrophic wildfires. The 2018 Camp Fire (85 fatalities, $16.5B damages), 2020 Oregon Fire Siege (11 fatalities, $6B damages), and 2021 Marshall Fire (2 fatalities, $5B damages) were all ignited by electrical infrastructure.

**(4) Wildland-Urban Interface Expansion**
The Wildland-Urban Interface (WUI) has expanded by 40% since 1990, placing more communities at risk. An estimated 44 million homes are now located in the WUI. Current building codes in most WUI jurisdictions do not require fire-resistant construction materials or defensible space.

**(5) Vegetation Management Deficit**
Decades of fire suppression have created dangerous fuel loads on federal, state, and private lands. The USDA Forest Service estimates 80 million acres of national forestland require immediate vegetation management. Current treatment rates address less than 2% of the annual need.

**(6) Grid Hardening Underinvestment**
Electrical utilities have underinvested in grid hardening despite known ignition risks. Only 2,500 miles of the 200,000+ miles of overhead lines in high fire risk areas have been hardened. At current rates, full hardening would take 80+ years.

**(7) Early Warning Gap**
Early warning and detection systems cover less than 15% of high fire risk areas. Average alert time in covered areas is 8 minutes — insufficient for evacuation of vulnerable populations. False alarm rates of 35% reduce public trust and compliance.

**(8) Response Force Obsolescence**
Federal wildfire response forces rely on aging aircraft and inadequate coordination frameworks. The average age of the federal air tanker fleet is 52 years. Interagency coordination protocols have not been updated since 2003.
