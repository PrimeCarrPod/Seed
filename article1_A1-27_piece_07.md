# Piece 07: Supermultiplet Structure — 256 States in Superconformal Reps

The 256-dim Hilbert space decomposes into irreducible representations (supermultiplets) of the superconformal algebra $\mathfrak{su}(1,1|1)$. The decomposition is:

$$
\mathcal{H} = \bigoplus_{\text{supermultiplets}} \mathcal{M}_\lambda
$$

where each supermultiplet $\mathcal{M}_\lambda$ is labeled by its lowest-weight state (conformal primary) with quantum numbers $(h, r)$ under $(D, R)$.

**BPS supermultiplets (short multiplets):**

The 78 BPS states form short supermultiplets that saturate the unitarity bound $h = |r|/2$. For our algebra, the unitarity bound is $E \ge |Z|/2 = 39$, which is exactly the BPS condition. The BPS supermultiplets have dimension 2 (annihilated by $Q$ and $Q^\dagger$) or 4 (annihilated by one supercharge).

The 78 BPS states split into:
- 39 chiral primaries: annihilated by $Q$ and $\bar{S}$, with $h = r/2$
- 39 anti-chiral primaries: annihilated by $Q^\dagger$ and $S$, with $h = -r/2$

Each chiral primary $|\psi\rangle$ satisfies $Q|\psi\rangle = 0$, $\bar{S}|\psi\rangle = 0$. Its superconformal descendants are $Q^\dagger|\psi\rangle$, $S|\psi\rangle$, and $Q^\dagger S|\psi\rangle$. The full short multiplet has 4 states: $\{|\psi\rangle, Q^\dagger|\psi\rangle, S|\psi\rangle, Q^\dagger S|\psi\rangle\}$. But for our BPS states, $Q^\dagger|\text{BPS}\rangle = 0$ and $S|\text{BPS}\rangle = 0$, so the multiplet collapses to just the BPS state itself (dimension 1). Wait — the BPS states are annihilated by both $Q$ and $Q^\dagger$, so they are singlets of the superalgebra.

Correction: The BPS states are the ground states of the theory, annihilated by all supercharges. They form 78 singlet supermultiplets. Each is a 1-dim representation with $H=39$, $Z=78$, $R=0$.

**Long supermultiplets:**

The remaining 178 states form long supermultiplets of dimension 4 or 8. A long multiplet is built on a primary $|\phi\rangle$ with $h > |r|/2$. The descendants are generated by $Q, Q^\dagger, S, \bar{S}$. The dimension is $2^N$ where $N$ is the number of supercharges that don't annihilate the primary. For our N=1 algebra in 1D, $N=2$ (since $Q$ and $S$ are the two lowering operators), giving dimension 4.

The 178 non-BPS states form 44 long multiplets of dimension 4 (176 states) plus 2 additional states that are descendants of the Goldstino sector (bit 7 from A1-26 Piece 11).

**Explicit multiplet construction:**

For a primary $|\phi\rangle$ with $H|\phi\rangle = E|\phi\rangle$, $R|\phi\rangle = r|\phi\rangle$, the descendants are:
- Level 1: $Q|\phi\rangle$ (R-charge $r+1$), $S|\phi\rangle$ (R-charge $r-1$)
- Level 2: $Q S|\phi\rangle$, $S Q|\phi\rangle = -Q S|\phi\rangle$ (R-charge $r$)
- Level 2: $Q^\dagger|\phi\rangle$, $\bar{S}|\phi\rangle$
- Level 3: $Q^\dagger Q|\phi\rangle$, etc.

The supermultiplet closes at level 4 with $Q^\dagger Q S|\phi\rangle = 0$.

**Prime gap labeling:**

Each supermultiplet corresponds to a gap configuration. The BPS multiplets correspond to record gap configurations (78 of them). The long multiplets correspond to non-record gap configurations. The number of long multiplets is the number of gap configurations minus 78, divided by 4.

For directory 0.0 with 94,500 gaps, the number of gap configurations is $2^{255}$ (for 255 gap pairs). This is astronomically large. The 256-dim space is the 8-bit truncation, giving only 256 configurations. The supermultiplet decomposition in the 8-bit space is exactly as above: 78 singlets + 44 × 4 = 256.

**Connection to A1-31 Entanglement Entropy:**

The supermultiplet structure determines the entanglement spectrum. The BPS singlets contribute a topological entanglement entropy $\gamma = \log 78$. The long multiplets contribute area-law terms proportional to the number of cut bonds.

**Connection to Article 3 (Hilbert Space):**

The 8-bit Hilbert space of Article 3 is precisely the space of supermultiplets. The time evolution operator $U = e^{-iH\tau/\hbar}$ preserves the supermultiplet structure. The entanglement from gap correlations (A3-05) is organized by the supermultiplet decomposition.
