# TITLE III — PHARMACEUTICAL SUPPLY CHAIN

## SEC. 301. SHORT TITLE

**SEC. 301.** This title may be cited as the "Pharmaceutical Supply Chain Security Act of 2026."

---

## SEC. 302. FINDINGS SPECIFIC TO PHARMACEUTICALS

**SEC. 302.** The Congress finds the following with respect to pharmaceuticals:

**(1) Foreign Dependence**
Over 80% of active pharmaceutical ingredients (APIs) are manufactured overseas, primarily in China and India. This dependence creates significant supply chain vulnerability.

**(2) Drug Shortages**
Drug shortages affect millions of Americans annually. The COVID-19 pandemic exposed critical vulnerabilities in the pharmaceutical supply chain, resulting in widespread drug shortages.

**(3) National Security Risk**
Foreign dependence in pharmaceutical supply chains poses significant national security risks. Adversaries could use pharmaceutical supply as a geopolitical weapon.

**(4) Public Health Impact**
Drug shortages result in increased mortality, reduced quality of care, and increased healthcare costs. Ensuring domestic pharmaceutical production is essential for public health.

**(5) Economic Impact**
The United States spends $500 billion annually on pharmaceuticals. Domestic production would create 200,000 jobs and reduce pharmaceutical costs.

**(6) Quality Concerns**
Foreign pharmaceutical manufacturing raises quality concerns. Domestic production ensures compliance with FDA quality standards.

---

## SEC. 303. PURPOSES OF TITLE III

**SEC. 303.** The purposes of this title are to:

**(1)** Reduce U.S. dependence on foreign pharmaceutical imports from 80% to 50% by 2036.

**(2)** Create 200,000 direct jobs in pharmaceutical manufacturing.

**(3)** Invest $25 billion in domestic pharmaceutical production.

**(4)** Prevent drug shortages through domestic production.

**(5)** Ensure national security through secure pharmaceutical supply chains.

**(6)** Reduce pharmaceutical costs through domestic competition.

## SEC. 304. DEFINITIONS FOR TITLE III

**SEC. 304.** In this title:

**(1) "Active Pharmaceutical Ingredient (API)"**
Any substance or mixture of substances intended to be used in the manufacture of a drug product and that, when used in the production of a drug, becomes an active ingredient of the drug product.

**(2) "Finished Dosage Form"**
A pharmaceutical product in the form in which it is marketed for use, including tablets, capsules, injectables, and topicals.

**(3) "Generic Drug"**
A pharmaceutical product that is equivalent to a brand-name drug in dosage, strength, route of administration, quality, performance, and intended use.

**(4) "Essential Medicine"**
A medicine included on the World Health Organization Model List of Essential Medicines or the FDA list of essential medicines.

---

## SEC. 305. DOMESTIC PHARMACEUTICAL MANUFACTURING

**SEC. 305.** (a) ESTABLISHMENT.—There is established in the Department of Health and Human Services a Domestic Pharmaceutical Manufacturing Program.

**(b) MANUFACTURING GRANTS.—**

**(1) IN GENERAL.—**The Secretary of Health and Human Services shall provide grants for the construction or expansion of domestic pharmaceutical manufacturing facilities.

**(2) GRANT AMOUNT.—**Grants shall be up to $2 billion per facility.

**(3) PRIORITY.—**Grants shall be prioritized for:
(A) Facilities producing essential medicines
(B) Facilities in economically distressed communities
(C) Facilities with workforce development programs
(D) Facilities with environmental sustainability plans

**(c) API PRODUCTION GRANTS.—**

**(1) IN GENERAL.—**The Secretary shall provide grants for domestic API production facilities.

**(2) GRANT AMOUNT.—**Grants shall be up to $1 billion per facility.

## SEC. 306. DRUG SHORTAGE PREVENTION

**SEC. 306.** (a) EARLY WARNING SYSTEM.—

**(1) ESTABLISHMENT.—**The Secretary of Health and Human Services shall establish an early warning system for drug shortages.

**(2) FUNCTIONS.—**The early warning system shall:
(A) Monitor pharmaceutical supply chains
(B) Identify potential shortages
(C) Alert manufacturers and distributors
(D) Coordinate response to shortages

**(b) STRATEGIC STOCKPILE.—

**(1) ESTABLISHMENT.—**The Secretary shall establish a strategic stockpile of essential medicines.

**(2) STOCKPILE CONTENTS.—**The stockpile shall include:
(A) Antibiotics
(B) Anesthetics
(C) Cancer treatments
(D) Cardiovascular medications
(E) Insulin
(F) Other essential medicines

**(3) FUNDING.—**There is authorized to be appropriated $5 billion over 10 years.

---

## SEC. 307. AUTHORIZATION OF APPROPRIATIONS FOR TITLE III

**SEC. 307.** (a) IN GENERAL.—There is authorized to be appropriated to carry out this title $25,000,000,000 for the period of fiscal years 2027 through 2036.

**(b) ALLOCATION.—**Funds shall be allocated as follows:
(1) Manufacturing grants: $15,000,000,000
(2) API production grants: $5,000,000,000
(3) Drug shortage prevention: $5,000,000,000

## SEC. 308. PHARMACEUTICAL SUPPLY CHAIN ACCOUNTABILITY

**SEC. 308.** (a) ANNUAL PERFORMANCE REPORT.—**The Secretary of Health and Human Services shall submit an annual performance report on pharmaceutical supply chain programs.

**(b) GOVERNMENT ACCOUNTABILITY OFFICE REVIEW.—**The Comptroller General shall conduct a biennial review of pharmaceutical supply chain programs.

---

## SEC. 309. PHARMACEUTICAL SUPPLY CHAIN PREEMPTION

**SEC. 309.** (a) FEDERAL STANDARDS.—**The national pharmaceutical standards established under this title shall preempt any inconsistent state or local standards, except where state standards are more protective.

**(b) STATE AUTHORITY.—**States may adopt pharmaceutical standards that are more protective than the national standards.

---

## SEC. 310. SEVERABILITY

**SEC. 310.** If any provision of this title or the application thereof to any person or circumstance is held invalid, the remainder of the title and the application of such provision to other persons or circumstances shall not be affected thereby.

**SEC. 313.** (a) CIVIL PENALTIES.—**Any person who fails to comply with pharmaceutical supply chain requirements shall be subject to a civil penalty of up to $50,000 per violation.

**(b) CRIMINAL PENALTIES.—**Any person who commits fraud in connection with pharmaceutical supply chain activities shall be subject to criminal penalties.

## SEC. 316. PHARMACEUTICAL SUPPLY CHAIN PERFORMANCE METRICS

**SEC. 316.** (a) ESTABLISHMENT.—**The Secretary shall establish performance metrics for pharmaceutical supply chain programs, including:

**(1)** Domestic pharmaceutical manufacturing capacity
**(2)** Domestic API production capacity
**(3)** Drug shortage frequency and duration
**(4)** Dependence on foreign pharmaceutical imports
**(5)** Pharmaceutical quality compliance

**(b) REPORTING.—**The Secretary shall report annually on pharmaceutical supply chain performance against established metrics.

**(c) BENCHMARKS.—**The Secretary shall establish benchmarks for each performance metric and report on progress toward benchmarks.

---

## SEC. 317. PHARMACEUTICAL SUPPLY CHAIN EVALUATION

**SEC. 317.** (a) INDEPENDENT EVALUATION.—**The Secretary shall contract with an independent evaluator to conduct a comprehensive evaluation of pharmaceutical supply chain programs every 3 years.

**(b) REPORT.—**The independent evaluator shall submit a report to Congress on the evaluation findings.

## SEC. 318. PHARMACEUTICAL SUPPLY CHAIN SUNSET AND REVIEW

**SEC. 318.** (a) SUNSET.—**The authorities provided under this title shall expire on September 30, 2036, unless reauthorized by Congress.

**(b) REVIEW.—**Not later than September 30, 2035, the Secretary shall submit to Congress a comprehensive review of pharmaceutical supply chain activities.

---

## SEC. 319. PHARMACEUTICAL SUPPLY CHAIN TRANSITION

**SEC. 319.** (a) TRANSITION PLAN.—**Not later than September 30, 2035, the Secretary shall develop a transition plan for the orderly wind-down of pharmaceutical supply chain activities if the authorities are not reauthorized.

**(b) COMPLETION OF ACTIVITIES.—**The transition plan shall provide for the completion of all ongoing pharmaceutical supply chain activities and the fulfillment of all outstanding commitments.
