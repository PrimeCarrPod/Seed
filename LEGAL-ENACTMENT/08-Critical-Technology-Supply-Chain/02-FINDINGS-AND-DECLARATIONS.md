# FINDINGS AND DECLARATIONS — Act 08: Critical Technology Supply Chain Act

## SECTION 201. CONGRESSIONAL FINDINGS

The Congress finds the following:

**(1) Semiconductor Supply Chain Vulnerability**
The United States' share of global semiconductor manufacturing has declined from 37% in 1990 to 12% in 2026. Over 80% of advanced semiconductor manufacturing occurs in Taiwan and South Korea.

**(2) Rare Earth Minerals Dependence**
China controls 60% of global rare earth mining and 85% of rare earth processing. The United States imports 80% of its rare earth minerals from China.

**(3) Pharmaceutical Supply Chain Fragility**
Over 80% of active pharmaceutical ingredients (APIs) are manufactured overseas, primarily in China and India.

**(4) National Security Imperative**
Foreign dependence in critical technology supply chains poses significant national security risks.

**(5) Economic Competitiveness**
The United States' economic competitiveness depends on secure access to critical technologies.

**(6) Technology Leadership at Risk**
The United States' global technology leadership is at risk due to foreign competition.

**(7) Climate Change Nexus**
Critical technology supply chains are essential for addressing climate change.

**(8) Workforce Shortage**
The critical technology industries face severe workforce shortages.

## SECTION 201. CONGRESSIONAL FINDINGS — CONTINUED

**(9) Allied Cooperation Opportunity**
The United States has an opportunity to strengthen alliances through critical technology supply chain cooperation. Allied nations share concerns about supply chain vulnerabilities and are willing to cooperate on securing critical technology supply chains.

**(13) Funding Requirement**
Significant federal funding is needed to address critical technology supply chain vulnerabilities. Private sector investment alone is insufficient due to the scale of the challenge and the national security implications.

**(14) Timely Action Required**
Timely action is required to address critical technology supply chain vulnerabilities. Every year of delay increases U.S. vulnerability and allows competitors to strengthen their positions.

## SECTION 202. DECLARATIONS OF POLICY

**SEC. 202.** It is the policy of the United States that:

**(1) Supply Chain Security as National Priority**
Securing critical technology supply chains shall be a national priority on par with national defense and public health.

**(2) Domestic Manufacturing**
Domestic manufacturing of critical technologies shall be promoted through tax incentives, grants, and loan guarantees.

**(3) Research and Development**
Research and development in critical technologies shall be prioritized to maintain U.S. technology leadership.

**(4) Allied Cooperation**
Cooperation with allied nations on critical technology supply chains shall be strengthened.

**(5) Environmental Sustainability**
Critical technology production shall meet the highest environmental standards.

**(6) Workforce Development**
Workforce development programs shall be expanded to meet the needs of critical technology industries.

**(7) Equitable Distribution**
The benefits of critical technology investments shall be equitably distributed, with priority for disadvantaged communities.

**(8) Fiscal Responsibility**
Critical technology investments shall be managed with the highest standards of fiscal responsibility.

## SECTION 203. PURPOSES

**SEC. 203.** The purposes of this Act are to:

**(1)** Reduce U.S. dependence on foreign suppliers for critical technologies.

**(2)** Promote domestic manufacturing of semiconductors, rare earth minerals, and pharmaceuticals.

**(3)** Maintain U.S. technology leadership through research and development.

**(4)** Strengthen allied cooperation on critical technology supply chains.

**(5)** Create 1.5 million jobs in critical technology industries.

**(6)** Generate $500 billion in economic benefits over 10 years.

**(7)** Ensure national security through secure critical technology supply chains.

**(8)** Support the clean energy transition through secure supply chains for clean energy technologies.

**(9)** Promote environmental sustainability in critical technology production.

**(10)** Ensure equitable distribution of the benefits of critical technology investments.

---

## SECTION 204. DEFINITIONS

**SEC. 204.** In this Act:

**(1) "Active Pharmaceutical Ingredient (API)"**
Any substance or mixture of substances intended to be used in the manufacture of a drug product and that, when used in the production of a drug, becomes an active ingredient of the drug product.

**(2) "Critical Technology"**
Any technology designated as critical by the Secretary of Commerce, including semiconductors, rare earth minerals, pharmaceuticals, artificial intelligence, quantum computing, biotechnology, and advanced energy technologies.

**(3) "Domestic"**
Produced or manufactured within the United States or its territories.

**(4) "Foreign Entity of Concern"**
Any entity owned by, controlled by, or subject to the jurisdiction of a foreign country of concern, as designated by the Secretary of State.

**(5) "Rare Earth Minerals"**
The 17 chemical elements in the periodic table that are essential for modern technology, including neodymium, dysprosium, terbium, lanthanum, cerium, praseodymium, samarium, europium, gadolinium, holmium, erbium, thulium, ytterbium, lutetium, scandium, and yttrium.

**(6) "Semiconductor"**
A material that has electrical conductivity between that of a conductor and an insulator, used in electronic devices, including but not limited to integrated circuits, microprocessors, memory chips, and sensors.

**(7) "Strategic Reserve"**
A stockpile of critical materials or products maintained by the federal government to ensure supply chain resilience.

**(8) "Supply Chain"**
The network of organizations, people, activities, information, and resources involved in producing and delivering a product or service from supplier to customer.
