# TITLE II — SUPPLY CHAIN RESILIENCE
## Act 10: Coastal Port Infrastructure Act of 2026

### Short Title

**SEC. 401.** This title may be cited as the "Supply Chain Resilience Act of 2026."

### Definitions

**SEC. 402.** For purposes of this title:

**(A) "Supply Chain Resilience"** means the ability of the national supply chain to anticipate, prepare for, respond to, and recover from disruptions, whether caused by natural disasters, pandemics, cyberattacks, geopolitical events, or other factors.

**(B) "Port Capacity Diversification"** means the development of secondary and tertiary port facilities that can absorb trade flows during disruptions at primary ports.

**(C) "Strategic Reserve Port"** means a port designated under Section 412 of this title that is maintained in a state of operational readiness to activate during national emergencies, supply chain crises, or military contingencies.

**(D) "Supply Chain Visibility"** means the ability to track cargo flows, vessel positions, terminal capacity, and intermodal connectivity in real-time.

**(E) "Workforce Development"** means the training, education, and recruitment of the next generation of port workers.

### Authorization of Appropriations

**SEC. 403.** There is authorized to be appropriated to carry out this title $35,000,000,000 for the period of fiscal years 2027 through 2037, to be allocated as follows:

**(A) Port Capacity Diversification Program** — $15,000,000,000

**(B) Strategic Reserve Port Program** — $8,000,000,000

**(C) Supply Chain Visibility and Coordination Program** — $7,000,000,000

**(D) Workforce Development Program** — $5,000,000,000

### Subtitle A — Port Capacity Diversification Program

**SEC. 410.** Establishment of Program

**(a) IN GENERAL.** — The Secretary of Transportation, acting through the Administrator of the Maritime Administration, shall establish and carry out a Port Capacity Diversification Program to invest in the development of secondary and tertiary port facilities.

**(b) PROGRAM GOALS.** — The goals of the Port Capacity Diversification Program are:

> (1) Reduce concentration risk in the national container handling system;

> (2) Develop underutilized ports on the East Coast, Gulf Coast, and Great Lakes;

> (3) Create redundant capacity that can absorb trade flows during disruptions at primary ports;

> (4) Increase the resilience of the national supply chain.

**SEC. 411.** Regional Diversification Investments

**(a) EAST COAST DIVERSIFICATION.** — The Secretary shall allocate $6,000,000,000 to the expansion of capacity at secondary East Coast ports, including the Port of Charleston, the Port of Jacksonville, the Port of Wilmington (North Carolina), and the Port of Miami.

**(b) GULF COAST DIVERSIFICATION.** — The Secretary shall allocate $5,000,000,000 to the development of Gulf Coast ports as alternatives to West Coast gateways, including the Port of New Orleans, the Port of Mobile, and the Port of Tampa.

**(c) GREAT LAKES DIVERSIFICATION.** — The Secretary shall allocate $4,000,000,000 to the establishment of regular container services connecting Great Lakes ports to European and Asian markets via the St. Lawrence Seaway.

### Subtitle B — Strategic Reserve Port Program

**SEC. 412.** Designation of Strategic Reserve Ports

**(a) DESIGNATION.** — The Secretary of Transportation, in consultation with the Secretary of Defense and the Secretary of Homeland Security, shall designate 10 Strategic Reserve Ports based on geographic distribution, existing infrastructure, channel depth, intermodal connectivity, and military utility.

**(b) INITIAL DESIGNATIONS.** — The initial Strategic Reserve Ports are:

> (1) Port of Tacoma, Washington;

> (2) Port of Oakland, California;

> (3) Port of Charleston, South Carolina;

> (4) Port of Jacksonville, Florida;

> (5) Port of Mobile, Alabama;

> (6) Port of Gulfport, Mississippi;

> (7) Port of New Bedford, Massachusetts;

> (8) Port of Philadelphia, Pennsylvania;

> (9) Port of Duluth, Minnesota;

> (10) Port of Anchorage, Alaska.

**(c) MAINTENANCE AND READINESS.** — Each Strategic Reserve Port shall maintain the capability to achieve full operational capacity within 72 hours of activation and sustain operations for a minimum of 90 days without external support.

**(d) ACTIVATION AUTHORITY.** — The Secretary of Transportation, in consultation with the Secretary of Defense and the Secretary of Homeland Security, may activate Strategic Reserve Ports during declared national emergencies, significant supply chain disruptions, or military contingencies.

### Subtitle B — Strategic Reserve Port Program (Continued)

**SEC. 413.** Strategic Reserve Port Funding

**(a) MAINTENANCE FUNDING.** — Each Strategic Reserve Port shall receive annual maintenance funding of $10,000,000–$25,000,000 for minimum maintenance dredging, equipment pre-positioning, workforce training, and regular activation exercises.

**(b) ACTIVATION FUNDING.** — Upon activation, Strategic Reserve Ports shall receive supplemental funding for surge operations, including:

> (1) Emergency dredging;

> (2) Equipment mobilization;

> (3) Workforce recruitment and training;

> (4) Security enhancements;

> (5) Operational subsidies.

**(c) COMPENSATION.** — Port authorities shall be compensated for activation-related costs, including the costs of returning the port to its pre-activation condition.

**SEC. 414.** Strategic Reserve Port Exercises

**(a) ANNUAL EXERCISES.** — Each Strategic Reserve Port shall conduct annual activation exercises that test the port's ability to achieve full operational capacity within 72 hours.

**(b) JOINT EXERCISES.** — Strategic Reserve Ports shall conduct joint exercises with the Department of Defense, the Department of Homeland Security, and other federal agencies that test the coordination of port activation with military and emergency response operations.

**(c) AFTER-ACTION REVIEWS.** — Each exercise shall include an after-action review that identifies lessons learned and recommendations for improvement.

### Subtitle C — Supply Chain Visibility and Coordination Program

**SEC. 415.** National Port Data Integration Platform

**(a) ESTABLISHMENT.** — The Secretary of Transportation shall establish a National Port Data Integration Platform that provides real-time visibility into cargo flows, vessel positions, terminal capacity, and intermodal connectivity.

**(b) DATA SOURCES.** — The platform shall integrate data from:

> (1) Customs and Border Protection;

> (2) The Coast Guard;

> (3) Terminal operators;

> (4) Shipping lines;

> (5) Railroads;

> (6) Trucking companies;

> (7) Warehouse operators;

> (8) Other supply chain participants.

**(c) DATA STANDARDS.** — The platform shall use standardized data formats and application programming interfaces (APIs) that enable seamless data sharing while protecting commercially sensitive information and national security data.

**(d) ACCESS.** — The platform shall be accessible to federal agencies, port authorities, terminal operators, and other authorized users. Public-facing dashboards shall provide real-time information on port conditions and performance.

**SEC. 416.** Predictive Analytics and Early Warning System

**(a) DEVELOPMENT.** — The Secretary of Transportation shall develop artificial intelligence and machine learning systems that analyze data from the National Port Data Integration Platform to predict supply chain disruptions before they occur.

**(b) EARLY WARNING.** — The system shall provide early warning of potential disruptions 7–14 days in advance, giving supply chain participants sufficient time to adjust their plans and avoid or minimize the impacts of disruptions.

**(c) ADAPTIVE MANAGEMENT.** — The system shall incorporate adaptive management capabilities that adjust predictions based on new data and changing conditions.

**SEC. 417.** Information Sharing and Coordination Center

**(a) ESTABLISHMENT.** — The Secretary of Transportation shall establish a 24/7 Information Sharing and Coordination Center staffed by representatives of federal agencies, port authorities, and supply chain participants.

**(b) FUNCTIONS.** — The Center shall:

> (1) Monitor supply chain conditions in real-time;

> (2) Coordinate responses to supply chain disruptions;

> (3) Share information with supply chain participants;

> (4) Provide technical assistance to port authorities and terminal operators.

**(a) IN GENERAL.** — All iron, steel, manufactured products, and construction materials used in projects funded under this title shall be produced in the United States.

**(a) DESIGN STANDARDS.** — All infrastructure funded under this title shall be designed to withstand projected climate conditions through 2100.

**(b) NATURE-BASED SOLUTIONS.** — Projects shall incorporate nature-based solutions where feasible.

**SEC. 434.** Cybersecurity

**(b) ASSESSMENTS.** — Each project that includes digital systems shall include a cybersecurity assessment.

### Legislative Findings and Purpose

**SEC. 436.** The Congress finds that:

**(a)** The COVID-19 pandemic exposed fundamental fragilities in the national and global supply chain systems that depend on U.S. port infrastructure.

**(b)** U.S. container trade is concentrated at a small number of ports: the top 10 container ports handle 87 percent of total U.S. container volume, creating significant concentration risk.

**(c)** The 2021–2022 supply chain crisis demonstrated that congestion at the Ports of Los Angeles and Long Beach alone cost the U.S. economy an estimated $150–$250 billion in lost output.

**(d)** The shift to just-in-time manufacturing and retail inventory systems has reduced the buffer capacity that previously absorbed supply chain disruptions.

**(e)** The Bureau of Labor Statistics projects a 15 percent shortage in qualified port workers by 2030.

**(f)** The development of secondary and tertiary port facilities, the maintenance of Strategic Reserve Ports, the improvement of supply chain visibility, and the investment in workforce development are essential for the resilience of the national supply chain.

**SEC. 437.** The purpose of this subtitle is to:

**(a)** Reduce concentration risk in the national container handling system;

**(b)** Develop Strategic Reserve Ports that can be activated during national emergencies;

**(c)** Improve supply chain visibility through the National Port Data Integration Platform;

**(d)** Train the next generation of port workers;

The East Coast container market is heavily concentrated at the Port of New York and New Jersey, the Port of Savannah, and the Port of Virginia. ## II. STRATEGIC RESERVE PORT PROGRAM ANALYSIS

### A. Designation Criteria

Strategic Reserve Ports are selected based on:

**1. Geographic Distribution.** Ports are selected to ensure geographic distribution across the nation, ensuring that Strategic Reserve Ports are available to support military operations, disaster response, and supply chain recovery in all regions.

**2. Existing Infrastructure.** Ports with existing infrastructure that can be maintained at minimum cost and activated quickly are preferred.

**3. Channel Depth.** Ports with adequate channel depth to accommodate military and commercial vessels are preferred.

**4. Intermodal Connectivity.** Ports with adequate rail and highway connections are preferred.

**5. Military Utility.** Ports that can support military operations, including the movement of military vehicles and equipment, are preferred.

### B. Maintenance and Readiness Standards

Each Strategic Reserve Port shall maintain:

**1. Minimum Maintenance Dredging.** Annual maintenance dredging that ensures the channel is maintained at its fully authorized depth.

**2. Equipment Pre-Positioning.** Critical equipment, including container cranes, yard tractors, and generator sets, shall be pre-positioned at the port or at nearby locations for rapid deployment.

**3. Workforce Training.** Annual workforce training that ensures the port has the skilled workers needed to achieve full operational capacity within 72 hours of activation.

**4. Activation Exercises.** Annual activation exercises that test the port's ability to achieve full operational capacity within 72 hours.

### C. Activation Procedures

**1. Activation Authority.** The Secretary of Transportation, in consultation with the Secretary of Defense and the Secretary of Homeland Security, may activate Strategic Reserve Ports during:

**(a)** Declared national emergencies;

**(b)** Significant supply chain disruptions;

**(c)** Military contingencies.

**2. Activation Timeline.** Strategic Reserve Ports shall achieve full operational capacity within 72 hours of activation.

**3. Compensation.** Port authorities shall be compensated for activation-related costs, including the costs of returning the port to its pre-activation condition.

### A. National Port Data Integration Platform

**1. Platform Architecture**

The National Port Data Integration Platform is a secure, interoperable data platform that integrates information from multiple supply chain participants. The platform uses standardized data formats and application programming interfaces (APIs) to enable seamless data sharing while protecting commercially sensitive information and national security data.

**2. Data Sources**

The platform integrates data from:

**(a)** Customs and Border Protection — Cargo declarations, security filings, and inspection results;

**(b)** The Coast Guard — Vessel positions, navigation safety information, and port security data;

**(c)** Terminal operators — Terminal capacity, berth availability, and cargo handling status;

**(d)** Shipping lines — Vessel schedules, container positions, and cargo manifests;

**(e)** Railroads — Train schedules, rail car positions, and capacity information;

**(f)** Trucking companies — Truck positions, delivery schedules, and capacity information;

**(g)** Warehouse operators — Inventory levels, storage capacity, and throughput data.

**3. Data Standards**

The platform uses standardized data formats based on international standards, including:

**(a)** UN/EDIFACT (United Nations Electronic Data Interchange for Administration, Commerce and Trade);

**(b)** ISO 6346 (Container identification and marking standards);

**(c)** GS1 (Global standards for supply chain visibility);

**(d)** API standards based on RESTful architecture.

**4. Cybersecurity**

The platform incorporates robust cybersecurity measures, including:

**(a)** Zero-trust architecture;

**(b)** AES-256 encryption for data at rest and in transit;

**(c)** Multi-factor authentication for all user access;

**(d)** Regular cybersecurity assessments and penetration testing;

**(e)** Incident response procedures.

### B. Predictive Analytics and Early Warning System

**1. Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning**

The predictive analytics system uses artificial intelligence and machine learning algorithms to analyze data from the National Port Data Integration Platform and predict supply chain disruptions before they occur. **3. Adaptive Management**

The system incorporates adaptive management capabilities that adjust predictions based on new data and changing conditions. The system continuously learns from new data, improving the accuracy of its predictions over time.

## I. CASE STUDIES IN SUPPLY CHAIN RESILIENCE

### A. The 2021–2022 Supply Chain Crisis

**1. Background**

The COVID-19 pandemic exposed fundamental fragilities in the national and global supply chain systems. In 2021–2022, congestion at the Ports of Los Angeles and Long Beach reached unprecedented levels, with container ships waiting offshore for weeks, warehouses reaching capacity, and manufacturers facing critical shortages of imported components.

**2. Economic Impact**

The 2021–2022 supply chain crisis cost the U.S. economy an estimated $150–$250 billion in lost output. The crisis revealed that the nation's port system lacks the redundancy and flexibility necessary to absorb major disruptions.

**3. Lessons Learned**

The crisis demonstrated the importance of:

**(a)** Port capacity diversification — The concentration of container trade at a small number of ports created systemic vulnerability;

**(b)** Strategic reserve capacity — The absence of reserve capacity meant that there were no alternatives when primary ports became congested;

**(c)** Supply chain visibility — The lack of real-time visibility into cargo flows prevented proactive interventions;

**(d)** Workforce capacity — The shortage of qualified port workers limited the ability of ports to increase throughput.

### B. Hurricane Katrina and Gulf Coast Ports

**1. Background**

**2. Preparation.** The ability to prepare for potential disruptions through contingency planning, reserve capacity, and workforce training.

**3. Response.** The ability to respond quickly and effectively to disruptions, minimizing their economic and social impacts.

**4. Recovery.** The ability to rapidly restore normal operations following a disruption, including the repair of damaged infrastructure and the restoration of supply chain flows.
