# TITLE VII — OFFSHORE WIND PORT INFRASTRUCTURE
## Act 10: Coastal Port Infrastructure Act of 2026

### Short Title

**SEC. 901.** This title may be cited as the "Offshore Wind Port Infrastructure Act of 2026."

### Purpose

**SEC. 902.** The purposes of this title are:

> (1) To establish a national network of offshore wind port facilities to support the development of offshore wind energy;

> (2) To fund the construction, expansion, and modernization of port infrastructure needed to manufacture, assemble, stage, and deploy offshore wind components;

> (3) To promote the development of a domestic offshore wind supply chain;

> (4) To create jobs and economic development in coastal communities through offshore wind port investments;

> (5) To ensure that offshore wind port infrastructure is designed and constructed to withstand climate change impacts;

> (6) To coordinate federal, state, and local activities related to offshore wind port development;

> (7) To establish environmental protection standards for offshore wind port construction and operations;

> (8) To support workforce development for offshore wind port construction and operations.

### Definitions

**SEC. 903.** For purposes of this title:

**(A) "Offshore Wind Energy"** means electrical energy generated from wind turbines located in offshore waters of the United States.

**(B) "Offshore Wind Port"** means a port facility that is used for the manufacture, assembly, staging, deployment, or maintenance of offshore wind components.

**(C) "Offshore Wind Component"** means any component of an offshore wind energy facility, including turbine blades, nacelles, towers, foundations, transition pieces, substations, and cables.

**(D) "Staging Area"** means a port area used for the temporary storage and assembly of offshore wind components prior to deployment.

**(E) "Manufacturing Facility"** means a facility located at or near a port for the manufacture of offshore wind components.

**(F) "Assembly Facility"** means a facility located at or near a port for the assembly of offshore wind components.

**(G) "Deployment Facility"** means a facility located at a port for the loading and deployment of offshore wind components to offshore sites.

**(H) "Operations and Maintenance Facility"** means a facility located at a port for the maintenance and servicing of offshore wind energy facilities.

**(I) "Wind Turbine Installation Vessel"** means a vessel specifically designed for the installation of offshore wind turbines.

**(J) "Feeder Vessel"** means a vessel used to transport offshore wind components from a port to an offshore installation site.

**(K) "Heavy-Lift Capability"** means the ability to lift and move components weighing in excess of 1,000 tons.

**(L) "Deep-Water Access"** means navigational access to waters of sufficient depth for wind turbine installation vessels.

### Subtitle A — Offshore Wind Port Construction and Expansion Program

**SEC. 910.** Establishment of Program

**(a) IN GENERAL.** — The Secretary of Transportation, acting through the Administrator of the Maritime Administration, in coordination with the Secretary of Energy and the Secretary of the Interior, shall establish and carry out an Offshore Wind Port Construction and Expansion Program to fund the construction, expansion, and modernization of port infrastructure needed to support offshore wind energy development.

**(b) PROGRAM GOALS.** — The goals of the Offshore Wind Port Construction and Expansion Program are:

> (1) Construct or expand at least 15 offshore wind ports by 2035;

> (2) Ensure that each offshore wind port has the heavy-lift capability needed for offshore wind component handling;

> (3) Provide deep-water access for wind turbine installation vessels;

> (4) Create sufficient port capacity to support the deployment of 30 gigawatts of offshore wind energy by 2035;

> (5) Ensure that offshore wind ports are designed and constructed to withstand climate change impacts.

**SEC. 911.** Port Eligibility and Designation

**(a) ELIGIBILITY.** — To be designated as an offshore wind port, a port must:

> (1) Be located in a coastal region with planned or permitted offshore wind energy development;

> (2) Have or plan to develop deep-water access (minimum 40-foot depth) for wind turbine installation vessels;

> (3) Have sufficient land area for offshore wind component staging and assembly (minimum 50 acres);

> (4) Have or plan to develop heavy-lift capability (minimum 1,000-ton capacity);

> (5) Have intermodal transportation connections (rail, highway, or barge);

> (6) Commit to meeting the design and operational standards established under this title.

**(b) DESIGNATION PROCESS.** — The Secretary of Transportation shall:

> (1) Issue a request for proposals from eligible ports;

> (2) Evaluate proposals based on offshore wind development potential, port readiness, and community benefit;

> (3) Designate offshore wind ports in consultation with the Secretary of Energy and the Secretary of the Interior;

> (4) Publish a list of designated offshore wind ports in the Federal Register.

**(c) PRIORITY.** — Priority for designation shall be given to ports:

> (1) Located in regions with the greatest offshore wind energy potential;

> (2) That serve disadvantaged communities with limited economic opportunities;

> (3) That can be developed most quickly to support near-term offshore wind deployment;

> (4) That have existing port infrastructure that can be adapted for offshore wind use.

**SEC. 912.** Port Construction and Expansion Grants

**(a) GRANTS.** — The Secretary of Transportation shall provide grants to designated offshore wind ports for:

> (1) Construction of new offshore wind port facilities;

> (2) Expansion of existing port facilities to support offshore wind operations;

> (3) Dredging and deepening of navigational channels and berths;

> (4) Construction of heavy-lift quays and wharves;

> (5) Installation of heavy-lift cranes and equipment;

> (6) Construction of staging areas and component storage facilities;

> (7) Improvement of intermodal transportation connections;

> (8) Installation of backup power and resilient communications systems.

**(b) FUNDING ALLOCATION.** — Grants shall be allocated based on:

> (1) The offshore wind energy potential of the region;

> (2) The port's readiness for offshore wind operations;

> (3) The economic and community benefits of the project;

> (4) The cost-effectiveness of the proposed improvements;

> (5) The port's commitment to long-term offshore wind operations.

**(c) COST SHARE.** — The federal share of project costs shall not exceed 80 percent, except for ports serving disadvantaged communities, where the federal share may be up to 90 percent.

**(d) FUNDING.** — The program provides $10,000,000,000 for offshore wind port construction and expansion.

**SEC. 913.** Port Design Standards

**(a) HEAVY-LIFT CAPABILITY.** — Each offshore wind port shall be designed with:

> (1) Quays and wharves capable of supporting loads of at least 1,000 tons per square meter;

> (2) Heavy-lift cranes with a minimum capacity of 2,000 tons;

> (3) Roll-on/roll-off capabilities for large components;

> (4) Sufficient quay length for simultaneous loading of multiple wind turbine installation vessels (minimum 500 feet);

> (5) Adequate berth depth for wind turbine installation vessels (minimum 40 feet).

**(b) STAGING AREAS.** — Each offshore wind port shall include:

> (1) Staging areas of at least 50 acres for component storage and assembly;

> (2) Paved surfaces capable of supporting heavy components;

> (3) Adequate drainage and stormwater management;

> (4) Security fencing and access control;

> (5) Lighting for 24-hour operations.

**(c) DEEP-WATER ACCESS.** — Each offshore wind port shall have:

> (1) Navigational channels with a minimum depth of 40 feet;

> (2) Berths with a minimum depth of 40 feet;

> (3) Turning basins of sufficient size for wind turbine installation vessels;

> (4) Navigational aids and vessel traffic management systems;

> (5) Dredging maintenance plans to ensure continued deep-water access.

**(d) RESILIENCE STANDARDS.** — Each offshore wind port shall be designed to:

> (1) Withstand a 500-year storm event;

> (2) Operate during and after a 100-year flood event;

> (3) Incorporate sea-level rise projections through 2100;

> (4) Include backup power systems for critical operations;

> (5) Include resilient communications systems.

**(d) FUNDING.** — The program provides $500,000,000 for supply chain development.

### Subtitle C — Offshore Wind Staging and Deployment Facilities Program

**SEC. 930.** Establishment of Program

**(a) IN GENERAL.** — The Secretary of Transportation, in coordination with the Secretary of Energy, shall establish and carry out an Offshore Wind Staging and Deployment Facilities Program to develop the port infrastructure needed to stage and deploy offshore wind components.

**(b) PROGRAM GOALS.** — The goals of the Offshore Wind Staging and Deployment Facilities Program are:

> (1) Develop staging areas at all designated offshore wind ports;

> (2) Ensure that staging areas can accommodate the largest offshore wind components;

> (3) Provide deployment berths for wind turbine installation vessels;

> (4) Establish feeder vessel operations for component transport;

> (5) Create efficient logistics systems for offshore wind component delivery.

**SEC. 931.** Staging Area Development

**(a) REQUIREMENTS.** — Each offshore wind port shall develop staging areas that:

> (1) Have a minimum area of 50 acres;

> (2) Have paved surfaces capable of supporting loads of 1,000 tons per square meter;

> (3) Include adequate drainage and stormwater management;

> (4) Have security fencing and access control;

> (5) Have lighting for 24-hour operations;

> (6) Include component handling equipment (cranes, transporters, and forklifts);

> (7) Have adequate utilities (power, water, and communications).

**(b) COMPONENT STORAGE.** — Staging areas shall include designated storage areas for:

> (1) Turbine blades (minimum 50 blades);

> (2) Nacelles (minimum 20 nacelles);

> (3) Tower sections (minimum 60 sections);

> (4) Foundations (minimum 20 foundations);

> (5) Substations (minimum 5 substations);

> (6) Cables (minimum 100 miles of cable).

**(c) FUNDING.** — The program provides $1,500,000,000 for staging area development.

**SEC. 932.** Deployment Berths

**(a) REQUIREMENTS.** — Each offshore wind port shall develop deployment berths that:

> (1) Have a minimum length of 500 feet;

> (2) Have a minimum depth of 40 feet;

> (3) Can accommodate wind turbine installation vessels;

> (4) Have heavy-lift capability (minimum 2,000 tons);

> (5) Include roll-on/roll-off capabilities;

> (6) Have adequate fendering and mooring systems.

**(b) VESSEL ACCOMMODATION.** — Deployment berths shall accommodate:

> (1) Wind turbine installation vessels (WTIVs);

> (2) Feeder vessels;

> (3) Crew transfer vessels;

> (4) Cable-laying vessels;

> (5) Heavy-lift vessels.

**(c) FUNDING.** — The program provides $1,000,000,000 for deployment berth construction.

**SEC. 933.** Logistics and Supply Chain Management

**(a) LOGISTICS SYSTEMS.** — Each offshore wind port shall implement logistics management systems that:

> (1) Track component inventory in real-time;

> (2) Coordinate component delivery with installation schedules;

> (3) Manage staging area utilization;

> (4) Coordinate with shipping lines and transportation providers;

> (5) Provide real-time data to project developers and installation contractors.

**(b) DIGITAL PLATFORM.** — The Secretary of Transportation shall develop a digital logistics platform that:

> (1) Integrates data from all offshore wind ports;

> (2) Provides real-time visibility into component availability;

> (3) Optimizes component delivery schedules;

> (4) Coordinates vessel scheduling;

> (5) Supports supply chain planning and forecasting.

**(c) FUNDING.** — The program provides $500,000,000 for logistics systems and the digital platform.

> (5) Support the development of a domestic operations and maintenance industry.

**SEC. 941.** Operations and Maintenance Facilities

**(a) REQUIREMENTS.** — Each offshore wind port shall develop operations and maintenance facilities that:

> (1) Include office space for operations and maintenance staff;

> (2) Include warehouse space for spare parts and tools;

> (4) Include docking facilities for crew transfer vessels;

> (5) Include helicopter landing facilities;

**(a) VESSEL REQUIREMENTS.** — Offshore wind ports shall support crew transfer vessels that:

> (1) Can safely transport maintenance crews to offshore wind facilities;

> (2) Are designed for the specific conditions of the region;

> (3) Meet all applicable safety standards;

**(b) VESSEL TYPES.** — Crew transfer vessels may include:

> (2) Surface effect vessels;

> (3) Small waterplane area twin hull (SWATH) vessels;

> (4) Helicopter transfer services.

**(c) FUNDING.** — The program provides $500,000,000 for crew transfer vessel infrastructure.

### Supplementary Analysis AG — Offshore Wind Port Public-Private Partnership Framework

#### PPP Models

Offshore wind port projects may use the following public-private partnership models:

- **Design-Build (DB)**: Private sector designs and constructs the port facility
- **Design-Build-Finance (DBF)**: Private sector designs, constructs, and finances the facility
- **Design-Build-Finance-Maintain (DBFM)**: Private sector designs, constructs, finances, and maintains the facility
- **Design-Build-Finance-Operate-Maintain (DBFOM)**: Private sector designs, constructs, finances, operates, and maintains the facility
- **Concession**: Private sector operates and maintains the facility for a specified period
- **Joint Venture**: Public and private sectors share ownership and operation of the facility

#### PPP Procurement

Offshore wind port PPP projects shall follow a competitive procurement process:

- Request for Qualifications (RFQ)
- Request for Proposals (RFP)
- Best and Final Offer (BAFO)
- Contract negotiation
- Financial close

#### PPP Risk Allocation

Offshore wind port PPP projects shall allocate risks as follows:

- **Public sector risks**: Permitting, environmental review, regulatory changes
- **Private sector risks**: Construction, financing, operations, maintenance
- **Shared risks**: Demand, force majeure, changes in law
