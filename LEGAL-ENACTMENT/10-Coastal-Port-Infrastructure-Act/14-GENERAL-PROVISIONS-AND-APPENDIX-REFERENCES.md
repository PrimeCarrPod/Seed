# GENERAL PROVISIONS AND APPENDIX REFERENCES
## Act 10: Coastal Port Infrastructure Act of 2026

### Short Title

**SEC. 1401.** This section may be cited as the "General Provisions and Appendix References of the Coastal Port Infrastructure Act of 2026."

### Purpose

**SEC. 1402.** The purposes of this section are:

> (1) To establish general provisions applicable to all titles of this Act;

> (2) To provide definitions applicable throughout this Act;

> (3) To establish conforming amendments to existing Federal law;

> (4) To provide savings clauses preserving existing authorities and rights;

> (5) To establish severability provisions;

> (6) To establish effective dates for provisions of this Act;

> (7) To provide appendix references to supporting documents and data.

### Subtitle A — General Provisions

**SEC. 1410.** Applicability

**(a) IN GENERAL.** — The general provisions of this section apply to all titles and programs established under this Act unless otherwise specified.

**(b) CONFLICTS.** — In the event of a conflict between a general provision and a title-specific provision, the title-specific provision shall control.

**SEC. 1411.** Rules of Construction

**(a) HEADINGS.** — Headings and titles in this Act are for convenience only and shall not affect the interpretation of any provision.

**(b) REFERENCES.** — References to any statute, regulation, or other law include amendments, successor provisions, and implementing regulations.

**(c) SEVERABILITY.** — If any provision of this Act or the application thereof to any person or circumstance is held invalid, the remainder of this Act and the application of such provision to other persons or circumstances shall not be affected thereby.

**(d) SUNSET.** — Unless otherwise specified, the programs and authorities established under this Act shall expire on September 30, 2036.

**SEC. 1412.** Federal Preemption

**(a) MINIMUM STANDARDS.** — This Act establishes minimum standards and does not preempt any State or local law or regulation that provides greater protection for the environment, public health, or safety.

**(b) STATE AUTHORITY.** — Nothing in this Act shall be construed to limit the authority of any State to adopt and enforce laws and regulations that are more protective than the requirements of this Act.

**SEC. 1413.** Tribal Sovereignty

**(a) GOVERNMENT-TO-GOVERNMENT RELATIONSHIP.** — Nothing in this Act shall be construed to diminish or eliminate the government-to-government relationship between the United States and tribal governments.

**(b) TREATY RIGHTS.** — Nothing in this Act shall be construed to diminish or eliminate treaty rights or trust obligations of the United States to tribal governments.

**(c) TRIBAL SELF-GOVERNANCE.** — Nothing in this Act shall be construed to infringe upon tribal sovereignty or self-governance.

**SEC. 1414.** Nondiscrimination

**(a) IN GENERAL.** — No person shall be excluded from participation in, denied the benefits of, or subjected to discrimination under any program or activity receiving Federal financial assistance under this Act on the basis of race, color, national origin, sex, age, or disability.

**(b) ENFORCEMENT.** — The nondiscrimination requirements of this section shall be enforced in accordance with:

> (1) Title VI of the Civil Rights Act of 1964 (42 U.S.C. 2000d et seq.);

> (2) Section 504 of the Rehabilitation Act of 1973 (29 U.S.C. 794);

> (3) The Age Discrimination Act of 1975 (42 U.S.C. 6101 et seq.);

> (4) Title IX of the Education Amendments of 1972 (20 U.S.C. 1681 et seq.).

**SEC. 1415.** Labor Standards

**(a) DAVIS-BACON ACT.** — All laborers and mechanics employed by contractors and subcontractors on projects funded under this Act shall be paid wages at rates not less than those prevailing on projects of a similar character in the locality, as determined by the Secretary of Labor under the Davis-Bacon Act (40 U.S.C. 3141 et seq.).

**(b) CONTRACT WORK HOURS AND SAFETY STANDARDS ACT.** — No contractor or subcontractor contracting for any part of the work under this Act shall require or permit any laborer or mechanic to work in excess of 40 hours in any workweek unless such laborer or mechanic receives compensation at a rate not less than one and one-half times the basic rate of pay for all hours worked in excess of 40 hours.

**(c) COPLAND ACT.** — No contractor or subcontractor on any public building or public work covered by this Act shall induce any person to give up any part of the compensation to which such person is entitled under the Copland Act (40 U.S.C. 3145).

**SEC. 1416.** Environmental Compliance

**(a) NATIONAL ENVIRONMENTAL POLICY ACT.** — All projects funded under this Act shall comply with the National Environmental Policy Act of 1969 (42 U.S.C. 4321 et seq.).

**(b) ENDANGERED SPECIES ACT.** — All projects funded under this Act shall comply with the Endangered Species Act of 1973 (16 U.S.C. 1531 et seq.).

**(c) CLEAN WATER ACT.** — All projects funded under this Act shall comply with the Federal Water Pollution Control Act (33 U.S.C. 1251 et seq.).

**(d) CLEAN AIR ACT.** — All projects funded under this Act shall comply with the Clean Air Act (42 U.S.C. 7401 et seq.).

**(e) MARINE MAMMAL PROTECTION ACT.** — All projects funded under this Act shall comply with the Marine Mammal Protection Act of 1972 (16 U.S.C. 1361 et seq.).

**(f) NATIONAL HISTORIC PRESERVATION ACT.** — All projects funded under this Act shall comply with the National Historic Preservation Act (54 U.S.C. 306108).

**SEC. 1417.** Buy America Compliance

**(a) DOMESTIC CONTENT.** — All iron and steel products, manufactured products, and construction materials used in projects funded under this Act shall be produced in the United States, consistent with the Build America, Buy America Act.

**(b) WAIVER.** — The head of the relevant Federal agency may waive the domestic content requirement only in accordance with section 1230(c).

**(c) REPORTING.** — Any waiver issued under this section shall be reported to the appropriate congressional committees and included in the annual report.

### Subtitle H — Appendix References

**SEC. 1480.** Appendix A: National Coastal Vulnerability Assessment Methodology

**(a) SCOPE.** — The National Coastal Vulnerability Assessment shall evaluate:

> (1) Coastal flood risk for all United States coastal counties;

> (2) Sea level rise projections based on the most recent National Climate Assessment;

> (3) Storm surge exposure based on historical hurricane and nor'easter data;

> (4) Coastal erosion rates based on historical shoreline change data;

> (5) Infrastructure vulnerability based on asset condition and exposure assessments.

**(b) METHODOLOGY.** — The Assessment shall employ:

> (1) LiDAR-based elevation data;

> (2) Hydrodynamic modeling of storm surge and wave action;

> (3) Sea level rise projections from the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change;

> (4) Asset condition data from the Army Corps of Engineers and port authorities;

> (5) Economic impact modeling of flood damage scenarios.

**(c) DELIVERABLES.** — The Assessment shall produce:

> (1) County-level vulnerability scores;

> (2) Infrastructure risk rankings;

> (3) Economic impact projections;

> (4) Adaptation priority recommendations;

> (5) Interactive mapping tools.

**SEC. 1481.** Appendix B: Harbor Deepening Priority List

**(a) CRITERIA.** — The harbor deepening priority list shall rank ports based on:

> (1) Economic significance (cargo volume, economic output, employment);

> (2) Navigation safety risk (vessel groundings, collisions, near-misses);

> (3) Readiness for construction (environmental review status, design completion);

> (4) Cost-benefit ratio;

> (5) Environmental justice considerations.

**(b) METHODOLOGY.** — The priority list shall be developed using:

> (1) Army Corps of Engineers channel condition data;

> (2) U.S. Customs and Border Protection cargo data;

> (3) Bureau of Economic Analysis economic data;

> (4) Coast Guard navigation safety data;

> (5) Environmental justice screening tools.

**(c) UPDATES.** — The priority list shall be updated annually.

**SEC. 1482.** Appendix C: Port Resilience Hub Designation Criteria

**(a) MINIMUM REQUIREMENTS.** — Port Resilience Hubs shall meet the following minimum requirements:

> (1) Serve a population of at least 50,000 within the port's service area;

> (2) Handle at least 1 million tons of cargo or 100,000 TEUs annually;

> (3) Have documented vulnerability to coastal flooding or storm surge;

> (4) Have a port resilience master plan;

> (5) Have demonstrated community engagement and environmental justice commitments.

**(b) DESIGNATION PROCESS.** — The designation process shall include:

> (1) Application by the port authority;

> (2) Review by the Department of Transportation;

> (3) Public comment period;

> (4) Final designation by the Secretary of Transportation.

**(c) BENEFITS.** — Designated Port Resilience Hubs shall receive:

> (1) Priority consideration for funding under this Act;

> (2) Technical assistance from the Department of Transportation;

> (3) Coordination support from the Interagency Task Force.

**SEC. 1483.** Appendix D: Green Shipping Corridor Designation Criteria

**(a) MINIMUM REQUIREMENTS.** — Green shipping corridors shall meet the following minimum requirements:

> (1) Connect at least two ports with documented vessel traffic;

> (2) Have commitments from participating ports to provide zero-emission bunkering;

> (3) Have commitments from participating vessel operators to deploy zero-emission vessels;

> (4) Have a corridor implementation plan with measurable emissions reduction targets;

> (5) Have international agreements with partner nations, as applicable.

**(b) DESIGNATION PROCESS.** — The designation process shall include:

> (1) Application by participating ports and vessel operators;

> (2) Review by the Department of Transportation;

> (3) Consultation with international partners;

> (4) Final designation by the Secretary of Transportation.

**SEC. 1484.** Appendix E: Green Port Certification Program

**(a) CERTIFICATION LEVELS.** — The Green Port Certification Program shall include the following levels:

> (1) **Bronze.** Basic environmental compliance and commitment to continuous improvement;

> (2) **Silver.** Demonstrated environmental performance improvements and implementation of best practices;

> (3) **Gold.** Leadership in environmental performance and innovation;

> (4) **Platinum.** Excellence in environmental performance, innovation, and community engagement.

**(b) CERTIFICATION CRITERIA.** — Certification shall be based on:

> (1) Air quality performance;

> (2) Water quality performance;

> (3) Energy efficiency and renewable energy use;

> (4) Waste management and recycling;

> (5) Environmental management systems;

> (6) Community engagement and environmental justice.

**(c) CERTIFICATION PROCESS.** — The certification process shall include:

> (1) Self-assessment by the port;

> (3) Third-party verification;

> (4) Certification decision by the Department of Transportation;

> (5) Periodic recertification.
