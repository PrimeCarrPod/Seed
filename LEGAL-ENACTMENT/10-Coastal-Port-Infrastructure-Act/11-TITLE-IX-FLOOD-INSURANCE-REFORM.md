# TITLE IX — FLOOD INSURANCE REFORM
## Act 10: Coastal Port Infrastructure Act of 2026

### Short Title

**SEC. 1101.** This title may be cited as the "Coastal Flood Insurance Reform Act of 2026."

### Purpose

**SEC. 1102.** The purposes of this title are:

> (1) To reform the National Flood Insurance Program to better protect coastal port infrastructure and communities;

> (2) To establish risk-based flood insurance rates that reflect the true risk of flooding;

> (3) To promote flood mitigation and resilience through insurance incentives;

> (4) To ensure the long-term financial sustainability of the National Flood Insurance Program;

> (5) To protect taxpayers from bearing the cost of repeated flood losses;

> (6) To encourage the adoption of flood-resistant construction standards;

> (7) To provide affordable flood insurance for low- and moderate-income property owners;

> (8) To integrate climate change projections into flood risk assessments.

### Definitions

**SEC. 1103.** For purposes of this title:

**(A) "Flood Insurance"** means insurance coverage against physical damage by flood to real and personal property.

**(B) "National Flood Insurance Program (NFIP)"** means the program established under the National Flood Insurance Act of 1968.

**(C) "Flood Map"** means a map produced by the Federal Emergency Management Agency that shows flood risk areas.

**(D) "Base Flood Elevation"** means the elevation of a 100-year flood.

**(E) "Repetitive Loss Property"** means a property that has received two or more flood insurance payments of at least $1,000 each within any 10-year period.

**(F) "Severe Repetitive Loss Property"** means a property that has received four or more flood insurance payments of at least $5,000 each, or two or more payments that exceed the property's value.

**(G) "Mitigation"** means measures taken to reduce the risk of flood damage.

**(H) "Community Rating System"** means the system that provides insurance discounts for communities that exceed minimum floodplain management requirements.

**(I) "Sea-Level Rise"** means the increase in sea level due to climate change.

**(J) "Residual Risk"** means the flood risk that remains after structural flood protection measures.

### Subtitle A — Flood Insurance Rate Reform Program

**SEC. 1110.** Establishment of Program

**(a) IN GENERAL.** — The Administrator of the Federal Emergency Management Agency shall establish and carry out a Flood Insurance Rate Reform Program to transition the National Flood Insurance Program to risk-based rates.

**(b) PROGRAM GOALS.** — The goals of the Flood Insurance Rate Reform Program are:

> (1) Transition all NFIP policies to risk-based rates by 2035;

> (2) Ensure that flood insurance rates reflect the true risk of flooding;

> (3) Provide affordable coverage for low- and moderate-income property owners;

> (4) Encourage property owners to undertake flood mitigation measures;

> (5) Improve the financial sustainability of the NFIP.

**SEC. 1111.** Risk-Based Rate Implementation

**(a) RISK-BASED RATES.** — Beginning January 1, 2028, all new NFIP policies shall be issued at risk-based rates that reflect:

> (1) The property's flood risk based on updated flood maps;

> (2) The property's elevation relative to the base flood elevation;

> (3) The property's distance from flood sources;

> (4) The property's construction type and flood resistance;

> (5) The property's claims history;

> (6) Sea-level rise projections through 2100.

**(b) RATE PHASE-IN.** — For existing policies, risk-based rates shall be phased in over 7 years:

> (1) **Year 1 (2028)**: 15% of the difference between current and risk-based rates;

> (2) **Year 2 (2029)**: 30% of the difference;

> (3) **Year 3 (2030)**: 45% of the difference;

> (4) **Year 4 (2031)**: 60% of the difference;

> (5) **Year 5 (2032)**: 75% of the difference;

> (6) **Year 6 (2033)**: 90% of the difference;

> (7) **Year 7 (2034)**: 100% of risk-based rates.

**(c) RATE CAPS.** — Annual rate increases shall be capped at:

> (1) 15% per year for primary residences;

> (2) 25% per year for non-primary residences and businesses;

> (3) No cap for severe repetitive loss properties.

**SEC. 1112.** Affordability Provisions

**(a) MEANS-TESTED ASSISTANCE.** — The Administrator shall establish a means-tested assistance program that provides:

> (1) Premium discounts for low- and moderate-income property owners;

> (2) Grants for flood mitigation measures;

> (3) Relocation assistance for properties with severe flood risk;

> (4) Transition assistance for properties newly mapped into flood zones.

**(b) ELIGIBILITY.** — To be eligible for means-tested assistance, a property owner must:

> (1) Have a household income at or below 120% of the area median income;

> (2) Own and occupy the insured property as a primary residence;

> (3) Have a flood insurance policy through the NFIP;

> (4) Not have a history of repeated flood claims.

**(c) FUNDING.** — The program provides $1,000,000,000 for means-tested assistance.

**SEC. 1113.** Mitigation Credits

**(a) MITIGATION CREDITS.** — Property owners who undertake flood mitigation measures shall receive premium credits:

> (1) **Elevation**: 10-40% premium reduction for elevating above base flood elevation;

> (2) **Floodproofing**: 5-20% premium reduction for floodproofing measures;

> (3) **Relocation**: 50-100% premium reduction for relocating outside the flood zone;

> (4) **Acquisition**: Full premium elimination for properties acquired by the government;

> (5) **Natural infrastructure**: 5-15% premium reduction for natural flood mitigation.

**(b) COMMUNITY MITIGATION CREDITS.** — Communities that implement flood mitigation measures shall receive Community Rating System credits:

> (1) **Class 9**: 5% premium discount;

> (2) **Class 8**: 10% premium discount;

> (3) **Class 7**: 15% premium discount;

> (4) **Class 6**: 20% premium discount;

> (5) **Class 5**: 25% premium discount;

> (6) **Class 4**: 30% premium discount;

> (7) **Class 3**: 35% premium discount;

> (8) **Class 2**: 40% premium discount;

> (9) **Class 1**: 45% premium discount.

### Subtitle K — Flood Insurance Rate Methodology

**(a) RISK-BASED RATE CALCULATION.** — The Administrator shall calculate risk-based rates using the following methodology:

> (1) **Base Rate**: Determined by flood zone and base flood elevation;

> (2) **Elevation Adjustment**: Adjusted based on the property's elevation relative to the base flood elevation;

> (3) **Distance Adjustment**: Adjusted based on the property's distance from flood sources;

> (4) **Construction Adjustment**: Adjusted based on the property's construction type and flood resistance;

> (5) **Claims Adjustment**: Adjusted based on the property's claims history;

> (6) **Climate Adjustment**: Adjusted based on sea-level rise and precipitation projections;

> (7) **Mitigation Credit**: Reduced for flood mitigation measures;

> (8) **Community Credit**: Reduced for Community Rating System participation.

**(b) RATE FACTORS.** — The following rate factors shall be used:

> (1) Flood zone (VE, AE, X, etc.);

> (2) Base flood elevation;

> (3) Lowest floor elevation;

> (4) Number of floors;

> (5) Enclosure type;

> (6) Building type;

> (7) Occupancy type;

### Supplementary Analysis A — Flood Insurance Rate Methodology Detailed Technical Specifications

#### Risk-Based Rate Calculation Framework

The risk-based rate calculation shall use a comprehensive actuarial methodology that incorporates the following components:

**Base Rate Determination**

Base rates shall be determined using the following approach:
- Historical flood claims data (minimum 30-year record)
- Hydrologic and hydraulic modeling
- Catastrophe modeling for coastal flooding
- Statistical analysis of flood frequency and severity
- Regional calibration to local conditions

The base rate shall be expressed as a rate per $100 of coverage and shall vary by:
- Flood zone classification (VE, AE, X500, X, etc.)
- Base flood elevation
- Coastal vs. riverine flooding
- Storm surge exposure
- Wave action potential

**Elevation Factor Calculation**

The elevation factor shall be calculated using the following formula:

Elevation Factor = max(0.2, min(3.0, 1 + (BFE - LFE) × 0.05))

Where:
- BFE = Base Flood Elevation (feet above NAVD88)
- LFE = Lowest Floor Elevation (feet above NAVD88)
- Factor capped at minimum 0.2 (for structures 6+ feet above BFE)
- Factor capped at maximum 3.0 (for structures 6+ feet below BFE)

**Distance Factor Calculation**

The distance factor shall account for the property's proximity to flood sources:
- Distance to coastline (for coastal flooding)
- Distance to river or stream (for riverine flooding)
- Distance to storm surge inundation line
- Topographic position relative to flood pathways

Distance Factor shall range from 0.8 (farthest from flood source) to 1.5 (closest to flood source).

**Construction Factor Calculation**

The construction factor shall account for:
- Building type (wood frame, masonry, steel, concrete)
- Foundation type (slab, crawlspace, basement, piles)
- Number of stories
- Enclosure type (open, partially enclosed, fully enclosed)
- Flood-resistant materials usage

Construction Factor shall range from 0.7 (most flood-resistant) to 1.5 (least flood-resistant).

**Climate Change Adjustment Factor**

The climate change adjustment factor shall incorporate:
- Sea-level rise projections through 2100 (NOAA intermediate-high scenario)
- Changes in precipitation intensity (downscaled CMIP6 projections)
- Increased storm intensity projections
- Updated flood frequency analysis accounting for non-stationarity

Climate Factor shall range from 1.0 (no adjustment) to 2.0 (maximum adjustment for high sea-level rise scenarios).

### Supplementary Analysis R — Flood Insurance Detailed Rate Tables and Examples

#### Residential Rate Examples

**Example 1: Elevated Home in AE Zone**

- Location: AE Zone, BFE = 10 feet
- Home elevation: 14 feet (4 feet above BFE)
- Coverage: $250,000 structure, $100,000 contents
- Deductible: $2,000

Rate Calculation:
- Base Rate: $0.50 per $100 (AE Zone)
- Elevation Factor: 0.8 (4 feet above BFE)
- Construction Factor: 1.0 (wood frame, elevated)
- Climate Factor: 1.2 (moderate sea-level rise)
- Mitigation Credit: 0.85 (elevated + floodproofing)
- Community Credit: 0.90 (CRS Class 8)

Annual Premium = $250,000 / 100 × $0.50 × 0.8 × 1.0 × 1.2 × 0.85 × 0.90 = $918

**Example 2: Non-Elevated Home in AE Zone**

- Location: AE Zone, BFE = 10 feet
- Home elevation: 8 feet (2 feet below BFE)
- Coverage: $250,000 structure, $100,000 contents
- Deductible: $2,000

Rate Calculation:
- Base Rate: $0.50 per $100 (AE Zone)
- Elevation Factor: 1.1 (2 feet below BFE)
- Construction Factor: 1.2 (wood frame, basement)
- Climate Factor: 1.2 (moderate sea-level rise)
- Mitigation Credit: 1.0 (no mitigation)
- Community Credit: 1.0 (CRS Class 10)

Annual Premium = $250,000 / 100 × $0.50 × 1.1 × 1.2 × 1.2 × 1.0 × 1.0 = $1,980

**Example 3: Coastal Home in VE Zone**

- Location: VE Zone, BFE = 12 feet
- Home elevation: 16 feet (4 feet above BFE)
- Coverage: $350,000 structure, $150,000 contents
- Deductible: $2,000

Rate Calculation:
- Base Rate: $1.20 per $100 (VE Zone)
- Elevation Factor: 0.8 (4 feet above BFE)
- Construction Factor: 0.9 (pile foundation)
- Climate Factor: 1.5 (high sea-level rise, coastal)
- Mitigation Credit: 0.75 (elevated + breakaway walls)
- Community Credit: 0.85 (CRS Class 7)

Annual Premium = $350,000 / 100 × $1.20 × 0.8 × 0.9 × 1.5 × 0.75 × 0.85 = $2,893

1. System identifies policies expiring in 90 days
2. System recalculates premium based on updated risk
3. Renewal notice is sent to policyholder
4. Policyholder reviews and accepts renewal
5. Policyholder pays renewal premium
6. Policy is renewed and effective

**Endorsement Procedures**

1. Policyholder requests endorsement
2. Agent/broker submits endorsement request
3. System recalculates premium based on changes
4. Endorsement is issued and effective
5. Premium adjustment is processed

#### Claims Processing Procedures

**Claim Filing**

1. Policyholder files claim (online, phone, or agent)
2. System creates claim record
3. System assigns claim number
4. Policyholder receives claim acknowledgment
5. System assigns adjuster

**Adjuster Inspection**

1. Adjuster contacts policyholder within 24 hours
2. Adjuster schedules inspection within 7 days
3. Adjuster conducts inspection and documents damage
4. Adjuster estimates damage and prepares report
5. Adjuster submits report to claims system

**Claim Determination**

1. Claims reviewer reviews adjuster report
2. Claims reviewer verifies coverage
3. Claims reviewer calculates payment amount
4. Claims reviewer approves or denies claim
5. Policyholder receives determination notice

**Payment Processing**

1. Approved claims are processed for payment
2. Payment is issued to policyholder
3. Payment is recorded in claims system
4. Claim is closed

### Supplementary Analysis BM — Flood Insurance Catastrophe Bond Framework

#### Catastrophe Bond Structure

The Administrator may issue catastrophe bonds with the following structure:

**Principal**: Up to $5 billion
**Trigger**: Industry loss index or parametric
**Term**: 3-5 years
**Interest Rate**: SOFR + spread
**Investors**: Institutional investors (pension funds, hedge funds, insurance-linked securities funds)

#### Catastrophe Bond Types

**Indemnity Trigger**
- Based on actual NFIP losses
- Requires detailed loss reporting
- Lower basis risk
- Higher moral hazard

**Industry Loss Trigger**
- Based on industry-wide losses
- Uses published industry loss data
- Lower moral hazard
- Higher basis risk

**Parametric Trigger**
- Based on physical parameters (flood depth, storm surge height)
- Uses independent data sources
- Fastest settlement
- Highest basis risk

**Modeled Loss Trigger**
- Based on modeled losses using catastrophe model
- Uses NFIP exposure data
- Lower basis risk than parametric
- Requires detailed modeling

#### Catastrophe Bond Process

1. **Structuring**: Determine bond structure, trigger, and terms
2. **Documentation**: Prepare offering documents and legal agreements
3. **Rating**: Obtain rating from rating agencies
4. **Marketing**: Market bonds to investors
5. **Pricing**: Determine interest rate and pricing
6. **Execution**: Execute bond issuance
7. **Investment**: Invest proceeds in collateral account
8. **Monitoring**: Monitor trigger conditions
9. **Settlement**: Settle bond if trigger is met

### Supplementary Analysis BY — Flood Insurance Detailed Actuarial Analysis

#### Loss Development Factors

The Administrator shall use loss development factors to estimate ultimate losses:

**Case Incurred Loss Development**
- 12 months: 60% of ultimate
- 24 months: 80% of ultimate
- 36 months: 90% of ultimate
- 48 months: 95% of ultimate
- 60 months: 98% of ultimate
- 72 months: 100% of ultimate

**Paid Loss Development**
- 12 months: 40% of ultimate
- 24 months: 65% of ultimate
- 36 months: 80% of ultimate
- 48 months: 90% of ultimate
- 60 months: 95% of ultimate
- 72 months: 100% of ultimate

#### Expense Ratios

The Administrator shall use the following expense ratios:

**Acquisition Expenses**
- Commissions: 15% of premium
- Other acquisition: 3% of premium
- Total acquisition: 18% of premium

**Operating Expenses**
- Salaries and benefits: 5% of premium
- Rent and occupancy: 2% of premium
- Technology: 3% of premium
- Other operating: 3% of premium
- Total operating: 13% of premium

**Total Expense Ratio**
- Acquisition: 18%
- Operating: 13%
- Total: 31%

#### Profit and Contingency

The Administrator shall include a profit and contingency provision of 5% of premium.

#### Indicated Rate Change

The indicated rate change is calculated as:

Indicated Rate Change = (Loss Ratio + Expense Ratio) / (1 - Profit Provision) - 1

Where:
- Loss Ratio = Losses / Premium
- Expense Ratio = Expenses / Premium
- Profit Provision = 5%

Example:
- Loss Ratio: 70%
- Expense Ratio: 31%
- Profit Provision: 5%

Indicated Rate Change = (0.70 + 0.31) / (1 - 0.05) - 1 = 1.01 / 0.95 - 1 = 6.3%

## FINAL NOTE ON FLOOD INSURANCE REFORM IMPLEMENTATION

The successful implementation of Title IX requires sustained commitment from all stakeholders, including federal agencies, state and local governments, insurance companies, agents and brokers, property owners, and the public. The Administrator of the Federal Emergency Management Agency shall provide leadership and coordination to ensure that the Coastal Flood Insurance Reform program is implemented effectively and efficiently.

The Coastal Flood Insurance Reform Act of 2026 represents a comprehensive approach to reforming the National Flood Insurance Program. By combining risk-based rates, mitigation assistance, community resilience, flood mapping, and affordable coverage, this title provides a robust framework for protecting coastal port infrastructure and communities from flood damage while ensuring the long-term financial sustainability of the NFIP.

The program's phased implementation timeline, comprehensive standards, and accountability measures ensure that the investments authorized under this Act will generate significant returns in terms of reduced flood damage, improved community resilience, and enhanced financial sustainability.

The Coastal Flood Insurance Reform program will serve as a model for flood insurance reform worldwide, demonstrating that it is possible to combine risk-based pricing with affordability measures, promote mitigation and resilience, and ensure equitable access to flood insurance for all property owners.

As climate change continues to increase flood risk in coastal areas, the investments authorized under this title will become increasingly important for protecting the nation's coastal port infrastructure, communities, and economy. The Coastal Flood Insurance Reform program is a critical component of the nation's climate resilience strategy.
