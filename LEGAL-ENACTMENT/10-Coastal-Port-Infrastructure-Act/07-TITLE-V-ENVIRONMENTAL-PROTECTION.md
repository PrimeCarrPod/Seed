# TITLE V — ENVIRONMENTAL PROTECTION
## Act 10: Coastal Port Infrastructure Act of 2026

### Short Title

**SEC. 701.** This title may be cited as the "Port Environmental Protection and Sustainability Act of 2026."

### Purpose

**SEC. 702.** The purposes of this title are:

> (1) To establish comprehensive environmental protection standards for all port infrastructure projects receiving federal funding under this Act;

> (2) To reduce air pollution, water pollution, and greenhouse gas emissions from port operations and maritime transportation;

> (3) To protect and restore marine ecosystems, wetlands, and coastal habitats affected by port activities;

> (4) To ensure that port development and expansion projects incorporate environmental justice considerations and do not disproportionately burden disadvantaged communities;

> (5) To promote the use of clean energy, zero-emission technologies, and sustainable practices in port operations;

> (6) To establish monitoring, reporting, and enforcement mechanisms to ensure compliance with environmental standards;

> (7) To create a framework for interagency coordination on environmental reviews of port projects;

> (8) To authorize funding for environmental remediation, habitat restoration, and pollution prevention at coastal ports.

### Definitions

**SEC. 703.** For purposes of this title:

**(A) "Environmental Justice"** means the fair treatment and meaningful involvement of all people regardless of race, color, national origin, or income with respect to the development, implementation, and enforcement of environmental laws, regulations, and policies.

**(B) "Disadvantaged Community"** means a community that meets one or more of the following criteria: (i) has a median household income at or below 80 percent of the state median; (ii) has a poverty rate at or above 150 percent of the national average; (iii) is located within a census tract designated as an environmental justice area by the state; (iv) is a tribal community or Alaska Native village.

**(C) "Zero-Emission Technology"** means any technology or system that produces no direct air pollutant or greenhouse gas emissions during operation, including but not limited to battery-electric, hydrogen fuel cell, and shore power systems.

**(D) "Shore Power"** means the provision of electrical power from the shore to a vessel at berth, allowing the vessel to shut down its auxiliary engines.

**(E) "Ballast Water"** means water taken on board or discharged from a vessel for stability, trim, or stress purposes, which may contain invasive species, pathogens, and pollutants.

**(F) "Dredged Material"** means sediment or material removed from the bottom of waterways during dredging operations, which may contain contaminants requiring special handling and disposal.

**(G) "Marine Sanctuary"** means any area of the marine environment designated as a national marine sanctuary under the National Marine Sanctuaries Act (16 U.S.C. 1431 et seq.).

**(H) "Critical Habitat"** means specific areas designated under the Endangered Species Act of 1973 (16 U.S.C. 1531 et seq.) as essential for the conservation of a listed species.

**(I) "Environmental Impact Statement"** means a detailed written statement prepared under the National Environmental Policy Act of 1969 (42 U.S.C. 4321 et seq.) that analyzes the environmental effects of a proposed action.

**(J) "Mitigation"** means a sequence of actions taken to avoid, minimize, rectify, reduce, or compensate for the adverse environmental effects of a proposed action.

**(K) "Port-Related Emissions"** means air pollutant and greenhouse gas emissions generated by vessels at berth, cargo handling equipment, heavy-duty vehicles, and other sources within the port boundary.

**(L) "Green Port"** means a port that has achieved certification under a recognized environmental sustainability program, including the Green Marine certification program or equivalent.

### Subtitle A — Port Air Quality Improvement Program

**SEC. 710.** Establishment of Program

**(a) IN GENERAL.** — The Administrator of the Environmental Protection Agency, in coordination with the Secretary of Transportation and the Secretary of Homeland Security, shall establish and carry out a Port Air Quality Improvement Program to reduce air pollutant and greenhouse gas emissions from port operations.

**(b) PROGRAM GOALS.** — The goals of the Port Air Quality Improvement Program are:

> (1) Reduce port-related nitrogen oxide emissions by 80 percent from 2025 levels by 2035;

> (2) Reduce port-related particulate matter emissions by 75 percent from 2025 levels by 2035;

> (3) Reduce port-related greenhouse gas emissions by 50 percent from 2025 levels by 2035;

> (4) Achieve zero-emission operations at all major ports by 2040;

> (5) Eliminate the idling of vessel auxiliary engines at berth through shore power deployment.

**SEC. 711.** Shore Power Requirements

**(a) MANDATORY SHORE POWER.** — Beginning January 1, 2030, all container ships, cruise ships, and tankers calling at ports receiving federal funding under this Act shall be required to use shore power while at berth, unless the vessel is equipped with equivalent zero-emission technology.

**(b) INFRASTRUCTURE DEPLOYMENT.** — The program provides $4,000,000,000 for the installation of shore power infrastructure at ports, including:

> (1) High-voltage shore connection systems;

> (2) Electrical substations and distribution networks;

> (3) Vessel-side electrical connection equipment;

> (4) Energy storage systems to manage peak demand.

**(c) PRIORITY PORTS.** — Priority for shore power infrastructure funding shall be given to ports located in nonattainment areas for ozone, particulate matter, or nitrogen dioxide under the Clean Air Act (42 U.S.C. 7401 et seq.).

**SEC. 712.** Clean Cargo Handling Equipment

**(a) ZERO-EMISSION REQUIREMENT.** — Beginning January 1, 2032, all cargo handling equipment at ports receiving federal funding under this Act shall be zero-emission.

**(b) FUNDING.** — The program provides $2,000,000,000 for the replacement of diesel-powered cargo handling equipment with zero-emission alternatives, including:

> (1) Battery-electric yard tractors;

> (2) Electric rubber-tired gantry cranes;

> (3) Hydrogen fuel cell top handlers;

> (4) Electric forklifts and reach stackers.

**(c) CHARGING INFRASTRUCTURE.** — The program provides $1,000,000,000 for the installation of charging and fueling infrastructure for zero-emission cargo handling equipment.

**SEC. 713.** Clean Drayage Trucks

**(a) ZERO-EMISSION DRAYAGE.** — Beginning January 1, 2033, all drayage trucks serving ports receiving federal funding under this Act shall be zero-emission.

**(b) FUNDING.** — The program provides $1,000,000,000 for:

> (1) Grants to drayage truck operators for the purchase of zero-emission trucks;

> (2) Charging and fueling infrastructure for zero-emission drayage trucks;

> (3) Technical assistance for fleet transition planning.

### Subtitle A — Port Air Quality Improvement Program (Continued)

**SEC. 714.** Vessel Emissions Standards

**(a) EMISSION CONTROL AREA.** — The Administrator of the Environmental Protection Agency shall, within 180 days of the date of enactment of this Act, designate all waters within 200 nautical miles of the United States coastline as an Emission Control Area under Annex VI of the International Convention for the Prevention of Pollution from Ships (MARPOL).

**(b) SULFUR CONTENT LIMITS.** — Within the Emission Control Area designated under subsection (a), the sulfur content of fuel oil used by vessels shall not exceed 0.10 percent mass by mass.

**(c) NITROGEN OXIDE EMISSIONS.** — Within the Emission Control Area, marine diesel engines shall meet Tier III nitrogen oxide emission standards established by the International Maritime Organization.

**(d) GREENHOUSE GAS EMISSIONS.** — The Administrator shall establish greenhouse gas emission standards for vessels calling at United States ports, including:

> (1) A requirement that all new vessels calling at United States ports achieve a 40 percent reduction in carbon intensity by 2030 and a 70 percent reduction by 2040, relative to a 2025 baseline;

> (2) A requirement that all vessels calling at United States ports report their annual greenhouse gas emissions;

> (3) A carbon intensity rating system for vessels, with financial incentives for high-performing vessels.

**SEC. 715.** Port Emission Inventories

**(a) ANNUAL INVENTORIES.** — Each port receiving federal funding under this Act shall prepare and submit to the Administrator an annual emission inventory that quantifies:

> (1) Emissions from vessels at berth, at anchor, and in transit within the port area;

> (2) Emissions from cargo handling equipment;

> (3) Emissions from heavy-duty vehicles serving the port;

> (4) Emissions from rail operations within the port area;

> (5) Emissions from port-owned facilities and buildings.

**(b) PUBLIC REPORTING.** — Each port shall make its annual emission inventory publicly available on its website and shall provide the inventory to the Administrator for inclusion in a national port emissions database.

**(c) REDUCTION TARGETS.** — Each port shall establish emission reduction targets consistent with the program goals set forth in section 710(b) and shall report annually on progress toward those targets.

**SEC. 716.** Low-Emission Port Zones

**(a) DESIGNATION.** — The Administrator may designate Low-Emission Port Zones in areas where port-related air pollution significantly affects air quality. Within a Low-Emission Port Zone:

> (1) All vessels at berth shall use shore power or equivalent zero-emission technology;

> (2) All cargo handling equipment shall be zero-emission;

> (3) All drayage trucks shall be zero-emission;

> (4) All rail operations shall use Tier 4 or cleaner locomotives.

**(b) INCENTIVES.** — Ports that achieve Low-Emission Port Zone designation shall be eligible for:

> (1) Priority consideration for federal funding under this Act;

> (2) Reduced environmental review timelines for qualifying projects;

> (3) Public recognition through a Clean Port Certification program.

### Subtitle B — Marine Ecosystem Protection and Restoration Program

**SEC. 720.** Establishment of Program

**(a) IN GENERAL.** — The Secretary of Commerce, acting through the Administrator of the National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration, in coordination with the Secretary of the Interior and the Administrator of the Environmental Protection Agency, shall establish and carry out a Marine Ecosystem Protection and Restoration Program to protect and restore marine ecosystems affected by port activities.

**(b) PROGRAM GOALS.** — The goals of the Marine Ecosystem Protection and Restoration Program are:

> (1) Protect and restore wetlands, seagrass beds, coral reefs, and other critical marine habitats;

> (2) Prevent the introduction and spread of aquatic invasive species through ballast water and hull fouling management;

> (3) Reduce the impacts of dredging on marine ecosystems;

> (4) Restore degraded marine habitats in and around port areas;

> (5) Establish marine protected areas in ecologically significant areas adjacent to ports.

**SEC. 721.** Wetland Protection and Restoration

**(a) WETLAND MITIGATION.** — Any port project that results in the loss of wetlands shall provide compensatory mitigation at a ratio of not less than 2:1, meaning that for every acre of wetland lost, at least 2 acres of wetland shall be restored, created, or enhanced.

**(b) WETLAND RESTORATION FUNDING.** — The program provides $2,000,000,000 for the restoration of degraded wetlands in and around port areas, including:

> (1) Re-establishment of tidal hydrology in diked or drained wetlands;

> (2) Removal of invasive plant species and replanting with native vegetation;

> (3) Construction of living shorelines that incorporate wetland vegetation;

> (4) Acquisition of wetland conservation easements.

**(c) PRIORITY PROJECTS.** — Priority for wetland restoration funding shall be given to projects that:

> (1) Provide storm surge attenuation and flood protection for port facilities;

> (2) Improve water quality by filtering pollutants from stormwater runoff;

> (3) Provide habitat for threatened or endangered species;

> (4) Are located in areas with a history of significant wetland loss.

**SEC. 722.** Seagrass and Coral Reef Protection

**(a) SEAGRASS PROTECTION.** — No port project shall result in the loss of seagrass beds unless:

> (1) There is no feasible alternative that avoids seagrass impacts;

> (2) The project includes compensatory mitigation at a ratio of not less than 3:1;

> (3) The project includes a long-term monitoring program to ensure mitigation success.

**(b) CORAL REEF PROTECTION.** — Port projects within 5 nautical miles of coral reef ecosystems shall:

> (1) Conduct a comprehensive coral reef impact assessment;

> (2) Implement measures to prevent sedimentation, turbidity, and pollutant discharge;

> (3) Establish buffer zones around coral reef ecosystems;

> (4) Contribute to a Coral Reef Restoration Fund established under this section.

**(c) CORAL REEF RESTORATION FUND.** — There is established in the Treasury a Coral Reef Restoration Fund, to be administered by the Administrator of the National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration, which shall receive:

> (1) Contributions from port projects under subsection (b)(4);

> (2) Appropriations of $500,000,000 for the period of fiscal years 2027 through 2037;

> (3) Penalties collected under section 760 of this title.

**SEC. 723.** Aquatic Invasive Species Management

**(a) BALLAST WATER MANAGEMENT.** — All vessels calling at United States ports shall comply with the following ballast water management requirements:

> (1) All vessels shall conduct ballast water exchange at least 200 nautical miles from shore before entering United States waters;

> (2) All vessels shall treat ballast water using an approved ballast water management system that meets the discharge standard established by the United States Coast Guard;

> (3) All vessels shall maintain a ballast water management plan and record book;

> (4) All vessels shall submit ballast water reporting forms to the National Ballast Information Clearinghouse.

**(b) HULL FOULING MANAGEMENT.** — All vessels calling at United States ports shall:

> (1) Maintain a hull fouling management plan;

> (2) Clean hulls at intervals not exceeding 12 months;

> (3) Use antifouling coatings that do not contain banned substances;

> (4) Report hull fouling management activities to the United States Coast Guard.

**(c) RESEARCH AND MONITORING.** — The program provides $300,000,000 for research and monitoring of aquatic invasive species, including:

> (1) Development of improved ballast water treatment technologies;

> (2) Monitoring of invasive species populations in port areas;

> (3) Research on the ecological and economic impacts of aquatic invasive species;

> (4) Development of rapid response protocols for new invasive species detections.

**SEC. 724.** Dredging and Dredged Material Management

**(a) ENVIRONMENTAL DREDGING STANDARDS.** — All dredging operations at ports receiving federal funding under this Act shall comply with the following environmental standards:

> (1) Dredging shall be conducted during environmentally sensitive windows to minimize impacts on fish spawning, bird migration, and other biological events;

> (2) Turbidity curtains or other containment measures shall be used to minimize sediment plumes;

> (3) Dredged material shall be tested for contaminants before disposal;

> (4) Contaminated dredged material shall be disposed of in confined disposal facilities or treated before beneficial use.

**(b) BENEFICIAL USE.** — The program promotes the beneficial use of clean dredged material for:

> (1) Wetland restoration and creation;

> (2) Beach nourishment;

> (3) Shoreline stabilization;

> (4) Habitat creation;

> (5) Land reclamation.

**(c) DREDGED MATERIAL MANAGEMENT FUNDING.** — The program provides $700,000,000 for:

> (1) Construction and maintenance of confined disposal facilities;

> (2) Research on beneficial use technologies;

> (3) Treatment of contaminated dredged material;

> (4) Monitoring of dredged material disposal sites.

### Subtitle B — Marine Ecosystem Protection and Restoration Program (Continued)

**SEC. 725.** Marine Protected Areas

**(a) DESIGNATION AUTHORITY.** — The Secretary of Commerce, acting through the Administrator of the National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration, may designate Marine Protected Areas in ecologically significant areas adjacent to ports, subject to the following requirements:

> (1) The designation shall be based on the best available scientific information;

> (2) The designation shall be subject to public notice and comment;

> (3) The designation shall be reviewed at least once every 10 years;

> (4) The designation shall not unreasonably restrict navigation or port operations.

**(b) PROTECTED AREA CATEGORIES.** — Marine Protected Areas designated under this section may include:

> (1) **No-Take Zones** — Areas where the extraction of marine resources is prohibited;

> (2) **Habitat Protection Zones** — Areas where activities that damage benthic habitats are restricted;

> (3) **Water Quality Protection Zones** — Areas where pollutant discharge is restricted to protect water quality;

> (4) **Seasonal Protection Zones** — Areas where certain activities are restricted during biologically sensitive periods.

**(c) PORT COORDINATION.** — Before designating a Marine Protected Area adjacent to a port, the Secretary of Commerce shall consult with:

> (1) The port authority;

> (2) The United States Coast Guard;

> (3) The Army Corps of Engineers;

> (4) The Environmental Protection Agency;

> (5) Affected stakeholders, including commercial and recreational fishing interests.

**(d) FUNDING.** — The program provides $500,000,000 for the management and enforcement of Marine Protected Areas designated under this section.

**SEC. 726.** Marine Mammal and Sea Turtle Protection

**(a) VESSELS SPEED RESTRICTIONS.** — Within port approach channels and harbor areas, vessel speed shall be restricted to:

> (1) 10 knots for vessels 65 feet or longer;

> (2) 15 knots for vessels less than 65 feet;

> (3) 5 knots in designated marine mammal protection zones.

**(b) MARINE MAMMAL MONITORING.** — The program provides $200,000,000 for:

> (1) Aerial and vessel-based surveys of marine mammal populations in port areas;

> (2) Acoustic monitoring of marine mammal vocalizations;

> (3) Research on the impacts of port noise on marine mammals;

> (4) Development of noise mitigation technologies for port operations.

**(c) SEA TURTLE PROTECTION.** — Port projects shall implement measures to protect sea turtles, including:

> (1) Use of turtle exclusion devices on dredging equipment;

> (2) Seasonal restrictions on dredging during sea turtle nesting seasons;

> (3) Lighting management to reduce disorientation of sea turtle hatchlings;

> (4) Rescue and rehabilitation programs for injured sea turtles.

**SEC. 727.** Fish and Wildlife Habitat Restoration

**(a) HABITAT RESTORATION PLANS.** — Each port receiving federal funding under this Act shall develop and implement a Fish and Wildlife Habitat Restoration Plan that:

> (1) Identifies degraded habitats in and around the port;

> (2) Establishes restoration priorities based on ecological significance;

> (3) Sets measurable restoration goals and timelines;

> (4) Includes monitoring and adaptive management provisions.

**(b) RESTORATION TECHNIQUES.** — Habitat restoration projects may include:

> (1) Creation of artificial reefs using clean, durable materials;

> (2) Restoration of oyster reefs for water filtration and habitat;

> (3) Planting of native vegetation along shorelines;

> (4) Removal of barriers to fish passage;

> (5) Creation of shallow water habitat for juvenile fish.

**(c) FUNDING.** — The program provides $1,000,000,000 for fish and wildlife habitat restoration projects.

### Subtitle C — Water Quality and Pollution Prevention Program

**SEC. 730.** Establishment of Program

**(a) IN GENERAL.** — The Administrator of the Environmental Protection Agency, in coordination with the Secretary of Homeland Security and the Secretary of Commerce, shall establish and carry out a Water Quality and Pollution Prevention Program to protect water quality in and around port areas.

**(b) PROGRAM GOALS.** — The goals of the Water Quality and Pollution Prevention Program are:

> (1) Reduce pollutant discharges from port operations to the maximum extent practicable;

> (2) Achieve zero discharge of untreated ballast water, bilge water, and other vessel waste streams;

> (3) Prevent and respond to oil spills and hazardous substance releases;

> (4) Reduce stormwater runoff pollution from port facilities;

> (5) Protect drinking water sources from port-related contamination.

**SEC. 731.** Stormwater Management

**(a) STORMWATER PERMITS.** — All port facilities shall obtain and comply with National Pollutant Discharge Elimination System (NPDES) permits for stormwater discharges.

**(b) BEST MANAGEMENT PRACTICES.** — Port facilities shall implement best management practices for stormwater management, including:

> (1) Bioretention systems and rain gardens;

> (2) Permeable pavement in parking lots and roadways;

> (3) Green roofs on port buildings;

> (4) Constructed wetlands for stormwater treatment;

> (5) Oil-water separators in stormwater systems;

> (6) Sediment basins and traps.

**(c) LOW-IMPACT DEVELOPMENT.** — Port development projects shall incorporate low-impact development techniques to minimize stormwater runoff, including:

> (1) Minimization of impervious surfaces;

> (2) Preservation of natural drainage features;

> (3) Use of vegetated swales for stormwater conveyance;

> (4) Rainwater harvesting for non-potable uses.

**(d) FUNDING.** — The program provides $1,500,000,000 for stormwater management infrastructure at port facilities.

**SEC. 732.** Vessel Waste Management

**(a) PROHIBITION ON DISCHARGE.** — No vessel shall discharge the following substances into the waters of the United States:

> (1) Untreated ballast water;

> (2) Bilge water containing oil in concentrations exceeding 15 parts per million;

> (3) Sewage within 3 nautical miles of shore;

> (4) Garbage of any kind;

> (5) Hazardous substances in harmful quantities.

**(b) RECEPTION FACILITIES.** — Each port shall provide adequate reception facilities for:

> (1) Ballast water;

> (2) Bilge water;

> (3) Sewage;

> (4) Garbage and solid waste;

> (5) Hazardous waste;

> (6) Oily residues.

**(c) NO-DISCHARGE ZONES.** — The Administrator may designate no-discharge zones in port areas where the discharge of any waste from vessels is prohibited.

**(d) FUNDING.** — The program provides $1,000,000,000 for the construction and upgrade of vessel waste reception facilities at ports.

**SEC. 733.** Oil Spill Prevention and Response

**(a) PREVENTION MEASURES.** — Port facilities that handle oil or hazardous substances shall implement the following prevention measures:

> (1) Secondary containment for all oil storage tanks and pipelines;

> (2) Oil spill prevention, control, and countermeasure plans;

> (3) Regular inspection and maintenance of oil handling equipment;

> (4) Employee training on oil spill prevention and response;

> (5) Deployment of boom and other containment equipment.

**(b) RESPONSE CAPABILITY.** — Each port shall maintain an oil spill response capability that includes:

> (1) A trained oil spill response team;

> (2) Oil spill response equipment, including boom, skimmers, and sorbents;

> (3) A port-specific oil spill response plan;

> (4) Mutual aid agreements with neighboring ports and response organizations;

> (5) Regular oil spill response exercises.

**(c) NATURAL RESOURCE DAMAGE ASSESSMENT.** — In the event of an oil spill at a port, the Administrator shall conduct a natural resource damage assessment to:

> (1) Determine the extent of injury to natural resources;

> (2) Quantify the loss of ecological services;

> (3) Develop a restoration plan for injured resources;

> (4) Recover damages from the responsible party.

**(d) OIL SPILL RESPONSE FUND.** — There is established in the Treasury an Oil Spill Response Fund, to be administered by the Administrator, which shall receive:

> (1) Appropriations of $500,000,000 for the period of fiscal years 2027 through 2037;

> (2) Reimbursements from responsible parties for response costs;

> (3) Penalties collected under section 760 of this title.

**SEC. 734.** Contaminated Sediment Management

**(a) SEDIMENT ASSESSMENT.** — The Administrator shall conduct assessments of contaminated sediments in port areas to:

> (1) Identify the extent and severity of sediment contamination;

> (2) Determine the sources of contamination;

> (3) Assess risks to human health and the environment;

> (4) Prioritize areas for remediation.

**(b) REMEDIATION.** — The program provides $1,000,000,000 for the remediation of contaminated sediments, including:

> (1) Dredging and disposal of contaminated sediments;

> (2) In-situ treatment using capping or chemical stabilization;

> (3) Monitored natural recovery;

> (4) Source control measures to prevent further contamination.

**(c) LONG-TERM MONITORING.** — Each remediated site shall be subject to long-term monitoring to ensure the effectiveness of remediation and to detect any recontamination.

### Subtitle C — Water Quality and Pollution Prevention Program (Continued)

**SEC. 735.** Industrial Wastewater Management

**(a) PRETREATMENT STANDARDS.** — Port facilities that discharge industrial wastewater to publicly owned treatment works shall comply with pretreatment standards established by the Administrator, including:

> (1) Limits on heavy metals, including mercury, lead, cadmium, and zinc;

> (2) Limits on organic pollutants, including polychlorinated biphenyls and polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons;

> (3) Limits on oil and grease;

**(b) ZERO LIQUID DISCHARGE.** — The Administrator may require zero liquid discharge for port facilities that handle hazardous substances or that discharge to sensitive receiving waters.

**(c) RECYCLING AND REUSE.** — Port facilities shall implement water recycling and reuse systems to minimize wastewater generation, including:

> (1) Recycling of wash water from vehicle and equipment cleaning;

> (2) Reuse of treated wastewater for irrigation and dust suppression;

> (3) Recovery of valuable materials from wastewater streams.

**(d) FUNDING.** — The program provides $500,000,000 for industrial wastewater treatment infrastructure at port facilities.

**SEC. 736.** Marine Debris Prevention

**(a) MARINE DEBRIS REDUCTION PLANS.** — Each port receiving federal funding under this Act shall develop and implement a Marine Debris Reduction Plan that:

> (1) Identifies sources of marine debris from port operations;

> (2) Establishes measures to prevent debris from entering waterways;

> (3) Includes regular cleanup activities;

> (4) Sets measurable reduction targets.

**(b) FISHING GEAR RECOVERY.** — The program provides $100,000,000 for the recovery and recycling of abandoned, lost, or discarded fishing gear in port areas.

**(c) MICROPLASTICS RESEARCH.** — The program provides $100,000,000 for research on:

> (1) The sources and pathways of microplastics in port areas;

> (4) Alternatives to plastic materials used in port operations.

### Subtitle D — Environmental Justice and Community Protection Program

**SEC. 740.** Establishment of Program

**(a) IN GENERAL.** — The Administrator of the Environmental Protection Agency, in coordination with the Secretary of Transportation and the Secretary of Health and Human Services, shall establish and carry out an Environmental Justice and Community Protection Program to ensure that port development and operations do not disproportionately burden disadvantaged communities.

**(b) PROGRAM GOALS.** — The goals of the Environmental Justice and Community Protection Program are:

> (1) Identify and address the cumulative environmental impacts of port operations on disadvantaged communities;

> (2) Ensure meaningful community participation in port planning and decision-making;

> (3) Provide technical and financial assistance to communities affected by port operations;

> (4) Reduce environmental health disparities in communities adjacent to ports;

> (5) Promote equitable distribution of the benefits and burdens of port development.

**SEC. 741.** Environmental Justice Assessments

**(a) REQUIRED ASSESSMENTS.** — Before any major port project may receive federal funding under this Act, the project sponsor shall conduct an Environmental Justice Assessment that:

> (1) Identifies the disadvantaged communities that may be affected by the project;

> (2) Evaluates the cumulative environmental impacts of the project on those communities;

> (3) Assesses the potential for disproportionate adverse effects;

> (4) Identifies measures to avoid, minimize, or mitigate disproportionate impacts;

> (5) Documents community input and concerns.

**(b) PUBLIC PARTICIPATION.** — The Environmental Justice Assessment process shall include:

> (1) Public notice in English and other languages spoken in the affected community;

> (2) Public hearings in accessible locations within the affected community;

> (3) A public comment period of not less than 60 days;

> (4) A written response to all substantive public comments;

> (5) Provision of technical assistance to community organizations.

**(c) AGENCY REVIEW.** — The Administrator shall review each Environmental Justice Assessment and shall not approve a project if the assessment demonstrates that the project would result in disproportionate adverse effects on disadvantaged communities, unless:

> (1) There is no feasible alternative that would avoid disproportionate effects;

> (2) The project includes measures to mitigate disproportionate effects to the maximum extent practicable;

> (3) The project provides offsetting benefits to the affected community.

**SEC. 742.** Community Health Studies

**(a) HEALTH ASSESSMENTS.** — The Administrator shall conduct health assessments in communities adjacent to major ports to:

> (1) Characterize the baseline health status of the community;

> (2) Identify health outcomes that may be associated with port-related pollution;

> (3) Evaluate the cumulative health impacts of multiple pollution sources;

> (4) Develop recommendations for reducing health risks.

**(b) HEALTH MONITORING.** — The program provides $500,000,000 for ongoing health monitoring in communities adjacent to ports, including:

> (1) Air quality monitoring for criteria air pollutants and hazardous air pollutants;

> (2) Water quality monitoring for drinking water sources;

> (3) Biomonitoring for exposure to port-related pollutants;

> (4) Health outcome tracking for respiratory disease, cardiovascular disease, and cancer.

**(c) HEALTH INTERVENTIONS.** — Based on the results of health assessments and monitoring, the Administrator may require:

> (1) Enhanced emission controls on port-related sources;

> (2) Relocation of sensitive receptors, such as schools and hospitals;
