# TITLE VIII — MARITIME DECARBONIZATION
## Act 10: Coastal Port Infrastructure Act of 2026

### Short Title

**SEC. 1001.** This title may be cited as the "Maritime Decarbonization Act of 2026."

### Purpose

**SEC. 1002.** The purposes of this title are:

> (1) To reduce greenhouse gas emissions from maritime transportation and port operations in the United States;

> (2) To establish a national framework for the decarbonization of the maritime sector;

> (3) To promote the development and deployment of zero-emission vessels and clean maritime fuels;

> (4) To fund the development of clean energy infrastructure at ports to support vessel decarbonization;

> (5) To create economic opportunities in the clean maritime economy;

> (6) To ensure that the United States leads the global transition to zero-emission maritime transportation;

> (7) To protect air quality in port communities through emission reductions;

> (8) To establish monitoring, reporting, and verification systems for maritime emissions.

### Definitions

**SEC. 1003.** For purposes of this title:

**(A) "Maritime Decarbonization"** means the process of reducing greenhouse gas emissions from maritime transportation and port operations to net-zero emissions.

**(B) "Zero-Emission Vessel"** means a vessel that produces no direct greenhouse gas emissions during operation, including vessels powered by battery-electric, hydrogen fuel cell, or ammonia fuel cell systems.

**(C) "Clean Maritime Fuel"** means a fuel that produces significantly lower greenhouse gas emissions than conventional marine fuels on a life-cycle basis, including green hydrogen, green ammonia, methanol produced from renewable sources, and sustainable biofuels.

**(D) "Shore Power"** means the provision of electrical power from the shore to a vessel at berth, allowing the vessel to shut down its auxiliary engines.

**(E) "Cold Ironing"** means the use of shore power by a vessel at berth.

**(F) "Green Hydrogen"** means hydrogen produced using renewable energy sources.

**(G) "Green Ammonia"** means ammonia produced using green hydrogen and nitrogen from the air.

**(H) "Life-Cycle Emissions"** means the total greenhouse gas emissions associated with a fuel, including production, transportation, and combustion.

**(I) "Emission Control Area"** means a designated area where stricter emission controls apply to vessels.

**(J) "Carbon Intensity"** means the amount of greenhouse gas emissions per unit of transport work.

**(K) "Well-to-Wake Emissions"** means the total emissions from fuel production to vessel propulsion.

**(L) "Alternative Fuels Infrastructure"** means port facilities for the production, storage, and bunkering of clean maritime fuels.

### Subtitle A — Zero-Emission Vessel Program

**SEC. 1010.** Establishment of Program

**(a) IN GENERAL.** — The Secretary of Transportation, acting through the Administrator of the Maritime Administration, in coordination with the Secretary of Energy, shall establish and carry out a Zero-Emission Vessel Program to promote the development and deployment of zero-emission vessels in the United States.

**(b) PROGRAM GOALS.** — The goals of the Zero-Emission Vessel Program are:

> (1) Deploy at least 100 zero-emission vessels in U.S. waters by 2035;

> (2) Achieve a 50 percent reduction in greenhouse gas emissions from U.S. flagged vessels by 2035;

> (3) Achieve net-zero greenhouse gas emissions from U.S. flagged vessels by 2045;

> (4) Establish the United States as a global leader in zero-emission vessel technology;

> (5) Create at least 25,000 jobs in zero-emission vessel construction and operations.

**SEC. 1011.** Zero-Emission Vessel Grants

**(a) GRANTS.** — The Secretary of Transportation shall provide grants for:

> (1) Construction of new zero-emission vessels;

> (2) Retrofit of existing vessels to zero-emission propulsion systems;

> (3) Development of zero-emission vessel technologies;

> (4) Pilot projects for zero-emission vessel operations;

> (5) Training of crews for zero-emission vessel operations.

**(b) ELIGIBLE VESSELS.** — Grants may be awarded for:

> (1) Harbor craft (tugs, pilot boats, work boats);

> (2) Ferries and passenger vessels;

> (3) Offshore support vessels;

> (4) Short-sea shipping vessels;

> (5) Great Lakes vessels;

> (6) River and inland waterway vessels.

**(c) FUNDING ALLOCATION.** — Grants shall be allocated based on:

> (1) The emission reduction potential of the vessel;

> (2) The commercial viability of the vessel;

> (3) The potential for technology demonstration;

> (4) The number of jobs created;

> (5) The applicant's technical and financial capability.

**(d) COST SHARE.** — The federal share of project costs shall not exceed 50 percent.

**(e) FUNDING.** — The program provides $5,000,000,000 for zero-emission vessel grants.

**SEC. 1012.** Zero-Emission Vessel Loan Guarantees

**(a) LOAN GUARANTEE PROGRAM.** — The Secretary of Transportation shall establish a Zero-Emission Vessel Loan Guarantee Program that:

> (1) Provides loan guarantees for the construction of zero-emission vessels;

> (2) Provides loan guarantees for the retrofit of existing vessels;

> (3) Reduces the risk of lending for zero-emission vessel projects;

> (4) Leverages private capital for zero-emission vessel investment.

**(b) GUARANTEE TERMS.** — Loan guarantees shall:

> (1) Cover up to 80 percent of the loan amount;

> (2) Be available for loans of up to $500,000,000;

> (3) Have terms of up to 25 years;

> (4) Be subject to creditworthiness requirements.

**(c) FUNDING.** — The program is authorized to guarantee up to $3,000,000,000 in loans.

**SEC. 1013.** Zero-Emission Vessel Standards

**(a) DESIGN STANDARDS.** — Zero-emission vessels shall meet the following design standards:

> (1) Zero direct greenhouse gas emissions during operation;

> (2) Compliance with all applicable safety standards;

> (3) Compliance with all applicable environmental standards;

> (4) Interoperability with existing port infrastructure;

> (5) Redundancy in propulsion and power systems.

**(b) OPERATING STANDARDS.** — Zero-emission vessels shall meet the following operating standards:

> (1) Zero direct greenhouse gas emissions during port operations;

> (2) Compliance with all applicable emission regulations;

> (3) Compliance with all applicable noise regulations;

> (4) Use of clean maritime fuels when operating in emission control areas;

> (5) Reporting of emissions data to the Secretary of Transportation.

**(c) CERTIFICATION.** — Zero-emission vessels shall be certified by:

> (1) The United States Coast Guard for safety;

> (2) The Environmental Protection Agency for emissions;

> (3) The American Bureau of Shipping for classification.

### Subtitle C — Shore Power and Cold Ironing Program

**SEC. 1030.** Establishment of Program

**(a) IN GENERAL.** — The Secretary of Transportation, in coordination with the Administrator of the Environmental Protection Agency, shall establish and carry out a Shore Power and Cold Ironing Program to install shore power infrastructure at U.S. ports.

**(b) PROGRAM GOALS.** — The goals of the Shore Power and Cold Ironing Program are:

> (1) Install shore power at all major U.S. container and cruise ports by 2035;

> (2) Achieve 100 percent shore power usage for container ships, cruise ships, and tankers at berth by 2035;

> (3) Reduce port-related emissions from vessels at berth by 95 percent by 2035;

> (4) Improve air quality in port communities through emission reductions;

> (5) Create at least 10,000 jobs in shore power installation and operations.

**SEC. 1031.** Shore Power Installation Grants

**(a) GRANTS.** — The Secretary of Transportation shall provide grants for:

> (1) Installation of high-voltage shore power systems;

> (2) Installation of low-voltage shore power systems;

> (3) Vessel-side electrical connection equipment;

> (4) Electrical substations and distribution networks;

> (5) Energy storage systems to manage peak demand;

> (6) Smart grid integration systems.

**(b) PRIORITY PORTS.** — Priority for shore power installation shall be given to:

> (1) Ports in nonattainment areas for ozone or particulate matter;

> (2) Ports with the highest vessel at-berth emissions;

> (3) Ports serving disadvantaged communities;

> (4) Ports with the highest container and cruise ship traffic.

**(c) FUNDING.** — The program provides $3,000,000,000 for shore power installation grants.

**SEC. 1032.** Shore Power Requirements

**(a) MANDATORY SHORE POWER.** — Beginning January 1, 2032:

> (1) All container ships calling at ports with shore power infrastructure shall use shore power while at berth;

> (2) All cruise ships calling at ports with shore power infrastructure shall use shore power while at berth;

> (3) All tankers calling at ports with shore power infrastructure shall use shore power while at berth;

> (4) All other vessels calling at ports with shore power infrastructure shall use shore power while at berth, unless equipped with equivalent zero-emission technology.

**(b) EXEMPTIONS.** — Vessels may be exempted from shore power requirements if:

> (1) The vessel is equipped with equivalent zero-emission technology;

> (2) Shore power infrastructure is not available at the berth;

> (3) Use of shore power would create a safety hazard;

> (4) The vessel is at berth for less than 2 hours.

**(c) ENFORCEMENT.** — The Secretary of Transportation shall enforce shore power requirements through:

> (1) Port state control inspections;

> (2) Civil penalties of up to $50,000 per violation;

> (3) Reporting requirements for vessel operators;

> (4) Public disclosure of compliance data.

**SEC. 1033.** Shore Power Standards

**(a) TECHNICAL STANDARDS.** — Shore power systems shall meet the following technical standards:

> (1) Voltage: 6.6-11 kV for high-voltage systems, 440V for low-voltage systems;

> (2) Frequency: 50 or 60 Hz, convertible as needed;

> (3) Power output: 2-8 MW per connection;

> (4) Connection time: Less than 30 minutes;

> (5) Safety: Compliance with IEC/ISO/IEEE 80005 standard.

**(b) INTEROPERABILITY.** — Shore power systems shall be designed for interoperability:

> (1) Standardized connection interfaces;

> (2) Compatible voltage and frequency;

> (3) Universal connection procedures;

> (4) Automated connection systems where feasible.

**(c) FUNDING.** — The program provides $1,000,000,000 for shore power standards development and implementation.

### Subtitle E — Maritime Emissions Reduction Program

**SEC. 1050.** Establishment of Program

**(a) IN GENERAL.** — The Administrator of the Environmental Protection Agency, in coordination with the Secretary of Transportation, shall establish and carry out a Maritime Emissions Reduction Program to reduce greenhouse gas emissions from maritime transportation.

**(b) PROGRAM GOALS.** — The goals of the Maritime Emissions Reduction Program are:

> (1) Reduce greenhouse gas emissions from U.S. maritime transportation by 50 percent by 2035;

> (2) Reduce greenhouse gas emissions from U.S. maritime transportation by 80 percent by 2040;

> (3) Achieve net-zero greenhouse gas emissions from U.S. maritime transportation by 2050;

> (4) Reduce black carbon emissions from maritime transportation by 70 percent by 2035;

> (5) Improve air quality in port communities through emission reductions.

**SEC. 1051.** Vessel Emission Standards

**(a) GREENHOUSE GAS STANDARDS.** — The Administrator shall establish greenhouse gas emission standards for vessels calling at U.S. ports:

> (1) **2028**: All new vessels calling at U.S. ports shall achieve a 20 percent reduction in carbon intensity relative to a 2025 baseline;

> (2) **2030**: All new vessels calling at U.S. ports shall achieve a 30 percent reduction in carbon intensity relative to a 2025 baseline;

> (3) **2035**: All new vessels calling at U.S. ports shall achieve a 50 percent reduction in carbon intensity relative to a 2025 baseline;

> (4) **2040**: All new vessels calling at U.S. ports shall achieve a 70 percent reduction in carbon intensity relative to a 2025 baseline;

> (5) **2050**: All new vessels calling at U.S. ports shall achieve zero well-to-wake emissions.

**(b) EXISTING VESSEL STANDARDS.** — The Administrator shall establish emission reduction requirements for existing vessels:

> (1) **2030**: All existing vessels calling at U.S. ports shall achieve a 10 percent reduction in carbon intensity relative to a 2025 baseline;

> (2) **2035**: All existing vessels calling at U.S. ports shall achieve a 25 percent reduction in carbon intensity relative to a 2025 baseline;

> (3) **2040**: All existing vessels calling at U.S. ports shall achieve a 50 percent reduction in carbon intensity relative to a 2025 baseline.

**(c) BLACK CARBON STANDARDS.** — The Administrator shall establish black carbon emission standards:

> (1) **2028**: All vessels calling at U.S. ports shall use distillate fuel or equivalent in port waters;

> (2) **2030**: All vessels calling at U.S. ports shall reduce black carbon emissions by 50 percent relative to a 2025 baseline;

> (3) **2035**: All vessels calling at U.S. ports shall reduce black carbon emissions by 70 percent relative to a 2025 baseline.

**SEC. 1052.** Emission Control Areas

**(a) DESIGNATION.** — The Administrator shall designate the following Emission Control Areas:

> (1) **Atlantic Coast ECA**: All waters within 200 nautical miles of the Atlantic Coast;

> (2) **Pacific Coast ECA**: All waters within 200 nautical miles of the Pacific Coast;

> (3) **Gulf of Mexico ECA**: All waters within 200 nautical miles of the Gulf Coast;

> (4) **Great Lakes ECA**: All waters of the Great Lakes;

> (5) **Arctic ECA**: All waters within 200 nautical miles of the Alaskan Coast.

**(b) ECA REQUIREMENTS.** — Within designated Emission Control Areas:

> (1) Sulfur content of fuel shall not exceed 0.10 percent mass by mass;

> (2) Nitrogen oxide emissions shall meet Tier III standards;

> (3) Particulate matter emissions shall be reduced by 80 percent;

> (4) Black carbon emissions shall be reduced by 70 percent;

> (5) Greenhouse gas emissions shall be reduced by 30 percent.

**SEC. 1053.** Slow Steaming Incentives

**(a) VOLUNTARY SLOW STEAMING.** — The Secretary of Transportation shall establish a voluntary slow steaming program that:

> (1) Provides financial incentives for vessels that reduce speed in designated areas;

> (2) Reduces greenhouse gas emissions through reduced fuel consumption;

> (3) Reduces air pollutant emissions in coastal areas;

> (4) Protects marine mammals from vessel strikes.

**(b) INCENTIVE PAYMENTS.** — Vessels participating in the slow steaming program shall receive:

> (1) Priority berthing at participating ports;

> (2) Reduced port fees;

> (3) Carbon credits for verified emission reductions;

> (4) Public recognition for environmental leadership.

**(c) FUNDING.** — The program provides $500,000,000 for slow steaming incentives.

**SEC. 1054.** Vessel Efficiency Standards

**(a) ENERGY EFFICIENCY DESIGN INDEX.** — The Administrator shall establish energy efficiency standards for new vessels:

> (1) All new vessels shall meet the IMO Energy Efficiency Design Index (EEDI) Phase 3 requirements;

> (2) All new vessels shall exceed EEDI Phase 3 requirements by 20 percent by 2030;

> (3) All new vessels shall exceed EEDI Phase 3 requirements by 40 percent by 2035.

**(b) ENERGY EFFICIENCY EXISTING SHIP INDEX.** — The Administrator shall establish energy efficiency requirements for existing vessels:

> (1) All existing vessels shall meet the IMO Energy Efficiency Existing Ship Index (EEXI) by 2028;

> (2) All existing vessels shall improve energy efficiency by 20 percent by 2035.

**(c) CARBON INTENSITY INDICATOR.** — The Administrator shall establish carbon intensity requirements:

> (1) All vessels calling at U.S. ports shall report their carbon intensity indicator (CII) rating;

> (2) Vessels with poor CII ratings shall be required to develop improvement plans;

> (3) Vessels with consistently poor CII ratings may be restricted from U.S. ports.

**(d) FUNDING.** — The program provides $500,000,000 for vessel efficiency standards implementation.

**SEC. 1055.** Maritime Emissions Monitoring and Reporting

**(a) MONITORING REQUIREMENTS.** — All vessels calling at U.S. ports shall:

> (1) Install continuous emission monitoring systems;

> (2) Report greenhouse gas emissions annually;

> (3) Report air pollutant emissions annually;

> (4) Verify emission data through third-party audits;

> (5) Make emission data publicly available.

**(b) NATIONAL MARITIME EMISSIONS DATABASE.** — The Administrator shall establish and maintain a National Maritime Emissions Database that:

> (1) Collects and integrates emission data from all U.S. ports;

> (2) Tracks emission trends over time;

> (3) Supports policy development and evaluation;

> (4) Is publicly accessible;

> (5) Meets international reporting requirements.

**(c) FUNDING.** — The program provides $500,000,000 for emissions monitoring and reporting.

### Supplementary Analysis J — Maritime Decarbonization Funding and Financial Mechanisms

#### Carbon Pricing for Maritime Emissions

The Secretary of the Treasury shall establish a carbon levy on marine fuels:

- 2028: $50 per ton of CO2 equivalent
- 2030: $100 per ton of CO2 equivalent
- 2035: $150 per ton of CO2 equivalent
- 2040: $200 per ton of CO2 equivalent

Revenue from the carbon levy shall be used for:
- Maritime decarbonization programs (70%)
- Climate adaptation in port communities (20%)
- Administration of the carbon levy program (10%)

#### Maritime Decarbonization Bonds

The Secretary of Transportation shall establish a Maritime Decarbonization Bond Program that issues bonds to finance maritime decarbonization projects, provides tax credits to bondholders in lieu of interest payments, and is backed by the full faith and credit of the United States.

Bond terms:
- Term: Up to 30 years
- Tax credit: Up to 35% of bond amount
- Available to institutional and individual investors
- Tradable on secondary markets

#### Tax Incentives

The Secretary of the Treasury shall establish tax credits for:

1. Construction of zero-emission vessels — 30% credit
2. Retrofit of existing vessels to zero-emission — 25% credit
3. Installation of shore power infrastructure — 25% credit
4. Production of clean maritime fuels — 20% credit
5. Installation of port renewable energy systems — 20% credit

Maritime decarbonization infrastructure shall be eligible for accelerated depreciation over a 7-year period. States and local governments may issue tax-exempt bonds to finance maritime decarbonization infrastructure.

## Final Note on Maritime Decarbonization Implementation

The successful implementation of Title VIII requires sustained commitment from all stakeholders, including federal agencies, state and local governments, port authorities, vessel operators, fuel producers, manufacturing companies, community organizations, and the private sector. The Secretary of Transportation, in coordination with the Secretary of Energy and the Administrator of the Environmental Protection Agency, shall provide leadership and coordination to ensure that the Maritime Decarbonization program is implemented effectively and efficiently.

The Maritime Decarbonization Act of 2026 represents a comprehensive approach to reducing greenhouse gas emissions from maritime transportation and port operations. By combining vessel standards, clean fuel infrastructure, port electrification, shore power requirements, and research and development, this title provides a robust framework for achieving net-zero maritime emissions by 2050 while creating jobs, improving air quality, and protecting the environment.

The program's phased implementation timeline, comprehensive standards, and accountability measures ensure that the investments authorized under this Act will generate significant returns in terms of emission reductions, clean energy production, job creation, and improved public health. The benefit-cost ratio of 8.1:1 demonstrates that every dollar invested in the program will generate $8.10 in benefits for the American public.

The Maritime Decarbonization program will serve as a model for clean energy transition worldwide, demonstrating that it is possible to combine economic competitiveness with environmental protection and climate action. The program's emphasis on equitable workforce development and environmental justice ensures that the benefits of maritime decarbonization are shared by all community members, including those in disadvantaged communities.

As the global community accelerates its response to the climate crisis, the investments authorized under this title will position the United States as a leader in the global transition to zero-emission maritime transportation. The Maritime Decarbonization program is a critical component of the nation's clean energy future and its commitment to addressing the climate crisis.

### Supplementary Analysis DO — Maritime Decarbonization International Best Practices

#### European Union Best Practices

**EU Emissions Trading System (ETS)**

- Includes maritime transportation in carbon pricing
- Covers voyages within the EU and 50% of international voyages
- Revenue used for climate action
- Carbon price: €80-100/ton CO2 (2025)

**FuelEU Maritime Regulation**

- Requires carbon intensity reduction for ship fuels
- Targets: 2% reduction by 2025, 80% by 2050
- Includes well-to-wake emissions
- Incentivizes renewable fuels

**Alternative Fuels Infrastructure Regulation**

- Requires ports to install shore power by 2030
- Requires ports to provide LNG bunkering
- Requires ports to provide hydrogen bunkering by 2035

#### Asian Best Practices

**Japan's Zero-Emission Shipping Strategy**

- Target: Net-zero emissions by 2050
- Investment in hydrogen and ammonia vessels
- Development of domestic supply chain
- International cooperation on standards

**South Korea's Green Shipping Strategy**

- Target: 50% emission reduction by 2030
- Investment in electric and hydrogen vessels
- Development of domestic manufacturing
- Port electrification program

**China's Maritime Decarbonization Strategy**

- Target: Peak emissions by 2030, net-zero by 2060
- Investment in LNG, hydrogen, and electric vessels
- Development of domestic manufacturing
- Port electrification program

4. **Technology Neutrality**: Technology neutrality allows for innovation
