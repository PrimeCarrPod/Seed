# TITLE I — PORT MODERNIZATION
## Act 10: Coastal Port Infrastructure Act of 2026

### Short Title

**SEC. 301.** This title may be cited as the "Port Modernization Act of 2026."

### Definitions

**SEC. 302.** For purposes of this title:

**(A) "Eligible Port"** means any coastal port as defined in Section 2 of this Act that handles commercial cargo and is open to public use.

**(B) "Navigation Channel"** means any federal or non-federal channel, waterway, or approach that provides access to a coastal port, including entrance channels, harbor channels, berthing areas, and turning basins.

**(C) "Terminal Facility"** means any structure, equipment, or system used for the loading, unloading, storage, or transfer of cargo at a coastal port, including container terminals, bulk terminals, breakbulk terminals, roll-on/roll-off terminals, and liquid bulk terminals.

**(D) "Intermodal Connection"** means any facility, system, or infrastructure that enables the transfer of cargo between maritime transportation and other modes of transportation, including rail, truck, pipeline, and air cargo.

**(E) "Port Modernization Project"** means any project that improves the efficiency, capacity, reliability, or sustainability of port infrastructure, including navigation channel improvements, terminal modernization, and intermodal connectivity improvements.

### Authorization of Appropriations

**SEC. 303.** There is authorized to be appropriated to carry out this title $62,500,000,000 for the period of fiscal years 2027 through 2037, to be allocated as follows:

**(A) Navigation Channel Improvement Program** — $28,000,000,000

**(B) Terminal Modernization Program** — $22,000,000,000

**(C) Intermodal Connectivity Program** — $12,500,000,000

### Subtitle A — Navigation Channel Improvement Program

**SEC. 310.** Establishment of Program

**(a) IN GENERAL.** — The Secretary of the Army, acting through the Chief of Engineers, shall establish and carry out a Navigation Channel Improvement Program to improve the depth, width, and condition of federal navigation channels serving commercial ports.

**(b) PROGRAM GOALS.** — The goals of the Navigation Channel Improvement Program are:

> (1) Eliminate the dredging backlog within ten years;

> (2) Achieve full authorized dimensions at all federal navigation channels serving commercial ports within ten years;

> (3) Reduce average vessel wait times at major container ports by 50 percent by 2032;

> (4) Enable all major U.S. container ports to accommodate fully laden post-Panamax and ultra-large container vessels by 2032;

> (5) Develop and deploy advanced dredging technologies that reduce costs and minimize environmental impacts.

**SEC. 311.** Priority Navigation Channel Improvements

**(a) PRIORITY SYSTEM.** — The Secretary of the Army shall establish a priority system for navigation channel improvements based on the following criteria:

> (1) Cargo volume — Channels serving ports with higher cargo volumes receive higher priority;

> (2) Vessel traffic — Channels with higher vessel traffic receive higher priority;

> (3) Safety implications — Channels where depth deficiencies pose safety risks receive higher priority;

> (4) Economic impact — Channels where improvements would generate the greatest economic benefit receive higher priority;

> (5) National security — Channels serving strategic military ports receive higher priority.

**(b) PRIORITY 1: TOP 50 CONTAINER PORTS.** — The Secretary of the Army shall allocate $12,000,000,000 to navigation channel improvements at the nation's top 50 container ports, with the goal of achieving a minimum channel depth of 50 feet (mean lower low water) at all such ports by 2032. Specific projects include:

> (1) Savannah Harbor deepening from 42 to 47 feet (with provisions for future deepening to 50 feet), at an estimated cost of $2,800,000,000;

> (2) Charleston Harbor deepening to 52 feet, at an estimated cost of $1,500,000,000;

> (3) New York and New Jersey Harbor deepening to 50 feet, at an estimated cost of $2,000,000,000;

> (4) Houston Ship Channel widening and deepening, at an estimated cost of $3,200,000,000;

> (5) Oakland Harbor deepening to 50 feet, at an estimated cost of $2,000,000,000.

**(c) PRIORITY 2: ENERGY EXPORT FACILITIES.** — The Secretary of the Army shall allocate $6,000,000,000 to navigation channel improvements at energy export facilities, with the goal of enabling these facilities to accommodate very large crude carriers and Q-max liquefied natural gas carriers.

**(d) PRIORITY 3: STRATEGIC MILITARY PORTS.** — The Secretary of the Army shall allocate $4,000,000,000 to navigation channel improvements at strategic military ports designated under the National Port Readiness Network.

**(e) PRIORITY 4: GREAT LAKES AND INLAND WATERWAY PORTS.** — The Secretary of the Army shall allocate $3,000,000,000 to navigation channel maintenance and improvement at Great Lakes and inland waterway ports.

**(f) ADVANCED TECHNOLOGY AND RESEARCH.** — The Secretary of the Army shall allocate $3,000,000,000 to research and development of advanced dredging technologies, including autonomous dredging vessels, real-time hydrographic survey systems, predictive sedimentation modeling, and beneficial use of dredged material.

**(f) ADVANCED TECHNOLOGY AND RESEARCH.** — The Secretary of the Army shall allocate $3,000,000,000 to research and development of advanced dredging technologies, including autonomous dredging vessels, real-time hydrographic survey systems, predictive sedimentation modeling, and beneficial use of dredged material.

### Legislative Findings and Purpose

**SEC. 315.** The Congress finds that:

**(a)** The nation's navigation channels are critical infrastructure that enables the movement of over 2.4 billion tons of foreign and domestic cargo annually, supporting the nation's economic growth and national security.

**(b)** Decades of deferred maintenance have resulted in a dredging backlog exceeding 300 million cubic yards, with only 35 percent of federal navigation channels maintained at their fully authorized depth and width.

**(c)** The average depth deficiency across restricted channels is 4.2 feet, forcing container vessels to reduce cargo loads by an average of 12 percent per voyage and increasing shipping costs by an estimated $8–12 billion annually.

**(d)** Fifteen of the nation's top 20 container ports face channel depth restrictions that prevent them from fully accommodating post-Panamax and ultra-large container vessels, limiting the competitiveness of American trade.

**(e)** Climate change is accelerating sedimentation rates at many navigation channels, increasing maintenance costs and requiring the development of advanced dredging technologies.

**(f)** The beneficial use of dredged material provides environmental and economic benefits, including coastal restoration, habitat creation, and beach nourishment.

**(g)** The elimination of the dredging backlog within ten years is essential for the nation's economic competitiveness, national security, and environmental sustainability.

**SEC. 316.** The purpose of this subtitle is to:

**(a)** Eliminate the navigation channel dredging backlog within ten years;

**(b)** Achieve full authorized dimensions at all federal navigation channels serving commercial ports within ten years;

**(c)** Enable all major U.S. container ports to accommodate fully laden post-Panamax and ultra-large container vessels by 2032;

**(d)** Develop and deploy advanced dredging technologies that reduce costs and minimize environmental impacts;

**(e)** Maximize the beneficial use of dredged material for coastal restoration, habitat creation, and other purposes;

**(f)** Improve the safety and efficiency of navigation at the nation's ports.

**SEC. 312.** Dredging Operations

**(a) MAINTENANCE DREDGING.** — The Secretary of the Army shall conduct maintenance dredging of federal navigation channels on a regular schedule that ensures channels are maintained at their fully authorized dimensions. The Secretary shall establish dredging schedules based on sedimentation rates, vessel traffic, and channel conditions, and shall prioritize dredging at channels where depth deficiencies pose safety risks or economic constraints.

**(b) CAPITAL DREDGING.** — The Secretary of the Army shall conduct capital dredging to deepen and widen federal navigation channels to dimensions that accommodate modern vessel sizes and traffic volumes. Capital dredging projects shall be designed to achieve the goals of the Navigation Channel Improvement Program, including enabling all major U.S. container ports to accommodate fully laden post-Panamax and ultra-large container vessels by 2032.

**(c) BENEFICIAL USE OF DREDGED MATERIAL.** — The Secretary of the Army shall maximize the beneficial use of dredged material, recognizing that dredged sediment is a resource rather than a waste product. Beneficial uses include:

> (1) Coastal restoration — Dredged material may be used to restore degraded coastal wetlands, beaches, and dunes;

> (2) Habitat creation — Dredged material may be used to create or enhance habitat for fish, shellfish, and wildlife;

> (3) Beach nourishment — Dredged material may be used to nourish eroding beaches;

> (4) Construction material — Dredged material may be processed for use as construction aggregate, fill material, or manufactured soil products;

> (5) Agricultural soil amendment — Dredged material meeting applicable quality standards may be used as agricultural soil amendment.

The Secretary shall ensure that a minimum of 50 percent of all dredged material from federal navigation channels is put to beneficial use.

**(d) ENVIRONMENTAL PROTECTION.** — All dredging operations shall comply with applicable environmental laws and regulations, including the Clean Water Act, the Marine Mammal Protection Act, the Endangered Species Act, and the Magnuson-Stevens Fishery Conservation and Management Act. The Secretary shall develop and implement environmental protection measures that minimize the impacts of dredging on aquatic ecosystems, including:

> (1) Seasonal restrictions on dredging during sensitive periods for fish and wildlife;

> (2) Use of environmental dredging equipment that minimizes turbidity and sediment dispersion;

> (3) Monitoring of water quality and aquatic life during dredging operations;

> (4) Development and implementation of mitigation measures for unavoidable impacts.

**SEC. 313.** Advanced Dredging Technology Research

**(a) RESEARCH PROGRAM.** — The Secretary of the Army shall establish a research program to develop and deploy advanced dredging technologies that reduce costs, improve efficiency, and minimize environmental impacts. The research program shall focus on:

> (1) Autonomous dredging vessels — Development of unmanned or minimally crewed dredging vessels that can operate continuously with minimal oversight;

> (2) Real-time hydrographic survey systems — Development of autonomous survey vessels and underwater sensors that provide real-time channel condition data;

> (3) Predictive sedimentation modeling — Development of artificial intelligence and machine learning models that predict sedimentation rates and optimize dredging schedules;

> (4) Advanced dredging equipment — Development of dredging equipment that reduces turbidity, minimizes environmental impacts, and improves efficiency;

> (5) Beneficial use technologies — Development of technologies for processing dredged material into construction products, manufactured soil, and other beneficial use products.

**(b) TECHNOLOGY DEPLOYMENT.** — The Secretary of the Army shall deploy advanced dredging technologies as they are developed, with the goal of reducing dredging costs by 30 percent and minimizing environmental impacts by 50 percent by 2037.

**SEC. 314.** Cost-Sharing Requirements

**(a) FEDERAL SHARE.** — The federal share of the cost of navigation channel improvements shall be 100 percent for maintenance dredging and 75 percent for capital dredging.

**(b) NON-FEDERAL SHARE.** — The non-federal share of the cost of capital dredging shall be provided by the port authority or other non-federal sponsor. The non-federal share may be provided in cash, in-kind services, or a combination thereof.

**(c) WAIVERS.** — The Secretary of the Army may waive or reduce the non-federal share requirement for capital dredging projects at ports that demonstrate financial hardship, provided that the port authority demonstrates a good-faith effort to secure non-federal funding.

**(c) WAIVERS.** — The Secretary of the Army may waive or reduce the non-federal share requirement for capital dredging projects at ports that demonstrate financial hardship, provided that the port authority demonstrates a good-faith effort to secure non-federal funding.

### Implementation Guidance for Navigation Channel Improvement

> (2) Expand terminal capacity to accommodate growing cargo volumes and larger vessels;

> (3) Modernize terminal technology to improve efficiency, reduce costs, and enhance safety;

**(f)** Public-private partnerships can leverage private capital, expertise, and innovation to accelerate port modernization while maintaining public oversight and accountability.

**(g)** The rehabilitation, expansion, and modernization of terminal facilities is essential for the nation's economic competitiveness, national security, and environmental sustainability.

# TITLE I — PORT MODERNIZATION — LEGISLATIVE HISTORY, PURPOSE, AND IMPLEMENTATION GUIDANCE

The Port Modernization Act of 2026 is the product of extensive congressional deliberation, stakeholder engagement, and expert analysis. ## III. IMPLEMENTATION GUIDANCE

### A. Navigation Channel Improvement Program

**1. Project Selection**

The Secretary of the Army shall establish a priority system for navigation channel improvements that evaluates projects based on cargo volume, vessel traffic, safety implications, economic impact, and national security. The priority system shall ensure that the most critical deficiencies are addressed first and that federal investments generate the maximum economic and security benefits.

**2. Environmental Compliance**

All dredging operations shall comply with applicable environmental laws and regulations, including the National Environmental Policy Act, the Clean Water Act, the Marine Mammal Protection Act, the Endangered Species Act, and the Magnuson-Stevens Fishery Conservation and Management Act. The Secretary shall develop and implement environmental protection measures that minimize the impacts of dredging on aquatic ecosystems.

**3. Beneficial Use Planning**

All dredging projects shall include a beneficial use plan that identifies opportunities for the beneficial use of dredged material. The Secretary shall ensure that a minimum of 50 percent of all dredged material from federal navigation channels is put to beneficial use, including coastal restoration, habitat creation, beach nourishment, construction material production, and agricultural soil amendment.

**4. Advanced Technology Deployment**

The Secretary shall deploy advanced dredging technologies as they are developed, with the goal of reducing dredging costs by 30 percent and minimizing environmental impacts by 50 percent by 2037. Advanced technologies include autonomous dredging vessels, real-time hydrographic survey systems, predictive sedimentation modeling, and advanced dredging equipment.

### B. Terminal Modernization Program

**1. Application Process**
