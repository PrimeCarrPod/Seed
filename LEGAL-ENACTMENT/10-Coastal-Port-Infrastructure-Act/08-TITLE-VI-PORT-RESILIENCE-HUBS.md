# TITLE VI — PORT RESILIENCE HUBS
## Act 10: Coastal Port Infrastructure Act of 2026

### Short Title

**SEC. 801.** This title may be cited as the "Port Resilience Hub Act of 2026."

### Purpose

**SEC. 802.** The purposes of this title are:

> (1) To establish a network of Port Resilience Hubs that serve as centers for disaster preparedness, response, and recovery in coastal regions;

> (2) To ensure continuity of port operations during and after natural disasters, climate events, and other disruptions;

> (3) To provide emergency services, supplies, and coordination capabilities to surrounding communities during disasters;

> (4) To integrate port infrastructure with regional resilience planning and emergency management;

> (5) To promote the use of resilient design, redundant systems, and adaptive management in port operations;

> (6) To establish funding mechanisms for resilience hub construction, equipment, and operations;

> (7) To create partnerships between ports, emergency management agencies, and community organizations;

> (8) To develop workforce training programs for port resilience and emergency response.

### Definitions

**SEC. 803.** For purposes of this title:

**(A) "Port Resilience Hub"** means a designated port facility or complex of facilities that has been designed, constructed, and equipped to serve as a center for disaster preparedness, response, and recovery operations.

**(B) "Resilience"** means the ability of a port and its associated infrastructure to anticipate, absorb, accommodate, and recover from the effects of a hazard in a timely and efficient manner.

**(C) "Critical Infrastructure"** means port facilities and systems that are essential for the movement of goods, including container terminals, bulk cargo facilities, fuel storage, and transportation connections.

**(D) "Redundancy"** means the duplication of critical components or functions of a system with the intention of increasing reliability of the system.

**(E) "Backup Power"** means electrical power provided by on-site generation, battery storage, or other systems that can operate independently of the electrical grid.

**(F) "Emergency Operations Center"** means a facility designated for the coordination of emergency response activities.

**(G) "Community Resilience"** means the ability of a community to withstand, adapt to, and recover from adverse situations, including natural disasters and climate change.

**(H) "Supply Chain Resilience"** means the ability of supply chains to prepare for unexpected events, respond to disruptions, and recover by maintaining continuity of operations.

**(I) "Mutual Aid Agreement"** means a formal agreement between ports, agencies, or organizations to provide assistance during emergencies.

**(J) "Disaster Recovery"** means the process of restoring port operations and infrastructure to pre-disaster conditions or better.

### Subtitle A — Port Resilience Hub Construction and Retrofit Program

**SEC. 810.** Establishment of Program

**(a) IN GENERAL.** — The Secretary of Transportation, acting through the Administrator of the Maritime Administration, in coordination with the Administrator of the Federal Emergency Management Agency, shall establish and carry out a Port Resilience Hub Construction and Retrofit Program to design, construct, and retrofit port facilities as Port Resilience Hubs.

**(b) PROGRAM GOALS.** — The goals of the Port Resilience Hub Construction and Retrofit Program are:

> (1) Designate and construct at least 25 Port Resilience Hubs in coastal regions by 2035;

> (2) Ensure that each Port Resilience Hub can operate independently for a minimum of 30 days following a disaster;

> (3) Provide emergency shelter, supplies, and services to surrounding communities during disasters;

> (4) Maintain continuity of critical cargo movements during and after disasters;

> (5) Serve as coordination centers for regional disaster response.

**SEC. 811.** Hub Designation Criteria

**(a) ELIGIBILITY.** — To be designated as a Port Resilience Hub, a port must:

> (1) Be located in a coastal region subject to hurricanes, storm surge, sea-level rise, flooding, or other climate-related hazards;

> (2) Handle a minimum annual cargo throughput of 500,000 twenty-foot equivalent units (TEUs) or equivalent bulk cargo;

> (3) Have existing or planned intermodal transportation connections;

> (4) Be located within 50 miles of a population center of at least 100,000 people;

> (5) Commit to meeting the design and operational standards established under this title.

**(b) DESIGNATION PROCESS.** — The Secretary of Transportation shall:

> (1) Issue a request for proposals from eligible ports;

> (2) Evaluate proposals based on resilience need, cargo volume, community benefit, and implementation feasibility;

> (3) Designate Port Resilience Hubs in consultation with the Administrator of the Federal Emergency Management Agency;

> (4) Publish a list of designated Port Resilience Hubs in the Federal Register.

**(c) PRIORITY.** — Priority for designation shall be given to ports:

> (1) Located in areas with high vulnerability to climate change impacts;

> (2) That serve as the primary cargo gateway for their region;

> (3) Located in areas with limited alternative port capacity;

> (4) That serve disadvantaged communities with limited disaster recovery resources.

**SEC. 812.** Hub Design Standards

**(a) STRUCTURAL RESILIENCE.** — Each Port Resilience Hub shall be designed to:

> (1) Withstand a 500-year storm event, including storm surge, wave action, and wind loads;

> (2) Operate during and after a 100-year flood event;

> (3) Withstand seismic events appropriate to the region;

> (4) Maintain structural integrity during extreme heat events (up to 120°F);

> (5) Incorporate sea-level rise projections through 2100 into design elevations.

**(b) OPERATIONAL RESILIENCE.** — Each Port Resilience Hub shall include:

> (1) Backup power systems capable of providing full operational power for a minimum of 30 days;

> (2) Water supply and treatment systems capable of operating independently for 30 days;

> (3) Communications systems with satellite backup;

> (4) Fuel storage for emergency vehicles and equipment;

> (5) Emergency operations center with redundant communications;

> (6) Warehousing for emergency supplies and equipment.

**(c) COMMUNITY SERVICES.** — Each Port Resilience Hub shall include:

> (1) Emergency shelter capacity for at least 500 people;

> (2) Medical triage and first aid facilities;

> (3) Food preparation and distribution facilities;

> (4) Sanitation facilities;

> (5) Child care and family reunification spaces;

> (6) Pet shelter facilities.

**(d) ACCESSIBILITY.** — Port Resilience Hub facilities shall:

> (1) Be accessible to people with disabilities in compliance with the Americans with Disabilities Act;

> (2) Be located near public transportation;

> (3) Have multiple access routes that can be maintained during emergencies;

> (4) Include signage in multiple languages as appropriate to the community.

**SEC. 813.** Construction and Retrofit Funding

**(a) GRANTS.** — The Secretary of Transportation shall provide grants to designated Port Resilience Hubs for:

> (1) Construction of new resilience hub facilities;

> (2) Retrofit of existing port facilities to meet resilience hub standards;

> (3) Installation of backup power systems;

> (4) Construction of emergency shelters and community service facilities;

> (5) Installation of resilient communications systems.

**(b) FUNDING ALLOCATION.** — Grants shall be allocated based on:

> (1) The resilience need of the port and surrounding community;

> (2) The cargo volume and economic importance of the port;

> (3) The population served by the port;

> (4) The cost-effectiveness of the proposed improvements;

> (5) The port's commitment to long-term resilience operations.

**(c) COST SHARE.** — The federal share of project costs shall not exceed 80 percent, except for ports serving disadvantaged communities, where the federal share may be up to 90 percent.

**(d) FUNDING.** — The program provides $10,000,000,000 for Port Resilience Hub construction and retrofit.

**(c) PHASE 3 (Fiscal Years 2033-2035).** — During Phase 3, the Secretary shall:

> (4) Establish ongoing maintenance and operations programs;

> (5) Prepare a comprehensive program evaluation report.

**SEC. 815.** Hub Retrofit Program

**(a) EXISTING FACILITIES.** — The Secretary shall establish a program to retrofit existing port facilities to meet Port Resilience Hub standards, including:

> (1) Structural reinforcement to withstand extreme weather events;

> (2) Elevation of critical infrastructure above projected flood levels;

> (3) Installation of backup power systems;

> (4) Construction of emergency shelters;

> (5) Installation of resilient communications systems;

> (6) Improvement of drainage and flood protection systems.

**(b) COST-SHARE.** — The federal share of retrofit project costs shall not exceed 75 percent.

**(c) FUNDING.** — Of the $10,000,000,000 authorized for this subtitle, $4,000,000,000 shall be allocated to the retrofit program.

### Subtitle B — Resilient Infrastructure and Redundant Systems Program

**SEC. 820.** Establishment of Program

**(a) IN GENERAL.** — The Secretary of Transportation, in coordination with the Secretary of Homeland Security and the Secretary of Energy, shall establish and carry out a Resilient Infrastructure and Redundant Systems Program to ensure that critical port infrastructure can continue to operate during and after disasters.

**(b) PROGRAM GOALS.** — The goals of the Resilient Infrastructure and Redundant Systems Program are:

> (1) Ensure that all critical port infrastructure has redundant power, water, and communications systems;

> (2) Establish backup transportation routes for cargo movement during disruptions;

> (3) Create distributed cargo handling capacity to reduce single points of failure;

> (4) Implement smart grid technologies to enhance energy resilience;

> (5) Develop regional port coordination plans for disaster response.

**SEC. 821.** Backup Power Systems

**(a) REQUIREMENT.** — Each Port Resilience Hub shall install backup power systems that:

> (1) Can provide full operational power for a minimum of 30 days;

> (2) Include a combination of on-site generation, battery storage, and renewable energy;

> (3) Can be activated within 10 seconds of a grid power failure;

> (4) Are fueled by diverse sources, including diesel, natural gas, hydrogen, and solar;

> (5) Are protected from flood, wind, and seismic hazards.

**(b) TECHNOLOGY.** — Backup power systems may include:

> (1) Diesel generators with a minimum 30-day fuel supply;

> (2) Natural gas generators with dedicated supply lines;

> (3) Hydrogen fuel cell systems;

> (4) Battery energy storage systems with a minimum 4-hour duration;

> (5) Solar photovoltaic systems with battery storage;

> (6) Microgrids that can operate independently of the main electrical grid.

**(c) FUNDING.** — The program provides $2,000,000,000 for backup power systems at Port Resilience Hubs.

**SEC. 822.** Redundant Water Systems

**(a) REQUIREMENT.** — Each Port Resilience Hub shall install redundant water systems that:

> (1) Can provide potable water for a minimum of 30 days;

> (2) Include on-site water storage, treatment, and distribution;

> (3) Are protected from contamination during floods and storms;

> (4) Can serve both port operations and community needs;

> (5) Include connections to multiple water sources.

**(b) COMPONENTS.** — Redundant water systems may include:

> (1) On-site water storage tanks with a minimum 500,000-gallon capacity;

> (2) Water treatment systems capable of treating raw water to potable standards;

> (3) Distribution systems with backup pumps;

> (4) Connections to municipal water systems with backup intakes;

> (5) Rainwater harvesting systems;

> (6) Desalination systems for coastal locations.

**(c) FUNDING.** — The program provides $1,000,000,000 for redundant water systems at Port Resilience Hubs.

**SEC. 823.** Resilient Communications

**(a) REQUIREMENT.** — Each Port Resilience Hub shall install resilient communications systems that:

> (1) Can operate independently of terrestrial communications infrastructure;

> (2) Include satellite communications backup;

> (3) Provide interoperable communications with emergency management agencies;

> (4) Support voice, data, and video communications;

> (5) Can serve as a communications hub for the surrounding community.

**(b) COMPONENTS.** — Resilient communications systems may include:

> (1) Satellite communications terminals;

> (2) High-frequency radio systems;

> (3) Microwave backup links;

> (4) Mesh network systems;

> (5) Emergency alert systems;

> (6) Public address systems.

**(c) FUNDING.** — The program provides $1,000,000,000 for resilient communications systems at Port Resilience Hubs.

**SEC. 824.** Redundant Transportation Routes

**(a) REQUIREMENT.** — Each Port Resilience Hub shall have:

> (1) At least two independent highway access routes;

> (2) At least two rail access routes, where feasible;

> (3) Barge or waterway access as an alternative to road and rail;

> (4) Emergency access routes that can be maintained during disasters;

> (5) Coordination with regional transportation agencies for route maintenance.

**(b) INFRASTRUCTURE IMPROVEMENTS.** — The program provides $1,000,000,000 for:

> (1) Construction of redundant highway access routes;

> (2) Improvement of existing access routes to withstand extreme weather;

> (3) Construction of emergency barge landing facilities;

> (4) Installation of intelligent transportation systems for route management;

> (5) Coordination with state and local transportation agencies.

**(c) FUNDING.** — The program provides $1,000,000,000 for redundant transportation route improvements.

**SEC. 825.** Distributed Cargo Handling

**(a) REQUIREMENT.** — To reduce single points of failure, Port Resilience Hubs shall:

> (1) Distribute cargo handling capacity across multiple terminal locations;

> (2) Ensure that no single terminal handles more than 60 percent of the port's total throughput;

> (3) Maintain backup cargo handling equipment at each terminal;

> (4) Establish mutual aid agreements with neighboring ports for cargo handling;

> (5) Develop contingency plans for cargo diversion during disruptions.

**(b) FUNDING.** — The program provides $1,000,000,000 for distributed cargo handling improvements.

### Subtitle B — Resilient Infrastructure and Redundant Systems Program (Continued)

**SEC. 826.** Smart Grid Integration

**(a) MICROGRID REQUIREMENT.** — Each Port Resilience Hub shall incorporate a microgrid that:

> (1) Can operate independently of the main electrical grid (island mode);

> (2) Integrates multiple generation sources, including renewable energy;

> (3) Includes energy storage systems for load balancing;

> (4) Uses smart grid technologies for demand management;

> (5) Can provide power to critical community facilities during emergencies.

**(b) RENEWABLE ENERGY.** — Port Resilience Hub microgrids shall include:

> (1) Solar photovoltaic systems with a minimum capacity of 5 MW;

> (2) Wind turbines where feasible;

> (3) Battery energy storage with a minimum capacity of 20 MWh;

> (4) Smart inverters and control systems;

> (5) Electric vehicle charging infrastructure.

**(c) GRID INTERCONNECTION.** — Port Resilience Hub microgrids shall:

> (1) Maintain interconnection with the main electrical grid for normal operations;

> (2) Be capable of seamless transition to island mode within 10 seconds;

> (3) Provide grid services, including frequency regulation and voltage support;

> (4) Export excess renewable energy to the grid when operating in grid-connected mode.

**(d) FUNDING.** — The program provides $500,000,000 for smart grid integration at Port Resilience Hubs.

**SEC. 827.** Flood Protection Systems

**(a) REQUIREMENT.** — Each Port Resilience Hub shall incorporate flood protection systems that:

> (1) Protect all critical infrastructure from a 500-year flood event;

> (2) Include both structural and non-structural flood protection measures;

> (3) Account for sea-level rise projections through 2100;

> (4) Include flood warning and monitoring systems;

> (5) Can be deployed quickly in advance of forecast flooding.

**(b) STRUCTURAL MEASURES.** — Structural flood protection measures may include:

> (1) Flood walls and levees;

> (2) Storm surge barriers;

> (3) Tide gates and flood gates;

> (4) Elevated critical infrastructure;

> (5) Flood-proofed buildings and equipment.

**(c) NON-STRUCTURAL MEASURES.** — Non-structural flood protection measures may include:

> (1) Flood warning systems;

> (2) Emergency evacuation plans;

> (3) Flood-proofing of existing structures;

> (4) Land use planning to restrict development in flood-prone areas;

> (5) Natural infrastructure, including wetlands and living shorelines.

**(d) FUNDING.** — The program provides $500,000,000 for flood protection systems at Port Resilience Hubs.

> (1) Develop comprehensive emergency plans for each Port Resilience Hub;

> (2) Conduct regular training exercises to test emergency plans;

> (3) Establish mutual aid agreements between Port Resilience Hubs;

> (4) Pre-position emergency supplies and equipment at each hub;

> (5) Coordinate port emergency response with regional emergency management.

> (1) Identifies potential hazards, including hurricanes, floods, earthquakes, tsunamis, and industrial accidents;

> (2) Establishes emergency response procedures for each hazard;

> (3) Defines roles and responsibilities for port personnel and partner agencies;

> (4) Includes evacuation plans for port facilities and surrounding communities;

> (5) Establishes communication protocols for emergencies;

> (6) Includes procedures for damage assessment and recovery;

> (7) Is reviewed and updated at least annually.

**(b) PLAN COMPONENTS.** — Emergency Operations Plans shall include:

> (1) **Prevention and Protection** — Measures to prevent or reduce the likelihood of emergencies;

> (2) **Preparedness** — Training, exercises, and pre-positioning of resources;

> (3) **Response** — Procedures for immediate response to emergencies;

> (4) **Recovery** — Procedures for restoring operations and infrastructure;

> (5) **Mitigation** — Measures to reduce the impact of future emergencies.

**(c) COORDINATION.** — Emergency Operations Plans shall be coordinated with:

> (1) State and local emergency management agencies;

> (2) The United States Coast Guard;

> (3) The Army Corps of Engineers;

> (5) Neighboring ports and mutual aid partners.

**(a) TRAINING PROGRAM.** — Each Port Resilience Hub shall establish a training program that includes:

> (1) Annual emergency response training for all port personnel;

> (2) Specialized training for emergency response teams;

> (3) Community emergency response training for surrounding residents;

> (4) Coordination training with partner agencies;

> (5) Training on the use of backup systems and emergency equipment.

**(b) EXERCISE PROGRAM.** — Each Port Resilience Hub shall conduct:

> (1) Annual tabletop exercises to test emergency plans;

> (2) Biennial functional exercises to test specific response capabilities;

> (3) Triennial full-scale exercises to test the entire emergency response system;

> (4) After-action reviews following each exercise;

> (5) Corrective action plans to address identified deficiencies.

**(c) FUNDING.** — The program provides $1,000,000,000 for emergency training and exercises.

**SEC. 833.** Mutual Aid Agreements

**(a) REQUIREMENT.** — Each Port Resilience Hub shall enter into mutual aid agreements with:

> (1) At least two other Port Resilience Hubs;

> (5) Facilitate communication during emergencies.

> (3) Has access to specialized equipment;

> (5) Coordinates with regional emergency response agencies.

**(b) TEAMS.** — Emergency Response Teams shall include:

> (1) Search and rescue teams;

> (2) Hazardous materials response teams;

> (3) Emergency medical teams;

> (4) Damage assessment teams;

> (5) Communications teams;

> (6) Logistics support teams.

**(c) FUNDING.** — The program provides $1,500,000,000 for Emergency Response Teams.

### Subtitle E — Workforce Training and Capacity Building Program
