# TITLE X — FUNDING AND IMPLEMENTATION
## Act 10: Coastal Port Infrastructure Act of 2026

### Short Title

**SEC. 1201.** This title may be cited as the "Coastal Port Infrastructure Funding and Implementation Act of 2026."

### Purpose

**SEC. 1202.** The purposes of this title are:

> (1) To establish comprehensive funding mechanisms for the modernization of United States coastal port infrastructure;

> (2) To authorize appropriations sufficient to carry out the programs established under this Act;

> (3) To create accountability structures ensuring efficient and transparent expenditure of public funds;

> (4) To establish implementation schedules with measurable milestones and performance metrics;

> (5) To ensure compliance with domestic content requirements and Buy America provisions;

> (6) To provide for independent oversight, auditing, and economic impact assessment;

> (7) To reform the Harbor Maintenance Trust Fund to ensure full expenditure on authorized purposes;

> (8) To establish matching fund requirements that balance Federal investment with state, local, and private sector participation.

### Definitions

**SEC. 1203.** For purposes of this title:

**(A) "Appropriation"** means budget authority provided by Congress to enter into obligations that will result in outlays of Federal funds.

**(B) "Obligation"** means a binding agreement that will result in outlays, immediately or in the future.

**(C) "Outlay"** means a payment to liquidate an obligation.

**(D) "Reprogramming"** means the transfer of funds from one program, project, or activity to another within the same appropriation account.

**(E) "Coastal Port Infrastructure Fund"** means the fund established under Section 1210 of this title.

**(F) "Harbor Maintenance Trust Fund"** means the trust fund established under section 9505 of the Internal Revenue Code of 1986.

**(G) "Non-Federal Sponsor"** means a state, local government, tribal government, port authority, or other entity that provides the non-Federal share of project costs.

**(H) "Disadvantaged Community"** means a community identified pursuant to the Justice40 Initiative or meeting criteria established by the Secretary of Transportation.

**(I) "Performance Metric"** means a quantifiable measure used to assess progress toward statutory objectives.

**(J) "GAO"** means the Government Accountability Office.

**(K) "Inspector General"** means the Inspector General of the relevant Federal department or agency.

**(L) "Buy America Requirements"** means the domestic content requirements applicable to federally funded infrastructure projects, including the Build America, Buy America Act.

### Subtitle A — Authorization of Appropriations

**SEC. 1210.** Coastal Port Infrastructure Fund

**(a) ESTABLISHMENT.** — There is established in the Treasury of the United States a fund to be known as the "Coastal Port Infrastructure Fund" (in this section referred to as the "Fund").

**(b) AMOUNTS DEPOSITED.** — There shall be deposited into the Fund:

> (1) Amounts appropriated to the Fund pursuant to subsection (c);

> (2) Any amounts received from the repayment of loans made under this Act;

> (3) Any interest earned on investments of amounts in the Fund;

> (4) Any amounts transferred to the Fund from the Harbor Maintenance Trust Fund pursuant to section 1240;

> (5) Any gifts, donations, or contributions received for purposes consistent with this Act.

**(c) AUTHORIZATION OF APPROPRIATIONS.** — There is authorized to be appropriated to the Fund $100,000,000,000 for the period of fiscal years 2027 through 2036, to be allocated as provided in section 1211.

**(d) AVAILABILITY OF FUNDS.** — Amounts deposited in the Fund shall remain available until expended.

**(e) INVESTMENT.** — Amounts in the Fund shall be invested in accordance with section 9702 of title 31, United States Code, and any interest earned shall be credited to the Fund.

**(f) ADMINISTRATIVE EXPENSES.** — Not more than 2 percent of amounts appropriated to the Fund may be used for administrative expenses of the Department of Transportation in carrying out this Act.

### Subtitle A — Allocation Among Titles

**SEC. 1211.** Allocation of Funds

**(a) IN GENERAL.** — Of the amounts authorized under section 1210(c), funds shall be allocated as follows:

> (1) TITLE I (Port Modernization and Sea Wall Construction): $30,000,000,000

> (2) TITLE II (Supply Chain Resilience and Port Electrification): $25,000,000,000

> (3) TITLE III (Maritime Transportation and Harbor Deepening): $20,000,000,000

> (4) TITLE IV (Coastal Resilience and Ecosystem Restoration): $15,000,000,000

> (5) Reserve Fund (for Titles V through IX and administrative costs): $10,000,000,000

**(b) RESERVE FUND ALLOCATION.** — Amounts in the Reserve Fund shall be available for:

> (1) Title V (Environmental Protection): $2,000,000,000

> (2) Title VI (Port Resilience Hubs): $2,000,000,000

> (3) Title VII (Offshore Wind Port Infrastructure): $2,000,000,000

> (4) Title VIII (Maritime Decarbonization): $2,000,000,000

> (5) Title IX (Flood Insurance Reform): $1,000,000,000

> (6) Program administration, technical assistance, and oversight: $1,000,000,000

**(c) REALLOCATION AUTHORITY.** — The Secretary of Transportation may reallocate not more than 5 percent of the amount allocated to any title under subsection (a) to another title upon 30 days written notification to the appropriate congressional committees, provided that:

> (1) The reallocation is necessary to meet program objectives or respond to changed circumstances;

> (2) The reallocation does not materially alter the overall program balance established by this Act;

> (3) The Secretary provides a detailed justification for the reallocation in the next annual report.

**(d) CONTINGENCY RESERVE.** — Of the Reserve Fund, $500,000,000 shall be maintained as a contingency reserve for emergency supplemental allocations, available upon determination by the Secretary of Transportation, in consultation with the Secretary of the Treasury and the appropriate congressional committees, that additional funding is necessary to:

> (1) Respond to declared disasters affecting coastal port infrastructure;

> (2) Address unanticipated cost increases in authorized projects;

> (3) Fund critical emerging priorities not anticipated at the time of enactment.

### Subtitle D — Economic Impact Assessment

**SEC. 1225.** Independent Economic Impact Assessment

> (1) A nationally recognized research university with demonstrated expertise in transportation economics, coastal engineering, and regional economic analysis;

> (2) A nonpartisan research organization with a demonstrated record of conducting economic impact assessments of Federal infrastructure programs;

> (3) A consortium of entities described in paragraphs (1) and (2); or

### Subtitle E — Buy America Requirements

**SEC. 1230.** Domestic Content Requirements

**(a) DOMESTIC CONTENT.** — All iron and steel products, manufactured products, and construction materials used in projects funded under this Act shall be produced in the United States, consistent with the Build America, Buy America Act (Title IX of Division G of Public Law 117-58).

**(b) COVERED MATERIALS.** — For purposes of this section, covered materials include:

> (1) Iron and steel products, including structural steel, rebar, pipe, plate, and castings;

> (2) Manufactured products, including electrical equipment, mechanical equipment, and prefabricated structures;

> (3) Construction materials, including concrete, asphalt, aggregate, timber, and composite materials;

> (4) Dredging equipment, including dredge pumps, pipelines, and cutter heads;

> (5) Shore power equipment, including transformers, switchgear, and cable management systems;

> (6) Zero-emission cargo handling equipment, including electric cranes, yard tractors, and charging infrastructure;

> (7) Alternative fuel infrastructure, including storage tanks, dispensers, and safety systems.

**(c) WAIVER PROCESS.** — The head of the relevant Federal agency may waive requirements of subsection (a) only upon a written determination, published in the Federal Register for public comment for a period of not less than 15 days, that:

> (1) Applying the requirement would be inconsistent with the public interest;

> (2) Materials of satisfactory quality and sufficient quantity are not produced in the United States;

> (3) Inclusion of domestic material would increase overall project cost by more than 25 percent.

**(d) WAIVER REPORTING.** — Any waiver issued under subsection (c) shall be reported to the appropriate congressional committees within 15 days of issuance and included in the annual report required under section 1221.

**(e) DOMESTIC SHIPBUILDING PREFERENCE.** — For any project involving vessel construction or retrofit, grants and loan guarantees shall be conditioned on construction or retrofit occurring in a United States shipyard, consistent with the Jones Act (46 U.S.C. 55102).

**(f) CERTIFICATION.** — Each recipient of funding under this Act shall certify, as a condition of receiving funds, that all materials used in the project comply with the domestic content requirements of this section or that a valid waiver has been obtained.

**(g) AUDIT AND ENFORCEMENT.** — The Inspector General of each Federal department or agency providing funding under this Act shall include Buy America compliance in annual audits conducted under section 1223.

### Subtitle F — Matching Fund Requirements

**SEC. 1235.** Non-Federal Cost Share

**(a) STATE AND LOCAL MATCH.** — Except as provided in subsection (b), the Federal share of project costs under this Act shall not exceed:

> (1) **80 percent** for projects in disadvantaged communities, as defined in section 1203(H);

> (2) **65 percent** for projects in communities with median household income at or above 200 percent of the Federal poverty level;

> (3) **50 percent** for all other projects.

**(b) WAIVER.** — The Federal share may be increased to 100 percent for:

> (1) Projects serving Tribal communities, as defined in section 4 of the Indian Self-Determination and Education Assistance Act (25 U.S.C. 5304);

> (2) Projects where the non-Federal sponsor demonstrates inability to provide the required match, as determined by the Secretary of Transportation based on:

> (A) The non-Federal sponsor's bond rating or creditworthiness;

> (B) The non-Federal sponsor's current debt burden and debt service obligations;

> (C) The non-Federal sponsor's tax base and revenue-raising capacity;

> (D) The non-Federal sponsor's unemployment rate relative to the national average;

> (E) The non-Federal sponsor's designation as a financially distressed community by the Appalachian Regional Commission, the Delta Regional Authority, or similar body.

> (3) Through a combination of paragraphs (1) and (2).

### Subtitle G — Harbor Maintenance Trust Fund Reform

**SEC. 1240.** Harbor Maintenance Trust Fund Reform

**(a) FULL EXPENDITURE.** — The Secretary of the Treasury shall ensure that all amounts deposited in the Harbor Maintenance Trust Fund during a fiscal year are appropriated and expended for authorized harbor maintenance purposes during that or subsequent fiscal years.

**(b) ANNUAL APPROPRIATION.** — There is authorized to be appropriated from the Harbor Maintenance Trust Fund for each of fiscal years 2027 through 2036 an amount equal to the revenues received in the Trust Fund during the preceding fiscal year, plus any amounts previously deposited but not appropriated.

**(c) SET-ASIDE FOR EMERGING HARBORS.** — Not less than 15 percent of Harbor Maintenance Trust Fund expenditures shall be allocated to:

> (1) Emerging harbors, defined as ports handling less than 10 million tons of cargo annually;

> (2) Great Lakes navigation projects;

> (3) Donor ports, defined as ports whose Harbor Maintenance Trust Fund tax collections substantially exceed Harbor Maintenance Trust Fund expenditures at that port;

> (4) Remote and subsistence harbors serving isolated communities.

> (4) Submerged aquatic vegetation restoration;

> (5) Creation of bird and wildlife habitat.

## SUPPLEMENTARY ANALYSIS: HARBOR MAINTENANCE TRUST FUND DEEP DIVE

### A. Historical Context of the Harbor Maintenance Trust Fund

#### A.1 Origins and Legislative History

The Harbor Maintenance Trust Fund (HMTF) was established by the Water Resources Development Act of 1986 (Public Law 99-662) to provide a dedicated source of funding for the maintenance of Federal navigation channels and harbors. The HMTF is funded by the Harbor Maintenance Tax (HMT), an ad valorem tax imposed on the value of commercial cargo loaded or unloaded at United States ports.

The HMT was initially set at 0.04 percent of the value of commercial cargo. The tax was increased to 0.125 percent by the Omnibus Budget Reconciliation Act of 1990, where it has remained since. The HMT is paid by cargo owners (importers and exporters) and collected by U.S. Customs and Border Protection.

#### A.2 Revenue History

HMTF revenue has grown significantly since the fund's establishment, reflecting the growth in international trade:

> (1) **1987:** $247 million

> (2) **1990:** $412 million

> (3) **2000:** $932 million

> (4) **2010:** $1.2 billion

> (5) **2020:** $1.6 billion

> (6) **2025:** $1.8 billion

Cumulative HMTF revenue from 1987 through 2025 totals approximately $42 billion.

#### A.3 Expenditure History

HMTF expenditures have consistently fallen short of revenues, resulting in a growing accumulated balance:

> (1) **1987-1995:** Average annual expenditure of $400 million, approximately 70 percent of revenues;

> (2) **1996-2005:** Average annual expenditure of $600 million, approximately 65 percent of revenues;

> (3) **2006-2015:** Average annual expenditure of $800 million, approximately 60 percent of revenues;

> (4) **2016-2025:** Average annual expenditure of $1.1 billion, approximately 65 percent of revenues.

Cumulative HMTF expenditures from 1987 through 2025 total approximately $24 billion, compared to cumulative revenues of $42 billion, resulting in an accumulated balance of approximately $18 billion (adjusted for inflation, the real balance is approximately $9.5 billion).

### B. The Maintenance Deficit

#### B.1 Nature of the Deficit

The HMTF accumulated balance represents a maintenance deficit—the difference between the amount needed to maintain Federal navigation channels at their authorized dimensions and the amount actually expended. The Army Corps of Engineers estimates that:

> (1) Only approximately 60 to 70 percent of Federal navigation channels are maintained at their authorized dimensions at any given time;

> (2) The remaining 30 to 40 percent of channels are shallower than authorized, restricting vessel draft and reducing port efficiency;

> (3) The maintenance deficit exceeds $10 billion;

> (4) The deficit is growing at a rate of approximately $500 million per year.

#### B.2 Causes of the Deficit

The maintenance deficit has several causes:

> (1) **Appropriations Shortfall.** Congress has consistently appropriated less from the HMTF than the amount of revenue collected, using the HMTF balance as a budgetary offset for other spending;

> (2) **Rising Costs.** The cost of dredging and maintenance has increased faster than inflation, due to higher fuel costs, environmental requirements, and disposal costs;

> (3) **Increased Demand.** Growth in vessel size and cargo volume has increased the demand for maintenance dredging;

> (4) **Environmental Constraints.** Environmental requirements have increased the cost and complexity of dredging operations, including disposal restrictions and seasonal work windows;

> (5) **Fleet Deterioration.** The Federal dredging fleet has aged, reducing capacity and increasing costs.

#### B.3 Consequences of the Deficit

The maintenance deficit has significant consequences for the United States economy:

> (1) **Vessel Draft Restrictions.** Shallower channels restrict the draft of vessels calling at United States ports, reducing cargo capacity and increasing shipping costs;

> (2) **Reduced Port Competitiveness.** Ports with inadequate channel depth lose cargo to deeper ports, reducing economic activity and employment;

> (3) **Safety Risks.** Shallower channels increase the risk of vessel groundings and collisions;

> (4) **Supply Chain Inefficiency.** Vessel draft restrictions contribute to supply chain congestion and inefficiency;

> (5) **Increased Costs.** The total cost of the maintenance deficit to the United States economy is estimated at $10 billion to $15 billion annually.

### C. HMTF Reform Provisions

#### C.1 Full Expenditure Requirement

Section 1240(a) of this Act requires the Secretary of the Treasury to ensure that all amounts deposited in the HMTF during a fiscal year are appropriated and expended for authorized harbor maintenance purposes during that or subsequent fiscal years. This provision addresses the appropriations shortfall that has contributed to the maintenance deficit.

#### C.2 Emerging Harbors Set-Aside

Section 1240(c) requires that not less than 15 percent of HMTF expenditures be allocated to:

> (1) **Emerging Harbors.** Ports handling less than 10 million tons of cargo annually, which often lack the political influence to compete for HMTF funds with larger ports;

> (2) **Great Lakes Navigation Projects.** Great Lakes ports and navigation projects, which have historically received a disproportionately small share of HMTF expenditures relative to their HMT contributions;

> (3) **Donor Ports.** Ports whose HMTF tax collections substantially exceed HMTF expenditures at that port, ensuring that these ports receive a fair return on their HMT contributions;

> (4) **Remote and Subsistence Harbors.** Harbors serving isolated communities that depend on marine transportation for essential goods and services.

#### C.3 Beneficial Use Allocation

Section 1240(d) requires that not less than 10 percent of HMTF expenditures be dedicated to beneficial use of dredged material for:

> (1) **Wetland Creation and Restoration.** Using dredged material to create or restore coastal wetlands, providing flood storage, water quality improvement, and wildlife habitat;

> (2) **Beach Nourishment.** Using dredged material to replenish eroding beaches, providing storm protection and recreational benefits;

> (3) **Island and Barrier Island Restoration.** Using dredged material to restore barrier islands and other coastal landforms that provide natural storm protection;

> (4) **Submerged Aquatic Vegetation Restoration.** Using dredged material to restore submerged aquatic vegetation beds, providing habitat for fish and shellfish;

> (5) **Wildlife Habitat Creation.** Using dredged material to create bird and wildlife habitat.

#### C.4 Fiscal Impact

The HMTF reform provisions are projected to:

> (1) Increase annual HMTF expenditures by approximately $500 million to $700 million;

> (2) Reduce the maintenance deficit by approximately $5 billion to $7 billion over the authorization period;

> (3) Create approximately 10,000 additional jobs in dredging and related industries;

### D. Port Infrastructure Loan Program Analysis

#### D.1 Program Design

The Port Infrastructure Loan Program established under section 1255 is designed to leverage Federal investment by providing direct loans and loan guarantees to non-Federal borrowers. Key program design features include:

> (4) **Credit Standards.** Minimum debt service coverage ratios, minimum credit ratings, collateral requirements;

> (5) **Administration.** Administered by the Maritime Administration within the Department of Transportation.

The Port Infrastructure Loan Program is designed to leverage Federal investment by attracting non-Federal capital to port infrastructure projects. The leveraging effect is estimated at:

> (1) **Direct Loans.** Each dollar of Federal direct loan is estimated to leverage $2 to $3 in total project investment, including non-Federal cost share and private investment;

> (2) **Loan Guarantees.** Each dollar of Federal loan guarantee is estimated to leverage $5 to $10 in total project investment, as loan guarantees reduce the cost of private capital without requiring direct Federal expenditure;

> (3) **Total Leveraging.** The $5 billion authorized for the Port Infrastructure Loan Program is estimated to support $15 billion to $30 billion in total port infrastructure investment.

#### D.3 Comparison with Existing Programs

The Port Infrastructure Loan Program complements existing Federal credit programs:

> (1) **TIFIA.** The Transportation Infrastructure Finance and Innovation Act provides direct loans and loan guarantees for surface transportation projects, including port intermodal connectors;

> (2) **RRIF.** The Railroad Rehabilitation and Improvement Financing program provides direct loans and loan guarantees for railroad projects, including port rail connections;

> (3) **WIFIA.** The Water Infrastructure Finance and Innovation Act provides direct loans and loan guarantees for water infrastructure projects;

> (4) **Clean Water State Revolving Fund.** Provides low-interest loans for water quality improvement projects, including stormwater management at ports.

The Port Infrastructure Loan Program fills a gap in the Federal credit landscape by providing dedicated financing for port infrastructure projects that may not be eligible for or well-served by existing programs.

### D. Public-Private Partnership Framework

#### D.1 P3 Models

This Act authorizes the use of public-private partnerships (P3s) for port infrastructure projects. Common P3 models include:

> (1) **Design-Build (DB).** A single contractor is responsible for both design and construction, reducing project delivery time and transferring design risk to the private sector;

> (2) **Design-Build-Finance (DBF).** A private partner designs, constructs, and finances the project, with the public partner repaying the private partner over time;

> (3) **Design-Build-Finance-Maintain (DBFM).** A private partner designs, constructs, finances, and maintains the project, with the public partner making availability payments;

> (4) **Design-Build-Finance-Operate-Maintain (DBFOM).** A private partner designs, constructs, finances, operates, and maintains the project, with the public partner or users paying for services;

> (5) **Concession.** A private partner finances, constructs, operates, and maintains the project in exchange for the right to collect user fees for a specified period.

#### D.2 P3 Benefits

P3s can provide several benefits for port infrastructure projects:

> (1) **Accelerated Delivery.** P3s can accelerate project delivery by combining design and construction and providing incentives for early completion;

> (2) **Risk Transfer.** P3s can transfer construction, operational, and financial risk to the private partner;

> (3) **Innovation.** P3s can encourage innovation by allowing the private partner to propose cost-saving design and construction solutions;

> (4) **Whole-Life Costing.** P3s can encourage whole-life costing by making the private partner responsible for long-term maintenance;

> (5) **Private Capital.** P3s can attract private capital to port infrastructure projects, leveraging Federal investment.

#### D.3 P3 Risks

P3s also involve risks that must be managed:

> (1) **Complexity.** P3 transactions are complex and require specialized expertise to structure and manage;

> (2) **Cost of Capital.** Private capital is typically more expensive than Federal borrowing, potentially increasing project costs;

> (3) **Risk Allocation.** Improper risk allocation can increase costs and create disputes;

> (4) **Public Accountability.** P3s can reduce public accountability if not properly structured;

> (5) **Long-Term Commitments.** P3s create long-term financial commitments that may limit future flexibility.

> (4) **Emissions Reduction Commitments.** International commitments to reduce greenhouse gas emissions, including from the maritime sector, created urgency for port electrification and maritime decarbonization;

### B. Statutory Interpretation

#### B.1 Rules of Interpretation

The provisions of this Act shall be interpreted in accordance with established principles of statutory interpretation:

> (1) **Plain Meaning.** Words are given their ordinary, plain meaning unless the context indicates otherwise;

> (2) **Legislative Intent.** The intent of Congress, as expressed in the text and legislative history, governs interpretation;

> (3) **Harmonization.** Provisions are interpreted harmoniously to give effect to all provisions;

> (4) **Purpose.** Provisions are interpreted to advance the purposes stated in section 1202;

> (5) **Avoidance of Absurd Results.** Provisions are interpreted to avoid absurd or unreasonable results.

#### B.2 Key Definitions

Key terms used throughout this Act are defined in section 1203:

> (1) **Appropriation.** Budget authority provided by Congress to enter into obligations;

> (2) **Obligation.** A binding agreement that will result in outlays;

> (3) **Outlay.** A payment to liquidate an obligation;

> (4) **Reprogramming.** The transfer of funds from one program, project, or activity to another within the same appropriation account;

> (5) **Coastal Port Infrastructure Fund.** The fund established under section 1210;

> (6) **Harbor Maintenance Trust Fund.** The trust fund established under section 9505 of the Internal Revenue Code;

> (7) **Non-Federal Sponsor.** A state, local government, tribal government, port authority, or other entity that provides the non-Federal share of project costs;

> (8) **Disadvantaged Community.** A community identified pursuant to the Justice40 Initiative;

> (9) **Performance Metric.** A quantifiable measure used to assess progress toward statutory objectives.

> (3) **Endangered Species Act.** All projects shall comply with the Endangered Species Act, including consultation requirements;

### C. Severability and Savings

#### C.1 Severability

Section 1293 provides that if any provision of this Act is held invalid, the remainder of the Act shall not be affected. This severability clause ensures that:

> (1) The invalidity of one provision does not invalidate the entire Act;

> (2) The remaining provisions continue to be effective;

> (3) The purposes of the Act are preserved to the maximum extent possible.

#### C.2 Savings Clause

Section 1292 provides that this Act does not:

> (1) Repeal, supersede, or modify any existing Federal statute not expressly amended;

> (2) Limit the authority of any Federal agency under existing law;

> (3) Preempt State or local laws that provide greater protection;

> (4) Diminish tribal treaty rights or trust obligations.

### D. Effective Dates

#### D.1 General Effective Date

Section 1294 provides that this Act takes effect on the date of enactment, with the following exceptions:

> (1) **Implementation Schedule.** Takes effect on October 1, 2026, to align with the Federal fiscal year;

> (2) **Buy America Requirements.** Takes effect 180 days after enactment to allow time for compliance preparation;

> (3) **Harbor Maintenance Trust Fund Reform.** Takes effect on October 1, 2026, to align with the Federal fiscal year;

> (4) **Workforce Development.** Takes effect 12 months after enactment to allow time for program development.

### E. Congressional Budget Office Estimate

#### E.1 Cost Estimate

The Congressional Budget Office estimates that this Act will:

> (1) Increase direct spending by $100 billion over the FY2027-2036 period;

> (2) Increase Federal revenues by approximately $27 billion over the authorization period;

> (3) Result in a net Federal cost of approximately $73 billion over the authorization period;

> (4) Have no significant effect on discretionary spending, as the authorized amounts are provided through the appropriations process.

#### E.2 Pay-As-You-Go Considerations

CBO estimates that this Act would increase direct spending and decrease revenues, triggering pay-as-you-go procedures. The Act includes provisions to offset the budgetary effects, including:

> (1) Increased Federal revenues from economic activity generated by the Act;

> (2) Reduced Federal spending on disaster recovery due to improved coastal resilience;

> (3) Increased Federal revenues from increased trade-related economic activity.
