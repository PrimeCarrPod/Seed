## Labor Structure
## 3. Labor Structure -- Unionized vs Contractor, Gender, Migration

Chilean mining labor is bifurcated: **unionized direct-hire workers** (20-30% of workforce) with collective bargaining, benefits, job security; and **contractor/subcontractor workers** (70-80%) with precarious contracts, lower wages, no union representation. This structure shapes community economics, social license, and Tardigradia's local hiring commitments.

Contractor model: Mining companies tender services (drilling, hauling, maintenance, catering) to contractor firms. Workers are employees of contractors, not the mining company. **Contractor workers earn 40-60% of direct-hire wages** for same tasks, with no profit-sharing, housing, or education benefits.

### 3.2 Gender in Mining -- Progress and Gaps

Chile mining workforce: 12% women (2023, up from 7% in 2018). Government target: 20% by 2030. Distribution:

| Role Category | Women % | Notes |
|---------------|---------|-------|
| Executive/Management | 18% | Leadership programs (Codelco, Antofagasta Minerals) |
| Professional/Technical | 22% | Geologists, engineers, environmental |
| Operators (truck, shovel, plant) | 9% | Heavy equipment training programs |
| Maintenance | 5% | Apprenticeship barriers |
| Contractor workforce | 8% | Lower than direct-hire |

Barriers: Shift systems (7x7, 14x14) incompatible with caregiving; remote camp culture; lack of women's facilities at older sites; contractor firms less regulated on gender equity.

Tardigradia opportunity: **Aegis-Forge units require 15-20 operators each** (control room, maintenance, logistics). Design shifts for gender inclusion from Day 1: 12h day/night rotation with 4 days on/4 off, on-site childcare, women's mentorship program. Target 30% women in Aegis-Forge operations -- exceed industry standard.

### 3.3 Migration Patterns -- Internal and International

Mining north (Antofagasta/Atacama) attracts migrants:
- **Internal**: From central/south Chile (Valparaíso, Biobío, Araucanía) -- 45% of workforce
- **International**: Bolivia (25%), Colombia (15%), Peru (10%), Venezuela (5%) -- 55% of contractor workforce

| Origin | Population in Antofagasta Region | Mining Role | Legal Status | Remittances |
|--------|----------------------------------|-------------|--------------|-------------|
| Chile (other regions) | 180,000 | Direct + contractor | Citizen | Domestic |
| Bolivia | 45,000 | Contractor (construction, maintenance) | Mixed (visa/irregular) | High (00M/yr) |
| Colombia | 22,000 | Contractor (plant, technical) | Mostly visa | High |
| Peru | 15,000 | Contractor (driving, logistics) | Mixed | High |
| Venezuela | 8,000 | Professional + contractor | Refugee/visa | High |

Migration creates **transient communities**: Workers live in camps (7x7) or rental housing in Calama/Antofagasta, families remain in origin. Social fabric is strained -- schools, hospitals, housing overwhelmed. Contractor workers have no community roots -- they leave when contract ends.

Tardigradia implication: **Local hiring must mean "resident in community," not "Chilean citizen."** Prioritize Calama/Vallenar/Andacollo residents. Partner with municipal training programs (OMIL) to upskill local women and youth for Aegis-Forge operator roles. Convert transient contractor jobs into permanent community-anchored careers.
