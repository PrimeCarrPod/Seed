# 11. Dark Matter Direct Detection from Gaps

The dark matter mass spectrum is:

m_{DM}(d) = ℏ / (κ · d_{missing})

For the first missing gap d = 8:
m_{DM}(8) ≈ ℏ / (κ · 8) ≈ (254/8) × m_e ≈ 1.4 MeV

For d = 10:
m_{DM}(10) ≈ 1.1 MeV

For d = 12:

These are sub-MeV to MeV scale dark matter candidates.

## 11.2 Direct Detection Cross Sections

Dark matter-nucleon scattering occurs through gap-mediated interactions. The worldline fold that couples dark matter (missing gap sector) to nucleons (gap 4, 6, 14 sectors) gives:

σ_{DM-N} = (μ_{DM-N}² / π) |ℳ|²

where the matrix element is:

ℳ = (1/ℓ_P²) A_{d_{DM}, d_N}^{grav} (q²/m_{Pl}²) f_{DM}(q)

For the lightest DM candidate (d=8) on xenon (A=131):

A_{8,14}^{grav} ≈ 3.2 × 10⁻⁴ (gap correlation from PrimeBookOne)
μ_{DM-N} ≈ m_{DM} ≈ 1.4 MeV

σ_{SI}(DM-Xe) ≈ 2.3 × 10⁻⁴⁶ cm²

This is within reach of XENONnT, LUX-ZEPLIN, and future DARWIN.

For the d=10 candidate:
σ_{SI} ≈ 1.8 × 10⁻⁴⁶ cm²

The cross section scales as σ ∝ 1/d⁴, so heavier missing gaps have smaller cross sections.

## 11.3 Annual Modulation Signal

The gap origin of dark matter predicts a distinctive annual modulation pattern. The dark matter velocity distribution is determined by the gap statistics in the solar neighborhood:

f(v) ∝ exp(-v²/2σ_{gap}²)

where σ_{gap}² is the variance of the gap distribution. The modulation amplitude is:

S_m / S_0 ≈ 0.02 (v_{earth} / σ_{gap}) ≈ 0.05

consistent with DAMA/LIBRA and testable by COSINE-100, ANAIS.
