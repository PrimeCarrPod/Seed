# TITLE II — GRID HARDENING

## SEC. 401. SHORT TITLE

**SEC. 401.** This title may be cited as the "Grid Hardening and Resilience Act of 2026."

---

## SEC. 402. FINDINGS SPECIFIC TO GRID HARDENING

**SEC. 402.** The Congress finds the following with respect to electrical grid hardening:

**(1) Ignition Risk**
Electrical infrastructure is the ignition source for the most destructive wildfires in U.S. history. The 2018 Camp Fire (85 fatalities, $16.5B damages), 2020 Oregon Fire Siege (11 fatalities, $6B damages), and 2021 Marshall Fire (2 fatalities, $5B damages) were all ignited by electrical infrastructure during extreme weather events.

**(2) Underinvestment**
Electrical utilities have systematically underinvested in grid hardening despite known ignition risks. Only 2,500 miles of the 200,000+ miles of overhead lines in high fire risk areas have been hardened. At current rates, full hardening would take 80+ years — far too slow to address the current crisis.

**(3) Cost of Inaction**
The cost of grid hardening ($1.5M per mile for covered conductors, $3-5M per mile for undergrounding) is far less than the cost of wildfire damages ($8.5M per mile in wildfire damages prevented). Every dollar invested in grid hardening returns $5.70 in avoided wildfire costs.

**(4) Technology Availability**
Technologies exist today that can dramatically reduce ignition risk. Covered conductors reduce ignition risk by 95%. Rapid fault isolation prevents 80% of ignitions from conductor contact. Fast curve switching reduces ignition risk from damaged equipment by 70%.

**(5) Regulatory Failure**
State utility commissions have failed to mandate adequate grid hardening. Only 5 states have adopted comprehensive grid hardening standards. Most states rely on voluntary utility programs that have proven insufficient.

**(6) Climate Change Acceleration**
Climate change is increasing the frequency and intensity of extreme fire weather events. The number of Red Flag Warning days has increased 40% since 2000. Grid hardening standards must account for future climate conditions, not just historical weather patterns.

## SEC. 403. PURPOSES OF TITLE II

**SEC. 403.** The purposes of this title are to:

**(1)** Establish mandatory grid hardening standards for all electrical infrastructure in high fire risk areas.

**(2)** Accelerate the deployment of covered conductors, undergrounding, and other hardening technologies to reduce ignition risk.

**(3)** Create a dedicated funding mechanism for grid hardening that is sustainable and sufficient to address the scale of the need.

**(4)** Modernize grid infrastructure to improve reliability, reduce outage frequency, and support renewable energy integration.

**(5)** Establish accountability metrics and reporting requirements to ensure utilities achieve measurable risk reduction.

**(6)** Protect ratepayers from bearing the full cost of grid hardening through federal cost-share programs.

**(7)** Ensure that grid hardening investments prioritize disadvantaged communities and high-risk areas.

**(8)** Support workforce development for grid hardening construction and maintenance.

---

## SEC. 404. DEFINITIONS FOR TITLE II

**SEC. 404.** In this title:

**(1) "Grid Hardening"**
The physical modification of electrical infrastructure to reduce ignition risk during high-wind, low-humidity events, including but not limited to covered conductor installation, undergrounding, rapid fault isolation, fast curve switching, and equipment replacement.

**(2) "Covered Conductor"**
An electrical conductor encased in a thick layer of insulating material rated to withstand momentary contact with vegetation without arcing or ignition, consistent with IEEE 1202 standards.

**(3) "High Fire Risk Area"**
Any area designated as Fire Hazard Severity Zone "High" or "Very High" by the state fire marshal, or any area within the Wildland-Urban Interface as mapped by the USDA Forest Service.

**(4) "Public Safety Power Shutoff (PSPS)"**
The preemptive de-energization of electrical lines during extreme fire weather conditions, subject to the standards and protocols established under Sec. 406.

**(5) "Rapid Fault Isolation"**
Technology that detects and isolates faults on electrical lines within 1/10th of a second, preventing the sustained arcing that causes vegetation ignitions.

**(6) "Fast Curve Switching"**
Technology that reduces the energy delivered to a fault by modifying the protective relay settings, reducing ignition risk from damaged equipment.

**(7) "Microgrid"**
A group of interconnected loads and distributed energy resources within clearly defined electrical boundaries that acts as a single controllable entity with respect to the grid, as defined by IEEE 2030.7.

**(8) "Undergrounding"**
The replacement of overhead electrical lines with underground cables, eliminating the ignition risk from overhead line contact with vegetation.

## SEC. 405. NATIONAL GRID HARDENING STANDARDS

**SEC. 405.** (a) IN GENERAL.—Not later than 1 year after the date of enactment, the Secretary of Energy, in coordination with the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission (FERC), shall establish national grid hardening standards for electrical infrastructure in high fire risk areas.

**(b) STANDARDS.—**The standards shall include:

**(1) Covered Conductor Requirements**
All new overhead distribution lines in high fire risk areas shall use covered conductor. Existing overhead distribution lines in high fire risk areas shall be retrofitted with covered conductor within 10 years.

**(2) Undergrounding Requirements**
All new distribution lines in the highest fire risk areas (Fire Hazard Severity Zone "Very High") shall be undergrounded. Existing distribution lines in these areas shall be undergrounded within 15 years where technically feasible.

**(3) Rapid Fault Isolation**
All distribution lines in high fire risk areas shall be equipped with rapid fault isolation technology within 5 years.

**(4) Fast Curve Switching**
All protective relays on distribution lines in high fire risk areas shall be configured with fast curve switching settings within 2 years.

**(5) Equipment Replacement**
All aging equipment (conductors, insulators, transformers, switches) on distribution lines in high fire risk areas shall be replaced on an accelerated schedule, with priority for equipment over 40 years old.

**(6) Vegetation Management**
All vegetation within the utility right-of-way in high fire risk areas shall be managed consistent with the standards established under Sec. 309(b)(1).

**(7) Weather Monitoring**
All utilities in high fire risk areas shall deploy weather monitoring stations at intervals of no more than 10 miles along distribution lines.

**(8) System Hardening Plans**
All utilities shall develop and submit to the Secretary a system hardening plan that identifies the schedule, cost, and prioritization of hardening activities.

**(c) COMPLIANCE TIMELINE.—**

**(1) SHORT-TERM.—**Utilities shall achieve compliance with fast curve switching and weather monitoring requirements within 2 years.

**(2) MEDIUM-TERM.—**Utilities shall achieve compliance with rapid fault isolation and equipment replacement requirements within 5 years.

**(3) LONG-TERM.—**Utilities shall achieve compliance with covered conductor and undergrounding requirements within 10-15 years.

## SEC. 406. PUBLIC SAFETY POWER SHUTOFF STANDARDS

**SEC. 406.** (a) PSPS PROTOCOLS.—

**(1) IN GENERAL.—**The Secretary of Energy shall establish national standards for Public Safety Power Shutoff (PSPS) events, including:
(A) Weather thresholds for PSPS activation
(B) Notification requirements for affected customers
(C) Duration limitations for PSPS events
(D) Critical facility exemptions
(E) Post-event reporting requirements

**(2) NOTIFICATION.—**Utilities shall provide a minimum of 48 hours advance notice of potential PSPS events and 24 hours confirmation notice to all affected customers, including:
(A) Geographic scope of the shutoff
(B) Estimated duration
(C) Available resources and assistance
(D) Restoration timeline

**(3) DURATION LIMITATIONS.—**PSPS events shall not exceed 72 hours except in extraordinary circumstances, as determined by the applicable state public utilities commission.

**(4) CRITICAL FACILITIES.—**The following facilities shall be exempt from PSPS events to the maximum extent practicable:
(A) Hospitals and medical facilities
(B) Emergency response facilities
(C) Water and wastewater treatment plants
(D) Telecommunications facilities
(E) Public safety facilities
(F) Facilities serving vulnerable populations

**(b) PSPS MITIGATION.—**

**(1) MICROGRID DEPLOYMENT.—**Utilities shall deploy microgrids to maintain power to critical facilities during PSPS events, with the goal of covering all critical facilities within 5 years.

**(2) BATTERY STORAGE.—**Utilities shall deploy battery storage systems to provide backup power to customers during PSPS events, with priority for medical baseline customers and disadvantaged communities.

**(3) GENERATOR PROGRAM.—**The Secretary shall establish a program to provide backup generators to medical baseline customers and critical facilities in high fire risk areas.

**(c) PSPS ACCOUNTABILITY.—**

**(1) ANNUAL REPORT.—**Utilities shall submit annual reports to the Secretary on PSPS events, including:
(A) Number and duration of events
(B) Customers affected
(C) Weather conditions
(D) Mitigation measures deployed
(E) Customer complaints and resolutions

**(2) PERFORMANCE METRICS.—**The Secretary shall establish performance metrics for PSPS events, including:
(A) Notification compliance rate
(B) Duration compliance rate
(C) Critical facility exemption rate
(D) Customer satisfaction

## SEC. 407. GRID HARDENING FUNDING PROGRAM

**SEC. 407.** (a) NATIONAL GRID HARDENING FUND.—

**(1) ESTABLISHMENT.—**There is established in the Treasury of the United States a fund to be known as the "National Grid Hardening Fund" to provide financial assistance for grid hardening activities.

**(2) FUNDING.—**There is authorized to be appropriated to the Fund $30,000,000,000 for the period of fiscal years 2027 through 2036.

**(b) COST-SHARE PROGRAM.—**

**(1) FEDERAL COST-SHARE.—**The Secretary of Energy shall provide federal cost-share assistance for grid hardening projects, with the federal share not exceeding:
(A) 75% for investor-owned utilities
(B) 85% for publicly owned utilities
(C) 90% for rural electric cooperatives
(D) 95% for Tribal utilities

**(2) PRIORITY.—**Cost-share assistance shall be prioritized for:
(A) Highest fire risk areas
(B) Disadvantaged communities
(C) Aging infrastructure
(D) High-ignition-risk equipment

**(c) GRANT PROGRAM.—**

**(1) COMPETITIVE GRANTS.—**The Secretary shall provide competitive grants for grid hardening projects, with priority for projects that:
(A) Achieve the greatest ignition risk reduction per dollar invested
(B) Serve disadvantaged communities
(C) Deploy innovative technologies
(D) Create the greatest number of jobs

**(2) FORMULA GRANTS.—**The Secretary shall provide formula grants to states for grid hardening, allocated based on:
(A) Fire risk acreage
(B) Miles of overhead line in high fire risk areas
(C) Population in high fire risk areas
(D) Historical ignition data

**(d) LOAN PROGRAM.—**

**(1) LOW-INTEREST LOANS.—**The Secretary shall provide low-interest loans for grid hardening projects, with interest rates not exceeding 2% and terms up to 30 years.

**(2) LOAN FORGIVENESS.—**The Secretary may forgive up to 50% of loan principal for projects that achieve exceptional risk reduction or serve disadvantaged communities.

**(e) RATE RECOVERY.—**

**(1) FEDERAL GUARANTEE.—**The Secretary shall provide federal guarantees for utility rate recovery of grid hardening costs, ensuring that utilities can recover prudent hardening investments through rates.

**(2) RATEPAYER PROTECTION.—**Grid hardening costs recovered through rates shall be subject to reasonableness review by state public utilities commissions.

## SEC. 423. SEVERABILITY

**SEC. 423.** If any provision of this title or the application thereof to any person or circumstance is held invalid, the remainder of the title and the application of such provision to other persons or circumstances shall not be affected thereby.

---

## SEC. 424. RELATIONSHIP TO EXISTING LAW

**SEC. 424.** (a) FEDERAL POWER ACT.—**This title amends the Federal Power Act as provided in Title VIII.

**(b) STATE REGULATORY AUTHORITY.—**Nothing in this title shall be construed to diminish the authority of state public utilities commissions.

**(c) UTILITY REGULATION.—**This title does not change the regulatory status of electrical utilities.
