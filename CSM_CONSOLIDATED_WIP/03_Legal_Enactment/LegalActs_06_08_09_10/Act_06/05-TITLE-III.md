# TITLE III — VEGETATION MANAGEMENT

## SEC. 501. SHORT TITLE

**SEC. 501.** This title may be cited as the "National Vegetation Management Act of 2026."

---

## SEC. 502. FINDINGS SPECIFIC TO VEGETATION MANAGEMENT

**SEC. 502.** The Congress finds the following with respect to vegetation management:

**(1) Fuel Load Crisis**
Decades of fire suppression have created historically dangerous fuel loads on federal, state, and private lands. The USDA Forest Service estimates 80 million acres of national forestland require immediate vegetation management treatment. Current treatment rates address less than 2% of the annual need.

**(2) Treatment Backlog**
The backlog of acres needing vegetation management exceeds 100 million acres across all ownerships. At current treatment rates of 3 million acres annually, full treatment would take over 30 years — far too slow to address the current crisis.

**(3) Ecological Benefits**
Vegetation management is not only a wildfire prevention tool but also an ecologically beneficial practice. Thinning overcrowded forests improves forest health, increases drought resilience, enhances wildlife habitat, and promotes biodiversity.

**(4) Economic Opportunity**
Vegetation management creates economic opportunities in rural communities. Timber harvesting, biomass utilization, and ecosystem restoration provide jobs and revenue. Every million acres treated creates approximately 15,000 jobs.

**(5) Climate Benefits**
Healthy, well-managed forests sequester more carbon than overstocked, fire-prone forests. Vegetation management reduces wildfire carbon emissions and enhances long-term carbon sequestration.

**(6) Water Security**
Vegetation management in watersheds protects water quality and supply. Post-fire erosion contaminates water supplies for millions of Americans. Proactive vegetation management is more cost-effective than post-fire watershed restoration.

## SEC. 503. PURPOSES OF TITLE III

**SEC. 503.** The purposes of this title are to:

**(1)** Establish a national vegetation management program that treats 25 million acres annually by year 10.

**(2)** Prioritize vegetation management in Wildland-Urban Interface zones, critical watersheds, and emergency access corridors.

**(3)** Expand the use of prescribed fire and cultural burning as primary vegetation management tools.

**(4)** Create markets for biomass removed during vegetation management to offset treatment costs.

**(5)** Build a world-class vegetation management workforce through training and recruitment programs.

**(6)** Streamline environmental review for vegetation management projects while maintaining environmental protections.

**(7)** Establish accountability metrics and reporting requirements to ensure vegetation management programs achieve measurable results.

**(8)** Coordinate vegetation management across federal, state, Tribal, and private lands.

---

## SEC. 504. DEFINITIONS FOR TITLE III

**SEC. 504.** In this title:

**(1) "Vegetation Management"**
The planned manipulation of vegetation to achieve land management objectives, including wildfire risk reduction, forest health improvement, ecosystem restoration, and wildlife habitat enhancement.

**(2) "Hazardous Fuel Reduction"**
The removal or modification of vegetative material to reduce fire hazard and fire behavior potential.

**(3) "Forest Health"**
The condition of a forest as determined by its age structure, species composition, stocking density, and resistance to disturbance.

**(4) "Ecosystem Restoration"**
The process of assisting the recovery of an ecosystem that has been degraded, damaged, or destroyed.

**(5) "Biomass"**
Organic material derived from vegetation management activities, including trees, shrubs, slash, and other plant material.

**(6) "Shaded Fuel Break"**
A strategically located area of reduced tree canopy density designed to reduce fire intensity and provide safe areas for firefighter operations.

## SEC. 505. NATIONAL VEGETATION MANAGEMENT PROGRAM

**SEC. 505.** (a) ESTABLISHMENT.—There is established in the Department of Agriculture a National Vegetation Management Program to coordinate and accelerate vegetation management on federal, state, and private lands.

**(b) FEDERAL LAND TREATMENT TARGETS.—**

**(1) ANNUAL TARGETS.—**The Secretary of Agriculture and Secretary of Interior shall treat a combined minimum of:
(A) 10 million acres annually by year 1
(B) 15 million acres annually by year 3
(C) 20 million acres annually by year 5
(D) 25 million acres annually by year 10

**(2) PRIORITY AREAS.—**Treatment shall be prioritized in:
(A) Wildland-Urban Interface zones
(B) Critical watersheds serving municipal water supplies
(C) Emergency access corridors and evacuation routes
(D) Power line rights-of-way
(E) Critical habitat for threatened and endangered species
(F) Areas with highest fire hazard severity ratings

**(3) TREATMENT METHODS.—**Treatment shall employ the most cost-effective and ecologically appropriate methods, including:
(A) Prescribed fire ($50-500/acre)
(B) Mechanical thinning ($500-2,000/acre)
(C) Hand thinning ($1,000-3,000/acre)
(D) Mastication ($500-1,500/acre)
(E) Targeted grazing ($50-200/acre)
(F) Cultural burning ($50-200/acre)
(G) Biomass utilization (cost offset)

**(c) STATE AND PRIVATE LAND INCENTIVES.—**

**(1) COST-SHARE PROGRAM.—**The Secretary shall establish a cost-share program providing up to 75% federal cost-share for vegetation management on state and private lands in high fire risk areas.

**(2) TECHNICAL ASSISTANCE.—**The Secretary shall provide technical assistance to state and local governments, Tribal nations, and private landowners for vegetation management planning and implementation.

**(3) BLOCK GRANTS.—**The Secretary shall provide block grants to states for vegetation management programs, allocated based on fire risk, acreage, and treatment need.

## SEC. 506. PRESCRIBED FIRE PROGRAM

**SEC. 506.** (a) PRESCRIBED FIRE TARGETS.—

**(1) FEDERAL LANDS.—**The Secretary of Agriculture and Secretary of Interior shall collectively apply prescribed fire to a minimum of:
(A) 5 million acres annually by year 3
(B) 8 million acres annually by year 5
(C) 10 million acres annually by year 7

**(2) STATE AND PRIVATE LANDS.—**The Secretary shall provide technical and financial assistance to achieve prescribed fire application on 3 million acres of state and private lands annually by year 5.

**(b) PRESCRIBED FIRE LIABILITY REFORM.—**

**(1) FEDERAL LIABILITY.—**The United States shall assume liability for prescribed fire conducted in accordance with an approved prescription plan by federal agencies or their contractors.

**(2) STATE LIABILITY PROTECTION.—**The Secretary shall provide model legislation for states to limit liability for prescribed fire conducted in accordance with certified prescription plans.

**(3) CERTIFICATION PROGRAM.—**The Secretary shall establish a national prescribed fire certification program for prescribed fire practitioners.

**(c) AIR QUALITY COORDINATION.—**

**(1) INTERAGENCY AGREEMENT.—**The Secretary shall enter into agreements with the Environmental Protection Agency and state air quality agencies to coordinate prescribed fire smoke management.

**(2) SMOKE EXEMPTION.—**Prescribed fire conducted in accordance with an approved prescription plan shall be exempt from state air quality standards during the approved burn window.

**(3) MONITORING.—**The Secretary shall deploy smoke monitoring equipment in prescribed fire areas to ensure smoke impacts are minimized.

**(d) CULTURAL BURNING PROGRAM.—**

**(1) TRIBAL SOVEREIGNTY.—**Nothing in this section shall be construed to limit the right of Tribal nations to conduct cultural burns on their lands.

**(2) CO-MANAGEMENT.—**The Secretary shall enter into co-management agreements with Tribal nations for cultural burning on federal lands within their ancestral territories.

**(3) FUNDING.—**There is authorized to be appropriated $500 million annually for the cultural burning program.

## SEC. 509. ENVIRONMENTAL COMPLIANCE FOR VEGETATION MANAGEMENT

**SEC. 509.** (a) NATIONAL ENVIRONMENTAL POLICY ACT.—

**(1) PROGRAMMATIC ENVIRONMENTAL IMPACT STATEMENT.—**The Secretary shall prepare a programmatic environmental impact statement for vegetation management activities.

**(2) CATEGORICAL EXCLUSIONS.—**The Secretary shall develop categorical exclusions for routine vegetation management activities, including:
(A) Prescribed fire in accordance with approved prescription plans
(B) Mechanical thinning of trees less than 12 inches diameter
(C) Hand thinning and pruning
(D) Fuel break construction in previously treated areas
(E) Biomass removal from existing landings and roads

**(b) ENDANGERED SPECIES ACT.—**

**(1) PROGRAMMATIC BIOLOGICAL OPINION.—**The Secretary shall obtain a programmatic biological opinion for vegetation management activities.

**(2) CONSERVATION MEASURES.—**Vegetation management activities shall incorporate conservation measures for threatened and endangered species.

**(c) CLEAN WATER ACT.—**

**(1) STORMWATER MANAGEMENT.—**Vegetation management activities shall incorporate stormwater management measures to prevent erosion and water quality impacts.

**(2) BUFFER ZONES.—**Vegetation management activities shall maintain buffer zones around waterways and wetlands.

**(d) CLEAN AIR ACT.—**

**(1) PRESCRIBED FIRE SMOKE MANAGEMENT.—**Prescribed fire activities shall incorporate smoke management practices to minimize air quality impacts.

**(2) MONITORING.—**The Secretary shall deploy smoke monitoring equipment in prescribed fire areas.
