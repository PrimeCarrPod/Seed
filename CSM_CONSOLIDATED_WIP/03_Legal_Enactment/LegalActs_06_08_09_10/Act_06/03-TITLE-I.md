# TITLE I — WILDFIRE PREVENTION

## SEC. 301. SHORT TITLE

**SEC. 301.** This title may be cited as the "Wildfire Prevention Act of 2026."

---

## SEC. 302. FINDINGS SPECIFIC TO WILDFIRE PREVENTION

**SEC. 302.** The Congress finds the following with respect to wildfire prevention:

**(1) Prevention Gap**
Current federal spending allocates less than 5% of wildfire budgets to prevention. Over 95% of federal wildfire funding is directed toward suppression and response. This imbalance perpetuates a cycle of increasing fire severity and cost. Every dollar shifted from suppression to prevention yields a 3:1 return in avoided costs.

**(2) Vegetation Management Crisis**
Fuel loads on federal lands have reached historically dangerous levels. The USDA Forest Service estimates that 80 million acres require immediate vegetation management treatment. Current treatment rates address only 1.5 million acres annually — less than 2% of the need. At current rates, full treatment would take over 50 years.

**(3) Ignition Source Analysis**
Human-caused ignitions account for 85% of all wildfires. Electrical infrastructure ignitions, while representing only 10% of starts, account for 50% of total acreage burned due to ignition during extreme weather conditions. Targeted prevention of human ignitions could reduce total fire starts by 70%.

**(4) Defensible Space Deficit**
Only 15% of homes in the Wildland-Urban Interface have adequate defensible space. Communities with certified defensible space programs experience 60% lower structure loss rates. Current federal funding for defensible space programs is $50 million annually — less than $1 per acre of WUI land.

**(5) Prescribed Fire Backlog**
Prescribed fire is the most cost-effective vegetation management tool, costing $50-500 per acre compared to $500-5,000 per acre for mechanical treatment. Current prescribed fire application treats only 3 million acres annually. The backlog of acres needing prescribed fire exceeds 40 million acres.

**(6) Prevention Workforce Shortage**
The wildfire prevention workforce has declined 30% since 2000 despite increasing need. Federal agencies face 25% vacancy rates in prevention-focused positions. Training programs graduate fewer than 5,000 prevention specialists annually against a need of 25,000.

## SEC. 303. PURPOSES OF TITLE I

**SEC. 303.** The purposes of this title are to:

**(1)** Establish a national wildfire prevention strategy that prioritizes proactive risk reduction over reactive suppression.

**(2)** Dramatically scale vegetation management on federal, state, and private lands to reduce hazardous fuel loads to safe levels within 10 years.

**(3)** Mandate defensible space standards for all structures in the Wildland-Urban Interface.

**(4)** Expand the use of prescribed fire and cultural burning as primary vegetation management tools.

**(5)** Create a dedicated, sustainable funding mechanism for wildfire prevention that is insulated from suppression budget pressures.

**(6)** Build a world-class wildfire prevention workforce through training, recruitment, and retention programs.

**(7)** Deploy advanced ignition detection and prevention technologies across all high fire risk areas.

**(8)** Establish accountability metrics and reporting requirements to ensure prevention programs achieve measurable risk reduction.

---

## SEC. 304. DEFINITIONS FOR TITLE I

**SEC. 304.** In this title:

**(1) "Hazardous Fuel"**
Any vegetative material, live or dead, that constitutes a fire hazard and threat to public safety, property, or natural resources, including but not limited to dead trees, dense understory, invasive species, and ladder fuels.

**(2) "Fuel Break"**
A strategically located barrier of reduced fuel density designed to slow or stop fire spread, including but not limited to shaded fuel breaks, defensible space zones, and firebreaks.

**(3) "Prescribed Fire"**
Any fire intentionally ignited in accordance with a written prescription plan for the purpose of achieving land management objectives, including vegetation management, ecosystem restoration, and wildfire risk reduction.

**(4) "Cultural Burning**
The intentional use of fire by Indigenous peoples for cultural, spiritual, and land management purposes, consistent with Traditional Ecological Knowledge and practices developed over millennia.

**(5) "Community Wildfire Protection Plan (CWPP)"**
A plan developed by a community in consultation with federal, state, and local agencies that identifies and prioritizes areas for hazardous fuel reduction treatments, recommends measures to reduce structural ignitability, and recommends measures to assist community preparation and evacuation.

**(6) "Fire-Adapted Community"**
A community that has taken proactive measures to reduce wildfire risk through vegetation management, building code enforcement, defensible space compliance, and emergency preparedness planning.

**(7) "Ignition-Resistant Construction"**
Building methods and materials that meet or exceed the standards established under Sec. 310 of this title, designed to resist ignition from ember exposure, radiant heat, and direct flame contact.

**(8) "Prevention Treatment"**
Any activity undertaken to reduce wildfire risk, including vegetation management, defensible space creation, fuel break construction, prescribed fire, and ignition-resistant construction.

## SEC. 305. NATIONAL WILDFIRE PREVENTION STRATEGY

**SEC. 305.** (a) IN GENERAL.—Not later than 180 days after the date of enactment, the Secretary of Agriculture, in coordination with the Secretary of Interior, shall develop and publish a comprehensive National Wildfire Prevention Strategy.

**(b) CONTENTS.—**The strategy shall include:

**(1) Risk Assessment**
A national wildfire risk assessment that maps fire hazard severity, identifies at-risk communities, quantifies fuel loads, and prioritizes treatment areas based on risk reduction potential.

**(2) Treatment Targets**
Annual treatment targets for vegetation management on federal, state, and private lands, with the goal of treating 15 million acres annually by year 5 and 25 million acres annually by year 10.

**(3) Prescribed Fire Expansion**
A plan to increase prescribed fire application from 3 million acres annually to 10 million acres annually within 5 years, including liability reform, training, and air quality coordination.

**(4) Cultural Burning Integration**
A framework for integrating cultural burning into federal land management, including Tribal co-management agreements, Traditional Ecological Knowledge incorporation, and liability protection for cultural burn practitioners.

**(5) Community Protection Priorities**
A prioritized list of communities at highest risk, based on fire hazard severity, population vulnerability, evacuation capacity, and structural ignitability.

**(6) Workforce Development Plan**
A plan to recruit, train, and retain 50,000 wildfire prevention professionals within 5 years, including training curriculum, career pathways, and compensation standards.

**(7) Technology Deployment Plan**
A plan to deploy advanced prevention technologies, including AI-powered ignition detection, satellite-based fuel moisture monitoring, drone-based prescribed fire ignition, and automated defensible space assessment.

**(8) Funding Requirements**
A detailed funding plan that identifies the resources necessary to achieve the strategy's objectives, including federal, state, local, and private funding sources.

**(9) Performance Metrics**
Quantifiable performance metrics for measuring progress, including acres treated, structures protected, ignition reduction, and community preparedness levels.

**(c) UPDATES.—**The strategy shall be updated every 3 years, with annual progress reports submitted to Congress.

**(d) COORDINATION.—**The strategy shall be developed in coordination with state governors, Tribal nations, local governments, utilities, and public stakeholders.

## SEC. 306. VEGETATION MANAGEMENT PROGRAM

**SEC. 306.** (a) ESTABLISHMENT.—There is established in the Department of Agriculture a National Vegetation Management Program to coordinate and accelerate vegetation management on federal, state, and private lands.

**(b) FEDERAL LAND TREATMENT.—**

**(1) ANNUAL TARGETS.—**The Secretary of Agriculture and Secretary of Interior shall treat a combined minimum of 10 million acres of federal land annually for hazardous fuel reduction, increasing to 15 million acres annually by year 5.

**(2) PRIORITY AREAS.—**Treatment shall be prioritized in:
(A) Wildland-Urban Interface zones
(B) Critical watersheds
(C) Emergency access corridors
(D) Power line rights-of-way
(E) Community evacuation routes
(F) Critical habitat for threatened and endangered species

**(3) TREATMENT METHODS.—**Treatment shall employ the most cost-effective and ecologically appropriate methods, including:
(A) Prescribed fire
(B) Mechanical thinning
(C) Hand thinning
(D) Mastication
(E) Targeted grazing
(F) Cultural burning
(G) Biomass utilization

**(4) ENVIRONMENTAL COMPLIANCE.—**The Secretary shall develop programmatic environmental assessments and categorical exclusions to expedite vegetation management treatments while maintaining environmental protections.

**(c) STATE AND PRIVATE LAND INCENTIVES.—**

**(1) COST-SHARE PROGRAM.—**The Secretary shall establish a cost-share program that provides up to 75% federal cost-share for vegetation management on state and private lands in high fire risk areas.

**(2) TECHNICAL ASSISTANCE.—**The Secretary shall provide technical assistance to state and local governments, Tribal nations, and private landowners for vegetation management planning and implementation.

**(3) BLOCK GRANTS.—**The Secretary shall provide block grants to states for vegetation management programs, with funding allocated based on fire risk, acreage, and treatment need.

**(d) BIOMASS UTILIZATION.—**

**(1) MARKET DEVELOPMENT.—**The Secretary shall develop markets for biomass removed during vegetation management, including bioenergy, biochar, engineered wood products, and soil amendments.

**(2) PROCESSING INFRASTRUCTURE.—**The Secretary shall provide grants for biomass processing infrastructure in fire-prone regions, including mobile chipping equipment, biochar kilns, and small-diameter sawmills.

**(3) ECONOMIC VIABILITY.—**Biomass utilization programs shall be designed to offset treatment costs and create economic opportunities in rural communities.

## SEC. 307. PRESCRIBED FIRE EXPANSION PROGRAM

**SEC. 307.** (a) FINDINGS.—The Congress finds that:

**(1)** Prescribed fire is the most cost-effective and ecologically appropriate vegetation management tool available, costing $50-500 per acre compared to $500-5,000 per acre for mechanical treatment.

**(2)** Indigenous peoples have used fire as a land management tool for over 10,000 years, maintaining healthy fire-adapted ecosystems.

**(3)** The exclusion of fire from fire-adapted ecosystems over the past century has created the current wildfire crisis.

**(4)** Prescribed fire application must increase by at least 300% to address the current fuel backlog.

**(b) PRESCRIBED FIRE TARGETS.—**

**(1) FEDERAL LANDS.—**The Secretary of Agriculture and Secretary of Interior shall collectively apply prescribed fire to a minimum of 5 million acres annually by year 3 and 10 million acres annually by year 7.

**(2) STATE AND PRIVATE LANDS.—**The Secretary shall provide technical and financial assistance to achieve prescribed fire application on 3 million acres of state and private lands annually by year 5.

**(c) PRESCRIBED FIRE LIABILITY REFORM.—**

**(1) FEDERAL LIABILITY.—**The United States shall assume liability for prescribed fire conducted in accordance with an approved prescription plan by federal agencies or their contractors.

**(2) STATE LIABILITY PROTECTION.—**The Secretary shall provide model legislation for states to limit liability for prescribed fire conducted in accordance with certified prescription plans.

**(3) CERTIFICATION PROGRAM.—**The Secretary shall establish a national prescribed fire certification program for prescribed fire practitioners, including training requirements, experience standards, and continuing education.

**(d) AIR QUALITY COORDINATION.—**

**(1) INTERAGREEMENT.—**The Secretary shall enter into agreements with the Environmental Protection Agency and state air quality agencies to coordinate prescribed fire smoke management.

**(2) SMOKE EXEMPTION.—**Prescribed fire conducted in accordance with an approved prescription plan shall be exempt from state air quality standards during the approved burn window, provided that best smoke management practices are employed.

**(3) MONITORING.—**The Secretary shall deploy smoke monitoring equipment in prescribed fire areas to ensure smoke impacts are minimized.

**(e) CULTURAL BURNING PROGRAM.—**

**(1) TRIBAL SOVEREIGNTY.—**Nothing in this section shall be construed to limit the right of Tribal nations to conduct cultural burns on their lands.

**(2) CO-MANAGEMENT.—**The Secretary shall enter into co-management agreements with Tribal nations for cultural burning on federal lands within their ancestral territories.

**(3) FUNDING.—**There is authorized to be appropriated $500 million annually for the cultural burning program, including funding for Tribal burn practitioners, equipment, and training.

## SEC. 308. DEFENSIBLE SPACE PROGRAM

**SEC. 308.** (a) NATIONAL DEFENSIBLE SPACE STANDARD.—

**(1) IN GENERAL.—**Not later than 1 year after the date of enactment, the Secretary shall establish a national defensible space standard for all structures in the Wildland-Urban Interface.

**(2) REQUIREMENTS.—**The standard shall require:
(A) Zone 1: 0-5 feet from structure — Non-combustible landscaping, no combustible mulch, no vegetation within 5 feet of structure
(B) Zone 2: 5-30 feet from structure — Low-fuel landscaping, spaced vegetation, no ladder fuels, pruned tree limbs to 6 feet minimum
(C) Zone 3: 30-100 feet from structure — Reduced fuel density, spaced tree crowns, removed dead vegetation, managed understory
(D) Zone 4: 100-200 feet from structure (where applicable) — Strategic fuel breaks, reduced canopy cover, managed vegetation

**(3) COMPLIANCE TIMELINE.—**All structures in the WUI shall achieve compliance with the defensible space standard within 3 years of the standard's establishment.

**(b) DEFENSIBLE SPACE INCENTIVE PROGRAM.—**

**(1) REBATES.—**The Secretary shall provide rebates of up to $5,000 per property for defensible space creation and maintenance.

**(2) LOW-INCOME ASSISTANCE.—**The Secretary shall provide 100% cost coverage for defensible space creation on properties owned by low-income households.

**(3) CONTRACTOR CERTIFICATION.—**The Secretary shall establish a certification program for defensible space contractors, including training on fire-resistant landscaping, vegetation management, and structural vulnerability reduction.

**(c) COMMUNITY WILDFIRE PROTECTION PLANS.—**

**(1) REQUIREMENT.—**All communities in the WUI shall develop and maintain a Community Wildfire Protection Plan (CWPP) as a condition of receiving federal wildfire prevention funding.

**(2) PLAN CONTENTS.—**Each CWPP shall include:
(A) Community risk assessment
(B) Prioritized treatment areas
(C) Structural ignitability assessment
(D) Evacuation plan
(E) Public education strategy
(F) Funding strategy

**(3) APPROVAL.—**CWPPs shall be approved by the applicable state forester in consultation with the Secretary.

**(d) FIRE-ADAPTED COMMUNITIES CERTIFICATION.—

**(1) PROGRAM.—**The Secretary shall establish a Fire-Adapted Communities Certification Program that recognizes communities that have achieved comprehensive wildfire risk reduction.

**(2) CERTIFICATION CRITERIA.—**Certification shall require:
(A) Adopted and enforced defensible space ordinance
(B) Approved CWPP
(C) Building code compliance
(D) Evacuation plan tested within past 2 years
(E) Public education program
(F) Dedicated funding for maintenance

**(3) INCENTIVES.—**Certified Fire-Adapted Communities shall receive priority for federal wildfire prevention funding and reduced insurance premiums under the National Wildfire Insurance Program established under Sec. 605.

## SEC. 309. IGNITION PREVENTION PROGRAM

**SEC. 309.** (a) IGNITION SOURCE REGULATION.—

**(1) EQUIPMENT IGNITION STANDARDS.—**The Secretary shall establish standards for spark arrestors and exhaust systems on all motorized equipment operated in high fire risk areas during fire season.

**(2) CAMPFIRE AND OUTDOOR FIRE REGULATIONS.—**The Secretary shall establish regulations for campfires, outdoor burning, and recreational fire use in high fire risk areas, including:
(A) Seasonal fire restrictions based on fire danger levels
(B) Permit requirements for outdoor burning
(C) Mandatory fire safety equipment for campfires
(D) Penalties for violation of fire restrictions

**(3) FIREWORKS RESTRICTIONS.—**The Secretary shall prohibit the use of fireworks in high fire risk areas during fire season, with penalties of up to $10,000 per violation.

**(b) POWER LINE IGNITION PREVENTION.—**

**(1) VEGETATION MANAGEMENT AROUND POWER LINES.—**All electrical utilities shall maintain vegetation-free zones around power lines in high fire risk areas, consistent with the standards established under Title II.

**(2) EQUIPMENT MAINTENANCE STANDARDS.—**The Secretary shall establish standards for power line equipment maintenance to prevent ignitions, including:
(A) Regular inspection of conductors, insulators, and hardware
(B) Replacement of damaged or worn equipment
(C) Installation of covered conductors in high-risk areas
(D) Deployment of rapid fault isolation technology

**(c) PUBLIC EDUCATION AND AWARENESS.—**

**(1) NATIONAL CAMPAIGN.—**The Secretary shall conduct a national public education campaign on wildfire prevention, including:
(A) Ignition source awareness
(B) Defensible space practices
(C) Evacuation preparedness
(D) Fire-resistant construction

**(2) SCHOOL PROGRAMS.—**The Secretary shall develop wildfire prevention curricula for K-12 schools in fire-prone areas.

**(3) COMMUNITY OUTREACH.—**The Secretary shall fund community-based wildfire prevention outreach programs, with priority for disadvantaged communities.

**(d) IGNITION DETECTION TECHNOLOGY.—**

**(1) CAMERA NETWORKS.—**The Secretary shall deploy a network of AI-powered fire detection cameras in high fire risk areas, with the goal of detecting ignitions within 2 minutes.

**(2) SATELLITE MONITORING.—**The Secretary shall utilize satellite-based fire detection systems for real-time monitoring of fire starts.

**(3) DRONE PATROLS.—**The Secretary shall deploy autonomous drone patrols during extreme fire weather conditions to detect ignitions and monitor fire restrictions compliance.

**(4) INTEGRATION.—**All detection systems shall be integrated with local dispatch centers to enable rapid response to detected ignitions.

## SEC. 310. IGNITION-RESISTANT CONSTRUCTION STANDARDS

**SEC. 310.** (a) NATIONAL WUI BUILDING CODE.—

**(1) IN GENERAL.—**Not later than 1 year after the date of enactment, the Secretary shall establish a national building code for the Wildland-Urban Interface.

**(2) REQUIREMENTS.—**The code shall require:
(A) Class A roof assembly with non-combustible covering
(B) Ember-resistant vents with 1/8-inch mesh screening
(C) Non-combustible siding materials or ignition-resistant treatments
(D) Tempered or multi-pane windows with metal screens
(E) Non-combustible decking and fencing within 5 feet of structure
(F) Enclosed eaves and soffits
(G) Non-combustible gutter systems
(H) Spark arrestors on all chimneys

**(3) APPLICABILITY.—**The code shall apply to:
(A) All new construction in the WUI
(B) All substantial renovations (exceeding 50% of structure value) in the WUI
(C) All new construction within 1 mile of federal lands in high fire risk areas

**(b) RETROFIT INCENTIVE PROGRAM.—**

**(1) GRANTS.—**The Secretary shall provide grants of up to $25,000 per structure for retrofitting existing structures in the WUI to meet ignition-resistant construction standards.

**(2) LOW-INCOME ASSISTANCE.—**The Secretary shall provide 100% cost coverage for retrofitting of structures owned by low-income households.

**(3) INSURANCE INCENTIVES.—**Structures retrofitted to meet ignition-resistant construction standards shall qualify for reduced insurance premiums under the National Wildfire Insurance Program.

**(c) MATERIALS CERTIFICATION.—**

**(1) FIRE-RESISTANT MATERIAL CERTIFICATION PROGRAM.—**The Secretary shall establish a certification program for fire-resistant building materials, including testing standards, certification procedures, and labeling requirements.

**(2) APPROVED MATERIALS LIST.—**The Secretary shall publish and maintain a list of approved fire-resistant building materials that meet the standards established under this section.

**(3) INNOVATION INCENTIVES.—**The Secretary shall provide grants for research and development of new fire-resistant building materials and construction techniques.

**(d) CODE ENFORCEMENT.—**

**(1) LOCAL ENFORCEMENT.—**Local governments shall be responsible for enforcement of the WUI building code, with technical assistance from the Secretary.

**(2) STATE OVERSIGHT.—**States shall oversee local enforcement and report compliance rates to the Secretary annually.

**(3) FUNDING CONDITION.—**Federal wildfire prevention funding to states shall be conditioned on adoption and enforcement of the WUI building code.
