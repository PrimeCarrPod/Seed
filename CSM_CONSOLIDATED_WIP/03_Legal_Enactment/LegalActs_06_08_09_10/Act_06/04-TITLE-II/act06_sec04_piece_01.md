# TITLE II — GRID HARDENING

## SEC. 401. SHORT TITLE

**SEC. 401.** This title may be cited as the "Grid Hardening and Resilience Act of 2026."

---

## SEC. 402. FINDINGS SPECIFIC TO GRID HARDENING

**SEC. 402.** The Congress finds the following with respect to electrical grid hardening:

**(1) Ignition Risk**
Electrical infrastructure is the ignition source for the most destructive wildfires in U.S. history. The 2018 Camp Fire (85 fatalities, $16.5B damages), 2020 Oregon Fire Siege (11 fatalities, $6B damages), and 2021 Marshall Fire (2 fatalities, $5B damages) were all ignited by electrical infrastructure during extreme weather events.

**(2) Underinvestment**
Electrical utilities have systematically underinvested in grid hardening despite known ignition risks. Only 2,500 miles of the 200,000+ miles of overhead lines in high fire risk areas have been hardened. At current rates, full hardening would take 80+ years — far too slow to address the current crisis.

**(3) Cost of Inaction**
The cost of grid hardening ($1.5M per mile for covered conductors, $3-5M per mile for undergrounding) is far less than the cost of wildfire damages ($8.5M per mile in wildfire damages prevented). Every dollar invested in grid hardening returns $5.70 in avoided wildfire costs.

**(4) Technology Availability**
Technologies exist today that can dramatically reduce ignition risk. Covered conductors reduce ignition risk by 95%. Rapid fault isolation prevents 80% of ignitions from conductor contact. Fast curve switching reduces ignition risk from damaged equipment by 70%.

**(5) Regulatory Failure**
State utility commissions have failed to mandate adequate grid hardening. Only 5 states have adopted comprehensive grid hardening standards. Most states rely on voluntary utility programs that have proven insufficient.

**(6) Climate Change Acceleration**
Climate change is increasing the frequency and intensity of extreme fire weather events. The number of Red Flag Warning days has increased 40% since 2000. Grid hardening standards must account for future climate conditions, not just historical weather patterns.
