# TITLE I — SEMICONDUCTOR SUPPLY CHAIN

## SEC. 101. SHORT TITLE

**SEC. 101.** This title may be cited as the "Semiconductor Supply Chain Security Act of 2026."

---

## SEC. 102. FINDINGS SPECIFIC TO SEMICONDUCTORS

**SEC. 102.** The Congress finds the following with respect to semiconductors:

**(1) Manufacturing Decline**
The U.S. share of global semiconductor manufacturing has declined from 37% in 1990 to 12% in 2026. Over 80% of advanced semiconductor manufacturing occurs in Taiwan and South Korea.

**(2) National Security Risk**
The U.S. military depends on foreign suppliers for critical semiconductor components in weapons systems and military equipment. This dependence poses significant national security risks.

**(3) Economic Impact**
A disruption in semiconductor supply would cost the U.S. economy $500 billion annually. The 2020-2023 semiconductor shortage cost the global economy $500 billion.

**(4) Technology Leadership**
The United States maintains leadership in semiconductor design but has lost leadership in manufacturing. Rebuilding domestic manufacturing capacity is essential for maintaining technology leadership.

**(5) Workforce Shortage**
The semiconductor industry faces a severe workforce shortage. Over 100,000 skilled workers are needed in semiconductor manufacturing.

**(6) Investment Gap**
The United States invests less in semiconductor manufacturing than competitors. China invests $150 billion annually in semiconductor manufacturing. The CHIPS and Science Act of 2022 was a first step but is insufficient.
