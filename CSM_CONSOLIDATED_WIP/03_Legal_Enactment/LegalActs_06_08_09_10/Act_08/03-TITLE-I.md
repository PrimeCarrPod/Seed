# TITLE I — SEMICONDUCTOR SUPPLY CHAIN

## SEC. 101. SHORT TITLE

**SEC. 101.** This title may be cited as the "Semiconductor Supply Chain Security Act of 2026."

---

## SEC. 102. FINDINGS SPECIFIC TO SEMICONDUCTORS

**SEC. 102.** The Congress finds the following with respect to semiconductors:

**(1) Manufacturing Decline**
The U.S. share of global semiconductor manufacturing has declined from 37% in 1990 to 12% in 2026. Over 80% of advanced semiconductor manufacturing occurs in Taiwan and South Korea.

**(2) National Security Risk**
The U.S. military depends on foreign suppliers for critical semiconductor components in weapons systems and military equipment. This dependence poses significant national security risks.

**(3) Economic Impact**
A disruption in semiconductor supply would cost the U.S. economy $500 billion annually. The 2020-2023 semiconductor shortage cost the global economy $500 billion.

**(4) Technology Leadership**
The United States maintains leadership in semiconductor design but has lost leadership in manufacturing. Rebuilding domestic manufacturing capacity is essential for maintaining technology leadership.

**(5) Workforce Shortage**
The semiconductor industry faces a severe workforce shortage. Over 100,000 skilled workers are needed in semiconductor manufacturing.

**(6) Investment Gap**
The United States invests less in semiconductor manufacturing than competitors. China invests $150 billion annually in semiconductor manufacturing. The CHIPS and Science Act of 2022 was a first step but is insufficient.

## SEC. 103. PURPOSES OF TITLE I

**SEC. 103.** The purposes of this title are to:

**(1)** Increase the U.S. share of global semiconductor manufacturing from 12% to 24% by 2036.

**(2)** Create 300,000 direct jobs in semiconductor manufacturing.

**(3)** Invest $60 billion in domestic semiconductor manufacturing.

**(4)** Maintain U.S. technology leadership through research and development.

**(5)** Ensure national security through secure semiconductor supply chains.

**(6)** Strengthen allied cooperation on semiconductor supply chains.

---

## SEC. 104. DEFINITIONS FOR TITLE I

**SEC. 104.** In this title:

**(1) "Semiconductor"**
A material that has electrical conductivity between that of a conductor and an insulator, used in electronic devices, including but not limited to integrated circuits, microprocessors, memory chips, and sensors.

**(2) "Fabrication Facility" or "Fab"**
A manufacturing facility where semiconductors are produced from raw materials.

**(3) "Advanced Packaging"**
The process of assembling and testing semiconductor chips after fabrication.

**(4) "Node"**
A specific semiconductor manufacturing process technology, measured in nanometers (nm).

**(5) "Compound Semiconductor"**
A semiconductor made from two or more elements, such as gallium nitride (GaN) or silicon carbide (SiC).

## SEC. 105. DOMESTIC SEMICONDUCTOR MANUFACTURING PROGRAM

**SEC. 105.** (a) ESTABLISHMENT.—There is established in the Department of Commerce a Domestic Semiconductor Manufacturing Program.

**(b) FABRICATION FACILITY GRANTS.—**

**(1) IN GENERAL.—**The Secretary of Commerce shall provide grants for the construction or expansion of domestic semiconductor fabrication facilities.

**(2) GRANT AMOUNT.—**Grants shall be up to $5 billion per facility.

**(3) ELIGIBILITY.—**Grants shall be available to:
(A) Domestic semiconductor companies
(B) Foreign semiconductor companies building facilities in the U.S.
(C) Public-private partnerships

**(4) PRIORITY.—**Grants shall be prioritized for:
(A) Advanced node facilities (7nm and below)
(B) Facilities in economically distressed communities
(C) Facilities with workforce development programs
(D) Facilities with environmental sustainability plans

**(c) EQUIPMENT AND MATERIALS GRANTS.—**

**(1) IN GENERAL.—**The Secretary shall provide grants for domestic production of semiconductor equipment and materials.

**(2) GRANT AMOUNT.—**Grants shall be up to $1 billion per facility.

**(d) ADVANCED PACKAGING GRANTS.—**

**(1) IN GENERAL.—**The Secretary shall provide grants for domestic advanced packaging facilities.

**(2) GRANT AMOUNT.—**Grants shall be up to $500 million per facility.

## SEC. 106. SEMICONDUCTOR RESEARCH AND DEVELOPMENT

**SEC. 106.** (a) RESEARCH PROGRAM.—

**(1) IN GENERAL.—**The Secretary of Commerce shall establish a semiconductor research and development program.

**(2) FOCUS AREAS.—**The program shall focus on:
(A) Next-generation chip technologies (2nm and below)
(B) Advanced packaging technologies
(C) Compound semiconductors
(D) Photonics and quantum computing
(E) Artificial intelligence hardware

**(3) FUNDING.—**There is authorized to be appropriated $10 billion over 10 years.

**(b) PUBLIC-PRIVATE PARTNERSHIPS.—

**(1) IN GENERAL.—**The Secretary shall establish public-private research partnerships for semiconductor development.

**(2) INDUSTRY PARTICIPATION.—**Partnerships shall include participation from:
(A) Semiconductor companies
(B) Equipment manufacturers
(C) Materials suppliers
(D) Universities
(E) National laboratories

**(c) NATIONAL SEMICONDUCTOR RESEARCH CENTER.—

**(1) ESTABLISHMENT.—**There is established a National Semiconductor Research Center.

**(2) FUNCTIONS.—**The Center shall:
(A) Conduct basic and applied research
(B) Develop new manufacturing processes
(C) Train semiconductor researchers
(D) Coordinate research across institutions

## SEC. 107. WORKFORCE DEVELOPMENT

**(2) FUNDING.—**There is authorized to be appropriated $5 billion over 10 years.

(B) University degree programs
(C) Apprenticeship programs
(D) Incumbent worker training
(E) Career pathway programs

**(1) IN GENERAL.—**The Secretary shall provide scholarships and fellowships for students pursuing semiconductor-related degrees.

**SEC. 108.** (a) IN GENERAL.—There is authorized to be appropriated to carry out this title $60,000,000,000 for the period of fiscal years 2027 through 2036.

**(b) ALLOCATION.—**Funds shall be allocated as follows:

## SEC. 109. SEMICONDUCTOR SUPPLY CHAIN ACCOUNTABILITY

**SEC. 109.** (a) ANNUAL PERFORMANCE REPORT.—

**(1) IN GENERAL.—**Not later than 1 year after the date of enactment, and annually thereafter, the Secretary of Commerce shall submit to Congress an annual performance report on semiconductor supply chain programs.

**(2) CONTENTS.—**The report shall include:
(A) U.S. share of global semiconductor manufacturing
(B) Number of domestic fabrication facilities
(C) Domestic semiconductor production capacity
(D) Semiconductor workforce size
(E) Expenditures by program
(F) Performance metrics and trends

**(b) GOVERNMENT ACCOUNTABILITY OFFICE REVIEW.—**

**(1) BIENNIAL REVIEW.—**The Comptroller General shall conduct a biennial review of semiconductor supply chain programs.

**(2) CONTENTS.—**The review shall assess:
(A) Program effectiveness
(B) Efficiency of operations
(C) Adequacy of funding
(D) Compliance with standards
(E) Achievement of targets

---

## SEC. 110. SEMICONDUCTOR SUPPLY CHAIN PREEMPTION

**SEC. 110.** (a) FEDERAL STANDARDS.—**The national semiconductor standards established under this title shall preempt any inconsistent state or local standards, except where state standards are more protective.

**(b) STATE AUTHORITY.—**States may adopt semiconductor standards that are more protective than the national standards.

## SEC. 114. SEMICONDUCTOR SUPPLY CHAIN ENFORCEMENT

**SEC. 114.** (a) CIVIL PENALTIES.—

**(1) NON-COMPLIANCE.—**Any person who fails to comply with semiconductor supply chain requirements shall be subject to a civil penalty of up to $50,000 per violation.

**(2) FALSE REPORTING.—**Any person who falsely reports semiconductor supply chain data shall be subject to a civil penalty of up to $100,000 per violation.

**(b) CRIMINAL PENALTIES.—

**(1) FRAUD.—**Any person who commits fraud in connection with semiconductor supply chain activities shall be subject to criminal penalties.

**(2) EMBEZZLEMENT.—**Any person who embezzles semiconductor supply chain funds shall be subject to criminal penalties.

---

## SEC. 115. SEMICONDUCTOR SUPPLY CHAIN JUDICIAL REVIEW

**SEC. 115.** (a) REVIEW.—**Any person aggrieved by a final action of the Secretary under this title may seek judicial review in the United States Court of Appeals for the circuit in which the person resides.

**(b) STANDARD OF REVIEW.—**The court shall set aside any action found to be arbitrary, capricious, an abuse of discretion, or otherwise not in accordance with law.

---

## SEC. 116. SEMICONDUCTOR SUPPLY CHAIN SAVINGS CLAUSE

**SEC. 116.** Nothing in this title shall be construed to:

**(1)** Diminish the authority of state or local governments.

**(2)** Affect the regulation of insurance by state insurance commissioners.

**(3)** Affect the authority of the Occupational Safety and Health Administration over workplace safety.

**(4)** Affect the authority of the Environmental Protection Agency over environmental protection.

**(5)** Diminish the sovereignty of Tribal nations.

## SEC. 117. SEMICONDUCTOR SUPPLY CHAIN FOR CRITICAL FACILITIES

**SEC. 117.** (a) DEFENSE FACILITIES.—

**(1) SEMICONDUCTOR REQUIREMENTS.—**The Secretary of Defense shall identify semiconductor requirements for defense systems.

**(2) DOMESTIC SOURCING.—**Defense semiconductor requirements shall be sourced from domestic suppliers to the maximum extent practicable.

**(b) INTELLIGENCE FACILITIES.—

**(1) SEMICONDUCTOR REQUIREMENTS.—**The Director of National Intelligence shall identify semiconductor requirements for intelligence systems.

**(2) DOMESTIC SOURCING.—**Intelligence semiconductor requirements shall be sourced from domestic suppliers to the maximum extent practicable.

**(c) CRITICAL INFRASTRUCTURE.—

**(1) SEMICONDUCTOR REQUIREMENTS.—**The Secretary of Homeland Security shall identify semiconductor requirements for critical infrastructure.

**(2) DOMESTIC SOURCING.—**Critical infrastructure semiconductor requirements shall be sourced from domestic suppliers to the maximum extent practicable.

---

## SEC. 118. SEMICONDUCTOR SUPPLY CHAIN FOR SCHOOLS

**SEC. 118.** (a) SEMICONDUCTOR EDUCATION.—

**(1) K-12 EDUCATION.—**The Secretary of Education shall develop semiconductor education programs for K-12 schools.

**SEC. 121.** (a) SUNSET.—**The authorities provided under this title shall expire on September 30, 2036, unless reauthorized by Congress.

**(1)** Assessment of semiconductor supply chain effectiveness
**(2)** Recommendations for reauthorization

**(4)** Assessment of remaining semiconductor supply chain vulnerabilities
**(5)** Recommendations for future semiconductor supply chain activities

---

## SEC. 122. SEMICONDUCTOR SUPPLY CHAIN TRANSITION

**SEC. 122.** (a) TRANSITION PLAN.—**Not later than September 30, 2035, the Secretary shall develop a transition plan for the orderly wind-down of semiconductor supply chain activities if the authorities are not reauthorized.

**(b) COMPLETION OF ACTIVITIES.—**The transition plan shall provide for the completion of all ongoing semiconductor supply chain activities and the fulfillment of all outstanding commitments.
