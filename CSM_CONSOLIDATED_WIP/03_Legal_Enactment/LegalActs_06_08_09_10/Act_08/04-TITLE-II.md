# TITLE II — RARE EARTH MINERALS

## SEC. 201. SHORT TITLE

**SEC. 201.** This title may be cited as the "Rare Earth Minerals Security Act of 2026."

---

## SEC. 202. FINDINGS SPECIFIC TO RARE EARTH MINERALS

**SEC. 202.** The Congress finds the following with respect to rare earth minerals:

**(1) Chinese Dominance**
China controls 60% of global rare earth mining and 85% of rare earth processing. The United States imports 80% of its rare earth minerals from China.

**(2) Essential Applications**
Rare earth minerals are essential for defense systems, electric vehicles, wind turbines, consumer electronics, medical devices, and clean energy technologies.

**(3) Supply Chain Vulnerability**
The concentration of rare earth mining and processing in China creates significant supply chain vulnerability. China has used rare earth exports as a geopolitical weapon in the past.

**(4) Domestic Resources**
The United States has significant rare earth deposits, including the Mountain Pass mine in California. These resources can be developed to reduce foreign dependence.

**(5) Environmental Challenges**
Rare earth mining and processing create environmental challenges, including radioactive waste, water pollution, and habitat destruction. Strict environmental standards must be maintained.

**(6) Recycling Opportunity**
Rare earth recycling can reduce dependence on primary mining. Current recycling rates are less than 5%. Increased recycling can supplement domestic production.

---

## SEC. 203. PURPOSES OF TITLE II

**SEC. 203.** The purposes of this title are to:

**(1)** Reduce U.S. dependence on Chinese rare earth imports from 80% to 40% by 2036.

**(2)** Develop domestic rare earth mining and processing capacity.

**(3)** Create 100,000 direct jobs in rare earth industries.

**(4)** Invest $30 billion in domestic rare earth production.

**(5)** Promote rare earth recycling and sustainability.

**(6)** Ensure national security through secure rare earth supply chains.

## SEC. 204. DEFINITIONS FOR TITLE II

**SEC. 204.** In this title:

**(1) "Rare Earth Minerals"**
The 17 chemical elements in the periodic table that are essential for modern technology, including neodymium, dysprosium, terbium, lanthanum, cerium, praseodymium, samarium, europium, gadolinium, holmium, erbium, thulium, ytterbium, lutetium, scandium, and yttrium.

**(2) "Rare Earth Processing"**
The separation and refining of rare earth minerals into individual rare earth elements.

**(3) "Rare Earth Recycling"**
The recovery of rare earth minerals from end-of-life products and manufacturing waste.

**(4) "Magnet"**
A device made from rare earth minerals that produces a magnetic field, used in electric motors, wind turbines, and consumer electronics.

---

## SEC. 205. DOMESTIC RARE EARTH MINING AND PROCESSING

**SEC. 205.** (a) ESTABLISHMENT.—There is established in the Department of Energy a Domestic Rare Earth Mining and Processing Program.

**(b) MINING GRANTS.—**

**(1) IN GENERAL.—**The Secretary of Energy shall provide grants for the development of domestic rare earth mines.

**(2) GRANT AMOUNT.—**Grants shall be up to $2 billion per mine.

**(3) PRIORITY.—**Grants shall be prioritized for:
(A) Mines with significant rare earth deposits
(B) Mines in economically distressed communities
(C) Mines with environmental remediation plans
(D) Mines with workforce development programs

**(c) PROCESSING GRANTS.—**

**(1) IN GENERAL.—**The Secretary shall provide grants for the construction of domestic rare earth processing facilities.

**(2) GRANT AMOUNT.—**Grants shall be up to $3 billion per facility.

## SEC. 206. RARE EARTH RECYCLING PROGRAM

**SEC. 206.** (a) ESTABLISHMENT.—There is established in the Department of Energy a Rare Earth Recycling Program.

**(b) RECYCLING GRANTS.—**

**(1) IN GENERAL.—**The Secretary shall provide grants for rare earth recycling facilities.

**(2) GRANT AMOUNT.—**Grants shall be up to $500 million per facility.

**(3) FOCUS.—**Recycling shall focus on:
(A) Permanent magnets from electric motors and wind turbines
(B) Phosphors from lighting and displays
(C) Batteries from electric vehicles
(C) Electronics waste

**(c) RESEARCH AND DEVELOPMENT.—

**(1) IN GENERAL.—**The Secretary shall fund research and development of rare earth recycling technologies.

**(2) FUNDING.—**There is authorized to be appropriated $2 billion over 10 years.

---

## SEC. 207. AUTHORIZATION OF APPROPRIATIONS FOR TITLE II

**SEC. 207.** (a) IN GENERAL.—There is authorized to be appropriated to carry out this title $30,000,000,000 for the period of fiscal years 2027 through 2036.

**(b) ALLOCATION.—**Funds shall be allocated as follows:
(1) Mining grants: $15,000,000,000
(2) Processing grants: $10,000,000,000
(3) Recycling program: $5,000,000,000

## SEC. 208. RARE EARTH MINERALS ACCOUNTABILITY

**SEC. 208.** (a) ANNUAL PERFORMANCE REPORT.—

**(1) IN GENERAL.—**The Secretary of Energy shall submit an annual performance report on rare earth minerals programs.

**(2) CONTENTS.—**The review shall assess:
(A) Program effectiveness
(B) Efficiency of operations
(C) Adequacy of funding
(D) Compliance with standards
(E) Achievement of targets

---

## SEC. 209. RARE EARTH MINERALS PREEMPTION

**SEC. 209.** (a) FEDERAL STANDARDS.—**The national rare earth minerals standards established under this title shall preempt any inconsistent state or local standards, except where state standards are more protective.

**(b) STATE AUTHORITY.—**States may adopt rare earth minerals standards that are more protective than the national standards.

---

## SEC. 213. RARE EARTH MINERALS ENFORCEMENT

**SEC. 213.** (a) CIVIL PENALTIES.—

**(1) NON-COMPLIANCE.—**Any person who fails to comply with rare earth minerals requirements shall be subject to a civil penalty of up to $50,000 per violation.

**(2) FALSE REPORTING.—**Any person who falsely reports rare earth minerals data shall be subject to a civil penalty of up to $100,000 per violation.

**(b) CRIMINAL PENALTIES.—

**(1) FRAUD.—**Any person who commits fraud in connection with rare earth minerals activities shall be subject to criminal penalties.

**(2) EMBEZZLEMENT.—**Any person who embezzles rare earth minerals funds shall be subject to criminal penalties.

## SEC. 215. RARE EARTH MINERALS FOR CRITICAL FACILITIES

**SEC. 215.** (a) DEFENSE FACILITIES.—

**(1) RARE EARTH REQUIREMENTS.—**The Secretary of Defense shall identify rare earth requirements for defense systems.

**(2) DOMESTIC SOURCING.—**Defense rare earth requirements shall be sourced from domestic suppliers to the maximum extent practicable.

**(b) INTELLIGENCE FACILITIES.—

**(1) RARE EARTH REQUIREMENTS.—**The Director of National Intelligence shall identify rare earth requirements for intelligence systems.

**(2) DOMESTIC SOURCING.—**Intelligence rare earth requirements shall be sourced from domestic suppliers to the maximum extent practicable.

**(c) CRITICAL INFRASTRUCTURE.—

**(1) RARE EARTH REQUIREMENTS.—**The Secretary of Homeland Security shall identify rare earth requirements for critical infrastructure.

**(2) DOMESTIC SOURCING.—**Critical infrastructure rare earth requirements shall be sourced from domestic suppliers to the maximum extent practicable.

## SEC. 218. RARE EARTH MINERALS SUNSET AND REVIEW

**SEC. 218.** (a) SUNSET.—**The authorities provided under this title shall expire on September 30, 2036, unless reauthorized by Congress.

**(b) REVIEW.—**Not later than September 30, 2035, the Secretary shall submit to Congress a comprehensive review of rare earth minerals activities, including:

**(1)** Assessment of rare earth minerals effectiveness
**(2)** Recommendations for reauthorization
**(3)** Proposed modifications to rare earth minerals structure
**(4)** Assessment of remaining rare earth minerals vulnerabilities
**(5)** Recommendations for future rare earth minerals activities

---

## SEC. 219. RARE EARTH MINERALS TRANSITION

**SEC. 219.** (a) TRANSITION PLAN.—**Not later than September 30, 2035, the Secretary shall develop a transition plan for the orderly wind-down of rare earth minerals activities if the authorities are not reauthorized.

**(b) COMPLETION OF ACTIVITIES.—**The transition plan shall provide for the completion of all ongoing rare earth minerals activities and the fulfillment of all outstanding commitments.
