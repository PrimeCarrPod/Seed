# TITLE I — WATER INFRASTRUCTURE MODERNIZATION

## SEC. 101. SHORT TITLE

**SEC. 101.** This title may be cited as the "Water Infrastructure Modernization Act of 2026."

---

## SEC. 102. FINDINGS SPECIFIC TO WATER INFRASTRUCTURE

**SEC. 102.** The Congress finds the following:

**(1) Aging Systems**
Many water systems are over 100 years old. An estimated 240,000 water main breaks occur annually, wasting 2 trillion gallons of treated water.

**(2) Investment Gap**
The EPA estimates $472 billion is needed over 20 years to maintain and upgrade drinking water infrastructure. Current spending addresses less than 40% of the need.

**(3) Lead Contamination**
An estimated 6-10 million homes still receive water through lead service lines. Lead exposure causes irreversible neurological damage.

**(4) Sewer Overflows**
Combined sewer overflows discharge 850 billion gallons of untreated wastewater annually, contaminating waterways and causing public health risks.

**(5) Treatment Plant Obsolescence**
Many water treatment plants are operating well beyond their design life, with some facilities dating to the early 20th century.

**(6) Workforce Shortage**
The water industry faces a severe workforce shortage, with an estimated 30-50% of water workers eligible for retirement within 10 years.

## SEC. 103. PURPOSES OF TITLE I

**SEC. 103.** The purposes of this title are to:

**(1)** Replace aging water mains and service lines to reduce water loss and improve reliability.

**(2)** Upgrade water treatment plants to meet current and future water quality standards.

**(3)** Expand wastewater treatment capacity to eliminate combined sewer overflows.

**(4)** Modernize stormwater management systems to reduce flooding and improve water quality.

**(5)** Deploy smart water technologies to improve system efficiency and reduce losses.

**(6)** Eliminate lead service lines and reduce other contaminants in drinking water.

**(7)** Create jobs in the water infrastructure construction and maintenance sectors.

---

## SEC. 104. DEFINITIONS FOR TITLE I

**SEC. 104.** In this title:

**(1) "Lead Service Line"**
A water service line made of lead, including the portion on public property and the portion on private property.

**(2) "Combined Sewer Overflow (CSO)"**
The discharge of untreated wastewater from a combined sewer system during wet weather.

**(3) "Smart Water System"**
A water system that uses sensors, data analytics, and automation to optimize performance.

**(4) "Green Infrastructure"**
Infrastructure that uses natural processes to manage water, including rain gardens, green roofs, and permeable pavements.

## SEC. 105. WATER MAIN REPLACEMENT PROGRAM

**SEC. 105.** (a) ESTABLISHMENT.—There is established a Water Main Replacement Program to provide grants for the replacement of aging water mains.

**(b) FUNDING.—**There is authorized to be appropriated $20 billion over 10 years.

**(c) ELIGIBILITY.—**Grants shall be available to:
(1) Public water systems
(2) Tribal water systems
(3) Small and disadvantaged communities (priority)

**(d) PRIORITY.—**Grants shall be prioritized based on:
(1) Age and condition of existing mains
(2) Frequency of main breaks
(3) Water loss rates
(4) Environmental justice considerations

**(e) REQUIREMENTS.—**Grant recipients shall:
(1) Replace mains with ductile iron, PVC, or other approved materials
(2) Install smart leak detection sensors
(3) Develop asset management plans
(4) Provide workforce training

## SEC. 106. LEAD SERVICE LINE REPLACEMENT PROGRAM

**SEC. 106.** (a) ESTABLISHMENT.—There is established a Lead Service Line Replacement Program.

**(b) FUNDING.—**There is authorized to be appropriated $15 billion over 10 years.

**(c) GOAL.—**The program shall eliminate all lead service lines within 10 years.

**(d) REQUIREMENTS.—**
(1) Full service line replacement (public and private portions)
(2) Provision of filters during replacement process
(3) Post-replacement water quality testing
(4) Priority for communities with children and pregnant women
