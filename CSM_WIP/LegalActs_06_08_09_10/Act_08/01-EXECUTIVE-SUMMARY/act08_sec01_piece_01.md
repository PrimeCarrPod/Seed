# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY — Act 08: Critical Technology Supply Chain Act

## OVERVIEW

The Critical Technology Supply Chain Act of 2026 establishes a comprehensive framework for securing and strengthening the United States' critical technology supply chains. The Act addresses vulnerabilities in semiconductor manufacturing, rare earth minerals processing, pharmaceutical production, and other critical technology sectors.

---

## THE PROBLEM

The United States faces critical vulnerabilities in its technology supply chains:

**(1) Semiconductor Dependence**
The U.S. share of global semiconductor manufacturing has declined from 37% in 1990 to 12% today. Over 80% of advanced semiconductor manufacturing occurs in Taiwan and South Korea. A disruption in semiconductor supply would cost the U.S. economy $500 billion annually.

**(2) Rare Earth Dependence**
China controls 60% of global rare earth mining and 85% of rare earth processing. Rare earth minerals are essential for defense systems, electric vehicles, wind turbines, and consumer electronics. A disruption in rare earth supply would cripple multiple U.S. industries.

**(3) Pharmaceutical Dependence**
Over 80% of active pharmaceutical ingredients (APIs) are manufactured overseas, primarily in China and India. The COVID-19 pandemic exposed critical vulnerabilities in the pharmaceutical supply chain. Drug shortages affect millions of Americans annually.

**(4) National Security Risks**
Foreign dependence in critical technology supply chains poses significant national security risks. Adversaries could use supply chain leverage as a geopolitical weapon. The U.S. military depends on foreign suppliers for critical components.

---

## THE SOLUTION

The Critical Technology Supply Chain Act addresses these vulnerabilities through:

**(1) Domestic Manufacturing Incentives**
$60 billion in incentives for domestic semiconductor manufacturing, including tax credits, grants, and loan guarantees.

**(2) Rare Earth Development**
$30 billion for domestic rare earth mining, processing, and recycling, reducing dependence on Chinese suppliers.

**(3) Pharmaceutical Resilience**
$25 billion for domestic pharmaceutical manufacturing, including API production and finished dosage manufacturing.

**(4) Strategic Reserve**
$20 billion for a National Critical Technology Reserve to buffer against supply disruptions.

**(5) International Partnerships**
$10 billion for allied supply chain agreements with trusted partners.

**(6) Research and Development**
$15 billion for critical technology research and development.

**(7) Workforce Development**
$10 billion for workforce training and education programs.
