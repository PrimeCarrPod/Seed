# TITLE III — PHARMACEUTICAL SUPPLY CHAIN

## SEC. 301. SHORT TITLE

**SEC. 301.** This title may be cited as the "Pharmaceutical Supply Chain Security Act of 2026."

---

## SEC. 302. FINDINGS SPECIFIC TO PHARMACEUTICALS

**SEC. 302.** The Congress finds the following with respect to pharmaceuticals:

**(1) Foreign Dependence**
Over 80% of active pharmaceutical ingredients (APIs) are manufactured overseas, primarily in China and India. This dependence creates significant supply chain vulnerability.

**(2) Drug Shortages**
Drug shortages affect millions of Americans annually. The COVID-19 pandemic exposed critical vulnerabilities in the pharmaceutical supply chain, resulting in widespread drug shortages.

**(3) National Security Risk**
Foreign dependence in pharmaceutical supply chains poses significant national security risks. Adversaries could use pharmaceutical supply as a geopolitical weapon.

**(4) Public Health Impact**
Drug shortages result in increased mortality, reduced quality of care, and increased healthcare costs. Ensuring domestic pharmaceutical production is essential for public health.

**(5) Economic Impact**
The United States spends $500 billion annually on pharmaceuticals. Domestic production would create 200,000 jobs and reduce pharmaceutical costs.

**(6) Quality Concerns**
Foreign pharmaceutical manufacturing raises quality concerns. Domestic production ensures compliance with FDA quality standards.

---

## SEC. 303. PURPOSES OF TITLE III

**SEC. 303.** The purposes of this title are to:

**(1)** Reduce U.S. dependence on foreign pharmaceutical imports from 80% to 50% by 2036.

**(2)** Create 200,000 direct jobs in pharmaceutical manufacturing.

**(3)** Invest $25 billion in domestic pharmaceutical production.

**(4)** Prevent drug shortages through domestic production.

**(5)** Ensure national security through secure pharmaceutical supply chains.

**(6)** Reduce pharmaceutical costs through domestic competition.
