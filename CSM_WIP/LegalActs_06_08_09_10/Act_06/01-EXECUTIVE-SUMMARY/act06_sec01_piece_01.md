## WILDFIRE PREVENTION AND GRID RESILIENCE ACT OF 2026

---

## PURPOSE OF THE BILL

The Wildfire Prevention and Grid Resilience Act of 2026 (WPGR Act) establishes a comprehensive national program to reduce wildfire risk through vegetation management, grid hardening, building code modernization, early warning deployment, and response force modernization. The Act authorizes $46 billion over 10 years, fully offset by carbon pricing, subsidy redirection, and green bond proceeds.

---

## THE PROBLEM

Wildfires have reached crisis levels in the United States. The six largest fire seasons in recorded history have occurred since 2020. In 2025 alone, wildfires consumed 12.4 million acres, destroyed 47,000 structures, caused 312 fatalities, and generated $89 billion in direct damages. Climate change is extending fire seasons, increasing the geographic extent of fire-prone areas, and intensifying fire behavior beyond historical norms.

Electrical infrastructure has emerged as a significant ignition source. Power lines have caused some of the most destructive wildfires in California, Oregon, Colorado, and other western states. The 2018 Camp Fire, ignited by Pacific Gas & Electric transmission lines, killed 85 people and caused $16.5 billion in damages. The 2021 Marshall Fire, sparked by a telecom line, destroyed 1,084 homes in suburban Denver.

The Wildland-Urban Interface (WUI) has expanded by 40% since 1990, placing more communities at risk. Current building codes in most WUI jurisdictions do not require fire-resistant construction materials or defensible space. Early warning systems cover less than 15% of high-risk areas. Federal wildfire response forces rely on aging aircraft and inadequate coordination frameworks.
