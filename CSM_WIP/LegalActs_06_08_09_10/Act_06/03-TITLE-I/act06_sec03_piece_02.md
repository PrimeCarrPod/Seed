## SEC. 303. PURPOSES OF TITLE I

**SEC. 303.** The purposes of this title are to:

**(1)** Establish a national wildfire prevention strategy that prioritizes proactive risk reduction over reactive suppression.

**(2)** Dramatically scale vegetation management on federal, state, and private lands to reduce hazardous fuel loads to safe levels within 10 years.

**(3)** Mandate defensible space standards for all structures in the Wildland-Urban Interface.

**(4)** Expand the use of prescribed fire and cultural burning as primary vegetation management tools.

**(5)** Create a dedicated, sustainable funding mechanism for wildfire prevention that is insulated from suppression budget pressures.

**(6)** Build a world-class wildfire prevention workforce through training, recruitment, and retention programs.

**(7)** Deploy advanced ignition detection and prevention technologies across all high fire risk areas.

**(8)** Establish accountability metrics and reporting requirements to ensure prevention programs achieve measurable risk reduction.

## SEC. 304. DEFINITIONS FOR TITLE I

**SEC. 304.** In this title:

**(1) "Hazardous Fuel"**
Any vegetative material, live or dead, that constitutes a fire hazard and threat to public safety, property, or natural resources, including but not limited to dead trees, dense understory, invasive species, and ladder fuels.

**(2) "Fuel Break"**
A strategically located barrier of reduced fuel density designed to slow or stop fire spread, including but not limited to shaded fuel breaks, defensible space zones, and firebreaks.

**(3) "Prescribed Fire"**
Any fire intentionally ignited in accordance with a written prescription plan for the purpose of achieving land management objectives, including vegetation management, ecosystem restoration, and wildfire risk reduction.

**(4) "Cultural Burning**
The intentional use of fire by Indigenous peoples for cultural, spiritual, and land management purposes, consistent with Traditional Ecological Knowledge and practices developed over millennia.

**(5) "Community Wildfire Protection Plan (CWPP)"**
A plan developed by a community in consultation with federal, state, and local agencies that identifies and prioritizes areas for hazardous fuel reduction treatments, recommends measures to reduce structural ignitability, and recommends measures to assist community preparation and evacuation.

**(6) "Fire-Adapted Community"**
A community that has taken proactive measures to reduce wildfire risk through vegetation management, building code enforcement, defensible space compliance, and emergency preparedness planning.

**(7) "Ignition-Resistant Construction"**
Building methods and materials that meet or exceed the standards established under Sec. 310 of this title, designed to resist ignition from ember exposure, radiant heat, and direct flame contact.

**(8) "Prevention Treatment"**
Any activity undertaken to reduce wildfire risk, including vegetation management, defensible space creation, fuel break construction, prescribed fire, and ignition-resistant construction.
