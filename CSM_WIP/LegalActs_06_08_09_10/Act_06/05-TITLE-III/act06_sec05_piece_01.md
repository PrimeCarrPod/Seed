# TITLE III — VEGETATION MANAGEMENT

## SEC. 501. SHORT TITLE

**SEC. 501.** This title may be cited as the "National Vegetation Management Act of 2026."

---

## SEC. 502. FINDINGS SPECIFIC TO VEGETATION MANAGEMENT

**SEC. 502.** The Congress finds the following with respect to vegetation management:

**(1) Fuel Load Crisis**
Decades of fire suppression have created historically dangerous fuel loads on federal, state, and private lands. The USDA Forest Service estimates 80 million acres of national forestland require immediate vegetation management treatment. Current treatment rates address less than 2% of the annual need.

**(2) Treatment Backlog**
The backlog of acres needing vegetation management exceeds 100 million acres across all ownerships. At current treatment rates of 3 million acres annually, full treatment would take over 30 years — far too slow to address the current crisis.

**(3) Ecological Benefits**
Vegetation management is not only a wildfire prevention tool but also an ecologically beneficial practice. Thinning overcrowded forests improves forest health, increases drought resilience, enhances wildlife habitat, and promotes biodiversity.

**(4) Economic Opportunity**
Vegetation management creates economic opportunities in rural communities. Timber harvesting, biomass utilization, and ecosystem restoration provide jobs and revenue. Every million acres treated creates approximately 15,000 jobs.

**(5) Climate Benefits**
Healthy, well-managed forests sequester more carbon than overstocked, fire-prone forests. Vegetation management reduces wildfire carbon emissions and enhances long-term carbon sequestration.

**(6) Water Security**
Vegetation management in watersheds protects water quality and supply. Post-fire erosion contaminates water supplies for millions of Americans. Proactive vegetation management is more cost-effective than post-fire watershed restoration.
