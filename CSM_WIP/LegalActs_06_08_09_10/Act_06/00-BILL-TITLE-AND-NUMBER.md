## BILL LONG TITLE

A BILL

To establish a comprehensive national program for wildfire prevention, grid resilience, and fire-hardened infrastructure, to authorize funding for vegetation management and firebreak construction, to create standards for fire-resistant building materials in the wildland-urban interface, to modernize wildfire response capabilities, and for other purposes.

**SECTION 2. CONGRESSIONAL FINDINGS**

The Congress finds the following:
(1) Wildfires have increased in frequency, intensity, and destructiveness over the past two decades, with the 2020-2025 period recording the six largest fire seasons in U.S. history.
(2) Electrical infrastructure has been identified as the ignition source for multiple catastrophic wildfires, including the 2018 Camp Fire (85 fatalities, $16.5B damages) and 2021 Marshall Fire (2 fatalities, $5B damages).
(3) Climate change is extending fire seasons and increasing the geographic extent of fire-prone areas.
(4) The Wildland-Urban Interface (WUI) has expanded by 40% since 1990, placing more communities at risk.
(5) Grid hardening and vegetation management have proven effective in reducing ignitions but remain underfunded.
(6) Early warning systems and fire-resistant building materials can reduce structure loss by up to 80%.
(7) A coordinated national approach is needed to address wildfire risk across jurisdictional boundaries.

**SECTION 3. PURPOSES**

The purposes of this Act are to:
(1) Establish a dedicated funding mechanism for wildfire prevention and grid resilience
(2) Mandate vegetation management standards for utility rights-of-way
(3) Update building codes for fire resistance in the WUI
(4) Deploy advanced early warning and detection systems
(5) Modernize federal wildfire response capabilities
(6) Create economic incentives for fire-hardened infrastructure
(7) Protect lives, property, and natural resources from catastrophic wildfire

## DEFINITIONS AND SCOPE

### Key Definitions

**"Wildland-Urban Interface (WUI)"** — The zone where structures and other human development meet or intermingle with undeveloped wildland or vegetative fuels, as mapped by the USDA Forest Service SILVIS laboratory.

**"Vegetation Management Plan (VMP)"** — A documented plan for the systematic management of vegetation within utility rights-of-way and on federal lands to reduce wildfire ignition risk, consistent with the standards established under Title I of this Act.

**"Fire-Resistant Building Material"** — Any construction material that meets or exceeds the Class A fire resistance rating under ASTM E84 and has been certified under the Fire-Resistant Material Certification Program established under Sec. 302.

**"Grid Hardening"** — The physical modification of electrical infrastructure to reduce ignition risk during high-wind, low-humidity events, including but not limited to covered conductor installation, undergrounding, rapid fault isolation, and fast curve switching.

**"Public Safety Power Shutoff (PSPS)"** — The preemptive de-energization of electrical lines during extreme fire weather conditions, subject to the standards and protocols established under Sec. 204.

**"Defensible Space"** — The area within 100 feet of a structure where vegetation and combustible materials are managed to reduce fire spread potential, as defined by the California Department of Forestry and Fire Protection (CAL FIRE) or equivalent state authority.

**"Microgrid"** — A group of interconnected loads and distributed energy resources within clearly defined electrical boundaries that acts as a single controllable entity with respect to the grid.

**"Covered Conductor"** — An electrical conductor encased in a thick layer of insulating material rated to withstand momentary contact with vegetation without arcing or ignition.

### Geographic Scope

This Act applies to:
- All 50 states and the District of Columbia
- All U.S. Territories (Puerto Rico, Guam, USVI, American Samoa, Northern Mariana Islands)
- Tribal lands subject to federal trust responsibilities
- Federal lands managed by the Department of Interior and USDA Forest Service

### Temporal Scope

This Act takes effect upon enactment, with implementation phases as specified in Title VII.

   - Adds §1786 establishing federal vegetation management standards

2. **National Forest Management Act of 1976 (16 U.S.C. §1600 et seq.)**
   - Amends §1604 to require wildfire risk assessment in all forest plans
   - Adds §1615 establishing community firebreak grant program

3. **Federal Power Act (16 U.S.C. §791a et seq.)**

   - Adds §824w establishing grid hardening cost recovery mechanism

   - Adds §4332a establishing categorical exclusions for emergency vegetation management

(b) PENDING APPLICATIONS.—Applications for grants or assistance under existing law that are pending on the date of enactment shall be processed under the terms of existing law unless the applicant elects to apply under this Act.

### Grandfathering

Structures built prior to the effective date of Title III shall be exempt from the building code requirements for a period of 5 years, provided the owner implements defensible space requirements within 2 years.
