## WILDFIRE PREVENTION AND GRID RESILIENCE ACT OF 2026

---

## PURPOSE OF THE BILL

The Wildfire Prevention and Grid Resilience Act of 2026 (WPGR Act) establishes a comprehensive national program to reduce wildfire risk through vegetation management, grid hardening, building code modernization, early warning deployment, and response force modernization. The Act authorizes $46 billion over 10 years, fully offset by carbon pricing, subsidy redirection, and green bond proceeds.

---

## THE PROBLEM

Wildfires have reached crisis levels in the United States. The six largest fire seasons in recorded history have occurred since 2020. In 2025 alone, wildfires consumed 12.4 million acres, destroyed 47,000 structures, caused 312 fatalities, and generated $89 billion in direct damages. Climate change is extending fire seasons, increasing the geographic extent of fire-prone areas, and intensifying fire behavior beyond historical norms.

Electrical infrastructure has emerged as a significant ignition source. Power lines have caused some of the most destructive wildfires in California, Oregon, Colorado, and other western states. The 2018 Camp Fire, ignited by Pacific Gas & Electric transmission lines, killed 85 people and caused $16.5 billion in damages. The 2021 Marshall Fire, sparked by a telecom line, destroyed 1,084 homes in suburban Denver.

The Wildland-Urban Interface (WUI) has expanded by 40% since 1990, placing more communities at risk. Current building codes in most WUI jurisdictions do not require fire-resistant construction materials or defensible space. Early warning systems cover less than 15% of high-risk areas. Federal wildfire response forces rely on aging aircraft and inadequate coordination frameworks.

## BILL OVERVIEW — TITLE I: WILDFIRE PREVENTION

**National Wildfire Prevention Fund** ($15B/10yr mandatory)
- $8B: Federal lands vegetation management (USFS, BLM, NPS)
- $4B: State and Tribal wildfire prevention grants
- $2B: Community firebreak infrastructure
- $1B: Research and development (fire behavior, suppression technology)

**Vegetation Management Mandates**
- 100-foot clearance around all utility rights-of-way in high fire risk areas
- Annual inspection and certification requirements
- Federal standards superseding inconsistent state requirements
- Tribal sovereignty respected through government-to-government consultation

**Community Firebreak Infrastructure Program**
- Grants for shaded fuel breaks, fire roads, and strategic firebreaks
- Priority for disadvantaged communities and environmental justice areas
- 50% federal cost share (75% for disadvantaged communities)
- Coordination with existing land management plans

---

## TITLE II: ELECTRICAL GRID HARDENING

**Grid Resilience Program** ($12B/10yr mandatory)
- $7B: Undergrounding and covered conductor deployment
- $3B: Advanced fire detection and isolation systems
- $2B: Microgrid and distributed energy resource deployment

**Key Requirements**
- All new construction in high fire risk areas must use covered conductors or undergrounding
- Existing infrastructure must be hardened within 10 years
- Public Safety Power Shutoff (PSPS) protocols standardized nationally
- Rapid fault isolation required on all lines in high fire risk areas

**FERC/NERC Coordination**
- FERC directed to establish wildfire prevention reliability standards within 18 months
- NERC directed to develop grid hardening audit protocols
- States retain authority over implementation timelines

## TITLE III: FIRE-RESISTANT BUILDING STANDARDS

**Building Code Updates** ($8B/10yr discretionary)
- National WUI building code developed by NIST in consultation with ICC
- Class A roof assembly required for all new construction in WUI
- Ember-resistant vents (1/16" mesh minimum) required
- Non-combustible siding and decking materials required
- Dual-pane tempered glass windows required

**Structure Hardening Retrofit Incentives**
- Tax credits up to $15,000 per structure for fire-resistant retrofits
- Low-interest loans (1% APR) for low-income homeowners
- Grants for multi-family affordable housing retrofits
- Priority for structures in highest fire risk zones

**Defensible Space Requirements**
- 100-foot defensible space required for all structures in WUI
- Property owners responsible for maintenance
- Local governments authorized to enforce and bill non-compliant properties
- Financial assistance for low-income property owners

---

## TITLE IV: EARLY WARNING AND DETECTION

**National Early Warning System** ($3.5B/10yr discretionary)
- Satellite-based thermal detection (GOES-R, JPSS, commercial)
- Ground-based camera networks with AI fire detection
- Smoke and particulate monitoring stations
- Integration with NOAA weather forecasting

**Community Alert Systems**
- Wireless Emergency Alert (WEA) integration
- Redundant communication pathways (satellite, cellular, radio)
- Multilingual and accessible alert formats
- Annual testing and public education campaigns

---

## TITLE V: RESPONSE MODERNIZATION

**Federal Wildfire Response Force** ($6B/10yr discretionary)
- Next-generation air tanker fleet (20 aircraft)
- Type 1 helicopter fleet expansion (50 additional helicopters)
- Hot shot crew expansion (50 additional crews)
- Night operations capability (rotary wing)

**Interagency Coordination**
- National Wildfire Coordination Center (NWCC) expanded
- Joint operations protocols with DOD
- International mutual aid agreements (Canada, Australia, EU)
- Standardized training and qualification requirements

## CONCLUSION

The Wildfire Prevention and Grid Resilience Act of 2026 represents a comprehensive, fully-funded national response to the wildfire crisis. With $46 billion in authorized spending over 10 years, the Act will:

1. **Reduce wildfire ignitions** through mandatory vegetation management and grid hardening
2. **Protect communities** through updated building codes and defensible space requirements
3. **Save lives** through advanced early warning and detection systems
4. **Modernize response** through fleet expansion and interagency coordination
5. **Create jobs** through infrastructure investment and workforce development
6. **Protect property values** through risk reduction and insurance market stabilization
7. **Advance environmental justice** through prioritized funding for disadvantaged communities

The Act is fully offset, with no net cost to the federal government. The benefit-cost ratio of 5.9:1 demonstrates strong return on investment. Implementation begins immediately upon enactment, with full deployment within 10 years.
