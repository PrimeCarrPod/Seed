# TITLE IX — ECONOMIC IMPACT STATEMENT

## SEC. 1101. SHORT TITLE

**SEC. 1101.** This title may be cited as the "Wildfire Prevention and Grid Resilience Economic Impact Act of 2026."

---

## SEC. 1102. FINDINGS SPECIFIC TO ECONOMIC IMPACT

**SEC. 1102.** The Congress finds the following with respect to economic impact:

**(1) Wildfire Economic Toll**
Wildfires cost the U.S. economy $50-100 billion annually in direct damages, suppression costs, and indirect economic losses. The 2025 fire season alone caused $89 billion in direct damages.

**(2) Prevention Return on Investment**
Every dollar invested in wildfire prevention returns $5.90 in avoided damages, based on Congressional Budget Office analysis. Grid hardening costs $1.5M per mile but prevents $8.5M per mile in wildfire damages.

**(3) Insurance Market Collapse**
Major insurers have withdrawn from 12 western states due to wildfire risk. The California FAIR Plan has seen exposure increase from $12B to $180B in 3 years. Without intervention, an estimated 8 million homeowners will be uninsurable by 2030.

**(4) Economic Disproportionality**
Wildfire impacts disproportionately affect low-income communities, communities of color, and Tribal nations. 72% of structures destroyed in 2024 were in communities with median income below the state average.

**(5) Job Creation**
Wildfire prevention and grid hardening investments create significant employment. Every million acres treated creates approximately 15,000 jobs. Grid hardening creates 20 jobs per mile of line hardened.

**(6) Fiscal Impact**
Wildfire prevention investments reduce federal disaster spending, reduce state and local emergency response costs, and protect property tax bases in fire-prone areas.

## SEC. 1103. PURPOSES OF TITLE IX

**SEC. 1103.** The purposes of this title are to:

**(1)** Assess the economic impact of this Act on individuals, businesses, and governments.

**(2)** Identify the costs and benefits of this Act.

**(3)** Ensure that the economic benefits of this Act are equitably distributed.

**(4)** Minimize the economic burden of this Act on low-income households and disadvantaged communities.

**(5)** Maximize the economic return on investment of this Act.

**(6)** Provide for ongoing economic monitoring and reporting.

---

## SEC. 1104. DEFINITIONS FOR TITLE IX

**SEC. 1104.** In this title:

**(1) "Economic Impact"**
The effect of this Act on the economy, including costs, benefits, and distributional effects.

**(2) "Cost-Benefit Analysis"**
A systematic process for calculating and comparing benefits and costs of a project or program.

**(3) "Return on Investment (ROI)"**
The ratio of net benefits to costs, expressed as a percentage.

**(4) "Disadvantaged Community"**
Any community identified as disadvantaged under the Justice40 Initiative, CalEnviroScreen, or equivalent state screening tool.

## SEC. 1105. COST-BENEFIT ANALYSIS

**SEC. 1105.** (a) IN GENERAL.—Not later than 1 year after the date of enactment, the Secretary of Agriculture, in coordination with the Secretary of Energy and the Congressional Budget Office, shall conduct a comprehensive cost-benefit analysis of this Act.

**(b) CONTENTS.—**The cost-benefit analysis shall include:

**(1) Costs**
(A) Federal expenditures by program and activity
(B) State and local expenditures
(C) Private sector costs (utilities, homeowners, businesses)
(D) Administrative costs
(E) Compliance costs

**(2) Benefits**
(A) Avoided wildfire damages
(B) Avoided suppression costs
(C) Avoided health costs
(D) Avoided environmental damages
(E) Job creation and economic activity
(F) Insurance premium reductions
(G) Property value protection
(H) Ecosystem service benefits

**(3) Return on Investment**
(A) Overall ROI
(B) ROI by program
(C) ROI by region
(D) ROI by time period

**(c) REPORT.—**The Secretary shall submit the cost-benefit analysis to Congress within 1 year of enactment.

## SEC. 1106. ECONOMIC IMPACT ON INDIVIDUALS

**SEC. 1106.** (a) HOMEOWNERS.—

**(1) COSTS.—**Homeowners in the WUI may incur costs for:
(A) Defensible space creation ($2,000-5,000)
(B) Fire-resistant retrofits ($15,000-25,000)
(C) Insurance premium increases (without intervention)

**(2) BENEFITS.—**Homeowners in the WUI will receive benefits from:
(A) Reduced wildfire risk
(B) Insurance premium reductions (30-50%)
(C) Property value protection
(D) Federal grants and tax credits

**(b) LOW-INCOME HOUSEHOLDS.—

**(1) PROTECTIONS.—**Low-income households shall receive:
(A) 100% cost coverage for defensible space creation
(B) 100% cost coverage for fire-resistant retrofits
(C) Exemption from utility grid hardening fees
(D) Priority for federal assistance

**(2) BENEFITS.—**Low-income households will receive benefits from:
(A) Reduced wildfire risk
(B) Property value protection
(C) Job creation in wildfire prevention
