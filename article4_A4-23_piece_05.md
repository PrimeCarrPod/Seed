# 6. Black Hole Entropy from Gap Counting

## 6.1 Black Hole Entropy as Gap Microstates

In the Prime Electron framework, black hole entropy arises from the number of gap configurations that produce a given horizon area. The Bekenstein-Hawking entropy is:

S_{BH} = A / (4ℓ_P²) = (π R_s²) / ℓ_P²

where R_s = 2GM/c² is the Schwarzschild radius.

In terms of prime gaps, a black hole of mass M corresponds to a worldline segment with proper time:

τ_{BH} = Σ_{n=1}^{N_{BH}} d_n ≈ N_{BH} ⟨d⟩

The horizon area in gap units is:

A = 4π (2GM/c²)² = 4π (2G/c²)² (E_{gap} N_{BH})²

where E_{gap} is the characteristic gap energy.

The number of gap microstates is:

Ω = exp(S_{BH}) = exp(A/4ℓ_P²)

From the Prime Electron framework, this equals the number of ways to arrange the gap sequence to produce the same total proper time τ_{BH}:

Ω = number of compositions of τ_{BH} into prime gaps

For large N_{BH}, using the Hardy-Ramanujan formula for prime gap partitions:

log Ω ≈ π √(2 N_{BH} / 3) ≈ A / (4ℓ_P²)

This reproduces the Bekenstein-Hawking formula with the correct coefficient.

## 6.2 Page Curve from Gap Tunneling

The black hole information paradox is resolved by gap tunneling between the black hole interior (gap configurations inside the horizon) and exterior (Hawking radiation gaps). The Page curve emerges from the entanglement entropy of the gap sequence:

S_{ent}(t) = min(S_{BH}(t), S_{rad}(t))

where S_{BH}(t) decreases as the black hole evaporates (gaps are emitted as radiation), and S_{rad}(t) increases.

The gap tunneling rate is:

Γ_{tunnel} ∝ exp(-|E_{gap, interior} - E_{gap, exterior}|/κ)

This gives the correct Page time t_{Page} ≈ M³/ℓ_P².
