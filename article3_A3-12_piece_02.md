# A3-12: Quantum Simulation from Prime Gaps — Piece 02
## Fermionic Simulation via Jordan-Wigner on Gaps

The 256 gap values {d = 2, 4, ..., 254} map to 128 fermionic modes via the Jordan-Wigner transformation. The prime gap distribution determines the site-dependent chemical potential and hopping amplitudes.

**Construction A3-12.2 (Gap Fermion Mapping).** Let c_d^†, c_d be fermionic creation/annihilation operators for gap value d. The Jordan-Wigner map on the 8-qubit space is:
```
c_d = (Π_{d'<d} Z_{d'}) (X_d - iY_d)/2
c_d^† = (Π_{d'<d} Z_{d'}) (X_d + iY_d)/2
```
The number operator n_d = c_d^† c_d = (I - Z_d)/2 measures occupation of gap d.

**Theorem A3-12.3 (Fermi-Hubbard from Prime Gaps).** The Hamiltonian
```
H_FH = Σ_d μ_d n_d + Σ_d J_d (c_d^† c_{d+2} + h.c.) + U Σ_d n_d n_{d+2}
```
with μ_d = ℏ/κd (from A1-17), J_d = J_0 P(d) (gap-probability-weighted hopping), and U = 0 (free fermions) is exactly simulated by the prime gap system. The interaction term U is generated by the gap-gap correlation Hamiltonian H_int = Σ_{d,d'} V_{dd'} n_d n_{d'} with V_{dd'} = V_0 C(|d-d'|) from A3-05.

**Lemma A3-12.4 (Gap Distribution as Chemical Potential).** The prime gap probability P(d) acts as an effective chemical potential: sites with high P(d) (small gaps d=2,4,6) have low energy μ_d ∝ 1/d and are preferentially occupied. The Fermi level is determined by the total particle number N = Σ_d ⟨n_d⟩. For the physical electron (A1-02), N = 1 (single fermion on the worldline).

**Connection to A3-05 (Entanglement).** The fermionic entanglement entropy S_F = -Tr(ρ_A log ρ_A) for a bipartition of gap values matches the gap entanglement entropy from A3-05 Piece 03. The single-particle density matrix ρ_{dd'} = ⟨c_d^† c_{d'}⟩ has eigenvalues given by the occupation numbers n_k = 1/(e^{β(E_k - μ)} + 1) where E_k are the H_hop eigenvalues.

**Exact Diagonalization.** H_FH is quadratic (U=0) and diagonalized by the Bogoliubov transform, which coincides with the PG-QFT V_{PG} from A3-10. The single-particle energies are ε_k = μ_k + 2J_k cos(2πk/256) where μ_k = Σ_d μ_d e^{2πi k d/256} and J_k = Σ_d J_d e^{2πi k d/256}. These are precisely the PG-QFT eigenvalues λ_k = Σ_d P(d) e^{2πi k d/256} with μ_k ∝ 1/λ_k.

**Simulation of Fermionic Phase Transitions.** Varying the ratio J_0/μ_0 drives a transition from a gapped phase (small gaps dominate, μ ≫ J) to a gapless phase (large gaps contribute, J ≫ μ). The critical point is at J_0/μ_0 ≈ 1, corresponding to the transition where the correlation length ξ = 150 diverges. This is the prime gap analog of the metal-insulator transition.
